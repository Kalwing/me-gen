**Summary:** Part 2 of a breakdown of the SSV Normandy SR-1: the Tantalus drive core,
the Internal Emission Sink stealth system, the ship's extraordinary cost, its otherwise
standard frigate armament and ~50-person crew, and its service history from the Eden
Prime mission in 2183 through the Battle of the Citadel to its destruction over Alchera
by the Collectors. Part 1 covers the ship's purpose and interior.

## The Tantalus drive core

At the rear of main engineering is the Tantalus drive core, the vessel's central
nervous system, where power distribution, propulsion, mass effect field generation, and
ship-wide integration converge. Powered by a high-output antimatter reactor, it is
proportionally twice the size of any other ship's drive core — remarkable that it fits
in a frigate hull at all. Its mass effect field manipulation has noticeable
implications for ship stability (per Joker), but it also enables the Normandy's unusual
propulsion: generating controlled mass distortions the ship "falls into," reducing
reliance on heat-emitting thrusters during maneuvering. The ship keeps fusion drives
and conventional thrusters for combat and high acceleration, but the Tantalus system
gives it quiet mobility conventional vessels cannot match.

## Internal Emission Sink (IES)

For most of galactic history shipboard stealth was considered impossible: heat from
reactors, life support, and operations stands out against the near–absolute-zero
background of space. The Normandy temporarily stores waste heat in high-capacity
internal lithium heat sinks embedded throughout the hull.

Constraints are strict. It cannot be used during FTL travel, where relativistic effects
push emissions beyond the system's ability to compensate. Even at sublight speed the
ship is still vulnerable to direct visual confirmation or a sustained sensor lock; in
practice most engagements rely on long-range scanning, so the Normandy can run silent
for limited periods — typically several hours at full stealth output, longer if
drifting without maneuvering. Eventually all stored heat must be vented; failure to do
so means catastrophic internal temperature build-up and loss of the ship and crew.

Together the Tantalus core and IES give the Normandy its defining capability: sustained
deep-space operation with minimal detectability.

## Cost and armament

This came at extraordinary cost — estimated around 120 billion credits, roughly the
drive-core production cost of thousands of smaller fighters or one heavy cruiser. Rear
Admiral Mikhailovich called it an extreme allocation of resources for a single
prototype. The Normandy was built not for mass production but to test the limits of
stealth, mobility, and independent operation in Council space.

Otherwise she is equipped to standard Council-space frigate specification: standardized
GARDIAN point-defense lasers, kinetic barrier shielding, a spinal mass accelerator
cannon, and optional Javelin-class disruptor torpedoes (limited in availability).

## Crew

About 50 highly specialized personnel: commanding officer and executive officer, chief
navigator, chief medical officer, chief engineer, and marine detail commander, plus
bridge officers, CIC systems operators, technical and engineering crew, marine
security, support specialists, and a dedicated requisitions officer for equipment
distribution and logistics.

## Service history

The Normandy began service under Alliance officer Lieutenant Commander Elisander;
tensions with Turian Chief Engineer Octavio Tatum led to a reassignment, and Captain
David Anderson took command shortly after.

The ship was named for the 1944 Allied invasion of Normandy in World War II. After
trial runs — including an unofficial test flight by her eventual pilot Jeff "Joker"
Moreau — she launched on her first mission, to Eden Prime, in 2183.

During that deployment the Normandy became tied to the rise of humanity's first
Spectre, Commander Shepard. After the discovery of a conspiracy to revive the Reapers,
the ship was assigned as Shepard's command platform to pursue Saren Arterius, who —
with the geth and indoctrinated agents — had retreated into the Attican Traverse and
Terminus systems where conventional fleets struggle to operate. The pursuit culminated
in the Battle of the Citadel, where the Normandy played a critical supporting role in
the final confrontation with Sovereign.

Her service ended abruptly later that year during operations against remaining geth
forces: the Collectors overwhelmed her with firepower far beyond what she was built to
withstand. Severely damaged, she was destroyed above the planet Alchera, and her
wreckage became a symbol of both sacrifice and the limits of even the most advanced
Alliance and Turian engineering.
