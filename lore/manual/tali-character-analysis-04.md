**Summary:** Part 4 of a character analysis of Tali'Zorah. Covers Mass Effect 3: her
return to the Fleet as an Admiral filling her father's seat, her feelings of
inadequacy, being forced back into "the greater good comes first" when Daro'Xen's use of
Rael's research makes the geth war inevitable, the battle of Rannoch, Legion's
self-sacrifice or death depending on Shepard's choice, and — on a botched path — Tali's
suicide as her home ship burns. Also how surviving the ME2 Suicide Mission lets her
name and begin to correct her fatal flaw. Part 5 covers the romance arc and conclusion.

## Admiral Tali

If Tali survived the Suicide Mission, in ME3 she apologizes to Shepard for the trial and
says he was right: "If I were exiled, I couldn't speak out against lunatics like Xen. I
was too ready to sacrifice myself for the good of the Fleet. I need to stop doing that."
She takes an Admiral's seat specifically to push back against the Board's worst ideas.
This is the version of Tali the essayist prefers.

A similar resolution occurs even if she was exiled: she tells Shepard the trial showed
her where she belongs — "on the Normandy, helping you" — keeping her father's honor and
helping her people from the shadows.

## Feelings of inadequacy

Tali doubts her fitness to command: "I'm far too young to be a real Admiral… seventeen
million lives are riding on me and I don't know if I can save them." Only Shepard and
Shala'Raan reassure her. She is now responsible for honoring her father properly while
making sure people like Daro'Xen do not misuse his research — and she implies Rael's
work is what still makes the invasion possible, something that will haunt the flotilla,
and her, for the rest of her life.

## Forced back into the flaw

After speaking with Legion, Tali believes the quarians could sue for peace with the
geth. But once Daro'Xen turns Rael'Zorah's research into "an invasion too good to pass
up," Tali has no choice but to support it — "for the good of the Fleet." Her time back
among the Fleet regresses her, out of necessity, toward the sacrificial lamb of ME2.
She is a victim of politics again, and this time largely powerless.

## The quarian numbers

Tali says there are 17 million quarians in the flotilla, each relying on the others.
Resources are scarce; it is illegal for parents to have more than one child unless the
population is in decline. Every quarian must in some way contribute to the Migrant
Fleet's continuation. Auntie Shala'Raan is the reason Tali is even allowed to try to
retake the Alarei, and the reason the quarians have not destroyed it in her absence.

## Rannoch: the choice

At the climax Shepard must choose between peace, or geth genocide, or quarian genocide:
give the geth full sentience (the Reaper upgrade) at the cost of the quarian fleet, or
destroy the geth to save the quarians.

- **Shepard sides with the quarians:** Tali kills Legion (or shuts the geth down) with a
  "flat shovel blade." She has done what quarian society expects, but — because of her
  history with Legion — she expresses regret, and answers Legion's last question, "Does
  this unit have a soul?", with a solemn "Yes… yes, it does." Legion, in his last
  moments, ignores Shepard and turns all his attention to Tali, the comrade who stabbed
  him — "a mirror image of himself."
- **Shepard sides with the geth:** Tali begs Shepard not to choose the geth over her
  people. If Shepard uploads the code, Tali — as Admiral Tali'Zorah — calls off the
  quarian fleet's attack over the comm, and Legion sacrifices himself to disseminate the
  code ("copy code is insufficient; direct personality dissemination required").
- **Botched path (Shepard failed to broker peace and the war is one-sided):** Tali is
  powerless to stop Han'Gerrel, Rael, and Daro'Xen, whose hubris blinds them; as what
  remains of the quarian fleet burns in Rannoch's atmosphere — Captain Ysra of the
  Rayya over the radio — Tali, having done everything she could, takes her own life
  before her home ship burns. The essayist frames this as the completion of her arc: her
  instilled sense of duty is useless when there is no Fleet to serve, and her unrequited
  love for family can no longer hope for a brighter future.

## Naming the flaw

After Rannoch, aboard the Normandy, if the geth helped rebuild Rannoch Tali admits: "I'd
have killed the geth with no regrets, and I'd have been wrong." She would, like all
quarians, have killed the geth without hesitation when the cards were down; only now,
with her people safe, can she afford to be selfish for once.
