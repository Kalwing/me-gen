**Summary:** Part 3 of a breakdown of the Normandy SR-2: its four antiproton main
thrusters and the Heed Industries Helios thruster module; the oversized Tantalus drive
core and how it moves the ship by "falling into" a mass concentration; FTL travel; the
Internal Emission Sink (IES) stealth system and its limits; planetary probes; the
sensor suite including the Argus Advanced Mineral Scanner; the upgradeable medical bay;
and the Reaper IFF that enabled the Omega 4 transit but let the Collectors ambush the
ship. Parts 1–2 cover origin/EDI and armament; Part 4 covers the Alliance refit.

## Main propulsion

The Normandy's main propulsion is four large antiproton thrusters with aft and forward
exhausts. Antiprotons are injected into a chamber of hydrogen — specifically protium,
the most common, stable hydrogen isotope — and the matter/antimatter annihilation
provides unmatched motive power. (Because a proton is a composite particle of quarks
bound by gluons, proton/antiproton annihilation is more complex than
electron/positron: quarks annihilate to gluons, then the debris "hadronizes" into a
spray of mesons sharing the energy and momentum.) The exhaust is measured in millions
of degrees C — anything caught behind it "melts like wax in a blowtorch." If the
antiproton supply is depleted, the thrusters switch automatically to conventional,
markedly weaker hydrogen/oxygen fuel.

Even here the SR-2 is upgraded: the Heed Industries Helios Thruster Module uses
metastable metallic hydrogen, which burns far more efficiently than liquid hydrogen /
liquid oxygen. Navigators can make the countless small course corrections of a long
voyage without fear of exhausting fuel; a ship on antiprotons can also coast on the
Helios at an inferior but highly sustainable speed, lowering antiproton consumption
(a constant concern for any warship). Once used to the ship's more energetic responses,
a pilot can place the Normandy at any position and angle they want.

## Power and the Tantalus drive core

Power for the drive core and electrical systems comes from a nuclear fusion plant, with
hydrogen fuel cells for auxiliary power if the reactor is shut down.

The oversized Tantalus drive core is integral to the stealth system: it generates a
very powerful mass effect field ahead of the ship's center of mass, and — because of
the relationship between mass, distance, and gravity — the ship is pulled toward that
virtual mass, effectively "falling" into it, moving without heat-emitting thrusters. To
steer to port, the field is shifted left of center; to slow down, it is shifted behind
the ship; acceleration is set by field strength (stronger gravity, higher
acceleration). The core also generates the mass effect field around the ship that
enables FTL by decreasing the mass of a bubble of space-time around it — this gives the
potential for speed but no motive power, so the ship still uses its sublight thrusters
to move within FTL. The SR-2 core is based on the SR-1's but is oriented vertically
rather than horizontally and is three times the size of the SR-1's.

## Stealth (IES)

With the Tantalus core, the Normandy can sink its heat into the hull; combined with
refrigeration of the exterior hull, it can travel undetected for hours or drift
passively for days of covert observation. The stored heat must eventually be radiated
or it builds to levels that would cook the crew. The stealth system does not fool
Collector ship scanners, by means unknown.

The Internal Emission Sink is the ship's most notable feature. For centuries starship
stealth was assumed impossible because routine shipboard heat stands out against the
near–absolute-zero background of space. The IES stores that heat in lithium heat sinks
deep in the hull. Limits: it does not work in FTL (which blueshifts emissions beyond
the sinks' capacity), and even out of FTL a direct visual scan or a look out a window
will reveal the ship — rare, since most ships rely on scanners and spotting a ship in
space is hard. The Normandy can run silent for roughly 2–3 hours, or drift passively
for days, before it must vent and give away its position.

## Probes and sensors

The Normandy fires probes into planetary atmospheres (shielded against re-entry heat)
to recover resources and investigate anomalies; once deep enough the probe becomes
self-propelled, holds altitude, and scans for mineral, compound, and metal deposits,
logging the data for later mining teams before touching down.

The ship uses passive and active scanners — visual, thermographic, radio, radar, and
ladar (laser detection and ranging; narrower field of view than radar, but its
resolution can assemble images of detected objects). The Argus Advanced Mineral Scanner
deploys an orbital multistatic grid of 100 radar-emitting microsatellites to deliver a
global digital elevation model at 15 m per pixel (versus the previous scanner's best of
27 m per pixel); at slower scan speed it can resolve down to 0.001 mm per pixel, ideal
for geological, biological, archaeological, and long-term security work. The distributed
grid of near-untraceable emitters makes Argus virtually immune to electronic
countermeasures, and its spherical geometry allows superior cross-sectioning of
targets.

## Medical bay

The med bay can be upgraded with dermal regenerative units that rebuild soft tissue and
remove scar tissue entirely — invaluable deep in the field. It also allows certain
augmentations and cybernetic upgrades (heavy skin weave, bone weave, muscle weave,
biotic amplifier upgrades) to be performed on consenting crew.

## The Reaper IFF

The Reaper IFF (Identification Friend or Foe) is Reaper technology that allows safe
passage through the Omega 4 mass relay, presumably by making the relay use more accurate
mass transit methods. Shepard's squad recovered it aboard the derelict Reaper orbiting
Mnemosyne and integrated it into the SR-2. When installed and activated, the IFF
transmitted the Normandy's location and locked up its systems, letting the Collectors
ambush the ship and abduct most of the crew — forcing Joker to link EDI to the ship's
main systems.
