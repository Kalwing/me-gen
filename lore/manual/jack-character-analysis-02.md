**Summary:** Part 2 of a character analysis of Jack: the psychology behind her behavior
aboard the Normandy — her fixation on the past (tattoos, haircut, minimal clothing as a
test of intent), her conditioned response to killing, her "no regrets" outlook, the loss
of Murtock and the survivor's guilt it left, and the arc by which a full romance,
letting Aresh live, destroying the Teltin facility, teaching at Grissom Academy, and
reunions in Mass Effect 3 lead her to the family she always wanted. Part 1 covers her
origin and history. Much of this is the author's interpretation.

## State on joining the Normandy

Jack arrives broken: psychologically tortured and conditioned to enjoy killing, then, on
escaping her captors, used and discarded by everyone she met. She lives on edge, waiting
for her "friends" to betray her.

## Fixation on the past

Jack refuses to move on from her past. Her tattoos mark prisons she has been in,
memorable kills, personal reasons, and some "just because." Her haircut is from the cult
she ran with. She wears little clothing deliberately — believing nearly everyone just
wants to use her for sex and discard her, she uses it to read people's true intentions.
Kelly Chambers notices this: "She pushes people away yet approaches sex casually… I
wouldn't be surprised if she makes advances on you. If you want her respect, think
twice." Her body is a record she keeps so she never forgets to trust no one — tattoos
where others would have scars, each telling a story, each holding her in a kill-or-be-
killed mindset.

## Desensitization to death

Given her childhood, Jack is entirely desensitized to death, killing, and crime —
Cerberus built a conditioned (Pavlovian) link between narcotic euphoria and killing, so
where most people feel regret, she feels bliss. She kills without hesitation: "Every
time someone dies and it's not me, my chances of survival go up." She considers it
simpler, in a hostage situation, to kill the hostage.

## "No regrets"

The analysis frames her no-regrets stance as bordering on narcissism — a refusal of
accountability. Asked whether she would do anything differently: a flat "No… There's no
reason I should be alive, but I am. Instinct. It's worked for me so far and I'm not
going to change." Combined with the desensitization and the fixation on the past, this
points to the real wound: she wants a family and to be accepted, but pushes everyone
away first, believing relationships are a waste of time because everyone has used her.

## Murtock

Everyone used her except one man, Murtock, whom she loved and ran jobs with. After
robbing a heavily guarded weapons frigate they were separated; Murtock chose to come
back for her rather than take the score and run. Jack reached a shuttle and escaped;
Murtock was left behind. Days later the shuttle played a pre-recorded message set to
trigger if he never checked in — he speaks of the future they were meant to have and
how much he loves her. Jack outwardly denies fault ("You feel, you get sloppy… he found
out"), but shows survivor's-guilt signs — irritability, angry outbursts, obsessive
thoughts. Her summary of relationships: "You let someone get that close, it just means
they need a shorter knife. Lonely and alive works just fine, thanks."

## The romance and the softening

If Shepard talks with her repeatedly, Jack assumes he wants sex. If Shepard sleeps with
her early, she cuts off conversation — "nothing to talk about" — and the trust is
broken. If Shepard declines her advance, she is confused, and her shell begins to
crack. Non-sexual, genuine interest opens a gap in her armor.

## Aresh and the Teltin bomb

Wanting to move on, and thinking violently, Jack decides to blow up the facility that
made her. Shepard takes her back to Teltin on Pragia with a bomb. There she finds
another survivor, Aresh — a dark reflection of herself, also stuck in the past, but who
wants to restart the facility to give the suffering meaning. Jack wants to kill him;
Shepard can argue, "He's trapped in his past. You need to move on from yours… your past
doesn't have to control you." Letting Aresh live, destroying the facility, and choosing
not to kill gives Jack a revelation — Aresh showed her what she could have become, and
she resolves not to get stuck like that.

## Opening up

As Shepard keeps showing genuine care, Jack cannot understand it but slowly softens:
"It's just gonna hurt, or you'll mess around, or I'll end up killing you or something…
and that sucks." With continued emotional support a relationship forms; she admits
Shepard was right and that she really does need someone. In place of a sex scene, they
only kiss and lie in bed together — Shepard holding her — which, to her, means the
world.

## Grissom Academy and Mass Effect 3

After the Collector Base and Shepard's surrender to the Alliance, Jack is alone again —
until the Alliance offers her a teaching post at Grissom Academy training biotic
children ("Teltin if it were run by good people"). She becomes the positive figure for
kids that she never had. She now wears more clothing and grows her hair out — signs of
moving on. When the Alliance wants her students used as biotic artillery, she argues for
a defensive, protective support role: the old Jack would have relished commanding
destructive biotics; this Jack chooses to protect them.

Reunited with Shepard, she says his attitude rubbed off on her, that her students and
Shepard are the closest thing to family she has ever had, and grudgingly that "maybe
your little suicide squad taught me something about teamwork." During the Reaper War the
two are separated again by duty; on shore leave at a bar, doubt briefly reverts her to
her old self before Shepard again refuses to leave her.

To show her love, she gives Shepard the thing most personal to her — a tattoo. Asked if
she has ever tattooed anyone else: "Nobody else mattered enough." She inks a smaller
version of her back tattoo on Shepard's right shoulder blade, and confesses the reason:
so she can identify him "barely breathing, lying under a pile of rubble at the ass end
of this war."

## Conclusion

Jack ends the trilogy with happiness and a family — her students, Shepard, and those she
fought beside against the Collectors. The damaged "Psychotic Biotic" criminal learns she
is stronger than her past, and — crucially — makes the effort to change herself, moving
past her temper, her fixation on the past, her desensitization to death, and her
self-protective narcissism.
