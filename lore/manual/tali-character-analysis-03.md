**Summary:** Part 3 of a character analysis of Tali'Zorah. Covers her Mass Effect 2
loyalty mission: the treason trial staged over her father Rael'Zorah's illegal geth
experiments, the Admiralty Board's use of her as cover for the real fight (war with the
geth over Rannoch), the discovery of her father's body, her standoff and reconciliation
with Legion, and what the essayist identifies as her fatal flaw — an irrational drive to
be the sacrificial lamb for the greater good. Also the "betrayal" path where Shepard
submits the evidence against her wishes. Part 4 covers Mass Effect 3 and Rannoch.

## The trial as politics

Tali is charged with treason and put on trial before the Admiralty Board. In her
absence the Board strips her ship-name and renames her "vas Normandy" (after a human
vessel) to undercut her plea of innocence. Talking to the Admirals reveals the trial is
really about whether the quarians should go to war with the geth and retake Rannoch;
Tali's supposed treason is, in Zaal'Koris's words, "only peripherally related."

- **Zaal'Koris** — does not hate Tali, but wants a precedent set against "radical
  militants" like her father.
- **Daro'Xen** — cares only because Rael may have learned something valuable from the
  geth; she wants to "return the geth to the control of their rightful masters, the
  quarian race."
- **Han'Gerrel** — an old friend of Rael's; respects Tali for her help against Saren,
  but is set on war.
- **Shala'Raan** — recused herself due to her relationship with the family; can only
  call in favors to help.

Captain Kar'Danna of the Rayya (Tali's home ship) wishes he could do more but must stay
officially neutral; if Tali is exiled he is deeply disappointed in her. Most ordinary
quarians recognize the charge as a farce born of fear and want to help her — she is too
careful to have brought active geth to the Fleet by mistake.

Shepard can lean on Tali's past service to get her acquitted of all charges. Bringing
Legion to the trial makes the quarians openly hostile and does not help her case,
though it adds the geth perspective on the coming war.

## Legion and compromise

Keeping Legion after ME2 breeds conflict with Tali (the quarians have been at war with
the geth for 300 years). The two were written to be foils: Tali is the lens on quarian
politics and their dedication to defeating the geth; Legion is BioWare's attempt to
humanize the geth. After both complete their loyalty missions, a charisma check lets
Shepard defuse a standoff — Legion had scanned Tali's omni-tool to warn the geth of
quarian weapons tests; Tali wanted to protect the Fleet. Talked down, Tali agrees to
let Legion send non-classified data, and says she now understands why Legion would go
behind Shepard's back to protect his people, "just as Tali would do for hers." She is
willing to compromise for the sake of the mission.

## Rael'Zorah's death

The Admirals spring news of her father's death on her in the middle of the trial.
Fighting to the bridge of the Alarei, she finds his body and clings to hope he has "one
last trick" — a stasis program — before accepting it. She apologizes for her own
behavior though her grief is justified, then bottles up her emotions, refuses to
recover the body, and focuses on the mission: "Leave him. The mission comes first. It's
what he would have wanted." The essayist reads this as out of character for someone who
has shown such respect for the fallen — evidence that Rael's lesson ("serve and protect
the Fleet no matter the cost") overrides her nature when he is gone.

## The evidence — and her fatal flaw

The loyalty mission proves every charge against Rael'Zorah true: he deliberately
networked geth to run weapons tests, breaking the Fleet's most sacred laws. Tali wants
the evidence suppressed even at the cost of her own exile: "I can't go back into that
room and say that my father was the worst war criminal in our people's history." If
exiled, his name would be struck from every ship's registry and he would be remembered
as a traitor and a cautionary tale.

The essayist names this her fatal flaw: an innate, irrational desire to be the
sacrificial lamb for the greater good of her people — instilled by her father, by
Shala'Raan, and simply by growing up in a society where every quarian must stay in
lockstep for the Fleet to survive. Her favorite film, *Fleet and Flotilla* (a romance
between a turian and a quarian who have one night together before duty calls), is her
idealized fantasy of duty and love reconciled — a form of escapism from those very
expectations.

## The betrayal path

If Shepard submits the evidence to exonerate Tali against her wishes, she is cleared but
loyalty is lost, and she has a high chance of dying on the Suicide Mission. She is
furious not because she was proven innocent, but because Shepard denied her the final
act of personal agency — the self-sacrifice she wanted — before a mission she does not
expect to survive: "I spent my whole life watching my father make decisions I didn't
agree with… I didn't need another father back there. I needed a friend." She still backs
Shepard, but asks him not to talk to her unless he needs something. She can no longer be
romanced in ME2 or ME3 on this path.
