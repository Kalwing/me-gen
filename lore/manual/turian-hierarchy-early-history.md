**Summary:** A reconstruction of Turian civilization before first contact with the
Citadel Council: their evolution on Palaven, the rise of a unified meritocratic
global culture, the expansion that fragmented into the Unification Wars, the
Hierarchy's forced intervention and reunification, the origin of colony facial
markings, and the enormous standing navy that existed centuries before the Turians
met the Asari and Salarians around 704 CE.

## Palaven and Turian evolution

The Turians are an avian-like species that rose to prominence on the third planet of
the Trebia system — the world that would become Palaven. They evolved to withstand
the intense radiation Palaven receives because of its weak magnetic field, and likely
faced little competition from other predators, assuming dominance of their world
relatively quickly.

A distinctive pressure on Turian culture is that they are a dextro-based organism in a
galaxy dominated by levo-based life. This adversity is thought to have shaped the
entire culture around the idea that nothing is ever handed to them and everything must
be earned.

## Known to the Protheans, spared by the harvest

More than 50,000 years ago, before the dawn of their civilization, the early Turians
were known to elder spacefaring species such as the Protheans, but were considered too
primitive to be of any concern to the Prothean empire. As a result they survived the
Reaper harvest of that cycle.

## The proto-Hierarchy

There is no firm timeline for when early Turians began forming tribes and crafting
tools, but the earliest signs of a more sophisticated civilization appear around
13,000 BCE. The English translations of Turian nouns suggest the proto-Hierarchy
formed along lines similar to imperialist humans such as the Romans — a parallel the
Mass Effect writers clearly intended, even if in-universe it would be coincidence.

Many civilizations probably rose and fell across Palaven's history, but unchecked
imperialism eventually took hold: by the time the Turians took their first steps into
space they already had a single unified global culture, built on hierarchical
meritocracy. That form of governance had a high potential for misuse, but it was
tempered by a strong sense of civic duty and personal responsibility instilled from a
young age. Military service was not merely mandatory — it was considered the highest
form of merit, and every Turian wanted the chance to represent and defend the
Hierarchy. This produced a society wholly united under a single cause.

## Expansion and the Unification Wars

By 500 BCE the Turians had mastered spaceflight and pushed beyond their home system,
discovering several mass relays and founding colonies. They spread so fast that the
Hierarchy's influence over the new colonies wore thin. Local colonial chieftains,
known as Primarchs, began seeking autonomy. Without the central government's
galvanizing influence, colonies grew isolated and xenophobic. Colonists took to
wearing emblems or facial markings to distinguish themselves from other colonies, and
open hostility became common.

Around 300 BCE this erupted into open war — the beginning of the Unification Wars. The
Hierarchy officially stayed neutral at first, keeping to a strict policy of diplomacy
and refusing to intervene militarily. The conflicts were sparked by nationalistic
pride but were almost always fought over disputed territory. Years passed with little
progress; collateral damage rose sharply as the number of factions fell. With no end
in sight and no real demands beyond "give me all your territory," the Hierarchy finally
had to intervene to stop the continuing loss of Turian life.

After years of fighting, the Primarch chieftains were too weak to resist and had
likely lost the support of their own people. When terms were offered, they were forced
to end the conflicts and renew their allegiance to the Hierarchy. Peace was restored,
though it took several decades for the animosity between colonists to fade.

## The legacy of colony markings

To this day most Turians still wear the facial markings of their home colony. Lacking
markings is looked down on: the Turian term "bare-faced" means someone beguiling or not
to be trusted, and is also slang for a politician.

## A war machine without a war

Reunited under the Hierarchy, Palaven and its colonies returned to being a prosperous
nation with the military at its center. Despite having no enemies to fight, the Turian
navy expanded to enormous size, culminating in at least 32 individual fleets. The
Turian war machine needed no war to keep turning.

## First contact with the Council

About a thousand years later, around 704 CE, the Hierarchy made first contact with an
alien civilization: a conglomerate of species led by the Asari and Salarians known as
the Citadel Council. The Council welcomed the Turians and offered immediate membership
in Citadel space — an offer that, as the Turians would learn, came with an ulterior
motive.
