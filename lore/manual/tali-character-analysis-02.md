**Summary:** Part 2 of a character analysis of Tali'Zorah. Covers Mass Effect 2: her
older, more confident look and the name change from "nar Rayya" to "vas Neema," her
command of a squad of quarian marines, Haestrom and Kal'Reegar, the Normandy crew's
regard for her, and her two parental figures — Auntie Shala'Raan (family) and her father
Rael'Zorah (duty) — plus quarian immunodeficiency and suit-linking as the culture's
deepest gesture of trust. Part 3 covers the treason trial and her fatal flaw.

## Appearance and name

In ME1 Tali's suit is squared-away black and deep purple; in ME2 she has a gold neck
piece and a looser hood — "letting her hair down," older and more confident after two
years, and quietly showing individuality (she is one of the few quarians who
personalizes her suit). Her name has changed from Tali'Zorah nar Rayya (the "nar" marks
a quarian who has not completed the Pilgrimage) to Tali'Zorah vas Neema, reflecting
completion of her Pilgrimage. She has moved up in quarian society and, at 25, commands
her own squad of quarian marines — showing the leadership maturity she gained serving
with Shepard.

## Haestrom and Kal'Reegar

When Shepard recruits her for the Suicide Mission, on Haestrom, her whole squad is
willing to die for her — including Kal'Reegar ("We don't have enough people on our side
for you to take one for the team. Stand down."). She questions whether the losses were
worth the research the Admiralty Board demanded: "A lot of people died here. Some of
them were my friends. All of them were good at their jobs. That damn data better be
worth it." In the *Lair of the Shadow Broker* DLC, surveillance shows her trying and
failing to write letters home to the families of the men who died protecting her, saving
draft after draft.

## The Normandy crew

Tali is well-liked by the whole crew across all three games; nobody hates her. Engineer
Adams's dialogue establishes she is exceptionally intelligent and good with technology.
In Mass Effect 3, at the end of the quarian arc, Garrus is the only person to show a
hint of anger toward Shepard if Tali dies for the geth — "her choice, that'll be harder
to get over. She was a good friend."

## The Ashley parallel (brief)

The essayist draws an extended comparison between Tali and Ashley Williams: both come
from career-military families whose name and history shaped the children's careers (a
grandfather who surrendered to aliens at Shanxi in Ashley's case; a father tried
posthumously for treason in Tali's). Both overcome xenophobic hang-ups while serving on
the Normandy, both lost people under their own command, both are prone to
self-sacrifice to preserve another's honor, and both enjoy "sappy" poetry, musicals,
and literature (Ashley recites Tennyson's *Ulysses*; Tali loves the musical
*Fleet and Flotilla*). If Shepard romances Ashley in ME1 and Tali afterward, Ashley
uniquely approves — "Tali's like a sister."

## Two parental figures

- **Auntie Shala'Raan** — Admiral Shala'Raan vas Tonbay, a friend of Tali's father and a
  blood relative (her sister was Tali's mother). Tali treats her as family and hugs her
  warmly. Raan is willing to sacrifice her own power and call in favors to help family:
  "Do you know how many favors I had to call in… I wore holes in my suit to get you that
  opportunity." She represents the *family* side of quarian society.
- **Rael'Zorah** — Tali's father, a high-ranking figure who became distant by burying
  himself in his work; per Raan, he showed his love by working to give Tali "a home
  world." He confuses duty to the Fleet with love for his daughter — "the good of the
  people comes first; my father taught me that." He represents the *duty* side.

Between them, Tali is torn between an ingrained sense of duty to the Fleet and a
capacity to think as a loving individual.

## Suit culture and trust

Quarians live in their suits even among family. A simple suit rupture can kill. When
Raan took the infant Tali from her mother and sealed her in a sterile "bubble," Raan
and Tali's mother had first linked their suit environments to be in the same clean room
for the birth — an act that made both of them sick for a week. Linking suit environments
is the most intimate thing two quarians can do; it makes both parties badly ill,
potentially fatally, and is the culture's ultimate gesture of trust and acceptance.
Children do not wear suits until adolescence; getting one's first suit is a
coming-of-age.
