**Summary:** Part 5 of a character analysis of Tali'Zorah. Traces the Shepard romance
from a "stupid little crush" in Mass Effect 1, through the Mass Effect 2 courtship that
climaxes with her removing her mask despite the health risk, to Mass Effect 3 where she
becomes the one reassuring Shepard, the meaning of "home" in quarian culture, her final
words at the London beam run, which ending the essayist believes she would pick, and the
lessons he draws from her arc. Parts 1–4 cover ME1 through Rannoch.

## The crush (ME1)

At some point in ME1 Tali develops a crush on Shepard that she believes she can never
act on — they are too different, and she is not even a full adult by quarian standards.
She tells herself that helping her prove herself to her people will have to be enough,
and makes peace with that. Shepard has helped her without caveats or kickbacks — more
than she can say of most of her family — and the fact that a non-quarian can show the
same kindness as her idealized self charms her.

## The courtship (ME2)

After the treason trial, checking on Tali, Shepard finds she "got sloppy" repairing her
suit and is a little sick. She explains that linking suit environments is quarians'
deepest gesture of trust — "I haven't trusted anyone enough for that… except, well." She
stammers and gets embarrassed; Shepard says he feels the same way. Now that years have
passed and she has proven herself to her family, she feels she is Shepard's equal, and
she is the one who finds the courage to raise it, apologizing for having been
"unprofessional… stupid and selfish." She worries that wanting Shepard is itself selfish
and could make her sick and jeopardize the mission.

Before the Omega 4 relay she works up the nerve to be with him. Per Kasumi, after the
Suicide Mission Tali cannot stop talking about Shepard — "she took her mask off with
you; that took a lot of trust." Tali afterward: "I'm running a fever, I've got a nasty
cough, and my sinuses are filled with something I can't even describe — and it was
totally worth it."

## Miranda toast (ME3)

Tali drunkenly toasts Miranda: "I didn't like her… but I respected her. She did whatever
it took to stop her father. She never gave in to him, never changed herself to please
him." Shepard notes she sees herself in Miranda fighting her father. Tali: "I spent my
life trying to live up to him, then making up for his mistakes… When do we get to stop
reacting to our parents and start living for ourselves?" (This "emergency induction
port" / straw scene is where the running joke about how a suited quarian drinks comes
from.)

## Shepard reassures her (ME3)

Where earlier Shepard bolstered Tali, in ME3 she is the one who steadies him after
Thessia and the Cerberus base, when he starts to doubt himself: "When I feel that way,
I reach for you." She is arguably the only squadmate who asks whether Shepard is okay.
Before the assault on Earth: "I backed you when I was just a kid on my Pilgrimage. I
backed you when the Normandy was a Cerberus ship. Wherever you go, I'm with you."

## "I have a home"

If Rannoch is won and Tali is romanced, she chooses to leave with Shepard rather than
stay — the peace and quiet only reminds her of everyone she lost. She gives Shepard a
photo of her unmasked face, drinks with "my human boyfriend — my father would have hated
you," and parties with the crew.

Her last words at the London beam run are to call Shepard her home and beg him to
return alive. "Home" carries enormous weight for a quarian: exiled for 300 years, they
built Rannoch into a mythical place worth throwing countless lives at, and wrote poems
about its rock formations and grasslands. "Keelah se'lai" roughly translates as "by the
homeworld I hope to see one day." For Tali, calling someone your home means more than
"I love you," and Shepard has been her home since she was a kid on Pilgrimage.

## Which ending she'd pick

The essayist reasons: Tali would never choose Refuse (standing by while her fleet
burns). Destroy is the least desirable for her because it would also wipe out the geth
now helping rebuild Rannoch and risk the quarian liveships disabled in Earth orbit — but
it is viable, especially if Shepard had already destroyed the geth on Rannoch. Control
would eventually force her to keep putting down synthetic rebellions forever. Synthesis
would appeal most to a Tali who lived to see quarian–geth peace. But factoring in
Shepard's influence — whose goal was always to destroy the Reapers — the essayist
concludes she picks Destroy, ending the Reaper threat "with a few shots from a
carnifex."

## The lessons

- Do not be afraid to get involved in things bigger than yourself — but be ready to bail
  if things go bad.
- Do not be afraid to inspire change.
- Never forget the people who helped you become who you are; repay them out of kindness,
  not obligation. Your truest friends are the ones who will go to the ends of the
  universe for you — never forsake their help.
- Remember the lessons your parents taught you, but notice when they do not practice
  what they preach. Lessons of kindness matter most.
- Be ready to sacrifice yourself for the greater good — but within reason, and sparingly.
- Duty to a country or cause is important, but not more important than the individuals
  within it. Find the middle ground between duty and kindness and stay on it.
