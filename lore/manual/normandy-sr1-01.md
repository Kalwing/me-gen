**Summary:** Part 1 of a breakdown of the SSV Normandy SR-1: a stealth frigate
co-developed by the Systems Alliance and the Turian Hierarchy under Citadel Council
sponsorship, built for long-range reconnaissance and covert strike-team deployment, and
a symbol of post–First Contact War cooperation. This part covers the ship's design
philosophy and a deck-by-deck tour of the interior. Part 2 covers the Tantalus drive
core, the stealth system, cost, armament, crew, and service history.

## Origin and purpose

The Normandy was the first ship of its class, co-developed by the Systems Alliance and
the Turian Hierarchy under the sponsorship of the Citadel Council. Coming less than
three decades after the First Contact War, the joint project represented a remarkable
level of trust between two civilizations that had first met on the battlefield —
whether meant as a political gesture, a practical partnership, or both. It also let the
young Alliance integrate its technological infrastructure with the millennia-old
civilizations of Council space.

Rather than a conventional warship, the Normandy was optimized for long-range
reconnaissance and independent operations deep in unstable regions such as the Attican
Traverse and the Terminus systems. She is a compact three-deck stealth frigate built
around technologies that suppress energy emissions and reduce detectability across
multiple sensor types, letting her operate alone in hostile territory on missions no
other ship could attempt.

## Deck 1 — Bridge and Combat Information Center

Unlike most Alliance vessels, which centralize ship functions in one command space, the
Normandy distributes its core systems across the deck.

At the bow is the main bridge, more like an aircraft flight deck than a naval command
center, with the pilot's station in the middle and a secondary seat for co-pilot and
navigation. The cockpit has an internal kinetic barrier to protect the pilot from
vacuum if air pressure is lost, so the pilot can keep attitude control even if command
is compromised. Just behind the pilot is the main docking hatch — where most of the
crew embark and disembark — and, across from it, an escape pod dedicated to bridge
personnel.

A central walkway lined with workstations connects the bridge to the CIC. These
stations are likely integrated into tactical coordination and combat interfaces for
real-time control of the ship's defensive and offensive systems; the ship's modular
architecture lets specific roles shift with the mission rather than being fixed.

The CIC itself is cavernous, dominated by a large central console that from above
resembles an Alliance insignia, with the galaxy-map interface at its center for issuing
orders and choosing destinations in real time. Unusually, the commanding officer's
station sits toward the rear of the CIC rather than centrally — widely attributed to
Turian command doctrine, which favors elevated oversight with the commander slightly
removed from the operational floor. As a prototype, the Normandy was partly a test of
how this hybrid command structure performed under human-led operations; it proved
effective.

At the stern of Deck 1 is the communications room, used for post-mission debriefings
and as a secure channel for the CO to speak directly with Alliance Command and, when
required, the Citadel Council.

## Deck 2 — Habitation

Reached by stairwells on either side of the CIC, Deck 2 holds the crew's living areas:
the mess hall, crew sleeper pods, the captain's private cabin and office, escape-pod
access, and the medical bay. Behind the med bay is a small science lab that doubles as
auxiliary storage. The medical room has a medi-gel dispenser and several beds; lockers
here are likely for senior personnel — Commander Shepard's personal locker is on this
deck.

The habitation deck looks compact for the stated crew size. Aside from the sleeper pods
(used for stasis during crew rotations and non-relay flights) and the captain's double
bed, there are no obvious full-time bunks, and no clearly visible galley, expanded
storage, bathrooms, or exercise areas — implying either additional infrastructure
outside the visible layout or crew-support functions distributed in ways not shown.

## Deck 3 — Cargo, vehicle bay, and engineering

The elevator at the stern of Deck 2 descends to Deck 3: the cargo hold, the M-35 Mako
storage bay, and main engineering. The open space needed for vehicle storage and
maintenance makes this a multi-purpose area, housing an armory, a workshop, and storage
lockers for strike-team equipment. The Alliance requisitions officer is stationed here,
overseeing equipment through the forward garage bay beneath the bow.

Down the corridor from the hold is the engine room — a single platform for the
engineering staff with three main monitoring consoles — and, at the rear, the ship's
most important system: the Tantalus drive core (covered in Part 2).
