**Summary:** The Destiny Ascension was the flagship of the Citadel Fleet and the pride
of asari engineering — a dreadnought four times the size of the largest human vessel,
crewed by up to 10,000 asari under Matriarch Lidanya. Enormously expensive to operate,
it functioned mainly as a deterrent. During the Battle of the Citadel in 2183 its
drives were disabled by the geth and it had to be rescued by the Alliance Fifth Fleet;
it later fought in the battle to retake Earth.

## Origin and scale

The Ascension is unmatched in scope among Mass Effect dreadnoughts — no other ship
comes close — and only the fleets of the Citadel could field one. It is primarily of
asari origin, of similar design to most of the asari fleet, and fully crewed by up to
10,000 asari personnel, including its commanding officer, Matriarch Lidanya.

Citadel-race technology does not vary much, because most of it was reverse-engineered
from the same source (the Citadel and mass relays), yet the Ascension's design is
clearly asari-first. Its specifications are highly classified. A recording in the
Citadel archives shows that the asari vessel that first discovered the Citadel was of
similar design; there is no proof it was the Ascension itself, but it shows the class
dates back to at least 580 BCE.

Diplomats and guests given six-hour tours report seeing no more than 10% of the ship.
One volus visitor claimed it has more firepower than the entire asari fleet combined.
It is four times the size of the largest human vessel, and its huge crew makes it
essentially a mobile city.

## Armament and defenses

Like most dreadnoughts it is most effective at range. Its main gun, a super-heavy mass
accelerator cannon, can score a critical hit on virtually any known vessel in the Milky
Way.

For defense it uses Citadel space's standardized General Area Defense Integration
Network (GARDIAN) — automated anti-missile and anti-fighter laser turrets on the
exterior hull that need little crew management. Unless overburdened they can repel
attack indefinitely, but the point defenses can be brought down by cyber warfare and
overwhelming force, as happened at the Battle of the Citadel. That makes the ship an
easy target for smaller close-combat frigates and fighters, so it needs a small support
fleet.

## Mobility and doctrine

It carries an FTL drive, conventional thrusters, and backups such as ion engines and
antiproton drives. Its size makes deploying those drives very expensive, and running
the vessel costs more than its long-distance value is usually worth — though it is
quite capable of taking the fight to an aggressor. In practice it serves as a
deterrent: the hope is that an aggressor would choose diplomacy rather than risk war
with Citadel space.

## Battle of the Citadel, 2183

By the events of the first game the Ascension is used mainly to defend the Citadel
itself. When Sovereign's geth fleet attacks in 2183, the Council is evacuated to the
Destiny Ascension per standard emergency procedure, but it fails to escape — its main
drives are quickly taken offline by geth forces through overwhelming force and cyber
warfare, leaving the ship helpless until the arrival of the Alliance Fifth Fleet
(if Shepard orders the rescue).

If it survives, the Ascension spends several months in repairs, then is sent on a
20-colony "victory cruise" through 2185 — arguably propaganda, the Council doubling
down on justifying the ship's existence when its battle performance suggested resources
would be better spent on smaller support ships or Alliance/Turian-scale dreadnoughts.

## Reaper War

The Ascension proves a formidable asset in the war with the Reapers, finally used for
its true purpose of large-scale ship-to-ship combat in the battle to retake Earth.
