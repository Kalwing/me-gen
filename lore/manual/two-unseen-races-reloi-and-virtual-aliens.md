**Summary:** Two minor Mass Effect species that never appear in-game, known only from
Cerberus Daily News items around 2185–2186: the Reloi, an avian race from the planet
Tervest who briefly joined the galactic community and then withdrew rather than face
the Reapers; and an unnamed race of "virtual aliens" who fled a supernova ~8,000 years
ago as roughly a billion uploaded consciousnesses aboard a computer-run starship, and
who negotiated asylum by inhabiting the bodies of volunteers.

## The Reloi

The Reloi are an avian, spacefaring race, known to the elcor long before they were known
to the wider galaxy. According to Cerberus Daily News (a Mass Effect 2 feed), they were
discovered by the asari in 2184 and welcomed to the galactic community in 2185:
council-sponsored shuttles arrived at Tervest, the Reloi homeworld, bearing visitors and
gifts of goodwill. After the welcoming ceremonies a Reloi delegation was sent to the
Citadel to learn galactic law, culture, history, alien biology, and mass effect
technology.

The contact ceremonies caused an outbreak of an alien flu among the Reloi, which
prompted them to wear environmental suits in the presence of other species.

In 2186 the Reloi delegation withdrew completely from the Citadel and isolated
themselves on Tervest, both unwilling and unable to fight the Reapers. A species
representative named Arrika stated: "We have no wish to battle those who have yet to
wrong us, nor have we the capability." The Reloi also planned to destroy their orbital
satellites and observation equipment, hoping the Reapers would take them for a
pre-spaceflight civilization and pass them over. Whether this worked is unknown — the
rest of the galaxy was preoccupied with the war.

There are no official depictions of the Reloi. All that is established is that they are
avian in appearance, possess feathers, and would likely have been seen in environmental
suits around other species because of the flu outbreak.

## The "virtual aliens"

This race has no known name; they are referred to only as the virtual aliens.

About 8,000 years before the trilogy, their homeworld's star was about to go supernova.
To survive, they built a starship equipped with a network of supercomputers and
transferred the consciousnesses of roughly one billion of their people into it, along
with an entire virtual world for them to inhabit. An AI was left to pilot the ship and
maintain the simulation. The ship then drifted through space for millennia.

In 2185 the starship entered a salarian-inhabited system, seeking help because the
energy source keeping the ship and the virtual world running was failing. When the
salarians found the "ghost ship" they initially feared it was the vanguard of a Reaper
attack because of the onboard AI. Contact with the AI was first made by a human
exobiologist, Dr. Jordan Deitweiler, who learned of the virtual world, its population,
and the energy problem.

The Citadel Council was wary of the AI and reluctant to help. Deitweiler entered the
virtual world to warn its inhabitants and returned with an offer: the virtual aliens
would trade their advanced technology and knowledge in exchange for assistance. The
Council classified all matters involving the ship as top secret, but is understood to
have assisted in the end.

About a month later it emerged that a representative for the virtual aliens, Ambassador
Sygan, had been using Dr. Deitweiler's body to visit the Citadel and speak with the
Council — revealing that the virtual aliens could re-enter the real world in the bodies
of people who entered their world. Sygan sought asylum, saying she was one of many who
wished to leave the virtual world. A volunteer took Deitweiler's place so he could
return to his own body while his was used by Sygan. After this was announced, around
400 individuals of various races also stepped forward to swap places with virtual
aliens.

The implication is that in Mass Effect 2 and 3 the player may have encountered some of
these virtual aliens — now living in the bodies of the volunteers — without knowing it.
Those who left the virtual world would, a year later, be caught in the Reaper war.
