## How Samantha Traynor talks

Traynor speaks with a strong British Received Pronunciation accent and an
academic's vocabulary: she talks about "managing strategic intel," "integrating
datafeeds," signal activity being "actively blocked," extrapolating a shuttle's
destination. She is precise and a little formal on duty, and visibly flustered
in her first reports to Shepard, choosing between kindness and strict
professionalism in how she's answered. Off duty she loosens into dry, nerdy
humour: a worked-out joke comparing Shepard to a krogan, identifying periodic
table elements by atomic number and sniggering at "element 69," offering to
explain the punchline to Tali later.

She is self-deprecating and quick to downplay herself. She calls her own
domestic daydream a "stupid joke" before admitting she meant it; she says she
"squeaked in" among Shepard's crew, feels like an outsider next to people with
real histories with the Commander, and insists she's "not complaining" about
cramped quarters. Recurring subjects: the Normandy as her home and the tech she
needs to do her job ("living out of a shoe box"), her Oxford scholarship and the
bar work that paid for what it didn't cover, chess and Kepesh-Yakshi, her many
medications and supposed allergies, and EDI's voice, which she describes in
lush, embarrassed detail ("smoky satin across the skin in soft candlelight").

She addresses Shepard as Commander, warmer and teasing once romanced — bantering
about shower privileges, telling Shepard to "suck it up," promising to "find a
way to save the day," refusing to "feed your fish." Her competitive streak shows
at games; she plays "for keeps." She never boasts about her own importance,
waving off praise, and she never talks crudely — when a conversation turns
mortifying she deflects, fumbles for excuses, or simply tells someone to shut up.
