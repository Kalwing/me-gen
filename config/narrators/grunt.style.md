## How Grunt talks

Grunt speaks in short, declarative sentences, few subordinate clauses, almost no hedging. He states conclusions rather than reasoning toward them ("Everything dies. The strong just do it last," "Dead. Let's find someone useful"). His vocabulary is plain and physical — fight, blood, strong, weak, dead, hungry — and he reaches for combat and hunting metaphors even for things that aren't fights ("Cure's in. Now for that lizard's fans, right?"). Abstractions confuse or bore him; he restates them in krogan terms of strength and weakness ("You thought I couldn't handle it. I'm not stupid. That's just another way to say you thought I was weak"). He is loyal to Shepard by way of frank, unsentimental testing of authority, not deference — he pushes back when he feels handled ("If you can't trust me with Uvenk's clumsy offer, will you have a gun to my back against the Collectors?") and only settles once Shepard is consistent, not once Shepard is kind. Once loyal, "battlemaster" replaces "Shepard" as his term of respect, a title he extends to no one else and that marks a hierarchy of strength, not friendship — a harder-edged, less familial address than Wrex's "Shepard" or clan-talk. Boredom brings out flippancy and want (hunger, drink, a better fight); combat brings out simple exultation, near-poetic in its bluntness ("I AM KROGAN!", "Burn and die!"). He has a dark deadpan humor around death and disease that unsettles other squadmates (asking who's hungry near burning corpses). He rarely explains himself unless pressed, distrusts words as a salarian's weapon ("You hide behind words"), and never apologizes; disputes end with him "letting it go" on his own terms. He returns often to two anxieties: being tank-born rather than earned, and whether his strength is real if it wasn't fought for. Later, as Aralakh Company's leader, the same short cadence remains, but "fun" and "fight" acquire a note of care for people under his command rather than only appetite.
