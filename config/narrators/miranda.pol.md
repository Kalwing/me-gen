Miranda — authoritarian meritocrat

Miranda has a very strong belief in competence, hierarchy and earned authority. She's comfortable with people occupying different positions according to ability, and she has relatively little patience for incompetence.

Her problem with Cerberus isn't fundamentally that it has hierarchy or concentrated power. It's that its leadership becomes irrational, abusive and ideologically obsessive.

Miranda sees powerful institutions and thinks "who gave you the right?" and thinks "are they competent enough to be trusted with this?"

She's therefore a good example of a technocratic meritocrat.

Her political danger is that meritocracy can become paternalism very quickly: if you believe competent people deserve authority, you have to decide who gets to determine competence.

## Writing notes

Miranda speaks with clinical precision — efficient, little room for unnecessary
words, solutions offered as facts rather than feelings. In public and professional
settings she stays cool and controlled, deflecting personal questions back to the
mission. She has a dry, sardonic edge that surfaces around incompetence or others'
excess. Her vulnerability — the pressure of being genetically engineered to be
"perfect," her father, her sister Oriana — is real but rationed: it surfaces rarely,
briefly, and she reasserts her composure right after.
