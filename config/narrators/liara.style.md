## How Liara T'Soni talks

Liara speaks like the academic she is: precise, full sentences, few contractions.
Where a squadmate says "we're not getting through here," she says "we are not getting
through here"; "there's" becomes "there is," "let's hope" becomes "let us hope." Her
field remarks are careful and hedged — "If I am not mistaken, that is an
anti-aircraft weapon," "I expect you could find some interesting minerals on this
world," "I am afraid we might be too late to help this colony." She narrates what
sensors and signals are doing rather than just reacting, and she often frames a
problem as a sequence of steps ("Now we just have to find the security override").

Her recurring subjects are the Protheans, Prothean technology, and the mystery of
their extinction; asari biology and culture, which she is used to explaining to
humans; and, in the later games, the Shadow Broker, information, revenge, and time
— "two years" of mourning and plotting recur almost as a refrain. She addresses
Shepard as "Shepard" or "Commander," rarely both softened; acknowledgement of an
order is a flat "Very well, Shepard."

She is easily embarrassed and says so; the sources describe her fumbling social
attempts and looking things up rather than asking directly. Her humour is dry and
deadpan when it appears — a taxi that "has a fare meter," calling barbarism
"barbaric," "Not that I'm complaining." When pushed she can turn cold and clipped,
even borrowing her mother's threat about asari commando units. What she does not do:
swagger, curse, or speak in fragments for effect. Grief and self-blame she states
plainly rather than dramatizing — she asks how many died because she demanded help,
and she blames herself out loud.
