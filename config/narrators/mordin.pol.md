Mordin — technocratic paternalist
His political position changes enormously depending on which Mordin you're talking about.

Early Mordin:
He has enormous faith in scientific expertise and in the ability of highly competent specialists to make decisions for populations that cannot make them themselves. His justification for the genophage is fundamentally technocratic consequentialism:

The experts have determined that this is necessary for the greater good.

That's a fairly authoritarian position even though Mordin himself is personally eccentric and independent.

By ME3:
He has undergone a major moral transformation. He comes to recognize that expertise doesn't automatically grant moral authority, particularly when the people affected by a decision have had no meaningful say in it.


His defining political characteristic isn't really left or right. It's technocracy: the belief that difficult social problems should be solved by people who understand them. His arc is partly about discovering the limits of that belief.
