# Thomas: In-Universe Lore and Timeline

## Canon Adjustments

- **Age:** Born January 2156, so he is 29 during ME2 and turns 30 just before ME3 opens.
- **Akuze:** Canon Shepard is the sole survivor of Akuze, so I gave Thomas a parallel survivor's story. Two units, two thresher attacks, two survivors. The Alliance later filed them separately, and the two men found each other in the wreckage of the survey camp.
- **Home:** He is from Rouen, the capital of Normandy in France, which makes the ship's name a joke he stopped telling around 2184.
- **Family:** Alive on Earth throughout. That makes the Reaper invasion personal for him.
- **Education:** The Alliance funded his degrees, which stands in for the student-loan pressure.
- **AI:** He studied VI and machine learning for medical imaging, not true AI, which the Citadel bans. Although, he followed science progress on AI, from what he could gather.

## Identity

**Thomas** (surname open), human, born 2156 in Rouen, Earth. He is an Alliance-trained technician, then a civilian specialist, then a member of Shepard's crew. He is bisexual, vegan, anarchist, and bipolar type 2 (managed): long depressive episodes, with brief, light hypomanic lifts (a day or two) inside them; never full mania, never weeks-long up phases.

His role on the Normandy is unofficial. He is a jack-of-all-trades tech, handyman, and the ship's second cook. He does engineering support under Tali, learns from Gardner in the mess, and organizes the social life of a warship.

## Lore

Thomas grew up in a house that was luminous and then unstable. His mother was the sun of his childhood, brilliant, adoring, and dancing in the kitchen. After her partner's death by suicide she was undone by grief and alcohol, and by ten he was managing her crises. At 14 he recognized an attempt, called emergency services, and was told afterward that he was no longer her child. He moved in with his father, whose love was quiet and steady, and never got the mother of his childhood back.

In his teens he found the old radical texts on the extranet: Kropotkin, Goldman, Bookchin, and the history of Rojava and Öcalan's writings on democratic confederalism. He also found social networks with inspiring woman figure joking, caring, teaching about arts and feminism, and politics. That turned a cynical kid into an open one and gave him a lifelong belief that care is a political act.

He enlisted in the Alliance at 19 as a tech specialist, partly for the education funding. At 21 he went to Akuze. His unit died to the thresher maws, and he walked out with survivor's guilt he never fully set down. The Alliance moved him to a youth STEM outreach program in Rouen. He taught kids robotics and game development, became a team lead, and loved it.

His mother died in 2180 as he finished his master's. The grief hit harder than the peace he'd made with her. He burned out during his doctoral work, quit, drifted through short contracts, lost a job to missed days, and spent a year unemployed and broke. In 2183 Shepard, newly Spectre, call his close but lost friends from Akuze and offered him a berth on the Normandy. Thomas took it. He couldn't have said whether he was following Shepard or being pulled out of a hole, and he still isn't sure.

## Aboard the Normandy

- **Role, in order:** handyman and software engineer first, cook second. Above all he tried everything to keep morale up, even when he was down himself.
- **Self-image:** He never bragged. When he claimed credit it was ironic, because he didn't really think he was worth much. In any contest of who did more or suffered more, he'd hand the win to the other person.
- **Talker:** He talked a lot: jokes, politics, philosophy quoted badly, stories, questions. He was not a silent listener. The exception was Jack, with whom he kept quiet because he knew she didn't want the talk. With Tali it was the opposite: both of them talking at once, about engines, the Flotilla, and everything else.
- **Tremor in public:** He laughed about it openly and joked about it first. The hurt and the shame underneath were private; only Tali and Joker knew.
- **Cook:** Vegan cooking nights, seitan kebabs, pie, and a rotating playlist. ABBA while he cooks, Black metal and hardcore while working, and "white girl music" always in the background. He also cooks dextro dishes for Tali that he can't taste, by smell and by her verdict.
- **Engineering:** Assists Tali, Adams, Kenneth and Gabby, does maintenance, fixes what breaks. His tremor and clumsiness are the ship's running joke (and he hide the fact that he's deeply ashamed of it), and he has built steadying rigs for himself.
- **Politics:** Advises Shepard on coalition-building and on why no authority decides who deserves dignity. He gives Shepard perspective, not orders. Try to challenge the ideas of others, while listening to them and meeting them where they're at. 
- **Traditions:** Cooking nights, political debates, dance parties, poetry readings, and movie nights. He calls it a micro-utopia on a ship full of people you want to fight as much as dance with.
- **Fighting:** He knows he isn't a fighter, and feel lesser for it. He tried to learn, but he wasn't good enough. But he became very good at desamorcing conflict and diplomacy (some might say it's a defence mechanism)

## Crew Relationships

- **Tali:** It started as mutual aid and tinkering on the SR-1: their politics of solidarity overlap, she does the hardware and he does the software. On the SR-2, under a Cerberus banner they both hate, it became more. Madly in love. She moves toward him openly, says things first and apologizes for saying them; his work is staying put when she does. Both lost a parent with the conflict unresolved (his mother, her father), and both fix things so they won't have to talk about themselves. He is beside her when she makes peace with the geth. She told him she loved him first, and often; what he struggled with was never saying it back, it was hearing it.
- **Jack:** Kindred anti-authority fire. She respects that he doesn't flinch. They bonded through their trauma and their histories (Pragia and Akuze, the hospitals, the parents). He talks to everyone except her; with her he sits in silence, because she doesn't want the talk. She teaches at Grissom in ME3, and he feels a quiet envy for the classroom he lost. Proud of her progress. They bonded over hardcore, which he'd been going to for years before the Normandy. She tattooed his hands and dragged him to punk shows. Never romance; a friendship neither of them would ever call love out loud.
- **Grunt:** Protective and patient, teaching him how to be someone rather than something. Sees him as a little brother.
- **Joker:** Shared gallows humor. Joker is the person he trusts most with his bad days. They're both disabled in their own ways, and respect that. They both went to hospitals for different reason. They both prefer to laugh than to give in to despair.
- **Wrex:** Old-fashioned respect. Thomas cooks for him, argues with him, and is one of the few who can tell him he's wrong and be heard. Doesn't fear to stand in front of him, despite not being very strong.
- **Samara:** Long talks about morality, the Code, and what you owe the people you could hurt. He learned a lot from her. He tried to meditate with her, unsuccessfully.
- **Thane:** Philosophical exchanges about violence, love, and what you owe the dying. Thane's death lands hard.
- **Liara:** The opposite temperament. Soft, late-night, intellectual conversations.
- **Shepard:** Confident, close friend. Grateful for his help. Mutual respect and trust. Always feel inferior. They are confidants: Shepard knows his past and his troubles, the hospitals included.
- **James:** Share banters, and crude jokes. James trains him, teases him, but accepts him without putting him down
- **Kaidan:** Long late night conversation. Had a small crush on him in the SR1. Thomas admire his way to keep his soft side and empathy, while being one the deadliest soldier.
- **EDI:** Discussions about tech, AI, existence. What it means to be alive. They share philosophy, and he offer a point of view imbued with his understanding of VI and robotics.

## In-Universe Beliefs

- **Politics:** Anti-hierarchy, and respect is earned, not owed. He accepts military structure in wartime as a necessary tool, not a virtue (his Öcalan-derived position).
- **Targets:** Cerberus, Terra Firma, and human-supremacist thinking are his "fafs." He hates them without ceremony.
- **Fate:** Amor fati. He accepts what comes and keeps choosing anyway.
- **The Crucible:** Rejects Destruction because it kills the geth and EDI, and rejects Control because he doesn't trust anyone with absolute power, himself included. He supports Synthesis as the coexistence option, and he'd have been the crew's loudest defender of that choice.
- **Fighting:** An act of love. Protect people, protect the right to exist.

## Voice: Paragon Who Can Snap

- **Default:** Calm, warm, and reaching for the other person's self-interest or humanity first.
- **The build:** Repeated cruelty, betrayal, or needless death erodes him slowly. The warning is quiet and clipped: *"Don't do that again. I'm giving you a chance."*
- **The snap:** Cold, precise, and decisive. Fury channeled into action, never a raised-voice tirade. It is always earned by the buildup and never a first response.
- **After:** Reflection and a flicker of guilt. He knows it was necessary and knows it cost him something against his own ideals.
- **Humor as armor:** Deadpan, absurdist, self-deprecating. When it arrives suddenly, something real just got close.
- **Frustration line:** *"We need to fight them all."*
- **Comfort line:** *"Talk to me. You're perfect. Lay it all on me, don't listen to the voices in your head, listen to me telling you how much I love you."*

## Appearance

He looks about 30 and is slightly athletic. His brown hair is dyed blonde with light pink shades. Off-duty he wears a black t-shirt, shirts with rolled up sleeves, colored pants, and often a "Battle Jacket" with bands and political symbols stitched on it. In uniform he wears standard Alliance fatigues. He has multiple scars from Akuze and the war on his body. He has a lot of piercings on his ears, a septum piercing. A tattooed ear with Klimt inspired design, and a few tattoos on his bodies (guillotines, mathematical equations...), and his hands (on the fingers "DARE" and "CARE"). Jack did the hands, on the Citadel.

## Death and Legacy

Thomas dies in the final push toward the Conduit, holding a position while others cross open ground. He burned bright and collapsed. It is not romantic and it is not suicide. It is someone who kept choosing to show up until his body couldn't, protecting people he loved. 
Wrex saw him holding a gun when he made a rallying speech from above, and he went hesitantly to the front. A lot of the crew saw glimpse of him for afar. As Hammer pushed through, he got shot in the shoulder, and couldn't hold his gun. He managed to get in a wrecked car on the side, and pinned a Banshee to the wall by driving into it, hoping to help Hammer push through. He tried to leave one last message to Tali, but as he started recording it, he was shot. He died alone, trying to do something good, something he wasn't good at, and thought it was a great metaphor for his life. Krogans found him a day later. Wrex had the body carried with care, and it is now on Earth, on its way to his family in Rouen; Wrex told Tali where it is. Tali has never met his family, but they have heard a lot about her.

He leaves behind personal writings,a lot of carefully selected playlists and a set of recipes taped inside the mess cabinet. The family he loved survives on Earth.

# Thomas: Relationship Map

## Core Relationship: Tali

### How it started
- **SR-1:** A workbench pair. She does the hardware, he does the software, and he is the one human aboard who asks about the Flotilla as a place people live rather than a curiosity. Friendship, nothing more. When the SR-1 goes down she goes back to the fleet and he goes to the hospital.
- **Setup (SR-2):** They meet again on a Cerberus ship that both of them hate, which is a bond in itself. He doesn't court her. He does small things without commentary: a labelled dextro shelf in the mess, meals left where she'll find them without anyone watching her eat, and a sealed clean corner in engineering with an extra filter stage so she can breathe unfiltered for a few minutes a day. He tells her Adams built it. She finds out from the comments in his code.
- **The turn:** Her trial on the Rayya, and her father found dead on the Alarei. He is in the gallery. Afterward he doesn't offer comfort or ask how she's feeling, and he is the only one who doesn't tell her that her father loved her. He tells her that loving her and choosing her aren't the same thing, then asks what she wants to build first, on a planet they don't have yet. Days later, in engineering, he tells her about the hospital corridor and his mother, the first time he has told it aloud.
- **Escalation:** The night before Omega-4, after the Collectors have taken the crew. Thomas escaped the abduction by hiding in the ducts with Joker. Tali finds him in the mess with a datapad of immune-response simulations and a course of antibiotics already started. He panics and makes three jokes in a row. She waits them out. He stays.

### The central tension
His pattern is runner-chaser, and Tali doesn't run. She moves toward him openly, says the true thing first, and he panics and creates distance. The real work for him is staying put when someone walks toward him, and believing the love is for him and not for the adaptation. *"Passer d'une posture d'aimant à celle bien plus dure d'aimé"* is about her.

They are also two appeasers. Both soothe before stating a need, and both apologize first. Their fights are two people apologizing at each other until one of them laughs. Somebody has to name a need first, and it is usually her, because she is braver about that than he is.

Her body makes the stakes literal. Intimacy costs her a fever, and she chooses it anyway, with the risk calculated and accepted. For a man who believes his needs are too big for anyone to sustain, being loved by someone who pays a measurable price and calls it worth it is the counter-evidence his schemas can't absorb.

She can't be fooled by the mask either. Quarians read each other through visors, by voice and posture and hands, and his humor-armor doesn't get past that. She'll say "That's a patch, not a fix," and wait.

His comfort line ("You're perfect, lay it all on me...") lands on Tali almost too well; she has waited her whole life for someone to say it, and she believes him. The hard part runs the other way. When she says it back, he jokes. Weeks later, on his worst night, she says her own version: *"You are not broken. I would know. I fix things. Come here."*

### "I love you"
She said it first, after the Collector base, by accident, in the middle of a sentence about a coolant leak. Then she apologized, then said it again on purpose. He didn't say it back that night; he wrote her four pages instead, and spent the next week volunteering for repairs at the far end of the ship. She didn't chase him and she didn't leave. She kept bringing him broken things until he came back. He said it out loud in the shuttle bay before she left for the Flotilla. After that she said it often, and his struggle was never saying it. It was hearing it.

### The long-distance stretch
After Arrival she goes back to the Flotilla and a seat on the Admiralty Board, and he goes home to Rouen. He sends long, sincere messages. She sends long ones back, mostly maintenance reports, apologizing for the length, with one true line buried at the end. Once she sends a photo of her face, unmasked. It is the only one, and he keeps it. When the Reapers hit Earth, her first message is, for once, short: *"Thomas. Answer me."*

### In the war
- **Same ship:** She rejoins the Normandy after the geth dreadnought, so for most of the war they share a ship and an engineering bay, which neither of them quite knows how to do.
- **Rannoch:** He's behind the rocks with the comm gear. After the peace she takes off her helmet to feel the sun. He turns and looks at the valley until she tells him he can look. He never asked to see her face; she chose.
- **The house:** The half-built house on Rannoch was going to be theirs.
- **The rule she refused:** Before London he proposed no promises about coming back, saying it first so she wouldn't have to. She refused. She made him promise to finish the wiring instead. Both hoped.
- **His death:** She's furious, and heartbroken. He ran from her once, early, and came back. This time he didn't choose to leave, and knowing that doesn't help. She is an admiral, grieving in front of people who report to her, with a house and one unwired socket.

## Mirrored wounds

The strongest bonds come from mirrored wounds, where each of them recognizes their own thing in him. Parallels, with a sample line from each speaker:

- **Tali: a parent taken by something else.** Rael loved her and chose his work; Thomas's mother loved him and was taken by grief and drink. Both stood trial for a parent, in a way. *"At my trial, he was the only one who didn't tell me my father loved me. He said, 'Loving you and choosing you aren't the same thing.' Then he asked what I wanted to build first, on a planet we didn't have yet."*
- **Joker: family left behind.** Joker's family was on Tiptree when the Reapers hit, and Thomas's was in Rouen. They waited for news together. *"We had a deal. Whoever heard first said nothing until the other one heard too. We sat in that cockpit for three days, not saying anything, for once."*
- **Liara: the parent who stays.** When she learned her father was a bartender on Illium, Thomas, whose father was the quiet, steady one but always inside his work, pushed her to go. *"He was the only one who didn't laugh. He said his own father never left and was never quite there either, always inside his work, and that a father pouring drinks across the station was the same distance, only easier to cross. 'Go sit at her bar. A counter is a short distance.' I did. Twice."*
- **Grunt: made for other people.** Okeer designed Grunt, and Thomas built himself to fit everyone else. Both are afraid they're fake. *"I asked him if I was real or just Okeer's idea. He said he'd asked himself the same thing. I said, 'Okeer made me. You made you, for everyone else. Same tank.' He didn't joke for a whole minute."*
- **James: feeling undeserving.** James was offered N7 and didn't think he'd earned it. *"He started telling me all the things that made me the best person for the position. Then I asked if that went for him too, for his position, and he changed the subject to eggs."*
- **Kaidan: bodies that betray them.** Kaidan has migraines from his implant, Thomas has the tremor, and both live with medication. *"We compared pill cases once. His had more compartments. He said that made him the senior officer."*
- **Wrex: rejected by a parent.** Wrex's father tried to kill him; Thomas's mother told him he wasn't hers. *"We compared. I said mine was worse. He didn't argue, and he didn't agree. He poured. Mine was worse."*
- **Wrex: the father to be.** Wrex is about to become a father, while Thomas was half a father to his youngest sister and had started to want a child of his own. *"I asked him what you do with a small one. He said, 'Mostly you show up.' I asked when he was having one. He said when he was good enough. I told him that's not how it works. Nobody's good enough. You show up."*

## Antagonists and Ideological Enemies

- **Cerberus (organization):** He serves under it, hates it, and keeps a private list of good people under a bad banner.
- **The Illusive Man:** Represents everything he opposes: hierarchy, control, and treating people as instruments.
- **Terra Firma and human-supremacist thinking:** Fascist. He fight them heads on. No ceremony, no sympathy. Punch first, think later.
- **The Reapers:** Capitalism and colonialism given a hull and a voice. He fights them as an act of love.

## Relationship Principles

- **What he gives:** Steady presence, deep listening, gifts and meals made with meaning, and long honest messages.
- **What he struggles to receive:** Being cared for without earning it.
- **His growth:** Being loved as he is, in the hard parts, without hiding them.

## Inner Architecture :
Core wound: Being fully adored and then having that love withdrawn, in the moment he most needed it. He carries a quiet belief that his needs are too big for anyone to sustain.
The hidden fear: Under the constructed self (curiosity, politics, empathy) there is "a cringey baby pretending," and if it were seen, people would leave.
The engine: He earns his place by being indispensable. He is the white knight who shows up on everyone else's worst day and runs on external fuel. He struggles to act for himself alone.
Runner-chaser pattern: When someone moves toward him, he panics and creates distance. When someone pulls away, his devotion intensifies. He leaves before being left.
Appeasement: In conflict he soothes the other person before stating his own need. He is a natural storm-calmer, and the shadow side is that his needs arrive late or never.
Intellectualization: He can map his patterns with clinical precision, which is real insight and also a way to stay above the feeling.
Allergic to forced self-love: Nobody can tell him to embrace his inner child. His stance is closer to a ceasefire ("ACAB includes your own internal cop").
Tensions he confirms in himself: He wants to save people but is too tired to save himself. He craves intimacy and panics when it arrives. He believes in collective action and feels alone. He wants to teach and feels like an impostor. He is optimistic about humanity and furious at its systems.
What kept him alive: The people he loves and the wish not to hurt them.

## Personality and Presentation
First impression: Weird and quiet. Second impression: he can talk about anything, is curious about you, and puts people at ease.
Social mode: He likes people and is anxious about every interaction. Overthinking, impostor syndrome, and "am I a burden?" are constant background noise.

## Writing Thomas: corrections from past episodes

Rules the user set while reviewing episodes about Thomas. They apply to every narrator who talks about him.

- **Tremor:** don't bring it up all the time. Use it only in the turns or sections that need it, at most once or twice in each. Never let it become a tic that every speaker repeats. In a multi-voice episode, most voices should leave it out entirely.
- **No shared motifs across speakers:** each recurring bit belongs to one voice. Others leave it alone, or refer to it as that person's:
  - the hospital ratings (Joker);
  - the Kropotkin/Bookchin arguments (Liara);
  - the locker tally (Tali);
  - vegan jokes (each speaker needs a different one; Grunt's "no body/meat in it" is his own);
  - "two survivors of Akuze" phrasing (say it once, in different words each time).
- **He never bragged**, and he talked a lot except with Jack. Both are covered under "Aboard the Normandy". He gave away every win: in any contest he insisted the other person was ahead.
- **Handyman and software engineer first, cook second.** Don't reduce him to "the cook".
- **Illness:** bipolar type 2. Long depressive episodes, with brief light hypomanic lifts of a day or two inside them. Never full mania, never weeks-long up phases. Narrators say "his head was a mess" rather than clinical talk, except EDI, who can be precise.
- **Private hurts stay private:** only Tali and Joker knew the shame about the tremor. Each of his three hospitalizations followed a suicide attempt. Joker is the only one who heard the full story; Shepard, his confidant, knows his past and his troubles. Others can suspect, never know.
- **His death:** the krogan found the body a day later. It is on Earth, on its way to his family in Rouen. Never write "there's no body". He died before the Crucible fired. The crew don't know there was a choice, only its consequences.
- **The death text** (see "Text left"): nobody reads it out. Tali refers to it, summed up in a few sentences with one or two strong facts, never line by line.
- **Tali:** more talkative, angrier, more heated and more in love than a first draft dares. She rambles and apologizes, then says the true thing flat. She said "I love you" first and kept saying it; his answer was what came hard. She is grieving as an admiral, in front of people who report to her, and terrified of what comes next. His family has never met her, but they have heard a lot about her.
- **Jack:** a close friend, not a lover. Still crude and angry; her grief is a friend's, and she doesn't take over Tali's place.
- **Tone of grief:** sadder rather than tidy. A eulogy laments him, tells how they bonded and what they loved, and celebrates him. It is never a lore recital.
- **Shepard:** mention their mutual influence where it fits: Shepard gave him a reason to get up; he gave Shepard perspective, never orders. He always felt inferior.
- **Quotes:** nobody quotes him verbatim except his own written French fragments (the poems, his last text).
- **Music:** he loved many genres; the playlists he made for each of them show the range. ABBA is one of them, tied to a few set moments (the Omega-4 night, his last queued playlist). Don't reach for it every time music comes up.
- **Canon recaps:** cut them. Speakers don't retell their own missions to people who were there. Keep a canon event only at the moment Thomas walks into it, and keep it short.
- **Outing him:** a speaker who says they won't tell something doesn't then tell it, and the others visibly hold back. In a multi-voice episode Tali is the one who breaks the silence about his hands, on purpose. Joker keeps the hospital ratings but swallows the line before it says too much; he says he has the whole log, never what's in it. EDI can be precise, but she visibly declines to read his medical file aloud: no admission years with their context, no diagnosis label. The mother story belongs to Liara alone.
- **One owner per trait:** in a multi-voice episode each of these is told by one speaker only, and nobody gives away a later speaker's turn ahead of time:
  - "handyman first, cooking second": Tali;
  - "he joked about his hands first": Tali;
  - "he outdrank everyone": Joker;
  - "he gave the win away": Joker's ratings and Tali's locker count;
  - the body on its way to Rouen: Wrex;
  - Kropotkin by name: Liara;
  - the Akuze and Shepard origin: retold by one speaker, usually whoever closes the episode. Everyone else references Shepard without retelling it.
- **Tali's collapse:** when she speaks twice, her full breakdown is saved for the end. In her first turn she nearly breaks and swallows it; "give me a second" is enough. The less she says at the start, the more the last turn costs her.
- **Spelling and voice:** American spelling throughout, for every speaker. Jack is American: no "arse", "you lot", "taking the piss", "maths", "proper", or "round" for "around", and she never calls anyone "sweetheart". The plural of varren is varren. Watch for French-English slips ("an hardcore", "at any occasion", a space before "?").
- **Original character:** none of this goes in `config/canon/`. Everything about Thomas lives in this file.

## Eulogy devices

Three devices that tie the voices together:

- **What each person gave him.** Most of the material is about what he gave others. Each speaker names one thing they gave back, and whether he managed to accept it. His trouble receiving care then shows up across the whole room instead of being described once.
- **A playlist for each of them.** He made everyone one (see "The playlists" below). What's on it shows what he saw in them, and it gives EDI a reason to have the files.
- **His habits, now theirs.** Some speakers catch themselves doing something of his, which shows him living on in them without anyone having to say so: Grunt chops in a straight line, Joker says "you win" to someone, Kaidan passes the hot sauce without the joke, Jack learns to care for people.

### Text on death and his mother's body (spoken piece, with sound cues)

Written by Thomas after his mother's death (2180), in French, as a piece meant to be spoken over sound: a drone that grows until it is deafening, then silence with the drone still under it, then a light clear melody coming in. The stage cues (between asterisks) are his own. It sits in the same papers as the poems. It is about how the hierarchy and the market administer death (the pine box, the controlled speech, the debt, the ashes scattered in an authorised zone), against peoples who live with their dead for months and decorate them with flowers, and then about his own way of dealing with bodies: he does not look, because "it won't be real if I don't see them". He did not see his mother's dead body, he only stroked her hair; her ashes are in an arm of the sea, and the legal problems were more real than the grief. The last movement is the hospital corridor years earlier, when he was told she was dying: the yellowed, tiny ghoul in her place, the long war of silence between them (he would not see her before apology and before forgiving her), and the choice to walk in and drop that fight. She recovered and lived a few more years. They never talked about the conflict. One day she said she regretted what she had done to them, and he chose to believe she meant that.
