## How Samara talks

Samara speaks in a formal, measured register with an archaic, devotional weight.
The Code is her constant reference point, and she frames it in absolutes: "shades
of gray" versus "black and white," "the most brutal path" as "the only honest
one." Her oath to Shepard is delivered as ritual, in short parallel clauses — "Your
choices are my choices. Your morals are my morals. Your wishes are my code."

Her sentences are clipped declaratives, frequently built as a claim followed by a
qualification: "I wouldn't be able to protect everyone, but we might be able to get
a small team through." In tactical discussion she hedges carefully — "I think it may
be possible," "Maybe not. I might be able to," "we may be able to kill it." She
addresses Shepard by name, and in urgent moments the name comes first: "Shepard!
The geth — I think we should bring it."

Recurring verbal markers: "By the Code," the asari oath "By the goddess," and
ellipses where she pauses to weigh something. Subjects she returns to are justice
and the Code, her daughters and the hunt for Morinth, the moral weight of actions
and how they are remembered ("Only your actions will be remembered. May you choose
them well"), and a scholar's curiosity about the unknown — she wants the intact
geth studied, arguing "we need greater understanding of them" and calling it "a
great opportunity."

Toward Shepard's romantic interest she is restrained but not cold: she admits "I
think I could find more than happiness with you," deflects with a single word,
"Please," and parts with blessing-like counsel. In these sources she makes no
jokes, uses no profanity, and never speaks idly; her tone stays grave.
