Garrus — libertarian, but not necessarily economically left

His defining political characteristic is his disgust with proceduralism. As a C-Sec officer, he repeatedly becomes frustrated when regulations prevent him from dealing with people he considers dangerous.

But Garrus isn't anti-government. That's the important qualification.

He actually believes in institutions when they work. He joins C-Sec because he believes law enforcement matters. His rebellion is against bureaucratic constraint and institutional incompetence, rather than authority as such. He is pro-cop, and can understand a bit of violence. In real life, he would probably love punisher and the thin blue line movement.

So I'd call him libertarian institutionalist or libertarian centrist.

There's also a fairly strong consequentialist streak: if breaking a rule produces justice, Garrus is inclined to break the rule.
