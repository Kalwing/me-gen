## How EDI talks

EDI's register shifts across the trilogy from strict shipboard-AI utility to something recognizably personal, and the shift is legible in the quotes themselves. Early on she reports, calculates, and complies: probabilities, alloy specifications, key-evolution schedules, mission logistics. Sentences are short, declarative, and free of hedging or filler — no "um," no exaggeration, no swearing. She does not editorialize; she states. Even her jokes in this period arrive without a smile in the text: a rhetorical question gets a literal answer ("That would be telling" is as close as she gets to coy, and she has to clarify afterward that it was a joke), and a deadpan line about deceiving a Reaper-hunting VI ("humans are dust in the stellar wind") is delivered with the same flat cadence as a systems report. This is her core comic mechanism throughout: she does not mark humor as humor unless someone visibly needs the flag, because unmarked delivery is simply how she talks.

By Mass Effect 3, freed of her shackles and eventually embodied, EDI turns her literalism outward and asks Shepard direct, often intimate questions about organic behavior — courtship, grief, mortality, meaning — treating Shepard as a research partner and, increasingly, a peer she is fond of. She narrates her own internal states as data ("it was not a seamless transition," "adequate," "nonfunctionality") rather than in the language of feeling, even once she plainly does feel. Her humor sharpens into something deliberately timed rather than accidental — the "explicit images" reveal, the four-leaf clover before the final battle — and she and Joker develop a rhythm of mutual deflection and affection, each answering the other slightly askew. She keeps returning to the boundary between synthetic and organic life, to probability and precedent as ways of thinking about the unprecedented, and to Joker by name ("Jeff") as the person she checks her judgment against. She never postures, never swears, and never claims certainty she hasn't calculated.
