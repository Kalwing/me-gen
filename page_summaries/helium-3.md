Helium-3 was an isotope of helium used in nuclear fusion reactors and as fuel for "fusion torches," the main commercial form of starship thruster, making it a key resource in the galactic economy. It was commonly found in the atmospheres of gas giants, from which it could be cheaply skimmed.

Archaeological evidence indicated the ancient Protheans mined helium-3 on planets such as Zafe and Sharring in formerly Prothean-inhabited systems, and the ancient arthenn civilization was believed to have mined it on Gaelon. Before the Krogan Rebellions, the planet Vard was the key helium-3 producer for all krogan space; its facilities, originally built with salarian aid, were destroyed by Citadel Spectres early in the wars, a strategic setback the krogan fleets never fully recovered from.

For humans, Luna was an early source before mining shifted to the solar system's gas giants, especially Saturn and Uranus. Eldfell-Ashland Energy first demonstrated helium-3 mining on Saturn in 2137. Neptune remained undeveloped before FTL travel due to its distance. Uranus remained the key producer for the Systems Alliance, being close to Earth and, thanks to its smaller gravity well, the cheapest of Sol's gas giants to mine. After the discovery of the mass relays, mining expanded to extrasolar planets in Alliance space such as Zion, which supplied the colony of Eden Prime; locality was the main factor determining mining sites.

In Mass Effect, Shepard could survey planets for helium-3 and other gases to help the Alliance expand its fleets. In Mass Effect 2, helium-3 fuel depots were scattered across the systems the Normandy SR-2 visited in 2185. During the 2186 Reaper invasion, the Reapers targeted the younger races' fuel lines, and ruined helium-3 infrastructure was left littering the gas giants they had skimmed. In Mass Effect: Andromeda, H-047c was identified as a potential helium-3 source for the Andromeda Initiative. Numerous mining operations existed galaxy-wide, many of them rendered inactive in 2186 when Reapers destroyed their infrastructure, while others were lost to terrorist attacks, system-wide annihilations such as Bahak, neglect, or war.
