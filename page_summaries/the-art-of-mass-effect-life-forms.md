This page transcribes the second part of The Art of Mass Effect (2007), with commentary by Casey Hudson. The Life-Forms section covers the design of the game's creatures and alien civilizations, noting that alien animals were made by combining features of earthly animals in strange combinations while sapient aliens needed anatomy capable of human-like emotion.

It discusses the asari (conceived as beautiful "green" then blue "alien girls," exotic yet desirable love interests); the elcor, whose bipedal early stance was judged too human; the geth (originally written as a bat-like species, rewritten as synthetics as the organics-versus-machines theme emerged, given a lithe sinewy look and a single blue lens-flare eye); human clothing and armor; the jellyfish-inspired hanar; the reptilian, war-focused krogan; the quarians, whose scavenged suits echo geth anatomy; the salamander-inspired salarians and their councilor robes; the hawk-like turians, designed to support the villain's intimidating character; the portly, comical volus; the nearly 50-foot but insect-inspired rachni queen and the whip-armed rachni; the Thorian, shifted from plant-like to slug-like; the unused harvester (later reused in Mass Effect 2 and 3); ambient creatures such as gas bags and giant beetles; the dog-and-deep-sea-fish varren; the burrowing thresher maw; and the praying-mantis-based keepers.
