The sixth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary begins with Caroline Livingstone, a touring Western Canadian theater actress and single mother, who was recruited by her friend Shauna Perry to voice a healer in Neverwinter Nights and then became BioWare's in-house producer and director of voice-overs. Her work on "digital acting," well-directed line reads combined with blinking, expressive eyes and motion-captured movement, was central to Mass Effect's emotional impact.

When Mass Effect launched in November 2007 reviews praised its digital acting and characters but criticized the Mako's handling, the combat, and long elevator-ride load times. Hudson felt the game did not initially hit with the impact he had hoped, though a fan letter about a boy with Asperger's syndrome learning to read emotions after playing it signaled something special.

The chapter examines the romances, conceived as a three-act love story across the trilogy, with Ashley, Kaidan and Liara as first-game options and no same-sex relationships, which drew fan complaints and a January 2008 Fox News segment by pundit Cooper Lawrence attacking the game's "alien sex scenes"; Lawrence, who had not played it, later recanted in The New York Times.

It then covers Mass Effect 2's planning: Hudson analyzed feedback from 97 reviews, Drew Karpyshyn left mid-production for Star Wars: The Old Republic, and the team built the "dirty dozen" recruitment structure against the Collectors, culminating in a suicide mission where any character, including Shepard, could die. Walters revived the Cerberus concept, and Hudson defined the Illusive Man (voiced by Martin Sheen) as a self-appointed protector of humanity. A plan to permanently destroy the Normandy was softened to blowing it up and replacing it with the near-identical Normandy SR-2, after the art team defended the Solaris-inspired interior. Corey Gaspur co-wrote a memo proposing combat overhauls. Ashley and Kaidan were deliberately kept largely absent so players would feel the loss of their love interest. Released in January 2010, Mass Effect 2 earned the highest scores EA had ever seen for one of its games. A "By the Numbers" infographic lists endgame survival rates for the squad, noting Thane, Jack and Legion die automatically if the Normandy is not upgraded.
