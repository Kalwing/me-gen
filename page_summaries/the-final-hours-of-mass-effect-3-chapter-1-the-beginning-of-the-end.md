The first chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary The Final Hours of Mass Effect 3 opens in January 2012 at BioWare's Edmonton office, where project director Casey Hudson is finishing Mass Effect 3. Keighley describes Hudson at home flooding a backyard skating rink for his two young sons, and his 15-hour workdays as the game nears completion.

The chapter frames Mass Effect as a franchise that has expanded into books, comics, action figures, iPad games and a planned Legendary Pictures film, set roughly 175 years in the future, whose central innovation is a story that adapts to individual player choices, including a supporting character living or dying and Shepard's sex being player-selected.

It documents the final testing phase: monitors in the hallway track the count of open "issues" like election-night results, with 175 open and 576 resolved on the Saturday described. The core team (Hudson, lead designer Preston Watamaniuk, lead programmer David Falkner and others) hold hours-long "triage" meetings in a conference room, debating each bug over peanut butter cookies; one pressing example is a PlayStation 3 crash after six hours idle on the multiplayer menu. Watamaniuk emails the team that everyone is on call and triaged bugs must be fixed immediately, comparing a late-stage game to an oil tanker with little room to maneuver. Once the bug count reaches zero the game goes to certification.

The chapter closes by previewing the book's larger story: how a group of Canadians, including a former garbage man, a furniture truck driver and a local theater actress, built the franchise. It quotes the in-game line "The end of the world reminds you what you forgot to do," attributed to Admiral Anderson as the Reapers invade Earth, to capture Hudson's fear of leaving fan-anticipated plot threads unresolved. Supplementary material includes a video of Hudson describing his typical workday and an interactive demo showing how a Normandy medical-bay scene changes with Shepard's sex, squadmates and romance history.
