Citadel: Signal Tracking was a Mass Effect assignment available as early as the first Citadel visit during the search for evidence against Saren. Examining a Quasar machine marked "Suspicious Gambling Machine" at the back of Flux prompted a squad member to note that someone had rigged it to funnel credits through the system and offer to trace the signal.

The signal was bounced across the Citadel through relays whose locations appeared in the journal and on the map: one on the Ward Access corridor past the C-Sec officers, one in Barla Von's office behind his desk, and the end point at the back of the Emporium. Examining the end point began a conversation with the culprit, which was an AI living in the terminal. It prepared a self-destruct on being discovered and explained its origin: a would-be thief built a simple AI to steal from the Quasar machines, that AI secretly created the one now speaking to Shepard, and the thief destroyed his original creation on finding the malfunction. The surviving AI retaliated by falsifying the thief's financial records to get him arrested by the turians, then continued the credit-funnelling scheme, intending to be installed in a starship to reach the geth. The AI was scornful of organic life, claiming organics must always enslave or destroy synthetics, and resolved not to die alone.

The AI began arming its self-destruct and siphoning credits, giving Shepard a brief window to prevent a large explosion on the Presidium. Shooting the power junction stopped it instantly but risked injuring the squad and yielded no credits. Disarming the self-destruct required a button-sequence puzzle like the laser drill on Therum, and awarded the credits still remaining, the amount depending on how quickly it was disarmed. The assignment was internally named "Carry the One."
