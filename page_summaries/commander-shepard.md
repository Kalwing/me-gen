Commander Shepard was the customizable player protagonist of Mass Effect, Mass Effect 2, and Mass Effect 3, born April 11, 2154, a graduate of the Systems Alliance N7 program (service number 5923-AC-2826), a Skyllian Blitz veteran, and a biotic after secondary element zero exposure in 2168. Character creation ("Profile Reconstruction") set gender, appearance, first name (John or Jane by default), class (Soldier, Engineer, Adept, Infiltrator, Sentinel, Vanguard), a pre-service history (Spacer, Earthborn, Colonist), and a psychological profile (Sole Survivor of Akuze, War Hero of Elysium and recipient of the Star of Terra, or Ruthless at Torfan). These choices affected dialogue, bonus Paragon/Renegade points, and access to certain assignments; the stock Shepard was an Earthborn Sole Survivor Soldier. A Spacer Shepard could speak with mother Hannah Shepard.

In 2183 Shepard served as Executive Officer aboard the SSV Normandy under Captain David Anderson and was considered as humanity's first Spectre candidate. In Mass Effect 2, the Normandy was destroyed by an unidentified ship; Shepard died recovering Joker, was retrieved by Feron and Liara T'Soni, and was rebuilt over two years by Cerberus's Lazarus Project under the Illusive Man, then agreed to investigate human colony abductions by the Collectors. Facial scars glowed and shifted with Renegade choices unless surgically healed. In Mass Effect 3 (2186), Shepard, under Alliance arrest for working with Cerberus and the Bahak system's destruction, was reinstated by Anderson when the Reapers attacked Earth, and worked to unite the galaxy and build the Crucible while opposing the Illusive Man. Wounded by Harbinger in the final battle, Shepard reached the Citadel and met the Catalyst, who offered endings: Destroy (kills all synthetics including the geth and EDI), Control (uploads Shepard's mind as Reaper master), Synthesis (merges organic and synthetic life), or Refusal (the cycle continues). If the Crucible fired, Shepard became a legend remembered as "The Shepard," with a closest squadmate placing Shepard's name on the memorial wall. The page also noted the character was named after astronaut Alan Bartlett Shepard Jr., voiced by Mark Meer and Jennifer Hale, and detailed cut ideas such as Shepard being a hidden alien or a visible cyborg.
