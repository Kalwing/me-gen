"UNC: Asari Writings" was a Mass Effect collection assignment that appeared in the Journal after Shepard found the first of Matriarch Dilinaga's scattered writings. Sixteen pieces existed across the galaxy, of which ten had to be recovered to complete the assignment.

The writings' background: during the asari golden age of learning that followed interstellar colonization, the Matriarchs became obsessed with achieving "ultimate knowledge," and a small dedicated group chose to leave Thessia in search of true enlightenment. Within a few years twenty-eight Matriarchs departed with no destination and no plan to return; only six were ever heard from again, and of the remaining twenty-two only Matriarch Dilinaga's remains were found, along with writings made during her journey. The writings became the subject of intense scholarly study, were kept for generations at the Thessia Guildhall, and less than a century before the game were made public at the Farinah Museum, where the Dilinaga exhibit became one of Thessia's most popular historical attractions.

The page notes this background exists in the game files but never appears in-game. In Mass Effect 2, Captain Bailey mentions a Dilinaga Concert Hall in the Citadel's Tayseri Ward, still closed for repairs from Sovereign's debris. As a Mass Effect 3 consequence, obtaining at least 10 of Dilinaga's Writings, along with retrieving Gavin Hossle's data on Feros and purchasing the Elkoss Combine armory license, allowed Conrad Verner (if he survived both prior games) to analyze the schematics, adding a 5-point War Asset bonus to his dark energy dissertation.
