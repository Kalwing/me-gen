Mass Effect was a cancelled film adaptation of the first Mass Effect game, officially announced by BioWare on May 24, 2010. Legendary Pictures and Warner Bros. were to co-produce and co-finance it, with Thomas Tull, Jon Jashni and Avi Arad as producers and BioWare's Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk and Casey Hudson as executive producers.

Talk of a film began in 2007 with the first game's release. Avi Arad optioned the rights in September 2008, but progress stalled until Legendary acquired the feature rights in May 2010. Mark Protosevich (I Am Legend, Thor) was brought on to write; he later said the concept was a straight adaptation of the first game rather than a separate story. Early reports suggested an original story featuring Commander Shepard, but Legendary announced it would adapt the first game's plot, centering on Shepard's journey, with the film's Shepard fixed as male since player choices could not carry over. Protosevich left in early 2012 and was replaced on October 24, 2012 by Morgan Davis Foehl, tasked with a new draft. In August 2013, Legendary planned to present the film to Universal Studios, having distanced itself from Warner Bros.

The project was shelved. Mac Walters said it "felt like we were always fighting the IP" and questioned whether 90 to 120 minutes could do the story justice; after a leadership change, Legendary considered a television series instead. On November 23, 2021, Amazon Prime Video was reported to be in talks to adapt Mass Effect for streaming TV, and on November 7, 2024, a TV series was reported to be officially in development, with Avi Arad, Michael Gamble, Karim Zreik and Daniel Casey as executive producers and Casey also writing.
