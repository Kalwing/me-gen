Citadel: Scan the Keepers was a Mass Effect assignment given by Chorban, a salarian scientist, who had devised a way to scan the Citadel's keepers despite station authorities disliking interference with the insect-like creatures. After Shepard's first Council visit, Ashley or another companion noticed Chorban bending over a keeper in the Citadel Tower. Chorban explained his research fell into a legal grey area and gave Shepard a scanner that automatically transmitted data to him with each scan. If the player had first met the volus scientist Jahleed and taken Citadel: Jahleed's Fears, Chorban could instead be found in the lower markets, and discouraging his research allowed Jahleed to supply his own scanner, changing the journal entry.

The task was to scan all 21 keepers across the accessible Citadel, spread among the Citadel Tower, Presidium, Embassy Lounge and Embassies, Upper and Lower Wards, Flux, C-Sec Academy, Ward Access, and the Alliance docking bay at Dock 422. Each scan gave credits and experience, with a bonus for scanning all of them, and reporting back was not required. In Legendary Edition, 20 of the 21 keepers were required. The assignment could be abandoned through dialogue with the originating scientist for 8 Paragon points and experience, with no Renegade reward for completion; both rewards could be obtained by scanning all keepers before choosing the Paragon option.

Several keepers were prone to bugs, including one at the elcor and volus ambassadors' office near Udina's that could fail to appear or spawn partly under the floor, and one in the Wards alleyway that could be thrown off the map by grenades used to rescue Tali. In Mass Effect 2, if Chorban was not killed and his assignment was completed, he emailed Shepard a summary of his findings, noting he had failed to get anyone to take his discoveries seriously. The assignment was internally named "Keeper Secret." Cut content indicated Chorban was originally to be found scanning a keeper near the Avina terminal at the base of the Citadel Tower.
