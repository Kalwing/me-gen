Legion: A House Divided was Legion's loyalty mission in Mass Effect 2, available after the Reaper IFF mission if Legion was kept and activated rather than sold to Cerberus. Legion learned that the geth heretics had adapted a Reaper virus that, once uploaded, would compel the normal geth to join the heretics' war against organic life, and asked Shepard to help stop it at Heretic Station, a large, airless, low-gravity station some 15 kilometers long.

Boarding undetected via the Normandy's stealth systems, Shepard's team fought through geth troopers, hunters, primes and rocket drones, disabling geth "hubs" and hacking inactive rocket drones for support. Along the way Legion revealed an alternative to destroying the station: it could modify the completed virus to rewrite the heretics and bring them peacefully back into the geth consensus. Legion repeatedly said it lacked enough data to choose and deferred the decision to Shepard, because Shepard had fought the heretics. After deleting the virus at a central console and surviving four waves of geth, Shepard chose to rewrite the heretics (Paragon, an EMP that also damaged unshielded organic tissue) or destroy the station (Renegade, collapsing its antimatter confinement). Either choice left three minutes to evacuate past a Geth Prime to the airlock.

Back on the Normandy, if Tali's loyalty mission was complete and her evidence unused, Shepard had to mediate a confrontation in the AI Core after Legion tried to transmit classified Migrant Fleet data to the geth; a sufficient Paragon or Renegade option kept both squadmates loyal. In Mass Effect 3, rewriting the heretics gave a large bonus to the Geth Fleet war asset (and a matching penalty to Quarian assets) while destroying them did the reverse, though brokering peace made the totals equal; destroying the heretics also worked in favor of a peaceful resolution on Rannoch. Rewards included 30,000 credits, palladium, a Geth Shield Strength upgrade and Legion's Geth Shield Boost power. The mission was written by Chris L'Etoile.
