A starship's General ARea Defense Integration Anti-spacecraft Network (GARDIAN) consisted of anti-missile and anti-fighter laser turrets mounted on the exterior hull. Because the turrets operated under computer control, the gunnery officer needed only to activate the system and designate hostile targets. Since lasers travel at light speed, they could not be dodged by anything moving at subluminal speeds, making early-battle GARDIAN fire 100 percent accurate, though not always lethal; damaged fighters had to break off for repairs.

GARDIAN had two main limitations. Diffraction caused the beam to spread and lose energy density over distance, making high-powered lasers short-ranged weapons. Heat was the other constraint: weapons-grade lasers required cool-down time, and sustained firing built heat that reduced damage, range, and accuracy, so constant use could burn out a laser. As fighters attacked in swarms, the first few were reliably hit but later attackers could press closer as overheating degraded performance.

GARDIAN lasers typically operated in infrared frequencies for mechanical reliability. Salarians used near-ultraviolet lasers with six times the range, valuing extra time to intercept missiles, and the geth also used ultraviolet frequencies, with one geth dreadnought carving through multiple quarian ships at the battle over Rannoch. Lasers were not blocked by the kinetic barriers of capital ships, except partially by Reaper barriers, but their short range confined them to rare knife-fight-range engagements. Systems Alliance frigates carried GARDIAN; the stealthy SSV Normandy rarely needed hers. The SSV Iwo Jima used GARDIAN to destroy a ground rover but was overwhelmed and shot down when a mass accelerator cannon fired many small projectiles at short range while her barriers were down for landing. The Destiny Ascension relied on GARDIAN during the Battle of the Citadel. GARDIAN turrets were also used as ground defenses, and the Alliance deployed several to the colony of Horizon against suspected slavers.
