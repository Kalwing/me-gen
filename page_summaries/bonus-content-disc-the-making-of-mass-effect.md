"The Making of Mass Effect" was a documentary found on the bonus content disc of the 2007 Limited Collector's Edition of Mass Effect and on the bonus content disc released in 2008. Its interviewees were lead animator Jonathan Cooper, lead technical designer Dusty Everman, cinematic designer James Henley, project director Casey Hudson, lead writer Drew Karpyshyn, concept artist Matt Rhodes, audio director Steve Sim, uncharted world designer David Sitar, lead cinematic designer Ken Thain, lead level artist Mike Trottier, lead designer Preston Watamaniuk, art director Derek Watts, and cinematics lead Shane Welbourn.

Hudson said the project began after BioWare finished Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic in mid-2003, and that the team wanted to take that role-playing formula into an original universe with its own galaxy, ships and powers. He said world rules, such as whether to allow time travel, battlefield warp, cloning, or human-level artificial intelligence, had to be settled before the story was built, because they dictated what stories were possible, and that the player would command an actual military vessel with a crew. Karpyshyn described BioWare's writing process as three or four writers at a time, six or seven across a project, taking input from artists, programmers and animators.

Watts said the team gathered photographs and painted many planet concepts for the writers to choose from, and that Virmire began as a clean "utopian vacation world" based on islands in Vietnam and Thailand before becoming an enemy base. Trottier described building environments in Studio Max and the importance of lighting. Hudson explained the "uncharted worlds" system that let the galaxy expand beyond the core story, and Sitar described the creatures, bases, anomalies and minerals found on such worlds. Rhodes discussed designing the human, salarian, krogan, asari, turian and geth races, noting the asari brief was "design blue skin space babes with no men" and that the geth spent the longest in design, beginning as a skinned-looking organic creature before becoming machines. Sim described sound work, recounting how sound designer Ben Burtt recorded ship sounds through a vacuum cleaner tube aimed at a Los Angeles freeway, and how the voice of Sovereign was created from a recording of a bear-proof garbage can lid pitched down an octave with reverb. Everman, Henley, Thain and Cooper described tech design in the Kismet visual scripting tool, cinematic camera work, the NPC face customization sliders, and character rigging done alongside the character designers.
