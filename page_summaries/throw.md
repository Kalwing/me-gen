Throw was a biotic ability in Mass Effect, Mass Effect 2 and Mass Effect 3 that used mass effect fields to hurl a target away from the user with damaging force. (A separate Throw skill exists in Mass Effect: Andromeda.)

In the first game, Throw was mainly used to keep opponents at a distance by launching them away or knocking them over, leaving them helpless. It could knock enemies off ledges for environmental kills, break destructible objects to hit enemies with debris, and hurl movable boxes at foes for shield-bypassing damage; enemy biotics could do the same. Advanced and Master Throw could pin enemies against walls in enclosed spaces, and it was especially deadly against the fragile Thorian Creepers. Its effects compounded with Lift and with multiple casters, and the page cites Liara T'Soni's Singularity followed by simultaneous upgraded Throws from Urdnot Wrex and a biotic Shepard as a strong combination. Talent ranks scaled the force from 600 to 1250 Newtons, with the ability available to the Adept, Sentinel, Vanguard, Asari Scientist and unlockable Krogan Battlemaster.

In Mass Effect 2, Throw hurled enemies through the air and damaged them on impact, evolving at rank 4 into Heavy Throw (1200 N, single target) or Throw Field (multiple targets). When cast by Shepard it arced toward the crosshair and could curve around cover; squadmates such as Thane, Samara or Morinth fired it in a straight line. Distance and damage depended on target weight, so Pull, Singularity or Slam could lighten a target first. It was strong crowd control against husks, Abominations and LOKI mechs, and enemies thrown into the sky or ceiling were often killed automatically.

In Mass Effect 3, Throw had a very low cooldown, could detonate every type of Power Combo, and evolved toward options including Double Throw, added radius, and a multiplayer-only Force and Damage bonus. Pairing Throw with Warp, Reave, Annihilation Field or Dark Channel to trigger biotic explosions was one of the most effective tactics against Banshees, Brutes and Atlases. It was available single-player to the Adept, Sentinel and Samara, and in multiplayer to the Asari Adept, N7 Fury Adept and Human Sentinel. In the first game Throw shared a bug with Lift and Warp where it could fail to affect more than one target if the reticle was centered directly on an enemy.
