This Codex update, added with the Bring Down the Sky DLC, contained a single Secondary entry on translation technology. It noted that humanity remained linguistically divided across languages such as Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic, and Swahili, and that every alien race had a similarly broad range of languages and dialects. Most individuals knew only their mother tongue and relied on machine translation, which a few hundred credits of equipment (handheld PDAs, computers in clothing or jewelry, or sub-dermal implants) could provide in seamless real time; without it, galactic trade and culture would not exist. Governments distributed subsidized translation software updated over the public extranet, often as travelers approached spaceport customs, and even the isolationist batarians supplied up-to-date glossaries, widely assumed to be so they could keep exporting propaganda. Speaking without machine aid was still considered broad-minded and practical, children often took courses in alien languages, and most races could manage the simplified artificial "trade tongue." Some species depended on machine translation entirely: the hanar could not reproduce the spoken language of any humanoid species, and other races could not reproduce hanar bioluminescence without mechanical aid. Newly discovered or obscure races had no machine translation until linguists had studied them.
