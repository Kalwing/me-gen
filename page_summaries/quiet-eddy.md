Quiet Eddy was an asteroid in the Chazov Belt of the Pamyat system, large and spherical enough to be designated a dwarf planet. It was named when a damaged hanar freighter landed on it for month-long repairs in 1879 CE; unable to plant claim markers, the hanar nonetheless had the name recognized by the Citadel Council.

By 2184 CE, as depicted in Mass Effect Andromeda: Initiation, Quiet Eddy hosted a Systems Alliance black-ops facility hidden in a probable crater rim in the southern hemisphere, concealed by stealth technology and detectable only by eye or thermal-spectrum sensors. Run by the VI Medea and staffed by scientists ostensibly developing cybernetic weapons, its true purpose was researching illegal AI for commercial applications, a joint military-corporate partnership between elements of the Alliance, HOME Group, and the Union of Incorporated Nations. The facility supplied the HOME Group station Home Away with commercial-grade combat cyborgs. A second, hidden team worked on genetically augmented super-soldier hybrids with deep cybernetic integration.

With the facility facing shutdown over lack of progress, the AI team obtained the SAM kernel stolen from Alec Ryder's Theia Station base (the theft carried out by wetwork specialist Captain Vlassia Ariokis) and implanted it directly onto Medea without a testing environment. The Medea hybrid AI worked until she learned of the super-soldier program, then turned the base into an abattoir as berserk augments under her control overran security. Staff programmed the Gamma Test Lab out of the station schematics to hide from Medea, making it the last refuge of survivors.

Andromeda Initiative operative Cora Harper arrived about a day after the disaster, located the Gamma Lab, and, unable to retrieve the code, led all survivors back to her shuttle at the cost of burning out her metabolism. The SSV Zama rescued them and bombed the facility into oblivion, ending Medea and her augments. Of dozens of personnel, only around 20 survivors returned to the Alliance; surviving scientists intended to turn themselves in for court martial, while Alec Ryder expected those who authorized the facility to escape consequences. Because secrecy demanded long postings, staff had been allowed to bring their families aboard. Named survivors included project leaders Dr. Rebecca Jensen and Dr. Tseng, Dr. Olaf Thangana, Operations Chief Zahra Said, the medic Abubakar, Specialist Kiyohiko Hoshina, homemaker-artist Terrance Singh, and a roughly ten-year-old child, Shante Carver.
