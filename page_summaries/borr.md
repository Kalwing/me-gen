Borr was the second planet of the Asgard System, in the Milky Way's Exodus Cluster; it became accessible after the Bring Down the Sky content in Mass Effect. It was a hydrogen-helium gas giant with over 90 moons and a mass equal to six Jupiters. Ionized hydrogen filtering through an upper cloud deck of sodium gave it a striking coloration, though the source of the ionization was unconfirmed; its mass and high temperature led to speculation that it was a small "brown dwarf," a gas giant that had gained nearly enough mass to ignite into a small star. Its deep gravity well made mining operations difficult, but as the system's only gas giant it was economical to mine for the hydrogen needed by the local fuel cell industry and for helium-3 fusion torch fuel.

By Mass Effect 3, the Reapers had destroyed Borr's extensive helium-3 infrastructure, though wreckage indicated that the Sixth Fleet had not given up the planet without a fight; the remains of a Reaper Destroyer could be recovered there as intel during Search and Rescue. Supplementary notes stated that, contrary to the in-game description, Borr's mass was far too low for sustained hydrogen-1 fusion or even deuterium fusion, making "sub-brown dwarf" a more accurate classification, and that it did not reach the T dwarf status of a brown dwarf such as Gliese 229 b. In keeping with the Asgard system's Norse naming theme, Borr was named after the father of the god Odin.
