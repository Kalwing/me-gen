Morinth was an Ardat-Yakshi, an asari with a rare genetic disorder that killed her mates during melding while making her smarter, stronger and deadlier with each coupling. Born in the 1700s, she was one of Samara's three daughters and, like her sisters, was diagnosed at age forty and offered comfortable seclusion or execution. Addicted to melding, she chose to flee under the name "Mirala," prompting Samara to become a justicar and hunt her for over 400 years across asari space. On one planet Morinth set herself up as a goddess; villagers sacrificed their daughters to her, and when Samara arrived Morinth sent her worshippers in waves to cover her escape, leaving only young children alive.

In Mass Effect 2, Morinth fled asari space to Illium, then bribed Eclipse mercenaries to smuggle her offworld aboard the AML Demeter to Omega. There she seduced and murdered a young sculptor named Nef. Samara enlisted Shepard to trap her: Shepard acted as bait in the Afterlife Club's VIP section and went to Morinth's apartment, where Samara intervened and the two fought biotically as equals. If Shepard resisted Morinth's seduction, they chose which to kill. If Samara died, Morinth joined the squad, impersonating her mother; Kelly Chambers and Kasumi Goto noticed something off, and Morinth disabled EDI's surveillance of her quarters and spoke in her true voice there. She resented Samara's loveless, dutiful upbringing and rejected her self-denial. Romancing Morinth and mating with her after the suicide mission killed Shepard, contrary to her claims.

In Mass Effect 3, a surviving Morinth emailed Shepard a "belated goodbye" after slipping off the Normandy. Terminals at the Ardat-Yakshi Monastery on Lesuss and in Liara's Shadow Broker network revealed her emails to sisters Rila and Falere had been intercepted by extranet filters and deleted unread by Superintendent Jethra. During the assault on Earth, a Reaper-converted Morinth could appear as a standard Banshee. Her face was modeled on Rana McAnear and she was voiced by Natalia Cigliuti.
