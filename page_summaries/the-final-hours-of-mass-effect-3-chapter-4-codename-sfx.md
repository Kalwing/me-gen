The fourth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary recounts the origin of Mass Effect. In September 2003 Casey Hudson pitched BioWare co-founders Ray Muzyka and Greg Zeschuk over lunch in Edmonton on a science-fiction game codenamed SFX, described in a one-page document as a game that would "redefine the genre" and become a "must-have game for the Xbox 2." Hudson wanted a blend of KOTOR-style role-playing and Halo-style action, ship exploration inspired by Starflight, an Xbox Live trading system, and a commitment to a full trilogy, essentially BioWare's own Star Wars. Microsoft expressed early interest in an Xbox exclusive, and a 2007 launch was projected.

The chapter describes the first year spent building the fiction in four-hour daily meetings, debating whether the setting would have aliens or time travel and comparing properties like Star Trek, Babylon 5 and Firefly. The team settled on a near-future setting enabled by ancient alien technology and a James Bond / Jack Bauer style hero. Derek Watts's sketch of "a dinosaur with a gun" produced the krogan; budget limits on making male and female versions of every alien led to the all-female asari. A January 2004 document titled "Master Slaves" established that an extinct creator race (then "Nazari," their villain species "Batarians," the hero originally "Captain Anderson" versus "Saren") had left FTL technology, and that humans were a slave race the creators engineered.

Watamaniuk introduced Hideyuki Sawada's KTR voice robot video into meetings, prompting discussion of Ray Kurzweil's singularity and pushing the trilogy's theme toward the tension between organic and synthetic life. The team replaced the bat army with a robot army, the geth, and reworked the plot so the machines had risen long ago and now return to harvest sentient life. After rejecting names including Space Age, Element Zero and "Rainbow," Zeschuk suggested "Mass Effect." By mid-2005 the fiction was taking shape but the game itself was unfocused, with levels like Noveria built without a clear story, an unhappy main-character design, and pressure mounting toward Microsoft's planned October 2005 announcement.

The chapter reproduces the original SFX and Master Slaves pitch documents and a "Who's Who" of David Anderson, Steven Hackett, the Illusive Man, Kai Leng and Saren.
