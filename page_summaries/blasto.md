Blasto was a fictional hanar character, billed as the Milky Way's first hanar Spectre, who starred in a long-running series of in-universe action films spanning the 2180s and beyond. In 2184, footage from a Blasto production, Requiem for a Reaper!, was used as a distraction during a Cerberus operation to steal Shepard's clone, as depicted in Mass Effect: Foundation. Blasto's first film proper, Blasto: The Jellyfish Stings (2185), was an exploitation picture advertised on Illium; its use of "jellyfish," seen by many hanar as a slur, drew criticism from anti-defamation groups. A sequel, Blasto Saves Christmas, followed. In 2186, during the Reaper War, Blasto 6: Partners in Crime broke opening-week box-office records; producer Zhanmari Erinaceus donated proceeds to the Citadel's defense fund. Its plot paired Blasto with a gruff elcor C-Sec partner, Bubin, investigating a vorcha ambassador exploiting diplomatic immunity, and involved a romance between Blasto and Bubin's sister Honey, who turned out to be secretly working for the vorcha villain; Bubin was gravely wounded but survived, and the vorcha died in a self-inflicted explosion. During the Mass Effect 3: Citadel DLC, Javik was drafted into a satirical vid, Blasto 7: Blasto Goes to War!, alongside Shepard and a cast including the vorcha, salarian, and volus Councilors; Javik's real biotic attack on the actor playing the indoctrinated vorcha Councilor prompted the director to recast for Blasto 8: Blasto Cures the Genophage!. A separate 2012 Dark Horse comic, Blasto: Eternity is Forever, had Blasto team with asari actress-Spectre Cerulean Star to stop the krogan scientist Kronus, who had built a time-freezing device on Virmire; Kronus died fleeing after his base's self-destruct triggered an explosion. Centuries later, aboard the ark Keelah Si'yah, passenger Anax Therion had seen an eighth Blasto film, and in the Andromeda galaxy settlers continued the franchise with the holo Blasto vs. the Scourge and an alternate Blasto 8: The Biotic Awakens, while "Blast-Oh's" cereal and Blasto-branded athletic wear circulated among Initiative colonists. The character originated as a joke by writer Patrick Weekes describing an ideal hanar squadmate; Jennifer Hale, who voices female Shepard, also voiced Honey.
