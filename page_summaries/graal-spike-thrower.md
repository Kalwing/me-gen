The Graal Spike Thrower was a shotgun in Mass Effect 3, one of a long line of krogan weapons used to hunt thresher maws. Its ammunition was oversized flechettes meant to pierce thresher hide and create deep wound channels causing massive blood loss. It was double-barreled for extra firepower and carried blades to inflict internal injuries if the wielder was swallowed by a thresher. Used against humanoid targets it had grisly effects, and its shots could be charged for more damage.

In single-player the Graal was found during Priority: Tuchanka, against the safety rail where the Tomkahs stopped at the break in the road, and could be bought afterward from Cipritine Armory if missed. In multiplayer it was a rare random reward from item packs and required no expansion.

The Graal could be charged for up to double damage, expending only one shot whether charged or not, and the charge could be held through sprinting, rolling, dodging, melee, most abilities, and even while stunned. Despite its description it dealt all damage up front with no damage over time. Its flechettes had noticeable travel time, requiring players to lead moving targets, but had a high chance to stagger even some larger armored enemies. It could deal bonus damage to heads and weak points, unlike the similar Geth Plasma Shotgun. It ignored the damage-reduction penalty for shooting armor, penetrated armor plating on enemies such as Brutes and Atlases (though not Guardian shields), and ignored shield gates, letting it severely damage or one-shot barrier- or shield-protected enemies. Its flechettes could not pass through objects, so piercing mods gave no benefit, though Guardians and CAT6 Heavies could still be hit through gaps such as feet or eye slits. It was highly accurate even without the Shotgun Smart Choke, and was one of only three shotguns whose accuracy improved when aiming down sights, alongside the Venom Shotgun and N7 Crusader. It had a very high chance per shot to trigger power-combo priming effects such as Cryo Ammo, and was well suited to Vanguards, firing at the start of a Biotic Charge and again immediately after. Squadmates using it kept their distance and fired only uncharged shots.

The idea for the weapon came from lead gameplay designer Corey Gaspur's nightmare about a man chasing him with a nail gun. The Graal was one of very few weapons that fired rendered projectiles rather than tracers, leaving clusters of flechettes stuck in surfaces; charged shots left flechettes glowing like hot metal before cooling to black.
