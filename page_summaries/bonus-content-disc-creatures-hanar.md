This page transcribed the recorded artist commentary for the design gallery "The Creatures of Mass Effect: Hanar," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. All nine images were narrated by concept artist Matt Rhodes.

Rhodes described the process as filling a "void" with rapid black-and-white silhouettes to find shapes no one had seen before that players would still accept. Early rejected directions included a "spiderwick" creature whose narrow limbs did not read well on screen and felt too "fairy tale," a many-limbed creature with a pointed hat-like head and small front legs, and a scholarly, mortician-like "mystical librarian" alien tinged with blue that Rhodes liked but which did not fit. Several concepts wore modified space suits, a deliberate choice to tie all species together within a shared galactic economy and market.

One line of development came from Rhodes spotting a tiny background blob in a magazine illustration of Mass Effect, enlarging the low-resolution image, and painting over it to produce a red-robed, thin-armed, tall-headed "duck or platypus webbed priest." Another theme imagined aliens so ugly or incomprehensible that they had to modify or conceal themselves to function socially, leading to designs with inadequate hoods and small holographic face projections built over a creature with a large gaping mouth. The direction that resonated with the team was a foreign but appealing form with an ethereal glow that people would watch as it spoke regardless of what it said; Rhodes noted the technical leap from illustration to final model was difficult, but the hanar became a "magical" presence in crowd scenes.
