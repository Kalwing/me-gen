The Illusive Man was the elusive, well-informed leader of Cerberus, dedicated to making humanity ascendant above all other races. He had close-cropped silver-grey hair and prosthetic "steely blue" eyes with unique iris patterns, dressed in immaculate suits, and was described as combining humanity's best and worst traits. His real name and pre-Cerberus life were largely forgotten. After the Battle of Shanxi, an anonymous extranet message called for humanity to assert itself against alien contacts; Alliance intelligence, unable to identify the author, dubbed him an "illusive man" and the name stuck. He founded Cerberus, broke it from the Alliance military, and built Cord-Hislop Aerospace as a front, justifying that "if humanity is to survive, sacrifices must be made for the greater good."

Mass Effect: Evolution revealed him as Jack Harper, a mercenary under General Williams during the First Contact War. On Shanxi, Harper, Ben Hislop, and Eva Coré captured turian general Desolas Arterius, who was searching for a Prothean artifact (a Reaper-derived object). Ben was killed activating it and Jack was knocked unconscious and changed by it. Held prisoner by Saren Arterius, then released at Arcturus Station, Jack and Eva pursued Desolas and the artifact to Illium and then Palaven, where Desolas used it to create husk-like "meta-turians." Ben, transformed, ultimately sacrificed himself to stop Desolas; Eva died as well. Watching their memorial, Jack recorded a manifesto about a coming dark time and humanity's need to master alien technology to survive and ascend, then took his place as the Illusive Man.

Across the tie-in comics and novels he ran many simultaneous projects: backing Terra Firma and Charles Saracino via assassination; sabotaging Eldfell-Ashland/Eldfell-Ashland Energy starships over colonies to produce biotic children (one of whom he gave to Paul Grayson to raise as Gillian); infiltrating the Ascension Project; seeking the Migrant Fleet's transmission codes; and deploying operatives Rasa and Kai Leng (Mass Effect: Foundation). In Mass Effect: Redemption he allied with Liara T'Soni and the drell Feron to recover Shepard's body from the Shadow Broker and the Collectors.

In Mass Effect 2, he used Cerberus resources on the Lazarus Project to resurrect Shepard intact, refusing Miranda Lawson's suggestion of a control chip. He gave Shepard the Normandy SR-2 and a crew to fight the Collectors, leaked the Horizon attack, deliberately let Shepard walk into a trap on the Collector ship to learn to pass the Omega 4 Relay, and directed Shepard to the derelict Reaper for an IFF. After the Collector base, he urged Shepard to preserve it with a radiation pulse; if Shepard destroyed it he was furious, saying he knew Shepard would "choke on the hard decisions" and asserting "Cerberus is humanity." In Mass Effect: Retribution he implanted Reaper tech in Paul Grayson, who went on a rampage and was killed by Kai Leng; in Mass Effect: Invasion he conquered Omega via Oleg Petrovsky and Raymond Ashe, gaining exclusive access to the Omega 4 Relay.

By Mass Effect 3 (2186), his methods were overt and he was clearly indoctrinated. He sought to control rather than destroy the Reapers, sending the synthetic Eva Coré to steal and erase the Mars Prothean Archives data, attempting to stop the krogan cure on Sur'Kesh, bombing Tuchanka, backing Udina's Citadel coup, and funding Henry Lawson's Sanctuary on Horizon to study indoctrination. He confronted Shepard by hologram on Mars and Thessia and sent Kai Leng to take the Prothean VI Vendetta. At Cerberus HQ, video logs showed him being implanted with Reaper nanotech without anesthetic. He fled to the Citadel, alerting the Reapers to the Catalyst. There, using Reaper modifications to mimic indoctrination, he immobilized Shepard and Anderson and forced Shepard to shoot Anderson. Convinced by Shepard that the Reapers controlled him, he said he "tried" to resist and shot himself, or attempted to shoot Shepard and was killed; dying, he looked at Earth and wished Shepard could see it as he did. The Catalyst confirmed his theory that the Reapers could be controlled was correct, but that he personally never could, being already indoctrinated. He appears in a vision as the embodiment of the "Control" ending.

Casey Hudson conceived the character from cancelled first-game DLC as someone so powerful he directed galactic leaders while holding no office; the name was a play on an early graphics engine. He was compared to the X-Files' Cigarette Smoking Man, and voice actor Martin Sheen sucked a pen to simulate smoking. A cut plan had him transform into a Reaper creature as the trilogy's final boss.
