A Geth Dropship was an approximately frigate-sized geth vessel built to carry and rapidly deploy large numbers of geth troops. Its design appeared almost organic, resembling an oversized wingless hornet or wasp; according to The Art of Mass Effect, its profile was inspired by a hornet's posture just before it stings, and its loud engine roar served as an early warning of its approach. Dropships released geth units from overhead, often dropping Geth Armatures and Geth Colossi in mid-flight, and could provide air support with a powerful projectile attack. They also filled a defensive role, powering energy fields to block strategic points by latching on with three superstrong claws that were nearly immune to conventional weapons but could be destroyed by overpressurized hydraulic doors. Like other geth vehicles, a dropship was itself a sentient "mobile platform" for geth programs, able to transfer its programs to a smaller platform for repairs. Dropships were highly durable but could be worn down by sustained fire; one lingering to unload reinforcements risked accumulating fatal damage before it either exhausted its troops or fled.

In the original Mass Effect, Geth Dropships were central to the attack on Feros, anchoring to the ExoGeni headquarters and dropping units onto Zhu's Hope and the Prothean Skyway, and one delivered emergency reinforcements to the bomb site on Virmire. Pursuing Admiral Hackett's directive to destroy geth outposts brought encounters with many dropships, including one that stayed put over Casbin. During the Battle of the Citadel, Shepard's team fought one on foot on the Citadel Tower exhaust plain, damageable only by the Citadel's defense turrets. In Mass Effect 2 they deployed geth on worlds such as Corang and Haestrom, where one gave air support and collapsed a pillar, and three were docked at Heretic Station. In Mass Effect 3 many defended the Geth Dreadnought and joined the geth armada over Rannoch.
