Singularity was a biotic ability in Mass Effect, Mass Effect 2, and Mass Effect 3 that created a gravitational field akin to a black hole, drawing multiple enemies into a vortex where they floated helplessly and took extra damage. It could also pull in environmental objects, and enemies took damage colliding with solid objects inside the field.

In the original Mass Effect it was a talent for the Adept and Asari Scientist, scaling in radius, duration, and recharge across ranks up to Master Singularity (8.25 m radius, 8 sec, 40 sec recharge). It only affected enemies present at the moment the vortex formed, and higher ranks were required to affect larger foes such as krogan or Geth Primes, while some enemies like Geth Armatures were always immune.

In Mass Effect 2 it fired a slow-arcing projectile (Liara's version deployed instantly in a straight line) and only one vortex per user could be active. Enemies entering the field afterward were also pulled in, and the field had a unique "vortex hold" that staggered and slowly damaged even protected enemies, though holding protected targets caused the vortex to collapse early. Rank 4 evolved into Heavy Singularity (up to six targets, 45-second field, longer holds) or Wide Singularity (3-meter pull radius). Warp detonated unprotected suspended targets, collapsing the vortex for doubled area damage.

In Mass Effect 3 (single-player and multiplayer) it again allowed only one vortex per user and could not penetrate cover in effect. Evolutions covered recharge speed, duration, radius, lift damage, field expansion, and a Detonate option that inflicted 300 damage (500 multiplayer) when the field died. In single-player, targets were primed for Biotic Explosions only while lifted; in multiplayer, all targets within the active radius were primed once per cast, even armored ones. It was available to the Adept and Liara T'Soni, and in multiplayer to the Human Adept and Project Phoenix Adept.
