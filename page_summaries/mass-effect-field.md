Mass effect fields are created through the use of element zero, which can increase or decrease the mass content of space-time when subjected to an electrical current via dark energy. A positive current increases mass; a negative current decreases it. The stronger the current, the greater the magnitude of the effect.

In space, low-mass fields allow FTL travel and inexpensive surface-to-orbit transit, while high-mass fields create artificial gravity and push space debris away from starships. In manufacturing, low-mass fields permit evenly-blended alloys, while high-mass compaction creates dense, sturdy construction materials. Militaries make extensive use of mass effect mobility technology, with mass effect fighting vehicles being standard front-line issue in most forces; the Mako's small element zero core can reduce its mass enough to allow a safe drop from the Normandy. Mass effect fields are also essential to kinetic barrier shields that protect infantry on the ground and starships in orbit or in battle.

Many biotics can generate and control mass effect fields biologically, which requires intensive training and cybernetic implants but can produce powerful offensive or defensive effects. Some biotics are not strong enough for offensive use, but all biotics are sensitive to the presence of mass effect fields.

Using mass effect fields creates a static electrical charge. In starship drive cores this charge must be grounded at regular intervals, either by touching a planet's surface or interacting with its geomagnetic field, to prevent the electricity discharging into the hull and causing catastrophic damage. For biotics, the charge manifests as an occasional static shock when they touch metal or other people.
