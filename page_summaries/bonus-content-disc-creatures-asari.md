This page transcribed the recorded artist commentary for the design gallery "The Creatures of Mass Effect: Asari," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. All eighteen images were narrated by concept artist Matt Rhodes, for whom the asari were his first assignment after joining BioWare.

Rhodes described the early exploration as an attempt to make an obviously alien female that remained attractive, testing how far facial proportions could be pushed before appeal was lost. The team concluded that the asari face had to stay fully feminine and unaltered, so the alien qualities were moved to the area around the hair. Designs drew on aquatic and underwater motifs, giving the impression of gills, slits, and tentacle-like head crests, evoking a species that had evolved in a very wet environment. The decision to make their skin blue was described as a deliberate nod to older science fiction, and it helped communicate the species' identity. A refined pass painted asari features over a chosen actress's face.

Rhodes also covered costume work: a "sexy space nun" outfit painted on a human model, strap-heavy dresses, and an asari military uniform that was never used in the game but where rank was signaled by flared shoulders, thigh and shin flares, and heels, based on wetsuits and climbing gear. He detailed the asari commando look, with copper elements, buckles, and straps, and an unused "badass mode" asari in a shocking red outfit. A large portion of the commentary concerned the search for the matriarch character's appearance, moving through black leather bodysuits, "holy mother" coats, and priestess hoods; Casey and Derek picked favored elements that were consolidated, and the final version shifted from a purple dress to darker colors with a more intimidating hood.
