Biotic amplifiers, usually shortened to biotic amps or bio-amps, were devices that enabled biotic individuals to synchronize the dozens of element zero nodules within their nervous systems so those nodules could form mass effect fields large and strong enough for practical use. Amps could also enhance a specific biotic discipline or talent. Biotics manipulated mass effect fields using these nodules, which reacted to electric stimuli from the brain.

An implant was a surgically embedded interface port into which amps were plugged; such implants were also called "wetware" because of their cybernetic nature. In humans, the implant was usually placed at the base of the skull for convenient access, and the user had to keep it free of contaminants. Implant ports could accept a variety of amps, and there was a growing market for modifications and add-ons. The finest quality implants and amps were manufactured by asari artisans, but the Systems Alliance's L3 implants, first deployed in 2170 CE, marked a significant advance for human biotic amp manufacturers. By 2186 CE, enhanced L3 variants and more advanced L4 and L5 models had been developed, though they remained experimental and not widely used.

In Mass Effect, bio-amp models could be purchased from merchants, obtained in battle, or recovered from storage containers. Amps provided three attributes: a Duration Bonus (increased duration of all biotic talents), a Power Bonus (increased Throw and Lift force, Warp damage, and Barrier strength), and a Cooldown Bonus (reduced recharge times). The Adept, Vanguard, and Sentinel player classes could equip biotic amps, as could the squad members Liara T'Soni, Kaidan Alenko, and Urdnot Wrex. Named models included the Solaris Amp (Aldrin Labs), Prodigy (Armali Council), Gemini (Armax Arsenal), Polaris (Kassa Fabrication), Savant (Serrice Council), and the Unity Amp (Sirta Foundation). The Spectre Gear HMBA Basic, Advanced, and Master lines could only be acquired through console commands.
