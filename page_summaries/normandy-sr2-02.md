Part 2 of a breakdown of the Normandy SR-2, covering its weapons and defensive suite. As a stealth reconnaissance frigate, the SR-2 was not heavily armed and relied mostly on passive and defensive systems, but its armament was extraordinarily powerful.

The ship initially carried two Javelin disruptor torpedo launchers mounted on joints between the engine mounts and primary hull. The Javelin was an experimental close-assault system of paired disruptor torpedo tubes slung on the exterior hull; the torpedoes fired on converging trajectories and detonated in timed sequence so the dark energy of their warheads resonated, magnifying the space-time warp and shredding the target. Several volleys could destroy a large Collector vessel. Javelins were cold-launched by compressed inert gas before igniting their thrusters, and as missile weapons were subject to accurate GARDIAN defensive fire, so they had to be fired in large numbers at short range.

The SR-2 was also equipped with the Thanix magnetohydrodynamic weapon, a Turian-developed miniaturized version of the weapon Sovereign used against the Citadel. It used an electromagnetic field to accelerate a stream of superheated molten metal (a liquid alloy of iron, uranium, and tungsten) to relativistic velocity; the metal solidified into a projectile as fired, hitting with enough force to pierce any known shield or armor. It fired every five seconds, appeared as a bright blue beam, and could destroy a Collector cruiser in two shots.

Defenses were the ship's true tactical value. Kinetic barriers were mass effect fields halting incoming projectiles, ineffective against heat or radiation. Cyclonic barrier technology (CBT) multi-core shielding rotationally fired the mass effect field projectors to create rapidly oscillating barriers that slapped aside incoming force rather than stopping it; if an emitter was damaged the CBT reverted to a traditional shield array, making it most effective during opening volleys, and its many high-frequency sensors needed frequent maintenance. Ablative Silaris armor, asari-designed and retrofitted to the hull, boiled away when heated, scattering directed-energy beams; its carbon nanotube and CVD-diamond sheets were crushed by mass effect fields into superdense layers, but it was prohibitively expensive above frigate size and shockwaves could still destroy the superstructure beneath. GARDIAN point-defense laser turrets shot down missiles and fighters under computer control; because lasers move at light speed they could not be dodged, giving GARDIAN fire near-100% early accuracy, though diffraction limited laser range and sustained fire built heat that reduced performance. Salarians used longer-ranged near-ultraviolet GARDIAN lasers.
