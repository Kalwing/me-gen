This page transcribed the recorded artist commentary for the design gallery "The Creatures of Mass Effect: Elcor," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. All nine images were narrated by concept artist Matt Rhodes, who said the elcor was among the creatures he most enjoyed developing.

Rhodes explained that the design goal was a passive creature that was not an enemy but functioned as an obstacle in crowds because of its sheer size and slowness, described as a "lumbering mechanic creature" and "monolithic living boulders." Early sketches included a version influenced, unintentionally at first, by Baron Harkonnen from David Lynch's Dune, with multiple eyes along the side of the head, a large collar, and a fat body on thin arms; this was rejected as too much of a "jester" concept and not suited to the intellectual property. Other exploratory sketches placed the face low on the body or set an octopus over a humanoid head for horror effect, neither of which was used.

A rock-textured "big giant boulder" version applied literal stone texture to the skin, with large plate bones on the face borrowed from other creature designs. Rhodes noted that much of the useful work came from loose napkin doodles, one of which was painted up into a version very close to the final elcor. For the final creature the mouth was removed: because a face that detailed would be expected to animate, and full facial animation was too costly in memory and time, the team took the mouth away so players would not be disappointed when it did not move.
