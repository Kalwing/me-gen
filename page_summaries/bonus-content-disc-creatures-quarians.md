This page transcribed the recorded artist commentary for the design gallery "The Creatures of Mass Effect: Quarians," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. All nine images were narrated by concept artist Matt Rhodes.

Rhodes said early sketches, made before he knew much of the story, explored machine races, including a decaying "robot that was once a person" wearing a human cloak it was tearing off and rejecting its own face. Once story elements were in place, the direction became aliens who wore pieced-together space suits like a second skin, never removing them, moving from derelict ship to derelict world as scavengers with no homeworld. He layered clothing, robes, decorations, and wrapped cables over the suit, and enlarged the eyes so players could read emotion and feel pity for a scavenging people.

A key sequence of sketches, each drawn over the previous one, worked backward from the near-final geth design to find the quarian shape, driven by the story that the quarians created the geth. The goal was a creature that could believably have built the geth, with the geth as a "corruption" of that shape, so the reveal would feel earned. Because only one quarian was planned and she would be somewhat innocent and "motherly," Rhodes invoked Virgin Mary imagery, and the quarian's hood became the corrupted hood of the geth as its face shrank. Final work pushed arm and leg pipes closer to the body or removed them, developed the mask and face, and settled on purple coloring and colored eyes for the model sheet.
