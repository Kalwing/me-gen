"Tech" referred to abilities based on reverse engineering or hacking technologies, including hardsuit systems, weapons, and synthetic enemies such as the geth. Tech talents were deployed with omni-tools. In the original trilogy the three playable tech classes were the Engineer (pure tech focus, weaker with weapons), the Infiltrator (tech split with combat), and the Sentinel (tech split with biotics); in Mass Effect: Andromeda the class system was replaced by Profiles that still broadly corresponded to the six classes.

In Mass Effect, tech abilities were especially valuable against the game's many synthetic and shielded enemies, ranging from disabling shields and weapons, to hacking a synthetic to attack its own kind, to healing party members and reinforcing the Mako's shields; high-level tech allowed access to complex locks and computers. Tech resistance, mostly from armor, reduced their effect. Abilities included Damping, Decryption, Electronics, First Aid, Hacking, and Medicine. Mass Effect 2 overhauled tech powers, adding damaging offensive options and unique class powers such as Combat Drone, Tactical Cloak, and Tech Armor, and introduced armor and barrier protection layers less vulnerable to anti-shield powers. Mass Effect 3 kept tech powers central for stripping shields and barriers, removed the requirement to strip synthetics before hacking, added electrocution-based disabling and stun effects, and tied powers into Power Combos so they remained useful against unshielded, non-synthetic foes; multiplayer added many new tech powers. Mass Effect: Andromeda organized tech skills such as Assault Turret, Cryo Beam, Flamethrower, Incinerate, Invasion, Overload, and Remnant VI.
