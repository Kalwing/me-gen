This archive collected Cerberus Daily News reports from March 2185. The Systems Alliance heard Ford v. Huerta, the first case testing a human leader who used reconstructive data storage to keep functioning after a stroke left him legally dead for ninety minutes; Speaker of the House Lisa Ford argued the last year of President Huerta's term was illegitimate, with expert witnesses Dr. Samuel Wachhaus and Dr. Lin Shiyin disagreeing over whether Huerta had truly recovered or been replaced by a VI. The manhunt for Cerberus operative Skye Turnick ended when GMJ enforcers stormed a compound on Trident's island of Ness; two agents died and a fleeing gunship crashed into a halfway house for runaway youths. R&B singer Lady Sweat (Leila Montrose) died at 24 aboard a cruiser caught in a proton storm, and Aish Ashland drew criticism for mocking her death. GBC News correspondent Robin Lasky was arrested in the Republic of Ghurst on Tuchanka and held at Urlukah Federal Prison on spying accusations. The war on Garvug began on 28 March: corporate armies, many based in Citadel space, backed an aborted coup against the krogan clan government in exchange for planetary access, with eco-engineering firms Binary Helix and Guanghui Solutions and miner Sonax Industries seeking iridium and terraforming rights; fighting settled into a protracted battle for the capital Dhazil. Other reports covered a "Nekyia Corridor" production halt after lead actress Kate First walked off set; a tell-all e-book alleging the Citadel Council knew of the geth threat before Eden Prime; the manal removed from the endangered list; a Hitara Prize for Biology won by Earth scientists studying Prothean comm-tower radiation; the probe TR-15 Letus approaching a neutron star via the rarely-used Kappa Iota Relay and its Prothean "Dark Switches"; the apocalyptic Dalemitrian cult; installation artist June Fasha's "Connected" and its patron Donovan Hock; clawball star Neno Raxirian's return from suspension; a graduate student killed by pod crabs on Virmire; and Jonah Ashland topping Noveria Now's "50 CEOs Not to Mess With."
