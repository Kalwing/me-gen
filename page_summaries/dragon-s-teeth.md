"Dragon's teeth" was the nickname Alliance marines gave to the large spikes that the geth used to convert human corpses, and living victims, into husks, resembling ancient execution by impalement. The name derived from the myth of Jason and the Argonauts, in which teeth sown into a field sprang up as soldiers. Each device consisted of a tripod-like support and a retracting metal spike. The mechanism converted the muscle, organs, and water content of a body into cybernetic parts, then reanimated it with an electrical charge as a simple-minded husk that attacked anything nearby; releasing the husk retracted the spike for reuse. Because dragon's teeth were often found where no geth were present to operate them, it was speculated that the spikes acted as traps that impaled anyone approaching. The underlying technology did not resemble other geth technology and seemed disproportionate effort for minimal result, though its psychological effect on soldiers was significant.

In the first Mass Effect, dragon's teeth were found in systems far from geth space, including a buried cache on Trebin with no indication of who placed them or when. By Mass Effect 2 it was established that the geth had received the technology from the Reapers, and dragon's teeth were found aboard a Derelict Reaper; notably, the Collectors used husks but were never seen deploying dragon's teeth, and their husks were more advanced and varied than those on Eden Prime. In Mass Effect 3, data from the Sanctuary husk experiments revealed that husk conversion was accelerated by the amount of adrenaline (or a cross-species equivalent) in the subject, which served as the delivery medium for the conversion nanites; the shock of being impaled raised the victim's adrenaline and thus sped the process.
