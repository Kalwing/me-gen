The virtual aliens were a race of roughly one billion individuals who, long ago, downloaded their minds into a virtual world aboard a starship to escape the destruction of their civilization. Their name for themselves is unknown, and their biological characteristics were never recorded because none of them possessed physical bodies. To communicate with the Citadel Council, they demonstrated the ability to swap consciousnesses with other sentient beings and control their bodies.

Approximately 8,000 years ago, the virtual aliens learned their homeworld's star was about to go supernova. They built a starship equipped with a network of supercomputers containing an entire virtual world, and one billion of them transferred their consciousnesses into it. A purpose-built AI piloted the ship and maintained the virtual world as they began a millennia-long journey across the galaxy.

In 2185 CE, the starship entered the salarian-inhabited Antilin system. The ship's AI sought help because the energy source sustaining the ship and the virtual world was dwindling. When salarians first detected the "ghost ship," they mistook it for the vanguard of a geth attack because it emitted strong AI heuristics. The human exobiologist Dr. Jordan Detweiler boarded the vessel, established contact with the AI, and learned of the virtual world and the energy problem. The Citadel Council, wary of the AI, was prepared to rule against aiding the ship, which alarmed Detweiler enough that he entered the virtual world to warn its inhabitants. He returned with an offer: the virtual aliens would trade their advanced technology for assistance. The Council classified all matters involving the ship as top-secret.

One month later it emerged that a virtual alien representative, Ambassador Sygan, had been using Dr. Detweiler's body to visit the Citadel and speak with the Council. In a press conference Sygan explained the situation and requested asylum, stating she was one of many of her race who wished to leave the virtual world. When the question arose of what would become of Detweiler, whose mind remained in the virtual world, a volunteer offered to host Sygan's mind in his place, and roughly 400 individuals of various races subsequently stepped forward to swap places with other virtual aliens. The storyline was told through the Cerberus Daily News "Alien Ghost Ship Encounter" feed.
