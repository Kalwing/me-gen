Biotic Acclimation and Temperance Training, abbreviated BAaT and pronounced "baht," was a programme established in 2160 to train potential human biotics. Children exposed to element zero who showed biotic potential worth augmenting were sent by the company Conatix Industries to Gagarin Station, also known as Jump Zero, for training, with support from the Systems Alliance. Most of those sent were teenagers, who nicknamed the programme "Brain Camp."

Anxious to avoid interference, the authorities worked to separate the trainees from their families, convincing parents that biotic abilities, and therefore the children themselves, were dangerous. Isolated aboard a remote station with no contact from home, the teenagers formed close circles of friends who worked, trained, and socialized together. Officially, BAaT existed to evaluate the trainees' abilities so an understanding of biotics could be compiled, but the central problem was that humans knew almost nothing about biotics and had no idea how to train the students. As Kaidan Alenko put it, instructors struggled just to keep the children from breaking their own limbs.

Frustrated by slow progress, Conatix sought experts to speed things up but, to avoid making humans appear weak, declined to ask the Citadel for help. Instead, in 2166, the company quietly hired several biotic turian mercenaries, the most notable being Commander Vyrnnus. Vyrnnus was a poor teacher who, though he accelerated progress, was brutal with trainees and used hunger and thirst as training incentives. Several students broke under his regime, and a few died from training accidents or strain. When Vyrnnus deliberately injured a trainee named Rahna, he suffered a broken neck in the resulting melee and died. Partly because of the diplomatic problems this caused with the turians, the early BAaT programme was shut down in 2169 and its records classified to conceal the Alliance's mistakes.

Human biotic training afterward was handled by the military and some private firms, though some techniques developed during the original BAaT programme remained in use. Notable BAaT students included Hendel Mitra, Kaidan Alenko, and Rahna.
