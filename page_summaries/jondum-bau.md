Jondum Bau was a salarian Spectre who also worked for the STG. In 2186 he came across information suggesting that high-level hanar officials might be indoctrinated and emailed Commander Shepard for assistance. Bau had learned that the Alliance had conducted a black ops raid on a batarian research station with hanar help, aiming to seize Reaper technology the batarians were studying. The Alliance ended up with nothing, while hanar operatives were suspected of escaping with tech likely to indoctrinate them. Bau obtained the soul names of the hanar involved, one of whom was now a diplomat on the Citadel. He had acquired the intel either by paying off weapons dealer Donovan Hock or through an anonymous "gift" he believed came from master thief Kasumi Goto, whom he had hunted for years while admiring her skills as "almost salarian."

Meeting Shepard at the Citadel Embassies, Bau explained the investigation's economic and territorial stakes for the salarians and tasked Shepard, using Spectre authority, with checking records he could not access through formal inquiry. He also assured Shepard that he and many other Spectres had taken the Commander's Reaper warnings seriously. After two suspects were eliminated, the third was revealed as the diplomat Zymandis, who admitted to uploading a virus to cripple Kahje's defense network.

At the hanar embassy, Bau tried to block the transmission but was attacked and choked by Zymandis's human bodyguard. If Kasumi was not present, Shepard had to choose between saving Bau or stopping the upload. Bau urged Shepard to stop the upload; doing so left Bau to fight off and shoot the bodyguard and Zymandis, taking a gut wound but surviving, remarking it had been an honor working with Shepard. If Shepard instead saved Bau, he shot Zymandis but the upload succeeded. If Kasumi was present, she decloaked and stopped the upload herself, the console exploding and apparently killing her, while Bau shot Zymandis and survived. If Bau lived, he pledged to bring friends to the war effort. The writer Patrick Weekes named him after colleague John Dombrow.
