This archive collected Cerberus Daily News reports from July 2185. Director Morgan Bierster's long-delayed space epic "Nekyia Corridor," a 5.3-billion-credit simulstim, opened to record first-day receipts, spread a productivity-sapping "Nekyia flu" across Citadel space, and became the highest-grossing sim ever. A meteor strike delayed construction of the human-built Susskind Supercollider, a fermitron in Trikalon's orbit, pushing completion to 2186. The war on Taetrus continued: a Facinus propaganda virus video circulated, colonialist forces used a parachute-dropped thermobaric bomb to end the Kasatum Fortress standoff, and General Partinax later said drones had confirmed no hostages were inside. A patent dispute over the Eupulmos respiratory device turned deadly when its volus inventor, Ora Thubb, went missing on Irune and was found murdered; two turian Spectres pulled off Taetrus delivered three suspects to police, underscoring the turian-volus alliance, while Ambassador Din Korlack noted volus firms had funded roughly 45 percent of the war's cost. Systems Alliance Prime Minister Amul Shastri met Salarian Dalatrass Narra on the salarian New Year, and Spectre Tela Vasir was credited with a mass arrest of over 200 alleged slavers on Nasurn. On Garvug, the Sonax command ship Excelsior was destroyed in orbit by a warp bomb hidden in a supply payload; guerrilla commander Hailot Wrund, appearing to have lost an arm, taunted "Tag," and corporatist forces retaliated with over 150 house-to-house raids. Vallum Blast pilot Vamire Squaron was found dead of apparent cardiac arrest, and Primarch Idus Valen presided over the destruction of captured weaponized FTL plotters. A space-tourism flight ended in tragedy when the Sorenson family's ship fell into a decaying orbit around the gas giant Archanes; daughter Kristal Sorenson repaired the booster but died of decompression sickness during the spacewalk, after which her mother Freja flew the ship to safety, and comedian Cal DiCosimo apologized for a callous joke about the death.
