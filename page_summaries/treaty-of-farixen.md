The Treaty of Farixen was an arms-control agreement among the major Citadel races limiting the number of dreadnoughts each could keep in its fleets. Agreed at the Farixen Naval Conference, it fixed a construction ratio because of the ships' destructive potential. The peacekeeping turian navy, which makes up most of the Citadel Fleet, sat at the top of the pyramid with the largest allowance; the asari and salarians came next; and Council associate races such as the hanar and volus were allowed the fewest. The ratio was set at 5:3:1 (turian to Council to associate). Signing the treaty was a requirement for any race seeking a Citadel embassy and associate Council membership. Carriers, though as large as dreadnoughts, were not covered by the treaty, so any number could legally be built.

Humanity joined the treaty in 2183 after the Farixen Conference, and following the Battle of the Citadel and its Council seat was presumably entitled to the 3:5 Council ratio. If the Council was left to die aboard the Destiny Ascension during that battle, news reports said the turians no longer felt bound by the treaty and stepped up shipbuilding.

By 2186, an Alliance News Network interview by Diana Allers revealed that the quarian Migrant Fleet, after acquiring Blue Suns salvage on Korlus, had armed every ship in its Civilian Fleet, even the liveships, with Thanix cannons, effectively converting them into dreadnoughts and violating the treaty. When the Citadel condemned this, Admiral Han'Gerrel vas Neema argued that since the Council had closed the quarian embassy, the quarians were no longer bound by the treaty. Shepard could raise the matter with Admiral Shala'Raan vas Tonbay, who said it was not technically a violation because liveships were designed for food cultivation, not combat, and that she would formally apologize to the Council after the war if necessary. The treaty is modeled on the 1922 Washington Naval Treaty, and the unrestricted growth of carriers parallels that treaty's unintended boost to aircraft carrier development.
