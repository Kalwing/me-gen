Omni-gel was a technological material with a wide range of applications, composed of common, reusable industrial plastics, ceramics, and light alloys kept in a semi-molten state. It could be looted, scavenged, purchased from vendors, or, most usefully, synthesized by breaking down existing equipment. Rendering non-essential or obsolete hardware into omni-gel kept it useful while minimizing weight and storage requirements.

In Mass Effect, set in 2183, omni-gel was designed for use with omni-tools to perform electronics or decryption work, and could also repair vehicles such as the M35 Mako, sometimes requiring the vehicle to be temporarily immobile or offline. Breaking down unwanted weapons, armor, and upgrades typically yielded four units per item, and Commander Shepard could carry up to 999 units in a standard combat hardsuit.

By 2185, in Mass Effect 2, common security updates prevented omni-gel from being used to bypass locks or security systems. In the Lair of the Shadow Broker DLC, Shepard reminisced with Liara T'Soni about "the old days when you could just slap omni-gel on everything," and Liara noted that the security upgrade had made many people unhappy. During the Andromeda Initiative in Mass Effect: Andromeda, omni-gel was a major crafting resource for equipment based on Milky Way technology, generally transported in specialized canisters. A running gag across the first two games featured a human customer arguing with shopkeepers over a refund and a "Cision omni-gel converter."
