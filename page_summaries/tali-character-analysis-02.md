This hand-corrected transcript was the second part of a character analysis of Tali'Zorah, covering Mass Effect 2, with part 3 covering her treason trial and fatal flaw. Much of the content is the essayist's interpretation.

In Mass Effect 1 Tali's suit was squared-away black and deep purple; in Mass Effect 2 she wore a gold neck piece and a looser hood, presented as "letting her hair down," older and more confident after two years, and one of the few quarians to personalize her suit. Her name had changed from Tali'Zorah nar Rayya, where "nar" marks a quarian who has not completed the Pilgrimage, to Tali'Zorah vas Neema, reflecting its completion. At 25 she commanded her own squad of quarian marines, showing leadership maturity gained serving with Shepard.

On Haestrom, where Shepard recruited her for the Suicide Mission, her whole squad was willing to die for her, including Kal'Reegar ("We don't have enough people on our side for you to take one for the team. Stand down."). She questioned whether the losses were worth the research the Admiralty Board demanded: "A lot of people died here. Some of them were my friends. All of them were good at their jobs. That damn data better be worth it." Lair of the Shadow Broker surveillance showed her repeatedly drafting and failing to finish letters to the families of the men who died protecting her. Tali was well-liked by the entire Normandy crew across all three games; Engineer Adams's dialogue established her as exceptionally intelligent and skilled with technology. In Mass Effect 3, if Tali died for the geth, Garrus was the only person to show a hint of anger toward Shepard: "her choice, that'll be harder to get over. She was a good friend."

The essay drew an extended comparison with Ashley Williams: both came from career-military families whose history shaped their careers (Ashley's grandfather surrendered to aliens at Shanxi; Tali's father was tried posthumously for treason), both overcame xenophobic hang-ups aboard the Normandy, both lost people under their command and were prone to self-sacrifice to preserve another's honor, and both enjoyed "sappy" poetry, musicals and literature (Ashley recites Tennyson's Ulysses; Tali loves the musical Fleet and Flotilla). If Shepard romanced Ashley in Mass Effect 1 and Tali afterward, Ashley uniquely approved: "Tali's like a sister."

The piece identified two parental figures. Admiral Shala'Raan vas Tonbay, "Auntie Raan," was a friend of Tali's father and a blood relative whose sister was Tali's mother; Tali treated her as family, and Raan called in favors to help ("Do you know how many favors I had to call in… I wore holes in my suit to get you that opportunity."), representing the family side of quarian society. Rael'Zorah, Tali's father, was a high-ranking figure who became distant by burying himself in work and confused duty to the Fleet with love for his daughter, showing his love, per Raan, by working to give Tali "a home world"; he represented the duty side. Between them, Tali was torn between ingrained duty to the Fleet and a capacity to think as a loving individual.

Finally, the essay covered suit culture: quarians live in their suits even among family, a simple rupture can kill, and linking suit environments is the most intimate act two quarians can perform, making both parties badly and potentially fatally ill as the culture's ultimate gesture of trust. When Raan took the infant Tali and sealed her in a sterile bubble, Raan and Tali's mother first linked their suit environments to share a clean room for the birth, which made both of them sick for a week. Children do not wear suits until adolescence, and receiving a first suit is a coming-of-age.
