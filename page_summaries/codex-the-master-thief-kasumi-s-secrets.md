These Codex entries covered subjects tied to Kasumi Goto's loyalty mission. The graybox, or mnemonic neural recall stimulator, was a brain implant that assisted and prioritized memory by helping the amygdalae chunk incoming stimuli into recallable blocks; it was originally developed to slow Alzheimer's disease. Synthetic Insights released grayboxes in 2140, marketed as a way for humans to match salarian eidetic memory, but because implantation irreversibly shifted the brain's workload onto the device, software bugs or removal attempts could cause incapacitating brain damage, so use narrowed to researchers and spies. In 2175 the Systems Alliance outlawed graybox sale and implantation after an incident involving Abraham Rumoi, an Alliance Intelligence Agency employee believed to be the professional con man and thief Keiji Okuda; prosecutors could not use his assisted memories as evidence because of protections against self-incrimination, and Rumoi disappeared after his trial. Recovered grayboxes were read with a specialized reader plus a separate decryption key.

The Kassa Fabrications Model 12 Locust was a lightweight submachine gun built for the Alliance for high-gravity worlds, using an internal "floating bed" to absorb recoil and permit match-grade autotargeting. Nicknamed "the gun that killed two Presidents," it was used in 2176 by Virginian patent clerk Michael Moser Lang, who concealed one in a shoulder-mounted video camera and fired on United North American States president Enrique Aguilar; when Chinese People's Federation premier Ying Xiong tackled Aguilar clear, Lang's later bursts passed through Xiong and fatally wounded both men. Donovan Hock's collection held two antique ivory-handled Locusts modified for thermal clips, one bearing the serial number of Lang's original, with a hidden data drive of fabrication specifications.

The Lady Liberty entry recounted the Statue of Liberty's history: in 2096 the New York chapter of a group called Freedoms First smuggled 15.5 tons of explosives onto Liberty Island to protest Canada and Mexico joining the United North American States and detonated them on November 1st, toppling the statue and accidentally killing four of their own; the outrage helped ignite the Second American Civil War. President Kaitlin Cheung ordered a rebuild, completed in 2101 using about one-tenth of the original's salvaged steel and copper. The original's head, later displayed in Washington, D.C., vanished during shelling in the Battle of Washington and was reportedly photographed in a star freighter's hold in 2159.

Bekenstein was a Systems Alliance luxury colony near the Citadel, chosen after the First Contact War as a trading arm. After early economic struggles it found a sustainable niche in high-quality entertainment and luxury goods and came to boast more millionaires and billionaires per capita than any other human colony, alongside very high suicide and inflation rates and mostly white-collar, nonviolent crime.
