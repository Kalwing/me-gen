The fifth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary covers the design of Mass Effect's branching narrative. It opens with Ayn Rand's 1930s play The Night of January 16th, in which the audience served as jury and their verdict changed the ending, as an early example of interactive narrative, then contrasts it with the light/dark choice in Knights of the Old Republic.

For Mass Effect, Hudson wanted choices made from the start to cascade across the whole trilogy: a squadmate killed in the first game would be absent from the next two, and a romance would be remembered in sequels. Lead cinematics designer Parrish Ley notes the team accepted that much of their work would be seen by only a small fraction of players. These branches had to coexist with far higher cinematic production values, "digital actors" with skin wrinkles, motion capture, moving eyes and voice performances. The chapter describes the dialogue wheel letting players quickly pick a response's tenor (neutral, friendly, hostile, investigative) and the Paragon/Renegade morality tracking. Watamaniuk's team ended up tracking about 700 "plot states" carried between games.

It profiles Hudson as a polymath who draws, programs, composes music and designed his own house, and recounts him photographing steaks, ribs, circuit boards and screws in his basement one night in 2007 to build the Eden Prime beacon vision sequence. Writer Mac Walters (hired after building a Neverwinter Nights add-on) began defining alien personalities, and Shauna Perry auditioned actors; the male Shepard's love interest Ashley was recast after E.G. Daily's version failed to land.

The chapter outlines the first game's plot around Shepard investigating a Prothean beacon and confronting Saren, who builds a geth army and is revealed to be brainwashed by the Reapers. It records cut content: the entire Xbox Live trading system, a Cerberus human-experimentation plot on a planet called Misery, and a mining planet Caleston (elements of which folded into Therum). It highlights the Virmire confrontation with Wrex over the genophage cure as the moment Hudson's vision of interactive choice and consequence came together, helped by whether the player retrieved Wrex's family armor earlier. Mass Effect was submitted to Microsoft as build 635 (RC2) on September 25, 2007.
