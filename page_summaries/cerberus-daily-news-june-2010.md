This archive collected Cerberus Daily News reports from June 2185, overwhelmingly focused on the war on Taetrus. A turian named Alsadrus Tertius crashed an aircar bomb into the kinetic barriers outside the emergency government headquarters at Vallum. Facinus leader Kihilix Tanus condemned the colonialist air campaign and challenged General Partinax to single combat; after negotiations and a delay over separatist fears of a trap, the two fought a barrier-room pistol duel that Partinax won, killing Tanus, whose body and entourage were returned unharmed. A bootleg video of the duel drew hundreds of millions of views, and separatists released a doctored version alleging Partinax used an illegal kinetic barrier. The ground campaign opened in the Diluvian Wildlands, with colonialist forces besieging and capturing the city of Spaedar; General Partinax announced the strategy of "safe camps" to induce surrender followed by hastatim execution squads going house to house, with underground estimates of 1,000 to 3,000 killed in Spaedar alone. Human journalist Grant Keyes died in colonialist custody under disputed circumstances; his camerawoman Letha Maragos also died, and Sergeant Sanus Iheras, blamed for the deaths, committed suicide per turian custom, closing the investigation to his widow Lara Keyes's dismay. Improvised FTL plotters were found in the city of Madra in a shed belonging to suspected Facinus supporter Ulrixen Ravidus, thought to have harbored Vallum Blast pilot Vamire Squaron. Umsung Holdings completed a roughly 119-billion-credit friendly buyout of Sonax Industries, with CEO Iseul Rhee pledging to establish a permanent "peace zone" on Garvug alongside Admiral Lira Speight. By month's end, colonialist forces reported capturing or killing 21 of the 24 top Facinus leaders amid casualty estimates in the tens of thousands, a separatist raid on a safe camp that backfired and increased public sympathy for the Hierarchy, flash mobs pressuring resistance fighters to surrender, and a failed separatist attack on a weapons-destruction facility. Director Morgan Bierster's "Nekyia Corridor" held its New York premiere, and the weekend box office fell 17 percent, blamed on wall-to-wall war coverage.
