The Relay Monument was a scale representation of a mass relay standing as a statue on the Citadel Presidium. It was believed to be a piece of Prothean art and had been in place since before the asari arrived on the station. According to the VI Avina, it was a popular topic among Citadel academics, with two competing theories about its meaning: that it was an expression of Prothean vanity proclaiming their galaxy-wide mastery of mass effect technology, or that it was a symbol of unity acknowledging that the relays would eventually bring other races to the Citadel. Commander Shepard's squadmates each offered their own opinions if spoken to nearby.

In fact the Monument was not a statue but an actual small-scale, inactive mass relay. Its partner relay, the Conduit, lay on Ilos. Together they formed a "back door" onto the Citadel, built by hidden Prothean scientists who survived their race's annihilation, allowing passage that bypassed the signal the Reapers used to control the Citadel, in the hope of breaking the cycle for later races. Saren Arterius intended to use the Conduit to bypass the Citadel's defenses and hand control of the station to Sovereign. Shepard drove the Mako through the Conduit in pursuit of Saren and emerged through the Relay Monument on the Presidium, crushing several geth on landing. As the Conduit was already closing during the transit, the Relay Monument was likely inactive again afterward.

Two years later, during Mass Effect 2, the Monument remained on the Presidium and was by then referred to as the Conduit by the Presidium Groundskeeper.
