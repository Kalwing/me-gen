This page transcribes the third part of The Art of Mass Effect (2007), with commentary by Casey Hudson. The Transport section covers the design of the game's vehicles and starships, opening with how mass relays - enormous antennae left by an ancient civilization - allow ships to cross hundreds of light-years quickly, and how each fleet was given a unique visual style.

It discusses the Mako (an all-terrain combat vehicle needing large wheels and ground clearance, some concepts hovering, the final version sleek with a shark-like nose); the Normandy exterior (inspired by the F-14 Tomcat's canted intakes and swept wings, elongated to look capable of light-speed travel, with vectored engine nozzles); the Normandy's submarine-like interior across cockpit, mid-deck, engine room and cargo bay; human ships with organic shapes that "swim" through space; hornet-inspired geth ships with insect-like feelers; Sovereign, standing more than a mile tall and imagined disrupting the weather, with insect-like features tying it to the geth; freighters built from reusable containers for economy; plate-layered turian ships evoking feathers; smooth, ocean-vessel-like salarian ships; and the asari-built Destiny Ascension, one of the biggest ships in the game.
