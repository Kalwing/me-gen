Citadel: Homecoming was acquired after Citadel: Expose Saren, once Tali had joined the squad. Samesh Bhatia approached Shepard in front of the embassy reception desk in the Presidium and asked for help recovering the body of his wife, Nirali Bhatia, a soldier of the 212 killed on Eden Prime, from the Alliance so he could give her a respectful cremation. He handed out the assignment even if Shepard declined to help.

Clerk Bosker, leaning against a wall in the nearby Embassies Lounge, explained that Nirali's body was being retained for tests intended to improve Alliance defenses against geth attacks, with results possibly years away. Bringing Ashley Williams, who had served with Nirali in the 212, added dialogue; Kaidan would note Ashley's connection if she was absent. Shepard chose whether to let the Alliance keep the body or have it returned, using Charm for 8 Paragon points or Intimidate for 9 Renegade points on either Bosker or Samesh. Resolution rested solely on Samesh's reaction: allowing the Alliance to keep the body without using a morality option to persuade Samesh left him angry with no rewards. Whatever the outcome, both Samesh and Bosker then left the Citadel.

In Mass Effect 2, if the body was returned, Shepard received a thank-you email from Samesh, who had opened the restaurant his wife had planned and made it free to all Alliance personnel; a news report on Omega noted Alliance recruiting troubles amid doubts it could match geth technology. The assignment was known internally as "In Flanders Fields," after John McCrae's war poem. Cut Mass Effect 2 content would have rewarded leaving the body with the Alliance with weapons upgrades.
