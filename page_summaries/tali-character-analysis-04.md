The fourth part of a hand-corrected video-essay character analysis covered Tali'Zorah in Mass Effect 3. If she survived the Suicide Mission, she apologized to Shepard for the trial and admitted he had been right: "I was too ready to sacrifice myself for the good of the Fleet. I need to stop doing that." She took an Admiral's seat in her father's place specifically to push back against the Board's worst ideas, the version of Tali the essayist preferred. If exiled, she instead told Shepard the trial had shown her she belonged "on the Normandy, helping you," aiding her people from the shadows.

As Admiral she doubted her fitness to command, with seventeen million lives riding on her. After speaking with Legion she believed the quarians could sue for peace, but once Daro'Xen turned Rael'Zorah's research into "an invasion too good to pass up," Tali had no choice but to support it "for the good of the Fleet," regressing toward the sacrificial lamb of ME2 and becoming a near-powerless victim of politics again. The essay noted quarian scarcity: 17 million in the flotilla, one child per family unless the population declines, every quarian contributing to the Fleet's survival. Shala'Raan was the reason Tali was permitted to attempt to retake the Alarei.

At the Rannoch climax Shepard chose between peace, geth genocide, or quarian genocide. Siding with the quarians, Tali killed Legion (or shut the geth down) with a "flat shovel blade," expressed regret, and answered Legion's last question, "Does this unit have a soul?", with "Yes... yes, it does." Siding with the geth, Tali begged Shepard not to choose them over her people, then as Admiral called off the quarian fleet's attack while Legion sacrificed himself to disseminate the code. On the botched path, unable to stop Han'Gerrel, Rael and Daro'Xen, Tali watched what remained of the quarian fleet burn in Rannoch's atmosphere, Captain Ysra of the Rayya on the radio, and took her own life before her home ship burned. Afterward, if the geth helped rebuild Rannoch, Tali admitted: "I'd have killed the geth with no regrets, and I'd have been wrong."
