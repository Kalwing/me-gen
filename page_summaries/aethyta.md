Matriarch Aethyta was an asari who worked as a bartender in the Eternity lounge on Illium. In Mass Effect 2 she told Commander Shepard how she came to that job despite being a matriarch. Her father was a krogan who fought in the Rachni Wars and Krogan Rebellions, and her mother was an asari commando who also fought in the Rebellions. When her parents learned they had once been enemies during the Rebellions, they fought a duel that neither survived; Aethyta was then about a hundred years old and an exotic dancer. She later took many mates, including a turian, an elcor, and a hanar, and fathered a pureblood daughter she lost track of.

Aethyta held unconventional, free-spirited views. She supported the asari becoming more militant and active in galactic affairs and believed young asari should improve society during their maiden years rather than becoming strippers or mercenaries. Her proposal that the asari build new mass relays was ridiculed, prompting her to leave Thessia for Illium. Shepard met her while she fended off Conrad Verner, who was trying to obtain the deed to her bar on behalf of an "undercover cop" who claimed the place was a front for red sand dealers.

In Mass Effect 3, Aethyta appeared at Apollo's Cafe in the Presidium Commons. She confirmed she was Liara T'Soni's "father" and was watching Liara on behalf of other matriarchs, who were suspicious of Liara's work as an information broker, her ties to Matriarch Benezia, and her friendship with the formerly Cerberus-affiliated Shepard. She warned that the matriarchs might order a hit on Liara. If Shepard was friendly toward Liara, Aethyta was amused to hear Shepard echo her "nobody messes with my girl" line and conceded Shepard might be good enough for her daughter. If Shepard arranged for the two to talk, they discussed the matriarchs' spying, asari war doctrine, Liara's krogan grandfather, and Aethyta's century-long relationship with Benezia, whom Aethyta implied she had married and still loved. Reconciled, Aethyta sent asari mercenaries to aid the war effort.
