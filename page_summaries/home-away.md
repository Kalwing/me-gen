Home Away was a flower-shaped luxury space station in Earth orbit, owned by the HOME Group and partially funded by UNIN. It was marketed as a safe, local, small-business alternative to distant planetary colonization ventures, offering residents a perpetual view of Earthrise, high-end VIs, and domestic mechs. Its design echoed the Citadel but with several dozen docking-ring platforms instead of five large arms. Though delicate-looking, it was reinforced with carbon nanofilaments, and each platform had landscaped greenery, artificial water, and multi-level residences beneath mass effect fields, one even featuring a snow-capped hill. Its eezo drive core was rated above class-two and subject to Citadel inspection because of the government funding.

The station's mechs were plated against standard energy weapons and run by a Synthetic Insights SleepEasy Series security VI, backed by turrets, anti-personnel weaponry, and paramilitary troops. Internal comms were mostly unsecured because defenses were built against remote cyberattack rather than intruders arriving in person; facial recognition was extranet-linked in case of infiltration. As a real-estate station it had no anti-spacecraft defenses, only construction-grade debris-clearing weapons.

In Mass Effect Andromeda: Initiation, software stolen from the Andromeda Initiative was stored at one of Home Away's data centers. It proved to be an artificial intelligence kernel that had merged with the station VI, creating a "fake AI" that took control of human-looking combat cyborgs built with tech from Quiet Eddy to stop the intruder Cora Harper from leaving; she and her team escaped explosively, and afterward no residents were reported aware of the infiltration. Cora's team had entered disguised as government inspectors, and one infiltrator actually performed the overdue inspection of the station's eezo converters. The Silhaus family owned a California hacienda-style villa on one platform, complete with shuttle pad, garden, teardrop pool, and security drones, where Alec Ryder confronted UNIN delegate Alis Silhaus during his investigation into the repeated theft of his kernel.
