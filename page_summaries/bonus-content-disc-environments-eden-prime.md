This page transcribed art director Derek Watts's gallery commentary for "The Environments of Mass Effect: Eden Prime," from the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. Watts said Eden Prime was first conceived as a farm world of rolling hills, hedges and large oak trees resembling the English countryside, an agricultural planet under attack meant to evoke the D-Day landings across Europe. Early maps had the player land in the Normandy, move up through encounters, overlook large crop fields, and descend into a meteorite trench ploughed by the crashing Prothean ruin, where the dig camp sat; the trench was reworked to look more like a dig site.

Cut ideas included floating crop beds that flew over the surface and could crush enemies below or be started up to cross, and a notion that the whole planet was a man-made metal world with grass and crops laid on top. Large man-made towers were kept, stripped of extra fins, to give the planet a science-fiction feel. Watts described building the level in blocks, moving the train station and towers farther apart to reduce detailing work, and a planned combat sequence on top of a moving train that was cut because of grenade-physics problems and characters falling off, becoming a cinematic instead. Eden Prime was one of the first areas built and a showcase location, so it received the most time. An early near-final version with towers, floating seeds, water and a beautiful sky was rejected after feedback that players did not feel connected to it and it did not look sufficiently war-torn. A new pass added the geth attack: impaling spikes with people on them, a deep red sky, burnt-out towers, scorched ground and floating embers, keeping some life in the world rather than making it a baked-glass rock, plus the scorch mark where Sovereign set down.
