Sentry Turret was a tech power in Mass Effect 3's single-player and multiplayer modes, available to the single-player Engineer and to the multiplayer Quarian Engineer and Turian Saboteur Engineer. It deployed a stationary heavy-weapon turret for cover fire, thrown like a grenade rather than appearing on a target like the Combat Drone, and failed to deploy on many surfaces or too close to terrain. Rank 1 gave 25 damage in single-player (75 in multiplayer) and 500 turret shields (1000 multiplayer), with a 5-second recharge. Higher ranks improved recharge, shields, and damage, and offered branching upgrades: Rank 4 between more shields and damage or a stunning shock attack; Rank 5 between cryo ammo (chance to freeze for 3 seconds) or armor-piercing ammo (100% bonus damage versus armor); and Rank 6 between long-range rockets (150/390 damage in a small radius) or a close-range flamethrower (84.5 damage per second).

The turret's normal attack was a three-round burst every 2.5 seconds, whose base damage could only be raised by evolving the power itself (with the exception of Rank 6 Tech Mastery in single-player). It could stagger unprotected infantry, occasionally headshot, and sometimes miss. Many enemies ignored it entirely, including Husks, Abominations, Brutes, Scions, Praetorians, Seeker Swarms, Ravagers, Atlases, Dragoons, Adjutants, and Disruption Drones. Swarmers were drawn to it but could not attack it, making it a useful "bug zapper," especially with Shock or Flamethrower.

Sentry Turret could create Power Combos: Cryo Ammo primed frozen unprotected enemies for Cryo Explosions, and the Flamethrower primed health or armor for Fire Explosions. The turret blocked allied gunfire and powers, including its owner's. On higher difficulties enemies destroyed it quickly, so it worked best as a distraction, a flank guard, a scouting tool thrown around corners, and a source of crowd control. In multiplayer it dealt significantly more damage and, like the Geth Turret, would turn toward newly spawned enemies a few seconds into a wave, revealing approach routes.
