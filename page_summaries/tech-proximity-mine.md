Tech proximity mines were a delivery system in the first Mass Effect game for the tech abilities Sabotage, Overload, and Damping. They were the closest squadmates came to using grenades, less powerful but effectively unlimited once recharged. Their damage, radius, and recharge time improved with higher levels of Decryption, Damping, and Hacking respectively.

A mine usually detonated instantly when a tech talent was used directly on an enemy. Used on a wall or obstacle where enemies were approaching, it could be laid as a minefield that triggered when foes came close. The effects passed through terrain and obstructions. Unlike grenades, the mines had no timed fuse but vanished once combat ended or after a long lull with no enemies. Mines could not be launched through cover, with one exception: a squadmate manually ordered to use Sabotage, Overload, or Damping deployed the mine anywhere the player had line of sight, even if the squadmate did not; left to act on their own, squad AI tended to waste the ability by firing into nearby terrain. Mines could hang in the air at the limit of their range if not placed on a surface. Their detonation damage, though low, bypassed shield protection, though the delivered ability itself did not. Enemies with Sabotage, Overload, or Damping could deploy mines the same way, and like squad AI ignored obstructions when doing so, which could be exploited to avoid being hit.
