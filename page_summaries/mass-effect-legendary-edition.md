The Mass Effect Legendary Edition was a remaster of the Mass Effect trilogy, released May 14, 2021 for Xbox One, PlayStation 4, and PC, with forward compatibility and targeted enhancements on Xbox Series X and PlayStation 5. It bundled Mass Effect, Mass Effect 2, and Mass Effect 3 in one launcher along with over 40 single-player DLCs, remastered and optimized for 4K UltraHD. Mass Effect 3 Multiplayer and its DLCs, Pinnacle Station, and the M-597 Ladon weapon were not included. Visual improvements covered enhanced models, shaders, effects, lighting, depth of field, HDR support, and a photo mode across all three games (attributed to Technical Design Director Brenon Holmes). Commander Shepard's appearance used a unified character creator with all trilogy options available; the Mass Effect 3 default female Shepard face was retouched and backported. The first game in particular gained improved controls, cameras, weapon balance, squad AI, cover behavior, a full worldbuilding pass, shorter elevator loading, and a skip button for elevator conversations. Mass Effect 2's morality checks between arguing squadmates were relaxed. Several long-standing bugs were fixed, including the AMD "black blobs" bug on Noveria and music cutting off during Samara and Morinth's confrontation. All difficulty levels were available from the start. The announcement came on N7 Day, November 7, 2020. Pinnacle Station was cut because Demiurge Studios' code backups were corrupted and rebuilding it would have taken six months; game director Mac Walters called the decision "heartbreaking." Multiplayer was cut for logistical reasons, with related Mass Effect 3 story sections redistributed and War Assets rebalanced. The remaster kept Unreal Engine 3 rather than moving to Unreal Engine 4, since the switch would have changed how the games felt and played; BioWare instead pushed UE3, adding real-time dynamic reflections by rendering scenes twice. The Legendary Edition offered no new content, romances, or resurrected cut content. The current patch version noted was 1.03. The system menu's brightness and HDR submenu was named "Calibration," a nod to a recurring Garrus line.
