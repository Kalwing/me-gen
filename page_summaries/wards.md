The Wards were the five "arms" of the Citadel, its residential centres, each a self-contained city home to millions with population density comparable to Singapore or Hong Kong. Ward skyscrapers were sealed against vacuum above a certain height, and although residents rearranged buildings to their liking, the keepers periodically descended to rearrange areas themselves. In an emergency the Wards could be closed, turning the Citadel into an impenetrable cylinder to protect those inside. Design inspiration came from Dubai and Tokyo, and the arms resemble an O'Neill cylinder space habitat.

The five Wards were Zakera Ward (the only one visitable in 2185), Kithoi Ward (home to the Council Central Archives, Taralos Amphitheater and a Sensory Gallery where visitors could experience other species' senses; a sanitation worker, Danaria Kleris, was found dead there near the keepers' area), Tayseri Ward (home to the Dilinaga Concert Hall, heavily damaged by Sovereign's destruction), Bachjret Ward (where Jacob Taylor once lived), and a fifth Ward called either Shalta Ward (per Dr. Karin Chakwas) or Aroch Ward (per a C-Sec VI during the Cerberus coup).

In Mass Effect, the Upper Wards connected to the Presidium via the Wards Access corridor and held Dr. Michel's Med Clinic, the club Flux, and windows overlooking the Serpent Nebula and the passing Destiny Ascension; Terra Firma held an Armistice Day protest there. The Markets had two levels: the Upper Markets with the volus merchant Expat, and the less reputable Lower Markets with the salarian merchant Morlan and Conrad Verner nearby. The Lower Wards held Chora's Den, a club run by the crime boss Fist, and the C-Sec Academy with the Normandy's private docking bay. In Mass Effect 3: Citadel, the Wards included the Silversun Strip (Armax Arsenal Arena, Silver Coast Casino, Castle Arcade, Tiberius Towers) and the Citadel Archives, a restricted complex of movable mass-effect-sealed iridium vaults storing artifacts, recordings and identification records, which could go into lockdown in an emergency.
