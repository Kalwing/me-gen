Illium was a classic garden world in the Tasale System of the Crescent Nebula, the second planet, developed as an entrepot for trade between the Terminus Systems and the Asari Republics. Though colonized and operated by asari corporate interests rather than governed as an asari world, it enjoyed the same legal latitude as the human research enclaves of Noveria: Council customs laws on product safety, proscribed materials, and sapient trafficking were relaxed. It was one of the youngest asari colonies, settled during the 7th Expansion Wave, with the first child born there only reaching middle age. The world was hot and massive; ground settlement was possible only at high polar latitudes, while equatorial populations lived in arcology skyscrapers to escape the surface heat.

The Codex described Illium as a regional hub of asari commerce awash in riches, infamous for abusive labor practices, legal indentured servitude, and the legalization of nearly everything except murder. It was a preferred production site for weapons and pharmaceuticals illegal elsewhere, including biotics-related drugs made by the rising Dantius Corporation. Despite the dangers of its products, Illium was renowned for glamour, luxury, and safety maintained by near-total surveillance, making it a favored tourist and celebrity destination. Its capital was Nos Astra; other locales included Manjoros Plaza and Saefos Valley. Its main drawback was an extensive bureaucracy tolerated for the security it provided, and its self-congratulatory media glorified "sexiest CEOs" and "richest residents" lists with the arrogance of "new money." Visitors were warned not to sign anything due to lax legal regulation. Few quarians visited, as Illium barred the Migrant Fleet from its system.

Illium featured heavily in Mass Effect 2 as the setting for recruiting Thane Krios and Samara, Liara T'Soni's Shadow Broker work and Lair of the Shadow Broker, and Miranda: The Prodigal, plus numerous assignments. In Mass Effect 3, Reaper forces met surprising resistance: Illium's wealthy, paranoid leaders had outfitted a large defense fleet with many fission weapons and used them on the Reapers' troop transports rather than their capital ships, slowing the ground invasion to a crawl while the Reapers manufactured more troops.
