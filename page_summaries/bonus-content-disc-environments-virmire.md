The page transcribed art director Derek Watts' gallery commentary for "The Environments of Mass Effect: Virmire," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. Watts said Virmire was first concepted as a tropical vacation paradise, with references drawn from Thailand and the James Bond film The Man with the Golden Gun: tall rock formations, floating platforms, and turquoise water. When designers decided the location would instead be an enemy base, much of the work was redone with storm clouds, sail-like fabric structures, and darker, more menacing lighting.

Watts walked through the Mako vehicle level map, which routed the player through shallow pools and sandbars to gatehouses that forced dismounted combat; the salarian holding cells the player can choose to blast; Saren's communication room containing a Prothean beacon where the player speaks with Sovereign; and the krogan breeding grounds with capsule tubes, mechanical arms, and two gun turrets, one of which is destroyed before the fight with Saren. The giant gun turret was made by scaling up the sniper rifle model and adding shields. Detail meshes, such as concrete bases, wire clips, linkage joints, and a large metal door, took four to five days each and were reused across the level.

Level artists built terrain, then switched to static meshes for the vertical rock walls because the terrain could not achieve the desired shape. Railings were modified because they interfered with cover, and extra metal barriers were added for cover during playtesting. Cube-map shine was added to break up excessive concrete. Watts noted the walkways area, with background waterfalls and elevated platforms originally designed for the scrapped tropical version, became the pivotal point where the player decides whether to keep Wrex, speaks with Sovereign, and learns the location of Ilos.
