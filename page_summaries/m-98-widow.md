The M-98 Widow was a heavy single-shot anti-materiel sniper rifle appearing in Mass Effect 2, Mass Effect 3, and Mass Effect: Andromeda. Weighing 39 kilograms in its Mass Effect 2 incarnation, it was described as a rifle used by sniper teams against armored vehicles or krogan, effective against armor, shields, and biotic barriers, and powerful enough to defeat the kinetic barriers of military field generators. It was never designed to be fired by a human; the modified model could be carried, but no ordinary human could fire it without shattering an arm. In-game descriptions and Legion's dialogue during Rannoch: Geth Fighter Squadrons treated it as a geth weapon, though its true origins were left unknown and it appeared visually distinct from other geth arms, implying possible quarian design.

In Mass Effect 2 the Widow was one of three possible rewards from the Collector Ship mission, usable only by Soldier or Infiltrator Shepard; discussing upgrades with Legion made a version researchable that only Legion could wield. It offered devastating one-shot kills, especially paired with Adrenaline Rush or Tactical Cloak, but lost much of its power punching through shields or barriers. In Mass Effect 3 several research firms spent fortunes redesigning the weapon to reduce recoil until one company produced a market model. It was found during Priority: Thessia and could otherwise be bought from Kassa Fabrication; in multiplayer it was a rare weapon. It was among the heaviest and most damaging single-shot weapons, surpassed only by the Javelin, with slow reload, small ammo reserve, and innate 50cm penetration. In Mass Effect: Andromeda the Initiative modified acquired Widows for portability, though the researchable Isharay outclassed it in nearly every respect.

The rifle's model number displayed the text "NCC 1701A," the hull registry of the second USS Enterprise from Star Trek. The Black Widow was an Alliance variant. Mass Effect: Andromeda dropped the M-98 designation. A Widow could be scanned aboard the derelict Reaper in Mass Effect 2 for a damage upgrade.
