The Reegar Carbine was a shotgun appearing in Mass Effect 3 and Mass Effect: Andromeda. Despite its name, it functioned as a short-range automatic close-quarters weapon that fired a sustained stream of electricity with very limited range, comparable to a narrow flamethrower such as the M-451 Firestorm. It improved on the arc pistol's design by generating a sustained current on its target, and was named for the quarian Reegar family, whose marines served valiantly against the geth; the Andromeda codex specifically credits "the quarian Reegar," and describes an invisible laser that ionizes the air to create a path for a high-amperage electric shock.

In Mass Effect 3 the weapon's electric current counted mechanically as a stream of bullets, so bullet-based mods, ammo powers, and headshot multipliers all worked with it. It dealt 200% bonus damage to shields and barriers but only 50% against armor, making it strong for classes lacking shield-stripping abilities; a full clip could dissolve the shields or barriers of the toughest foes even on higher difficulties. It could fry Cerberus Guardians through their handheld shields without penetration mods. Its weaknesses were extremely limited range, a small magazine, and a very high ammo consumption rate, so a backup weapon and mods such as the Shotgun Spare Thermal Clip were recommended. It was useful against Reaper forces because it destroyed enemy bodies, denying Cannibals corpses to consume, and against Phantoms whose toughness lay mostly in their barrier. Vanguards and Infiltrators used it best because their powers kept them safe at close range; squadmates other than a melee-prone Urdnot Wrex misjudged its range. It was acquired via the Firefight Pack (single-player) or the Rebellion Pack (multiplayer, classified rare), and in Legendary Edition from the Elkoss Combine Arsenal Supplies terminal for 10,000 credits.

In Mass Effect: Andromeda it fired a more focused stream than the ME3 version, radiated energy to harm enemies near the main target, and could damage enemies through thin walls. It required research and development using resources including omni-gel, copper, fluorite, and element zero, and remained best suited to close-quarters brawler classes due to its total lack of damage beyond its short effective range.
