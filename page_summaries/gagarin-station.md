Gagarin Station, named after Yuri Gagarin and colloquially known as Jump Zero, was for decades before the discovery of the Charon Relay the furthest human-made structure from Earth. It gained the name Jump Zero because it served as the launching point for early failed attempts at faster-than-light travel before mass effect field technology was developed. The station was used for deep space telemetry, exploration, and experiments too dangerous to conduct near more valuable assets.

In the early years of biotic research, Gagarin Station served as a safe place to evaluate emergent biotic humans, and in rare cases alien experts were secretly brought in to assist with training, since humans lacked experience in the field. During the BAaT training programme the station was taken off the extranet for security to keep subjects isolated. After biotic training concluded, the Alliance used Jump Zero for AI research, and the Alliance's first experimental AI, Eliza, achieved sapience aboard.

As of 2183, Gagarin Station was the largest Alliance deep space station, with a permanent population of about 9,000. A proposal to move it to the gravitationally stable barycenter between Pluto and the Charon Relay, to serve as a gateway between the Sol and Arcturus systems, was delayed indefinitely by the high cost of relocating its mass. In 2184, a terrorist group known as Totenkopf attacked the station with a prion-based biowarfare agent called EHE (exotic humanoid encephalopathy); details were sparse, with speculation pointing to the asteroid Israfil in the Vallhallan Threshold or to synthesis in local labs. An earlier Codex edition stated the station was partially disassembled and moved beyond the near-Earth relay, contradicting later cost-prohibitive accounts.
