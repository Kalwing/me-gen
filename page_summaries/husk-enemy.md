Husks were the basic footsoldiers of the geth, Collector and ultimately Reaper armies, and this page covers them as an enemy unit across the trilogy.

In Mass Effect, husks were encountered in packs on numerous worlds, often supported by geth, who impaled dead and living bodies on mechanical spikes ("Dragon's Teeth") that drained water and trace minerals and replaced them with reanimating cybernetics. No one understood why a synthetic race would bother extracting such minimal resources. Husks counted as organic enemies, so Hacking failed against them but Neural Shock worked; they had shields, making Overload effective. Their signature Tesla Pulse was a close-range electrical overload that charged briefly then released a wide, cover-penetrating tech blast dealing about 3x damage to shields versus health and ignoring most damage-protection stats, though tech-hardening stats and light armor reduced it. They could be knocked down during the charge-up to prevent it, sprinted past, or kited. Their bodies were vulnerable to melee, and they could open doors indoors; outdoors the Mako could run them down. Husks on Eden Prime had only two shield bars versus three thereafter.

In Mass Effect 2 (2185), husks appeared as Collector cannon fodder or near Reaper technology, losing the shield-discharge ability and instead charging to maul with knockback melee attacks; they gained light armor on higher difficulties. They could be sidestepped, killed by leg shots that sever them, and instantly destroyed by force powers (Concussive Shot, Pull, Shockwave, Throw, Charge, Slam) when unprotected, or frozen and shattered by Cryo effects. Incinerate, Reave, Armor Piercing and Incendiary Ammo stripped armor; squadmates Grunt, Jack, Mordin, Zaeed, Samara and Thane were noted as effective.

In Mass Effect 3 (2186), husks returned en masse as the bulk of Reaper ground forces, first met during the invasion of Earth. They had lower health, no armor bar even on the highest difficulties, and no shields, but were often deployed with Barrier Generators and could receive armored plating from Marauders. Their main threat was a grab that pinned and drained health, escapable by rapid melee (triggering a throw-and-stomp kill). They were immune to electrocution stuns, moved erratically to foil headshots, could climb short walls, and served as first-wave cannon fodder; explosive-round weapons and area powers cleared groups quickly.
