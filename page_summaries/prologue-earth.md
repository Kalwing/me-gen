Prologue: Earth is the opening mission of Mass Effect 3, acquired automatically at the start of the game. It serves as the game's tutorial. After character customization and a series of cutscenes with morality-point dialogue, Shepard enters a training stage in war-torn Vancouver during the Reaper invasion. Anderson gives Shepard an M-3 Predator pistol, and the two move across rooftops while Oculi destroy Alliance fighters overhead. Shepard encounters husks climbing a wall and a child fleeing toward a building; Anderson eventually runs out of ammo and climbs down a ladder. Shepard fights husks in melee before a Reaper fires its laser at an adjacent building.

Moving through the ruins, Shepard opens a med kit, briefly encounters the boy again, and then witnesses an Alliance vessel destroyed by a Reaper while Ashley Williams or Kaidan Alenko comments over the radio. Shepard reaches two Alliance soldiers and fights the first engagement against Cannibals, which here do not use grenades. Anderson can now deal damage and be assigned squad points. After crossing a bridge, salvaging a second med kit, and picking up an M-8 Avenger, Shepard activates a radio and fights further Cannibal waves until the Normandy SR-2 arrives and clears the remaining enemies.

Shepard says goodbye to Anderson, who stays on Earth. The boy is killed when the Kodiak evacuating him is shot down by a Destroyer. The Normandy leaves Vancouver, passing burning human fleets. Aboard ship, James Vega is incredulous that Shepard intends to follow Anderson's orders and leave Earth to seek the Citadel Council's aid; the exchange allows Paragon or Renegade interrupts. Admiral Hackett then contacts Shepard and assigns the next mission, Priority: Mars.

Enemies are Cannibals and Husks. Rewards include three units of medi-gel, the M-3 Predator, and the M-8 Avenger. Trivia notes Vancouver was chosen because BioWare is a Canadian company; the ship shown destroyed is actually an Alliance cruiser rather than the dreadnought Ashley or Kaidan describe, a discrepancy noted in SolComms reports by Emily Wong; and fleeing civilians visible in the distance are 2D sprites resembling Jack.
