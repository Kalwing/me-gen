The M29 "Grizzly" Infantry Fighting Vehicle was the standard "battle taxi" of Systems Alliance Marines for nearly thirty years. Excellent in long-term planetary campaigns, its bulk and weight made it unsuitable for rapid deployment across the Alliance's expanding sphere, so the smaller M35 "Mako" was designed to fit the cargo bays of scouting frigates and largely replaced it.

Several meters longer than the Mako but roughly the same height when stripped of antenna and turret, the Grizzly seated six including the driver, split into a driver/antenna/engine compartment and a passenger/turret compartment. Front-mounted power cells ran onboard systems and drove all six wheels, each protected by wheel covers. The military-grade version came with an antenna/turret package or an antenna-only package; the oldest recorded use was a turretless variant in a First Contact War skirmish, later recreated as a Pinnacle Station simulation, painted in Alliance Navy white. A civilian-grade VT7, lacking antenna and turret, followed, painted brown with orange highlights. Grizzlies were maintained either parked over a trench (as at Port Hanshan on Noveria) or raised on gantries (as at a torch facility on Asteroid X57).

Military variants were used by the Alliance and by corporations handling planetary security (ERCS) and colonization (ExoGeni). The Alliance occasionally shipped Grizzlies to cooperating warlords, delivering two fully equipped M29s to Lord Darius on Nonuel for use against batarians in the Skyllian Verge, and some reached mercenary groups such as one on Presrop. Civilian VT7s served biotic cultists, mercenaries, scavengers, and wildcat miners, including an ExoGeni survey team on Trebin. Wrecked, abandoned, or stripped Grizzlies dotted many uncharted worlds, with seven on Feros and several destroyed by geth along Noveria's Aleutsk Valley. Designations included the M29A (turret) on Edolus, Feros, Nonuel, and Presrop; the M29B (antenna only) on Asteroid X57, Feros, and Noveria; and the VT7 on Klensal, Presrop, and Trebin. Developed as an early Mako concept the team liked visually but disliked in gameplay, it was kept as a static object; in the original Port Hanshan garage it was mislabeled a Mako and could be hacked and driven to help clear the geth, though not in Legendary Edition.
