The ninth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary covers Mass Effect 3's slipping schedule. The game was announced in December 2010 at the Spike Video Game Awards for a holiday 2011 release, using the London soldier's line "If Shepard doesn't bring help soon, there won't be an Earth left to save." By March 2011 BioWare general manager Aaron Flynn, also dealing with Dragon Age II, faced a room of Mass Effect developers worried about the date; he called Ray Muzyka, and within 24 hours he and EA games-label president Frank Gibeau agreed to move the launch to March 2012.

The extra months still required cuts. The first "CAT" (catalyst) mission, where the player would first meet a Prothean, was removed from the main plot and later became the From Ashes post-release content. This had knock-on effects: the Thessia mission had to be rewritten so the Illusive Man obtains the Prothean data another way, and the order of the Thessia and Citadel missions was swapped so Cerberus would not appear too late, meaning any character who dies on the Citadel is unavailable on Thessia.

The chapter notes gameplay refinements such as grenades to force player movement, and multiplayer designers helping tighten the single-player combat. It records that for months the team could not settle the Illusive Man's showdown; Kai Leng (with a snake tattoo) was introduced as Shepard's recurring foil, and a plan to have the Illusive Man become a larger cybernetic monster was dropped as too predictable a boss ending. Hudson and Corey Gaspur demonstrated a Rannoch Reaper-battle sequence at E3 2011 and announced the March 6, 2012 date. The chapter mentions requests the team could not fulfill, including a mission on Palaven, and ends with Hudson taking his wife on a flight over the Rockies on Canada Day before the final six-month push.

A "Who's Who" lists the Mass Effect 3 squad: Ashley Williams, EDI, Garrus Vakarian, James Vega, Kaidan Alenko, Liara T'Soni and Tali'Zorah nar Rayya.
