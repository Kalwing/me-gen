Cerberus Daily News - September 2010 archived the in-universe Cerberus Network news feed for September 2010, set in 2185 CE and given as dated dispatches without headlines. The month's dominant story was the "alien ghost ship." MIT exobiologist Jordan Detweiler, who had discovered a virtual alien civilization on a derelict spacecraft's supercomputer, was reported on the Citadel behaving strangely. His daughter Kyra Detweiler alleged the Citadel Council was concealing his deteriorating condition. Council spokesman Anton Galer eventually confirmed that Detweiler had "swapped" consciousness with an alien, Ambassador Sygan, whose people had fled into a virtual world when their sun went supernova. Sygan, inhabiting Detweiler's body, publicly petitioned the Council for asylum and refused to return. After deadlocked deliberations, the Council resolved to transfer Sygan into a volunteer's body so Detweiler could reclaim his own, and roughly 400 individuals of various species volunteered to swap places with virtual aliens. A second running story was the "Wee-Cee" simulstim bug, which cut static containing masked data into simulstim feeds and caused headaches and seizures; investigators traced it to start-up media company Shared Visions as a failed subliminal-advertising delivery system, leading to 15 arrests and lawsuits from over 23 million users seeking up to 3.8 billion credits, with rival executive Marcus D'Angelo dismissing the scheme as "quaint." The corporate war on Garvug ended when Sonax Industries' board voted 6-5 to withdraw troops after six months, an estimated 21 billion credits, and thousands of casualties, with Guanghui Solutions immediately following; commentators concluded corporate armies could not hold a planet against determined guerrillas. Krogan Rebellions history resurfaced with a mass grave of over 430 turian soldiers from 749 CE found on Uresium, which chief archaeologist Salur Darikun pledged to repatriate to Palaven. Lighter items covered Kurt Riven's best-selling e-book arguing against first contact, socialite Aish Ashland's near-instant divorce from singer Jenellen Lepp, record varren breeding rights, the "Galaxy of Fantasy" expansion "Waters of Kolono" and its server problems, musician Gretel Batts's onstage meltdown, an enormous fossil found on Sifurman, and protests on the human colony Sathur over governor Vien Suchart's plan to grow the ossilbir vine. Political commentator Erlan Golta, sparring with talk-show host Bryan Theodor, claimed the Councilors' offices had drawn up orders to charge Commander Shepard with obstruction of justice after the Battle of the Citadel.
