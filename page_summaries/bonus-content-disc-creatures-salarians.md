This page transcribed Matt Rhodes's gallery commentary for "The Creatures of Mass Effect: Salarians," from the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. Rhodes said the salarians began as a riff on the classic "grey" Roswell alien, an idea quickly dropped but useful as a launching-off point. Knowing the species was highly intelligent and almost poetic, the team imagined them as "warrior poets" with an approach to war that found beauty and contemplation in it. Design cues that persisted included very large, deep, watchful dark eyes derived from the grey concept and a long, low, pronounced mouth that let them appear to sneer in disdain, as an alien that thinks it is better than you "and probably is." Early heads featured head tattoos, earrings and ornamentation; variants explored sleek hand-to-hand body armor with an almost "ninja"-like agility and a more roguish mercenary treatment to show the species had its less admirable members.

Sung Kim designed the final salarian body, built to suit a previously approved head, drawing on aquatic themes with blue, shiny, soft, dolphin-like skin. Rhodes said everyone agreed the concave, sunken chest was essential to the character, reading well narratively as the galaxy's intellectualists or "thought police" having sunken chests, while the body still fit the human skeleton shared across the game's aliens. The approved sketch had the big eyes, brows, wide-set nostrils, upper nostrils on the forehead, and a hole through the head formed by joining what began as horns; that distinctive shape recurred in salarian costuming and artwork. Rhodes also described ceremonial salarian priest robes whose hood bore the head-hole symbol, and the idea that the concave chest was a social stigma, so costuming added a ring or chest piece, an "ego piece," to fake a puffed-out chest. He closed on pale, complicated salarian armor with a filler chest piece and light ceremonial detailing that the salarians felt lacked cultural impact.
