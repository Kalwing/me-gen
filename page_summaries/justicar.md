A justicar was an asari who forswore children, family, and all worldly possessions except weapons and armor, then swore herself to the Code of the Justicars and wandered asari space righting wrongs. Samara compared justicars to medieval knights-errant or Japanese samurai. Few existed, owing to high training mortality and the hardship of the life. The Justicar Code comprised more than five thousand sutras covering every situation a justicar might face, and every justicar was required to know it by heart. Under the Code there were no morally grey acts; every deed fell clearly into right or wrong, and lethal force was commonly prescribed for wrongdoers. Samara noted that curiosity was a liability, since a justicar bound to kill a murderer did not wish to learn anything that might redeem them.

Justicars swore the Oaths of Subsumation, pledging protection of the innocent, punishment of the guilty, and defense of common law and asari social norms; they would never overthrow a government. The Third Oath of Subsumation bound a justicar with total loyalty to another's whims, temporarily overriding even the Code, though upon release she could kill the one she had served if forced into dishonorable acts. Justicars were formidable warriors who honed asari biotics over decades to levels rivaling matriarchs; some single-handedly defeated bands of mercenaries and slavers, and at least one fought a Spectre to a draw. The Code permitted a justicar to submit to lawful detainment for a single day only. Justicars could leave the order, as Tristana and the Asari Duelist did. During the Reaper invasion some justicars returned to Thessia; their small numbers meant the order might not survive the war. Known justicars included Samara and Phora, who delivered Ardat-Yakshi to the monastery on Lesuss by terrifying them into coming.
