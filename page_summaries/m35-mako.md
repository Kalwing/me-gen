The M35 Mako Infantry Fighting Vehicle was designed for Systems Alliance frigates to provide the rapid deployment its predecessor, the M29 Grizzly, could not. Small enough to ride in a cargo bay and deploy on virtually any world despite a cramped interior, it mounted a turreted 155mm mass accelerator cannon and a coaxial machine gun. It was environmentally sealed, powered by a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell, and armored to survive high drops, extreme heat and cold, and rough terrain. Independently reversible wheels let it turn like a tank, and micro-thrusters plus a small element zero core allowed it to adjust mass for traction and be safely air-dropped without the usual 100m of open terrain, limiting a frigate's exposure to anti-aircraft fire. Laser detection arrays and ground-penetrating radar warned the crew of guided ordnance and mines. Support vehicles on the Mako chassis included a recon drone controller, a mobile air defense platform, and the M38 "Moby" military ambulance; shipboard Marines used only the armed base model. It carried eight: two in front and six in the hold.

In Mass Effect, the SSV Normandy carried a Mako that Shepard used to explore most landing sites. On Ilos, Shepard's squad drove it through the Conduit, crossing thousands of lightyears directly to the Citadel Presidium, where it smashed two geth troopers before overturning; badly damaged, it was abandoned. In Mass Effect: Paragon Lost, a merchant named Messner lent a used Mako to James Vega's Delta Squad and archaeologist Treeya Nuwani on Fehl Prime, with Mason and later Milque on its gun; the Collector attack rendered it irrelevant or destroyed. In Mass Effect 2 the Normandy's Mako was repaired or replaced; after the Collector Cruiser destroyed the ship, the Mako was found nearly intact in the ice of Alchera, and the UT-47 Kodiak shuttle became Shepard's primary transport aboard the Normandy SR-2. In Mass Effect 3, Makos formed the bulk of Hammer Team's mobile armor during the final battle for Earth, most destroyed trying to outrun Harbinger near the London Conduit. Named after the mako shark, the vehicle drew design cues from Russian BTR APCs, the Mowag Piranha, and the Big Trak toy. Cut content notes it was called the "rover" internally and spawned designs reused for the skycar, Grizzly, Tomkah, and M-44 Hammerhead.
