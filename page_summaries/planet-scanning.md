Planet Scanning was the resource-gathering system introduced in Mass Effect 2, replacing the Mako surface exploration of the first game. It became available whenever the Normandy SR-2 entered orbit of any planet not tied to a main-story mission. The player overlaid the planet with a grid, ran a slow mineral scanner, watched a data column for spikes indicating deposits, and fired probes to add minerals to the ship's stores. Four resources could be gathered: palladium, platinum, iridium, and the rare element zero (eezo). These resources funded research projects that upgraded weapons, equipment, and the Normandy itself. Each planet had an abundance rating (from poor to rich) that decreased as minerals were harvested; a planet's surface never regenerated, so a fully scanned world could be abandoned permanently.

Scanning also revealed anomalies. On entering orbit EDI would announce a detected anomaly, and a white line on the scanner pointed toward it; probing the small white blip unlocked an N7 assignment or loyalty mission, awarding experience, credits, and sometimes further assignments. For planets with radio transmissions, the muffled speech grew clearer as the scanner neared the blip.

Two upgrades directly affected scanning: the Advanced Mineral Scanner (from Miranda, after recruiting Mordin to open the tech lab) sped up scanner movement, and the Modular Probe Bay (from Thane) doubled probe capacity from 30 to 60. Samara's Extended Fuel Cells added 50% fuel capacity. Probes were sold in sets of five for 100 credits. Guides advised scanning only rich rocky worlds, aiming for large spikes (logarithmic yields, up to about 2500 units per probe), pulsing the scan trigger for speed, and combining mining with assignment worlds. Completing a Mass Effect 2 playthrough reduced the resources required for full upgrades by 50000 each, effectively removing the need to mine eezo on later playthroughs. A patch in May 2010 enlarged the scanner and increased its speed.

As trivia, probing the resource-depleted planet Uranus produced unique EDI dialogue: "Really, Commander?" on the first probe and, unamused, "Probing Uranus..." on the second.
