Ravagers were synthetic-organic creatures the Reapers created from rachni through implantation and genetic modification, appearing in 2186 as mobile artillery for Reaper ground forces. Functioning as walking organic turrets, they could both absorb and inflict considerable damage. Alliance scientists theorized that the Reapers found rachni-derived units easiest to control because of the species' neurological predisposition toward hive-mind consensus. In early concept art a Ravager's cannon was formed from a visible humanoid husk fused to the rachni's back, which was removed as too disgusting.

Ravagers were armed with two powerful long-range cannons fired in three-round bursts after locking on with visible blue targeting beams, lethal at range but rarely used at close quarters, where the Ravager preferred to retreat. They bore orange egg sacs on their bodies that continuously spawned Swarmers, small fragile creatures that were volatile in groups, could hinder shield regeneration with damage over time, and charged and exploded on contact. Ravagers spawned Swarmers whether the sacs were intact or not; the sacs could be punctured by ordinary gunfire, often releasing Swarmers but making close approach safer. At melee range a Ravager would often spontaneously burst its sacs, releasing acid and Swarmers for potentially massive damage. It had no conventional melee attack and no headshot weak point; its high armor served as its health. A dead Ravager expelled caustic gas and left an acidic puddle that dealt environmental damage over several seconds, and its body remained a solid obstacle until its death animation completed.

The page's tactics section recommended Dark Channel, Combat Drones and Sentry Turrets (which Ravagers ignored and whose fire could block Ravager shots), Stasis Bubble, Dominate, area-effect grenades and power combos against Swarmers, the Vorcha's Flamer, and dodging the telegraphed targeting lasers by rolling or reaching cover. The Kishock Harpoon Gun could not burst the sacs, making it ineffective. In cutscenes during Attican Traverse: Krogan Team, Ravagers were shown climbing vertical walls in insect fashion, though they could not do so in actual combat.
