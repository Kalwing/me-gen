This page transcribes the introduction and first part of The Art of Mass Effect (2007), with commentary by Casey Hudson. The introduction describes BioWare's goal of building "a seemingly idyllic future - a bright universe, with the darkest of secrets," using elegant geometry of compass arcs and straight lines, weathered surfaces, freely explored alien anatomy that could still express human emotion, and a soft, film-like rendering style with depth of field, bloom, vignetting and film grain.

The Heroes and Villains section covers character design. Real-life models were scanned for human faces while aliens were hand-sculpted. It discusses early sketches of Ashley, Kaidan, Anderson and Joker (Joker was originally meant to look sickly, then given a more muscular build); Commander Shepard's armor, which moved from white to the darker, weathered N7 suit with a scarlet stripe symbolizing human blood; Matriarch Benezia, designed for asari beauty and dark mystery to tie her to Saren; Tali and Liara; Garrus's facial-pattern iterations; Wrex's fiery red crest and facial scar; and Saren, whose look shifted from robes and cloaks (sometimes with a cane or sword) to elite turian armor, ending in a monstrous form with a grafted geth arm and exposed implants.
