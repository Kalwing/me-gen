The twelfth and final chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary is set on February 13, the day after Mass Effect 3 was finished, with only downloadable content left. Casey Hudson, whose Outlook calendar is suddenly bare and who half-wishes he were still working on the game, drives Keighley to a hangar near Edmonton to see the airplane he bought and learned to fly again during the trilogy's development. Hudson talks about buying a quarter section of empty land, 160 acres, outside Edmonton so his kids can have the sense of open space he had at his wife's grandparents' farm.

The chapter's title refers to a fan letter Hudson taped to his office door, written by seventh-grader Zachary Sentz of East Berlin, Pennsylvania, asking BioWare to "make the trilogy a little longer" and proposing that Shepard and a love interest have a child whom the player would then control, discovering a cave with a prophecy on the wall. Keighley connects it to the game's post-credits scene in which a boy asks a man, voiced by astronaut Buzz Aldrin, whether there are more tales of "The Shepard." Hudson explains that because every player's ending differs based on choices and galactic readiness, he wanted one final scene where everyone has the exact same experience.

Hudson states that while there will be more Mass Effect games, Commander Shepard's story has concluded, and that he never wants to tell stories set after Mass Effect 3: "Whatever we do would likely happen before or during the events of Mass Effect 3, not after." Asked whether he would fly to space on Virgin Galactic, he says he would rather pilot something than be a passenger, wanting control over where he goes and the choices he makes, which Keighley presents as the reason players connect with Mass Effect. An accompanying video has Hudson discussing how games can carry a message by letting players explore an issue, such as organic versus synthetic life, without the game making a statement.
