Din Korlack was the volus ambassador to the Citadel, sharing an embassy office with Calyn, the elcor ambassador. His name was frequently misspelled "Din Korlak" in subtitles and in the novel Mass Effect: Retribution.

In the first Mass Effect, Korlack was markedly mistrustful of humans and openly rude to Commander Shepard, to the point of being repeatedly chastised by Calyn. He believed the Council favored humanity at the expense of the volus, and resented that the volus held no Council seat despite their long history with the Citadel. When Shepard became a Spectre, he sarcastically remarked on how honored he was that the Commander would deign to speak with him.

In 2186, during the events of Mass Effect: Retribution, Korlack took part in high-level trade negotiations involving the Systems Alliance, the Turian Hierarchy, the Vol Protectorate, and the Courts of Dekuuna, where participants including Calyn and David Anderson found his behavior rude and irritating. During one break, Korlack and Ambassador Orinia sought out Anderson at the human embassy to file a formal complaint about a remark by one of his aides, but the turian ambassador talked Korlack down.

By Mass Effect 3, Korlack was still the volus ambassador during the Reaper invasion. He was revealed to have had Cerberus connections during the group's war with the Collectors, but grew uncertain once Cerberus openly opposed the Alliance, and cut ties after their failed coup on the Citadel. The turians, unaware of his change of heart, placed a bounty on him after Cerberus attacked a turian cruiser carrying senior Hierarchy officials. Korlack was captured by bounty hunters, among them Zaeed Massani if he had survived the Suicide Mission, but not before leaving a recording directing anyone who found it to his listening device. Investigating him at the prompting of Adrien Victus, Shepard and Zaeed rescued Korlack. He admitted Cerberus planned to attack a turian colony but refused to say which, fearing exposure as a traitor, and instead offered the volus bombing fleet. Shepard could take the fleet or his intel, or, if Zaeed's loyalty had been earned, convince Korlack to provide both.
