This hand-corrected transcript was the first part of a five-part character analysis of Tali'Zorah across the original trilogy, with parts 2 to 5 covering Mass Effect 2, the treason trial, Mass Effect 3 and Rannoch, and the romance and conclusion. Much of it is the essayist's interpretation rather than hard canon.

Tali was introduced when Shepard learned she held information incriminating Saren for the attack on Eden Prime. Her willingness to risk her life in a face-to-face deal with the Shadow Broker to get that evidence to the right people was presented as evidence of her ingrained independence, which the essay attributed to quarian society pushing its young toward self-reliance on the Pilgrimage, and of her selfless nature. Unlike Liara, Tali joined Shepard with little personal baggage, portrayed as a capable, decent person who could handle herself in a fight, briefly in over her head but keeping "a hand on her holstered six-shooter in case things go bad."

The essay noted an oddity in Mass Effect 1's hidden squadmate morality system, in which bringing party members from opposite ends of the Paragon/Renegade spectrum surfaced contrasting opinions on major decisions. Kaidan and Liara sat at the Paragon extreme and Ashley and Wrex at the Renegade extreme; on the Noveria rachni queen, Wrex always argued for genocide and Kaidan against, and swapping Kaidan for Ashley left Wrex arguing genocide while Ashley grew uncomfortable with it. Tali broke this pattern as a kind of "true neutral," arguing the Paragon option when Ashley was present and the Renegade option when Liara and Kaidan were present, taking a neutral position in every conflict without character development of her own. Because of this, the essayist declined to use her Mass Effect 1 dialogue on the rachni queen or the Council to define her character. Tali was also called the most overpowered Mass Effect 1 squadmate due to her tech attacks, so she tended to be in the party regardless.

The essayist rationalised her Mass Effect 1 characterisation by noting that at 22 she had the emotional intelligence to recognise the ramifications of Shepard's actions and defer to more experienced people, and that, since the exiled quarians remained under military jurisdiction, she was effectively in the quarian military and it made sense for her to defer to her commander. The piece also used quarian culture as background: constant danger of sickness, the need to balance population against food and ship capacity, 300 years of war with the geth, universal expectation to support the Fleet, and the Pilgrimage as the rite of passage into adulthood, requiring a young quarian to bring back something of value the Fleet could not produce itself, such as raw materials for hull patches. Personal beats in Mass Effect 1 were minimal: Shepard could help her finish her Pilgrimage and she had a brief bout of homesickness, but her character was built mainly in the sequels.
