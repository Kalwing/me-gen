Sabotage was a tech power in Mass Effect 3's single-player and multiplayer modes, distinct from the earlier Decryption-derived ability of the same name. It hacked synthetics so that compromised units fought on the caster's side, and applied a delayed "backfire" electrical discharge to organic targets. Despite its name and description, Mass Effect 3's Sabotage did not disable enemy weapons or attacks; the word "overheat" referred to the backfire effect, which dealt damage to organics roughly 1.5 seconds after the hit and could stun or electrocute unshielded infantry. Synthetics were hacked instead of taking backfire; both effects worked through active shields and barriers.

The power was a short-range hitscan ability. It could simultaneously hack up to two synthetics (one in multiplayer) and apply backfire to up to two enemies. Hacked enemies gained increasing resistance and became immune after three uses. It could hack Cerberus turrets, geth turrets and drones, and Atlases (despite organic pilots), reverse Cerberus shield generators to drain shields, and instantly destroy Reaper Barrier Engines. Ranks improved recharge speed, radius, duration, and backfire damage; rank 5 offered an explosive hack; rank 6 offered Berserk (buffed hacked allies) or Tech Vulnerability (increasing all tech power damage to the target by 100%/50% for 10 seconds). Tech Vulnerability boosted Power Combo damage, some heavy melee attacks, and drone damage, and did not stack between players.

Sabotage could prime Tech Bursts on organics but not synthetics, and could not detonate combos itself. It paired well with Overload follow-ups, sniper rifles, and Tactical Cloak. It was available in single-player to the Engineer, Infiltrator, and Tali'Zorah vas Normandy, and in multiplayer to the Turian Saboteur Engineer, Quarian Infiltrator, and Quarian Marksman Soldier.
