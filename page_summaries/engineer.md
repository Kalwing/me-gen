The Engineer was a tech-specialist class in Mass Effect, Mass Effect 2, and Mass Effect 3, and a Profile in Mass Effect: Andromeda. Engineers focused on shaping the battlefield, healing allies, and debuffing enemies by disabling weapons and lowering shields.

In the first Mass Effect, Engineers possessed all tech abilities but could equip only light armor and had no biotic skills. They began with pistol training and an omni-tool and could unlock the ability to hack enemy technology. They also had the First Aid and Medicine talents, making their healing very effective. The Engineer class talent reduced tech power recharge time and increased tech protection, and Engineers could specialize as Medic or Operative.

In Mass Effect 2, Engineers were the only class able to deploy combat drones, and were effective at blasting through enemy defenses and disabling opponents. Weapons training covered heavy pistols, submachine guns, and heavy weapons, with powers including Overload, Incinerate, Combat Drone, Cryo Blast, AI Hacking, and Tech Mastery.

In Mass Effect 3, Engineers retained combat drones alongside Incinerate, Overload, Cryo Blast, Sabotage, Sentry Turret, Tech Mastery, and Fitness. They had a low weight capacity, comparable to the Adept. A special Engineer-only Paragon Interrupt during Aria T'Loak's retaking of Omega let Shepard reroute reactor power away from a force field, satisfying both Aria and Nyreen.

In Mass Effect: Andromeda, the Engineer profile reconfigured Ryder's implant to maintain constant control over a small combat drone, granting a Combat Drone bonus power that improved tech recharge and self-destructed with an EMP pulse. According to published statistics, the Engineer was the least-picked Shepard class, used by only 3% of players in 2012 and 5% of Legendary Edition players by 2021.
