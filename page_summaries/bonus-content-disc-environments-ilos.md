The page transcribed art director Derek Watts' gallery commentary for "The Environments of Mass Effect: Ilos," included on the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. Watts explained that Ilos was concepted as the Prothean homeworld, a city largely consumed by jungle and lost for thousands of years, where the player would reach the archives and learn what happened to the Protheans. Visual references included rock canyons in Hunan Province, China (reshaped to resemble skyscrapers), the temple of Angkor Wat in Cambodia, and the deep Amazon for a humid, misty jungle feel. The team produced 2D maps with numbered encounter points for level artists, block levels, and SketchUp and 3ds Max prototypes; a lush green version was shown at E3 and believed to be final.

Watts described the elevator descent to the Prothean hologram, the long road section deliberately kept free of combat so the emotional conversation with the Prothean would not be interrupted, and the storage pods holding millions of Protheans maintained by a few caretakers, some of whom survived while others died. The final trench run leading to the mass relay that fires the player to the Citadel was built oversized and later shortened.

Dense vegetation caused performance problems and intersected with characters, obscuring enemies, so the level was redesigned: trees were removed and replaced with sparse branches, and the palette shifted to deep orange with coral-like rock growing over Prothean structures, influenced by Polish painter Beksiński. Turquoise "geth lights" were added as navigation focal points for players who became lost. The archives' lighting changed from deep blue to orange, with ceiling openings added to cast light through the roots. The opening area, where the rover drops in and Sovereign and Saren pass through the gate, kept its layout but was retextured.
