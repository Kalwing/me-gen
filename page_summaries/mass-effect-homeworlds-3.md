Mass Effect: Homeworlds 3, tagged "A Bullet for Your Sins," was published July 25, 2012, written by Mac Walters, John Dombrow, and Jeremy Barlow with art by Garry Brown. It follows Garrus Vakarian, framed by his childhood on Palaven learning to shoot a sniper rifle under his demanding father, who insisted that not giving up when things got hard was part of teaching him to be an adult.

In 2185 on Omega, mercenaries were being massacred by the vigilante Archangel. Overlooking his base, Garrus recorded what he believed might be his final words so Omega would know the truth about him. He recounted the path that brought him there. Years after the target practice, Garrus skipped a study-abroad scholarship trip to care for his injured mother after a hit-and-run, losing the opportunity permanently and concluding he would only ever be "an officer's son." Later, at C-Sec Academy, he clashed with his father, who had cut loose a suspect, Kishpaugh, over a weak case; his father told him to do things right or not at all.

After the Battle of the Citadel, frustrated that the public dismissed the threat of Sovereign and the geth and that C-Sec red tape blocked him, Garrus quit and pursued Kishpaugh, learning the drug source was Omega. There he stopped a vorcha mugging an elderly human couple, who called him a "real-life angel." At Afterlife he met Sidonis, and the two formed a squad against Omega's criminals; Garrus took the name "Archangel." The squad grew to twelve, including a salarian explosives expert and a batarian tech expert, declared war on the gangs while avoiding civilian casualties, and could not be stopped even when the gangs united. Garrus pushed the team past its limits rather than let members settle down. Sidonis, captured by the Blue Suns, was forced to lure Garrus away to the Kenzo District; Garrus returned to find the hideout bombed and his squad gunned down. Recognizing his father's lessons had been right, he contacted him to apologize; spotting a familiar emblem on an enemy's armor through his scope, he said he would come home when he could because "the odds just got a lot better."
