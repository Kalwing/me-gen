# Everyday

- Advanced medicine as luxury commerce: a cloned spare heart auctioned for charity by celebrity Aish Ashland sold for 115.6 million credits, bought by a daughter to save her ailing father. (source: Cerberus Daily News - December 2010)
- Advertising and consumer branding permeated daily Citadel life: Tupari sports drink ran an aggressive, exaggerated ad campaign (claiming 12 trillion bottles sold a day and the ability to "bring ancestors back from the grave"), competing against the rival brand Paragade; a Zakera Ward holo-projector ad for Serrice Council's "Sophist" biotic amps would name-drop whichever Normandy crew member's love interest happened to be standing nearby. (source: Drinks)
- After Shepard defeated Cerberus general Oleg Petrovsky during the Omega uprising, Aria T'Loak gave the Commander Petrovsky's personal chessboard as a keepsake, which was then displayed on the desk in the Normandy SR-2's captain's cabin. (source: Normandy SR-2)
- After the geth attack on the Citadel in 2183, trust in AI research decreased significantly, damaging Synthetic Insights' reputation to the point that the corporation barely hired anyone by 2185 to avoid Council disapproval. (source: Synthetic Insights, Ltd.)
- After the heavy security-personnel losses in the Battle of the Citadel, short-handed security companies filled their ranks with unmanned "mechs" in light (dexterous, versatile) and heavy (weapons-platform) varieties, plus quadrupedal "dog mechs" whose "faces" were contraband-detection sensor arrays; mechs ran narrow friend-or-foe VI routines and were considered unfit for real military duty since they couldn't improvise tactics, treat wounds, or plan an ambush. (source: Codex/Technology)
- A gastrointestinal immunosuppressant called "Reversal" let dextro and levo species safely sample each other's cuisine for under 24 hours with no real nutritional value, spawning a fad for dextro/levo food parties despite doctors' warnings to purge before the treatment wore off. (source: Cerberus Daily News - October 2010)
- Alliance marine Nirali Bhatia had joined the military under the "Deferred Education Plan" while dreaming of opening a restaurant once her service ended; after Commander Shepard returned her body to her husband Samesh for burial on Earth, he fulfilled her dream by opening that restaurant in her memory. (source: Nirali Bhatia)
- Alliance military vessel colors changed from red and white detailing to blue and white to reflect updated Alliance organizational colors. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 3)
- All Systems Alliance soldiers received gene therapy for improved strength and stamina, and recruited biotics were trained using techniques developed at BAaT with the newest L3 biotic implants considered a significant advancement. (source: Systems Alliance)
- Ambient Citadel chatter captured mundane bureaucratic and civic texture: an overworked volus diplomat on the Presidium groused about days of paperwork and joked about quitting to "work the mines," while a salarian and a volus in Chora's Den debated Council competence, traded suspicions about Traverse shipping losses, and agreed C-Sec's entry exams were "brutal" but kept high standards. (source: Mass Effect Cut Content/Citadel Voicelines)
- Ancient quarians were capable of consuming ryncol without supplementation before their immune systems weakened during Migrant Fleet exile. (source: Ryncol)
- Apollo's Cafe operated as a food and beverage establishment in the Presidium Commons. (source: Presidium Commons)
- Arcturus Station served as the massive and well-defended space station capital of the Systems Alliance, located in the strategically valuable Arcturus system containing several primary mass relays linking to major human colonies and the secondary relay to Sol and Earth. (source: Systems Alliance)
- Arms dealer Donovan Hock kept an eccentric black-market art vault that included Michelangelo's David (missing a leg and other pieces), a scale model of the sunken passenger liner the Kara (dubbed "the turian Titanic"), ancient quarian stone tablets, and the severed head of the Statue of Liberty; his vault's voice-password, "Peruggia," was the surname of the man who stole the Mona Lisa -- a painting Kasumi Goto herself was known to have later acquired. (source: Kasumi: Stealing Memory)
- A salarian businessman named Rulamin, overheard on a comm call in the Port Hanshan lounge, opened every conversation with "Don't hang up," which he explained was ancient salarian comm etiquette meant to signal a call's importance by disturbing the recipient's silence. (source: Mass Effect Cut Content/Noveria - Port Hanshan and Aleutsk Valley Voicelines)
- A sexually transmitted disease called "scale-itch" is carried exclusively by varren; it was detected aboard the Normandy by Mordin Solus. (source: Varren)
- At the Citadel nightclub Flux, ambient gossip among patrons ranged across topics of the day -- the goings-on at rival club Chora's Den, the attack on Eden Prime, and humanity's growing influence in galactic politics -- while the venue's mezzanine casino offered a card game called quasar alongside its ground-floor bar and dance floor. (source: Flux)
- A vast shroud positioned at Tuchanka's L1 Lagrange point maintains the planet's livable temperatures and is maintained by CDEM (Council Demilitarization Enforcement Mission) battlestations in orbit. (source: Tuchanka)
- Because there was no cure for the genophage, a persistent black market trade in krogan testicles existed among some krogan who believed (without evidence) that testicle transplants could counteract its effects. (source: Genophage)
- Candy assortment available at Sirta Supplies includes chocolates, hard candies, and caramels suitable only for levo-amino acid-based life forms. (source: Sirta Supplies)
- CCCP Luna 23, a real Soviet unmanned lunar lander from 1974, sits on Luna west of the Mass Effect 1 drop zone with its sample apparatus permanently disabled by a hard landing; it can be mined for resources. (source: Timeline)
- Cerberus trooper armor features distinctive rectangular eye slits and circular symbols over shoulders and chest as faction identification markers. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 3)
- Citadel Council space ran on a standardized Galactic Standard calendar: a 20-hour day of 100-minute hours and 100-second minutes (each second half a Terran second), making a standard day 15.7% longer than Earth's, and a standard year (averaging asari, salarian, and turian years) equal to 398.114 Earth days. (source: Citadel Council)
- Citadel customs and immigration confiscated biotic amps and weapons at ward checkpoints, and could flag travelers as security risks on subjective grounds, as when a Zakera Ward customs officer barred two asari from boarding a shuttle to Thessia over fears of geth infiltration until overruled by Spectre authority. (source: C-Sec Customs Officer)
- Citadel duct-rat children in Zakera Ward, marked by broken teeth, scabby knees and bare feet, scraped by as informants and errand-runners for Citadel operators like the assassin Thane Krios, paid in small treats such as real chocolate. (source: Mouse)
- Citadel exterior can fold into a defensive shell, with joints that move when the station transforms for protection. (source: The Art of Mass Effect/Alien Worlds)
- Citadel nightlife entertainment circa 2186 included the Silver Coast Casino's Quasar gambling machines and the Armax Arsenal Arena combat simulator, both visited by an off-duty N7 squad in "Profiles in Courage," alongside a celebrated pet varren, "Prince Grimrender of Gembat," profiled by Citadel Weekly. (source: Alliance News Network/Profiles in Courage)
- Citadel Presidium docks originally reserved for Alliance embassy deliveries were repurposed by Council motion into emergency civilian housing during the Reaper invasion, with adjoining cargo bays converted into a growing refugee camp featuring a poker game, a black-market batarian preacher rallying morale, a tattoo artist, and a trade negotiation for supplies led by a refugee leader named Tactus. (source: Docks: Holding Area)
- Citadel street food and snack culture included a Zakera Ward ramen stand, a Noodle House whose noodles Grunt found "a little spicy," Fishdog Food Shack's varren skewers and steaks (also sold on Illium), and McSorley's Cloaca Margarine, advertised as made from "free-range organic varren." (source: Foods)
- Citadel youth extreme sport "curve gliding" - jumping from height in a pressurized glider suit to hug the curve of a space station ward - originated as a joke on the drama "The Lower Wards" before real enthusiasts made it dangerous enough to draw a C-Sec crackdown. (source: Cerberus Daily News - September 2010)
- Clan food stores at Urdnot Camp are frequently raided by invasive pyjaks. (source: Urdnot Camp)
- Coffee culture crossed species lines as a social ritual, with an invitation to "get coffee" sometimes doubling as a euphemism for sex; Apollo's Cafe on the Presidium was a well-known Citadel coffee venue, and the Andromeda AI SAM credited its own existence to Alec Ryder's coffee habit. (source: Drinks)
- Comfort food carried emotional weight: Jack said chocolate chip cookies were the first thing she ate after escaping the Teltin facility on Pragia, and Dr. Chloe Michel bought turian chocolates for Garrus Vakarian on the Citadel after the Collector mission. (source: Foods)
- Cortez had a knack for procurements that made him valuable to command a flight deck, and he later became responsible for maintaining the Normandy's armory alongside Lt. James Vega. (source: Steve Cortez)
- C-Sec Commander Armando-Owen Bailey, homesick for Earth during the Reaper invasion, particularly missed sockeye salmon. (source: Armando-Owen Bailey)
- C-Sec drilled new Citadel officers on the practicalities of policing 9 million tourists a week: a mandatory twenty hours of foot patrol to build street contacts, and reading translated body language and slang closely enough to tell a drunk tourist from someone signaling an ambush. (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Captain Bailey)
- C-Sec maintained a security outpost in the Presidium Commons. (source: Presidium Commons)
- Cut dialogue between two Citadel dockworkers, Lors and an unnamed friend, sketched working-class station life: Lors regretted leaving Omega for "long boring days moving boxes," was warned that Aria T'Loak "would skin me alive" if he returned, had spent a night in C-Sec custody after an undercover officer caught him trying to trade unlicensed goods, and argued with his friend over risking savings on a rigged-sounding investment tip. (source: Mass Effect 2 Cut Content/Omega and Citadel Voicelines)
- Dextro-amino acid rations recovered from the agribusinesses of Triginta Petra before the Reaper invasion were preferred by turian troops, who favor a meat-heavy diet, and could restore morale to demoralized military units. (source: War Assets/Turian)
- Drinking was routine social currency aboard the Normandy and beyond: Dr. Chakwas favored the expensive, thousand-credit Serrice Ice Brandy; Kenneth Donnelly grumbled about the Illusive Man's preference for American bourbon over Islay scotch; and Samantha Traynor, a former college bartender, could name the exact ingredients of custom party cocktails (Full Biotic Kick, Quad Kicker, Tasty Tankard) on request. (source: Drinks)
- Early, later-cut concept material for Eden Prime depicted its farms using mobile "aerocultures," fields of crops moved by cargo train, and raising a local herd species called "hexa-cows"; in the released game, Eden Prime's native lace-in-lilac plant became one of the seeds Milky Way colonists carried 2.5 million light-years to try to grow in Andromeda. (source: Eden Prime)
- Earth's Halloween and the Hindu festival of Diwali both persisted as galactic-era human holidays: Halloween retained trick-or-treating and topical costumes (2010's top sellers included "Zombie Huerta" and "Geth Hunter"), while Diwali's traditional oil "diya" lamps were replaced with LED displays aboard starships where open flames were a hazard. (source: Cerberus Daily News - October/November 2010)
- Eclipse mercenaries on Illium dosed the volus Niftu Cal with biotic-enhancing drugs (likely Minagen X3 and/or red sand), turning the normally weak volus into a self-declared "biotic god" who boasted of immortality before Eclipse captain Wasea casually blasted him aside with her own biotics. (source: Niftu Cal)
- Eden Prime colony was originally conceived as blue-skied agricultural territory with rolling green hills before Reaper arrival scorched and battle-damaged the landscape. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Eden Prime was an agrarian world that produced millions of tons of food for sale to less arable planets and less fertile worlds, serving as a critical supply line during the Reaper War. (source: War Assets/Alliance)
- Elcor are capable of consuming ryncol. (source: Ryncol)
- Eldfell-Ashland Energy, formed from a 2137 merger of Ashland Energy's helium-3 extraction business with Eldfell Construction, built a "cradle-to-grave corporate experience" for its colonist-employees through subsidiaries like Second Star Living (colonist recruitment) and Excelsior (job training), supplying water, power, food and shipping in-house so that up to 90% of services stayed within the company, while its core revenue remained mining base elements and trading eezo futures. (source: Mass Effect 2 Cut Content/Miscellaneous Texts)
- Exiled quarian Golo'Mekk survived on Omega by striking a long-term supply deal with an elcor shopkeeper in the Carrd district for purified turian cuisine suited to quarian dietary needs. (source: Golo)
- Extranet bandwidth on the comm-buoy network was rationed by priority tier: the Citadel Council and Spectres had absolute priority, individual governments and militaries came next, then media conglomerates and corporations that paid for faster access, with ordinary citizens' free, unlimited extranet use placed last in the queue; wartime disruption could leave civilian communications lagging for hours or days. (source: Codex/Technology)
- Fa'yin is an edible nut native to Rannoch that existed on the world since the earliest proto-quarian period. (source: Rannoch)
- Fehl Prime, a human colony in the Terminus Systems, was one of the Systems Alliance's top pharmaceutical producers, its arid colony grounds set against native flora that included massive, bio-luminescent fungus-like growths; the planet also held an intact, functioning Prothean interstellar communication device roughly 70,000 years old that as of 2185 sat unstudied. (source: Fehl Prime)
- Flowers sold at Sirta Supplies are grown in Citadel hydroponic gardens. (source: Sirta Supplies)
- Fort Charles Upham was an Alliance base on Saturn's moon Titan where Alliance soldiers underwent Hostile Environment Assault Training. (source: Systems Alliance)
- Freighter ships throughout the galaxy employ simple spine-based designs holding together reusable cargo containers, prioritizing economy over aesthetics or performance. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Gagarin Station, also known as "Jump Zero," was a remote research facility on the outskirts of the Sol system operated by the Systems Alliance. (source: Systems Alliance)
- Geth ships are designed with insect-like appearance featuring "feelers" on their undersides mimicking organic insects. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Geth were designed with lithe, sinewy appearances featuring rubbery tubing resembling veins and muscle striations. (source: The Art of Mass Effect/Life-Forms)
- Haptic holographic interfaces replaced physical keyboards for most computer use, read via a microframe chip and motion accelerometers in a user's glove; committed users sometimes underwent cybernetic surgery to have the accelerometers implanted directly in their fingertips, a look nicknamed "going bareskin" that spared them the hassle of putting on gloves or wiping off the "clammy-hand smell." (source: Codex/Technology)
- Huerta Memorial Hospital was located on the Citadel and provided emergency medical care. (source: Priority: The Citadel I)
- Human civilian clothing designs in the 23rd century include specialized outfits for miners, doctors, and bar patrons reflecting occupational roles. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Human genetic engineering became commonplace by the 22nd century, letting the wealthy custom-build fetuses for strength, intelligence and appearance; the 2161 Sudham-Wolcott Genetic Heritage Act restricted the field into three legal tiers — free screening/corrective therapy for genetic disease, legal cosmetic/physical "enhancement" (adding wholly new traits, like tails, remained illegal), and tightly regulated "engineering" of designed life for terraforming or medicine, with creation of sapient life outlawed by both the Alliance and the Council. (source: Codex/Humanity and the Systems Alliance)
- Human parenting debates over robotic/VI babysitting mirrored 21st-century "screen time" anxieties: over half of urban households used synthetic childcare weekly by 2185, with pediatricians warning it substituted for real parental bonding even as some working parents said it was their only option. (source: Cerberus Daily News - November 2010)
- Humans can survive drinking ryncol, though a Dark Star Lounge bartender joked that consumers would "set off radiological alarms" afterward. (source: Ryncol)
- Human ships incorporate highly organic shapes designed to visually "swim through space" rather than appear mechanical. (source: The Art of Mass Effect/Transport)
- Ilos archive housed the preserved bodies of thousands of Protheans maintained in suspended animation after the species' extinction. (source: The Art of Mass Effect/Alien Worlds)
- In 2186, self-styled "conspiracy hunter" Bernard Plim of the Conspiracy Accountability League hacked the Alliance News Network's comm channel to accuse Admiral Hackett and Task Force Aurora of covering up Reaper secrets, soliciting reader donations, publishing a book ("What the Drell Don't Know: And the Hanar Won't Say"), and boasting of past scoops on Patavig volus-mafia funding and illegal biological-weapons purchases from Dekuuna; C-Sec eventually raided his hideout and jailed him overnight with five drunken elcor. (source: Mass Effect 3: Leviathan/Conspiracy Accountability League)
- In the Port Hanshan lounge on Noveria, two human corporate recruits awaiting job interviews with Elanus Risk Control and Binary Helix grumbled about the port's oppressive security and eerie silence -- "no one can talk about what they did at work... not without getting sued" -- over a meal of synthetic lobster with an off texture, while flirting about meeting for coffee if they ended up working together on Peak 15. (source: Mass Effect Cut Content/Noveria - Port Hanshan and Aleutsk Valley Voicelines)
- In-utero gene therapy was an available but controversial Citadel medical option: pregnant Rebekah Petrovsky, widowed when her husband Jacob died of a rare heart condition, weighed her brother-in-law Michael's push for the therapy against extranet reports calling it dangerous and less-effective alternative treatments; the couple kept arguing over their son Jake's genetic modifications for years afterward. (source: Citadel: Family Matter)
- James Vega ordered pizza delivery to Shepard's Citadel apartment from a volus delivery driver ("Double Pepperoni"), with the crew good-naturedly noting someone still had to pay the bill. (source: Citadel: Identity Theft II)
- Jon Grissom Academy, located in orbit over Elysium, served as an educational facility for gifted children. (source: Systems Alliance)
- Kahje's ambient ecology included the lerian, a bioluminescent sea fern with small pods native to the ocean covering 90% of the planet's surface, known as the Encompassing. (source: Kahje)
- Kanala Exports and Elkoss Combine Arsenal Supplies maintained retail shops in Presidium Commons. (source: Presidium Commons)
- Ke'sed are blind newborn offspring of qorach animals native to Rannoch. (source: Rannoch)
- Krogan catacombs beneath Tuchanka contained ancient artifacts and artistic depictions of thresher maws. (source: Priority: Tuchanka)
- Krogan on Tuchanka rode kakliosaurs, an extinct species revived via cloning from amber-preserved DNA at Sur'Kesh facilities, into battle against Reaper forces, tolerating the beasts marking their territory as part of using them as war mounts. (source: Citadel: Kakliosaur Fossil)
- Krud, a krogan pit boss, oversees the Quasar booths at Silver Coast Casino. (source: Silver Coast Casino)
- Lemek worms native to Rannoch are planarian analogues, small and slender with metallic rose-colored scales, capable of regenerating as new lemeks when cut into pieces. (source: Rannoch)
- Level 26 of Zakera Ward's Mid-Ward building housed Saronis Applications, a Shipping Warehouse, the Sirta Foundation, and the Stand establishment. (source: Zakera Ward)
- Level 27 of Zakera Ward's Mid-Ward building contained the C-Sec Office, Citadel Souvenirs shop, the Zakera Cafe, and a rapid transit hub. (source: Zakera Ward)
- Level 28 of Zakera Ward's Mid-Ward building housed the Dark Star Lounge, a Game Shop, the Palaven establishment, Rodam Expeditions, and rapid transit facilities. (source: Zakera Ward)
- Levo/dextro food chirality shaped everyday commerce and mishaps: batarian burgat ("the Other Blue Meat") was unsuitable for turians and quarians and could cause allergic reactions or parasites in non-batarians, with its distributor disclaiming liability; a Zakera Cafe customer asking for both amino-sinister and amino-dextrous spices made the shopkeeper do a double take. (source: Foods)
- Liara T'Soni displayed a painting of the ruined Prothean world Ilos in her apartment on Illium, treating the lost, second-hand-known world (likened by the Codex to Troy) as a piece of personal decor. (source: Ilos)
- Mars archive base features low architecture designed to withstand harsh Martian sandstorms and weather extremes on the red planet. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 3)
- Mass relays throughout the galaxy represent ancient alien technology left behind by an extinct civilization and serve as the primary infrastructure enabling faster-than-light travel. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Matriarch Dilinaga's writings are housed at Thessia Guildhall, the center for asari learning and research, before later being made available to the public. (source: UNC: Asari Writings)
- McSorley's Cloaca Margarine is advertised on the Citadel as being made from free-range "organic" varren. (source: Varren)
- Mercenaries operating throughout the galaxy often euphemistically branded themselves "private security organisations" while functioning as hired muscle, protection, or assassins for criminal organizations; drawn from every species but most commonly turian, human, batarian or krogan, they typically fielded superior equipment sourced from many different manufacturers rather than the single-supplier standardization preferred by formal militaries. (source: Mercenaries)
- Meridian Place Market served as a central commercial hub in Presidium Commons with multiple armed merchants. (source: Presidium Commons)
- Migrant Fleet liveships feature spherical designs specifically engineered to maximize crop yield for food production aboard nomadic vessels. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 2)
- Mordin Solus wears a metal collar designed to break up his silhouette, though its purpose is never explored in-game dialogue. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 2)
- Motion dampeners aboard the Shadow Broker base absorb storm vibrations and noise from Hagalaz's constant atmospheric disturbances. (source: Shadow Broker Base)
- Nearly one hundred corporations—human and alien—operate tele-operated mining, refining, and manufacturing facilities on Tyr; combined with Terra Nova's platinum lodes, these operations supply approximately 4% of the galactic market in hydrogen fuel cells. (source: Tyr)
- Newly self-educating about human and krogan culture, Grunt bought from an "Ultra Black Ops Mega Catalog" (engine oil, Fishdog Food Factory "Tastee Bites," Batax's Hot Fish Spice) and consumer media (a "Garr the Krogan Battlemaster" action figure, the schlock series When Fauna Attacks!), and worked through an omni-tool reading list of Hemingway audiobooks, finishing For Whom the Bell Tolls and The Old Man and the Sea. (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Grunt)
- Normandy cargo bay lighting employs pools of saturated color to reinforce the claustrophobic feeling of being inside a starship's belly. (source: The Art of Mass Effect/Transport)
- Noveria obtained plentiful deuterium for its fusion plants but imported all of its helium-3 from out-system sources. (source: Svarog)
- Noveria research stations are connected by transparent tubes suspended within ice and rock, nicknamed "hamster tubes" by researchers. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Nutrient paste was a staple ready-food across the galaxy: the Citadel's Foundations recycling systems produced free, nutritious but bland artificial organic pastes that poorer residents dressed up with sauces and spices, while imported foodstuffs were a luxury of the wealthy; quarians relied on nutrient paste routinely because eating solid food inside an enviro-suit was impractical. (source: Foods)
- Off-duty home life for a Citadel-based officer included catalog shopping (Dionysus Imports), imported wine and vodka, and streaming documentary/news-style video titles, as shown by Captain Anderson's intercepted purchase log, which listed works like "Saren: A Hero Betrayed" and "Afraid of the Dark: Reapers, Collectors and other Myths." (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Captain David Anderson)
- Omega's architecture evolved through haphazard vertical expansion by competing factions, creating a jellyfish-like silhouette as processing facilities extended upward from the original asteroid (source: Omega)
- Omega's Gozu District, home to the Kokomo Plaza and the Omega Clinic, ran on a life-support system independent of the rest of the station, allowing Omega's ruler Aria T'Loak to quarantine the district and post guards at its entrances when a Collector-engineered plague broke out there in 2185. (source: Gozu District)
- Omega's market ran on monopolistic pressure: the quarian salvager Kenn, stranded on his Pilgrimage after being robbed, was forced by rival merchant Harrot to sell his goods at inflated prices to undercut his business, until Shepard intervened to free him from the arrangement, negotiate a discount, or simply buy him passage off the station outright. (source: Kenn)
- Omega was originally mined for element zero by the Protheans, then later by humans and other species; the asteroid was mined to near-complete hollowness before being extensively built over, with element zero as the station's primary export (source: Omega)
- Omni-blades combine omni-tool technology with an ultrafast fabricator to instantly manufacture disposable blades for melee combat. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 3)
- On Omega, the elcor merchant Harrot ran Harrot's Emporium, a salvage shop that dominated the marketplace by contractually forbidding the quarian Kenn's Salvage from pricing competitively, illustrating the station's cutthroat, unregulated merchant economy; Harrot once sold a defective YMIR Mech to a Taitus miner, who cursed him in his logs after the mech failed in the field. (source: Harrot)
- On Omega, the salarian information broker Ish operated near the Afterlife Club gathering intelligence for hire, quietly surveilling one of Aria T'Loak's own trusted bodyguards for unauthorized outside deals — a glimpse of Omega's freelance information-trading economy operating even under the nose of the station's ruler. (source: Ish)
- On the batarian-terraformed world Aratoht, short-term profits came mainly from mining an extremely metal-rich crust using labor performed largely by indentured servants or slaves, a fact the Hegemony's propaganda omitted while touting the terraforming's modest oxygen gains as a sign of eventual victory. (source: Aratoht)
- On the mining moon Caleston, Eldfell-Ashland Energy's element-zero extraction made it the largest source of starship drive-core material in the Attican Traverse, at the cost of native biodiversity the Citadel Council's Committee on Habitable Worlds rated "threatened"; sapient life could only survive in domed cities because of the moon's thin, volcanic, silicon-based ecology. (source: Caleston)
- Party and mess-hall cooking was a recurring bonding ritual: Mess Sergeant Rupert Gardner made calamari gumbo (an asari recipe) for the Normandy crew given high-grade provisions, Kenneth Donnelly complained Gardner couldn't make haggis properly, and James Vega cooked huevos rancheros of dubious egg origin in the SR-2's kitchen. (source: Foods)
- Peak 15's corporate security prioritized protecting trade secrets over employee safety: turrets in the main garage faced inward to defend against the station's own workers rather than external threats (source: Peak 15)
- Peak 15's hot labs were constructed into a stable glacier with heating systems designed to sink the facility deeper into ice upon containment breach, complemented by a neutron purge system that unleashed lethal radiation to sterilize the area (source: Peak 15)
- Professional varren racers eat a specialized diet based on their wild prey diet but excluding radioactive materials, and require regular grooming and care. (source: Silver Coast Casino: Infiltration)
- "Psyotics," a body-modification trend, and a black market in asari telomerase (harvested by a plastic surgeon dubbed "Vidmar the Vampire" who murdered asari for it) show cosmetic/anti-aging medicine as a dark undercurrent of 2185 galactic culture. (source: Cerberus Daily News - August 2010)
- Qorach animals native to Rannoch are carnivorous bighorn sheep analogues whose bellies are always covered in blue-green fern pollen and seed casings. (source: Rannoch)
- Quarians require weeks of immuno-supplements and anti-virals before attempting ryncol consumption; the experience feels like "sipping knives" during ingestion. (source: Ryncol)
- Quarian trading decks aboard Migrant Fleet vessels serve as spaces for commerce between the fleet and external traders. (source: Seeto'Hodda nar Idenna)
- Quasar is a card game played at Silver Coast Casino where players start with 1-5 points, hit for additional points, and aim for exactly 20 points without going over, with payouts ranging from 25% (score 15) to 200% (score 20) of the initial bet. (source: Silver Coast Casino)
- Rannoch ecosystem lacks insect life; pollinating flora evolved to depend on animals for reproduction, creating a symbiosis that weakened quarian immune systems during their Migrant Fleet exile. (source: Rannoch)
- Red sand, the galaxy's most common illegal narcotic, was first manufactured on Mars from refined element zero that leaked out of the planet's Prothean ruins into the hands of criminal triads, and may take its name from Mars' distinctive red, arid coloring. (source: Mars)
- Research into artificial intelligence was illegal under the Citadel Conventions—a ban that had existed for 300 years since the quarians created the geth, which eventually turned against them. (source: Storyline)
- Rolan Quarn had worked in varied occupations including stunt driver, sous-chef, martial arts instructor, ambassador, varren tamer, news anchor, and stand-up comedian. (source: Rolan Quarn)
- Roulette at Silver Coast Casino uses chips costing 500 credits each, with bets on single numbers returning the highest payouts and ranged bets returning the lowest. (source: Silver Coast Casino)
- Rupert Gardner specializes in calamari gumbo, a recipe based on asari cuisine. (source: Rupert Gardner)
- Ryncol is a krogan beverage potent enough to incapacitate drinkers, served at upscale establishments like Silver Coast Casino. (source: Silver Coast Casino: Infiltration)
- Ryncol is a krogan liquor of unknown composition described by Grunt as hitting "like ground glass." (source: Ryncol)
- Ryncol is highly acidic and capable of melting through machinery. (source: Ryncol)
- Ryncol is highly flammable and can be used as an improvised incendiary weapon. (source: Ryncol)
- Ryncol is sold in pure and cocktail variants, including bottles at 190 proof strength. (source: Ryncol)
- Ryncol is unsafe for volus consumption. (source: Ryncol)
- Salarian ships possess smooth, rounded appearance making them resemble ocean-going vessels as much as starships. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect)
- Salrian researchers developed medical technology to regulate water temperature in salrian bodies to assist with krogan egg-laying procedures. (source: Presidium Commons)
- Samantha Traynor's toothbrush used tiny mass effect fields to remove plaque and massage the gums, and doubled in a pinch as an improvised tool to pop the Normandy's emergency airlock hatch. (source: Citadel Docks: Retake the Normandy)
- Samara's armor featured near-invisible kinetic-barrier technology that serves as her primary method of defense in combat. (source: The Art of the Mass Effect Trilogy/Mass Effect 2)
