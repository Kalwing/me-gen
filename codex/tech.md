# Tech

- "Tech" abilities, deployed via omni-tools, cover reverse engineering and hacking of hardsuits, weapons, and synthetics; the trilogy's tech classes are the Engineer, Infiltrator, and Sentinel (source: Tech).
- A "rogue VI" was a VI that deviated from its programming due to error or tampering; incidents included the Alliance VI Hannibal seizing a Luna training ground after scientists tried to make it a controllable AI, the Jarrahe Station VI locking down and killing all aboard, and the Cerberus Overlord VI on Aite becoming a virus (source: Virtual Intelligence).
- A 2183 attack on Sirta's Chohe facility by biotic fanatics nearly bankrupted the foundation; discounted platinum from asari colonists on Lusia was credited with saving it, or it prospered and cured an inter-species flu if the scientists survived (source: Sirta Foundation)
- A Virtual Intelligence (VI) was a sophisticated but non-self-aware program that assisted users and processed data, distinct from AIs like the geth; some carried "personality imprints" of specific individuals, though modeling a VI on a living person was illegal (source: Virtual Intelligence).
- A dreadnought main gun (800m to 1km) accelerates a 20kg slug to about 4025 km/s every two seconds, each impact carrying roughly 38 kilotons of TNT; Alliance dreadnoughts are named for Earth mountains (Everest and Kilimanjaro classes), and as of Mass Effect 2 the turians fielded 39, the asari 20, the salarians 16, and humanity 8. (source: Codex/Ships and Vehicles)
- A separate canon Batarian State Arms shop appeared on the Citadel in Mass Effect 3. (source: Batarian State Arms)
- Access is gated by the "Rich" achievement (one million credits) for tier VII and additionally level fifty for tier X; all Spectre weapons use adaptive fire control, making them the most powerful and accurate available. (source: Spectre - Master Gear)
- Acolyte: Mass Effect 3 heavy pistol designed for the asari resistance; fires only charged shots of resonant warp-bomb ammunition with a 500% damage bonus against shields and barriers, bypasses enemy shield gates, and its arcing explosive projectile can bounce off walls. Available via the Groundside Resistance Pack (single-player) or Earth DLC (multiplayer, rare). (source: Acolyte)
- Activating the Crucible transmits its energy through the relay network from the Citadel, overloading the relays' element zero cores; survivors (or the Reapers) rebuild them afterward (source: Mass Relay).
- Adas Anti-Synthetic Rifle: Mass Effect 3 assault rifle named for the quarians killed in the Morning War on the planet Adas; despite its name it deals equal damage to organic and synthetic targets but has a 2x modifier against shields and barriers, firing area-damage rounds that stun and knock back most enemies. Available via the Firefight Pack (single-player) or Reckoning DLC (multiplayer, rare). (source: Adas Anti-Synthetic Rifle)
- After Freedom's Progress, Mordin Solus used Veetor'Nara's recorded data to develop an experimental countermeasure making wearers theoretically invisible to Seeker Swarms; it worked on Horizon but failed against heavy concentrations in the Collector Base, where biotic fields were used instead (source: Seeker Swarm).
- Aldrin Labs: human equipment manufacturer on the Luna colony and a primary Systems Alliance supplier of affordable, reliable armor (Agent, Hydra, Onyx lines), omni-tools, and bio-amps; its kinetic-plastite armor and other gear had reduced stats versus competitors. Also made Ascension Project stunners and, in Mass Effect 2, the N7 Armor's Stabilization Gauntlets. Name may reference Buzz Aldrin. (source: Aldrin Labs)
- Alliance and quarian intelligence theorized the Geth Spitfire was designed to destroy other geth, with its field numbers suggesting a schism among the synthetics forced it into production; it was found on Rannoch and during Citadel Wards: Ambush, and was the heaviest, highest-sustained-DPS weapon in multiplayer. (source: Geth Spitfire)
- Ammunition Upgrades: weapon mods in the first Mass Effect that enhanced ammunition, dropping as loot and sold by merchants, each with ten upgrade levels. Categories included anti-organic (Anti-Personnel, Shredder), anti-armor/synthetic tungsten-based (Armor Piercing, Tungsten), incendiary (Incendiary, Inferno), cold (Cryo, Snowblind), explosive (High Explosive), high-impact (Hammerhead, Sledgehammer), shield-piercing particle bolts (Phasic, Proton), toxic healing-blocking (Chemical, Polonium), and anti-biotic/tech (Radioactive). Cut content included the unethical Harpoon Rounds. (source: Ammunition Upgrades)
- An OSD (Optical Storage Disc) was a small portable data unit for physically transferring data between systems; it could also be read remotely by omni-tools and was a standard part of upgrade kits, though its use for retail game distribution had faded by 2185 (source: OSD).
- Annihilation Field was a Mass Effect 3 multiplayer biotic power for the N7 Fury Adept and Asari Valkyrie Sentinel that spun a burning field around the user, primed and detonated Biotic Explosions on any nearby enemy, persisted while the user was downed, and could drain enemy shields to restore the user's barriers (source: Annihilation Field).
- Arc Projector: a Cerberus anti-synthetic weapon nicknamed "laser-guided lightning" with a single three-stage trigger; it irradiates a target with a neutron-driven gamma-ray laser to ionize a corridor of air, discharges a massive electrical impulse down that corridor, then uses autotargeting optical fibers to arc the current from target to target. (source: Codex/Weapons, Armor and Equipment)
- Arcturian Jade: a luxury passenger liner featured in Mass Effect Galaxy, captained by Reginald Tudge; attacked by batarian terrorists while en route to the Citadel and defended by the vacationing retired marine Jacob Taylor (source: Arcturian Jade)
- Ariake Technologies (Ariake Tech): an Earth-based electronics concern known for high-grade omni-tools, later branching into ablative-weave armor; manufactured Mercenary Armor, the M-23 Katana and M-27 Scimitar shotguns, the Kuwashii Visor, and Logic Arrest omni-tools; also developed telemetry data-cleaning algorithms (source: Ariake Technologies)
- Armax Arsenal also ran a galaxy-broadcast simulated combat arena on the Citadel (source: Armax Arsenal)
- Armax Arsenal: main supplier of elite turian military units, producing high-quality, high-priced weapons and armor difficult for non-turians to obtain, including Predator L/M/H Armor, the ML-77 Missile Launcher, the C77 Tyrus fighting vehicle, and the Gemini biotic amp (source: Armax Arsenal)
- Armor (combat hardsuit): protective body covering with ceramic plating, a kinetic barrier generator, and an onboard computer for environmental readouts and data access; usually customized per species, though asari use human armor. (source: Armor)
- Armory license: grants the Normandy Alliance Requisition Officer access to a manufacturer's supplies; fourteen exist, unlocking at levels 1, 18, and 36 at 100, 6,250, and 30,000 credits respectively, and do not carry over to new characters. (source: Armory license)
- Batarian State Arms (BSA) was a vast nationalized batarian arms institution infamous for waste and corruption, producing chemical weapons and NBC protective armor; it was a hidden, non-canon cut manufacturer in Mass Effect whose gear (Terminator rifles, Executioner shotguns, Judgement pistols, Lightning Strike sniper rifles, Partisan/Skirmish armor) was only accessible via PC console or the Bring Down the Sky PC DLC. (source: Batarian State Arms)
- Biotics generate and control mass effect fields biologically through training and cybernetic implants; all biotics can sense such fields even if not strong enough to use them offensively. (source: Mass Effect Field)
- Blackstar: a Reaper weapon so advanced Alliance scientists could only speculate about it; it appears to use an element zero core and mass effect fields to fire gravitational singularities (micro black holes) that revert to natural lethality on impact, theorized to alter the target's nuclei and trigger localized fusion or fission, destroying organic tissue and corroding armor; it has only single-launch capability. (source: Codex/Weapons, Armor and Equipment)
- By 2185 common security updates blocked omni-gel from bypassing locks and security systems; in Mass Effect: Andromeda it served as a major crafting resource for Milky Way-based equipment. (source: Omni-gel)
- Cerberus Ajax Armor: Mass Effect 3 armor set from the Alternate Appearance Pack 1 DLC, favored in-fiction by Cerberus soldiers who led formation-breaking charges; it boosted shields, weapon damage, power damage and ammo capacity, emphasizing kinetic-barrier recharge and glove coolant circulation. In Legendary Edition it unlocked on returning to the Normandy after Priority: The Citadel I, and with the Nightmare, Shade and Spirit sets it shared an 80% combined bonus structure. (source: Cerberus Ajax Armor)
- Cerberus Assault Armor: non-customizable shock-trooper armor in Mass Effect 2 and Mass Effect 3 (called simply Cerberus Armor in the latter), built for heavy shields and armor at the cost of weight, with the troops' motto "Out of shape going in, in shape coming out." In Mass Effect 2 it gave +10% heavy weapon ammo capacity, shields and health and shipped free via the Cerberus Network with a confirmation e-mail from the Illusive Man; in Mass Effect 3 it was sold on the Citadel for 50,000 credits. (source: Cerberus Assault Armor)
- Cerberus Harrier: Mass Effect 3 assault rifle, a Cerberus modification of the M-96 Mattock reworked to fully automatic fire with tamed recoil and issued to elite Cerberus troopers; it has excellent accuracy and negligible muzzle climb but a very low spare thermal clip reserve. Available in single-player via the Firefight Pack or from Spectre Requisitions for 150,000 credits in the Legendary Edition, and as an ultra-rare Rebellion Pack reward in multiplayer. (source: Cerberus Harrier)
- Cerberus Skunkworks: hidden, non-canon cut-content equipment manufacturer in the first Mass Effect representing black-ops hardware made by Cerberus operatives; particle-beam weapon lines named for Greek monsters (Gorgon rifles, Hydra shotguns, Harpy pistols, Titan sniper rifles) and Light/Medium/Heavy "Freedom/Hoplite" hardsuits billed as the best tech/biotic protection in the game. Fully in the game files but obtainable only via PC console commands or third-party software; name references Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works. (source: Cerberus Skunkworks)
- Chakram Launcher: Mass Effect 3 assault rifle by Ama-Lur Equipment that uses an internal fabricator to make explosive holographic-tracer ammunition discs, requiring thermal clips to bleed off the fabrication heat; its discs damage on impact and detonation, can be charged for up to 3x damage, stun light targets, ignore armor penalties, and pierce Guardian/CAT6 Heavy shields. Obtained as a Kingdoms of Amalur: Reckoning demo crossover bonus (with a Liara T'Soni e-mail), in the Omega DLC, or from Spectre Requisitions for 7,000 credits; designed by Spawn creator Todd McFarlane. (source: Chakram Launcher)
- Collector Assault Rifle: the Collectors' primary weapon, built on the same principles as a human assault rifle but with clearly organic components; its power source appears to be an internal organ with biotic capacitance and its ammunition resembles pellets of metallic enamel that strip shields with deadly efficiency. Cerberus reverse-engineered a version that accepts a standard thermal clip for Shepard. (source: Collector Assault Rifle)
- Collector Chitin Armor (called Collector Armor in Mass Effect 3): a suit Cerberus adapted for Shepard from salvaged Collector technology; the Collectors' chitinous armor is flexible and tougher than ballistic fibers, self-healing thanks to its organic construction, with muscle-like tissue that assists the wearer's movement. (source: Collector Chitin Armor)
- Collector Cruiser: the only known Collector ship type, a vast vessel of organic protrusions over a metallic superstructure with cave-like hive interiors and floating hexagonal platforms, able to land vertically on planets; armed with a multi-directional particle beam that ignores kinetic barriers and can cripple a frigate in a few shots, plus lateral "eye" point-defense weapons, sensors that pierce heat-based stealth, and an IFF system for safe transit of the Omega 4 Relay. Used to abduct entire human colonies with Seeker Swarms and stasis pods. (source: Collector Cruiser)
- Collector Particle Beam: a Mass Effect 2 heavy weapon of Collector origin firing a focused-radiation beam highly effective against shields, barriers, and armor; retrieved by Shepard on Horizon but impossible to reverse-engineer, though it suggested Normandy engineers might later build larger-scale beam weapons. It was fully automatic, consumed five ammo units per burst, caused no friendly-fire damage, and was the weapon wielded by Collector Assassins. (source: Collector Particle Beam)
- Collector SMG: a Mass Effect 3 multiplayer-exclusive submachine gun (ultra-rare, requires the Retaliation DLC) built from mixed mechanical and organic parts; it vents heat automatically rather than using thermal clips, extends a neural link to a Collector wielder's wrist, has low recoil suited to suppression, and deals a 50% damage bonus against armored targets. (source: Collector SMG)
- Collector Sniper Rifle: a Mass Effect 3 multiplayer-exclusive sniper rifle (rare, requires the Retaliation DLC) firing a sustained long-range particle beam that switches between anti-personnel and anti-materiel fire by holding the trigger; it uses no thermal clips, cooling through a liquid-filled sleeve and barrel fins, is a hitscan weapon, disintegrates its kills, and is the only sniper rifle with no unscoped accuracy penalty. (source: Collector Sniper Rifle)
- Destroyers were roughly 160-meter Reapers that formed the bulk of the Reaper fleet in 2186, armed with a cruiser-killing main gun and a folding beam weapon, able to walk as ground heavy walkers; their charging "eye" firing chamber was a weak point Shepard exploited on Rannoch, and their design was based on a leaf insect nymph. (source: Destroyer)
- Devlon Industries was a manufacturer of environmentally sealed armor lines (Explorer, Liberator, Thermal, Survivor) that reduced hazard-level timers, plus fusion-tech weapons (Raptor, Firestorm, Stinger, Striker) and the M-92 Mantis sniper rifle; its Citadel license cost 6,250 credits from Delan. (source: Devlon Industries)
- Disruptor torpedoes create unstable mass effect fields that tear a target apart; they use a mass-increasing field to pass through kinetic barriers, are slow and cold-launched, and are fired by fighters in ripple-fire waves at point-blank range to saturate GARDIAN defenses. (source: Codex/Ships and Vehicles)
- Dr. Amanda Kenson's Project staff, secretly indoctrinated by the Reaper artifact Object Rho, tried to stop Shepard from proving a relay could be destroyed by a large enough impact; after Shepard fought through Kenson and her guards, and Kenson died in the final struggle, an asteroid driven into the Bahak system's Alpha Relay destroyed the system in a supernova-scale blast, killing over 300,000 (source: Mass Relay).
- EDI was designed in part with technology from Sovereign's remains, making her partly based on Reaper technology, and she devoted significant processing power to analyzing anti-Reaper algorithms (source: The SSV Normandy SR-2 (Part 1) — Origin and EDI).
- Each relay is two 15-km curved arms around 5-km revolving gyroscopic rings holding an element zero core; built of the same resilient material as the Citadel and locked by a quantum shield that lets it survive a supernova's wake (source: Mass Relay).
- Elanus Risk Control Services (ERCS): originally a turian private security firm turned interstellar conglomerate providing security, mercenaries and anti-piracy ships; supplied Noveria's guards and armory, and manufactured the Banshee, Hurricane, Striker and Hammer weapon lines, Duelist/Guardian/Warlord armor, and later the M-3 Predator, M-9 Tempest, M-15 Vindicator and M-100 Grenade Launcher (source: Elanus Risk Control Services).
- Elbrus: heavily armed Cerberus cruiser under General Oleg Petrovsky, carrying weapons banned by the Citadel Conventions; sent to Omega to fight the Adjutant outbreak and taken through the Omega 4 Relay to Avernus Station; named after Mount Elbrus. For what Cerberus did with it afterward — restraining a dampened Aria T'Loak to seize a leaderless Omega, until she broke free and seized the ship herself — read `scenes/omega-invasion-avernus-aria-dampened.yaml` rather than a bullet: the betrayal and counter-betrayal do not survive compression (source: Elbrus).
- Elkoss Combine: volus manufacturer in the Terminus Systems producing cheap, functional armor, weapons, and omni-tools; its first-game gear used reverse-engineered tech with poor accuracy and reduced shields/tech protection. Made the M-4 Shuriken and M-8 Avenger in ME2/ME3, plus heatsinks, skycars, and the dreadnought Kwunu, with subsidiaries including Fishdog Food Shack. (source: Elkoss Combine)
- Equipment (Mass Effect: Infiltrator): store-bought armor and weapons for Cerberus operative Randall Ezno; four two-piece armor sets (Cerberus Assault, Aegis Defensive, Stealth Suit, Infiltrator) and upgradeable weapons M-96 Mattock Auto, M-22 Eviscerator, M-29 Incisor, and the Infiltrator-exclusive M-333 Particle Beam reverse-engineered from Collector tech (source: Equipment (Mass Effect: Infiltrator))
- Equipment: covers armor, weapons, biotic amps, omni-tools, and upgrades across the trilogy; Mass Effect used heat-managed mass accelerator weapons and omni-gel recycling, Mass Effect 2 introduced detachable thermal clips and heavy weapons with power cells, and Mass Effect 3 removed class weapon restrictions in favor of a weight-versus-cooldown system. Legendary Edition folded in all DLC gear. (source: Equipment)
- Executioner Pistol: Blood Pack-invented single-shot heavy pistol first improvised from scrap during an Omega territory dispute; fires one high-impact armor-piercing slug with 50 cm penetration, hitting harder than an M-92 Mantis at a fraction of the weight (source: Executioner Pistol)
- FTL drives use element zero cores to lower a ship's mass, raising the effective speed of light within the mass effect field; ships still need conventional thrusters for motive power, and a field collapse at FTL snaps the ship back to sublight and sheds lethal Cherenkov radiation. (source: Codex/Ships and Vehicles)
- GARDIAN (General ARea Defense Integration Anti-spacecraft Network): computer-controlled anti-missile and anti-fighter laser turrets on a starship's hull; light-speed lasers are unavoidable and highly accurate but limited by diffraction (short range) and heat buildup, and can burn out under sustained fire. (source: GARDIAN)
- GARDIAN fire is not blocked by capital-ship kinetic barriers (except partially by Reaper barriers); Alliance frigates carry it, the Destiny Ascension used it at the Battle of the Citadel, and turrets were also deployed as ground defenses on Horizon against slavers. (source: GARDIAN)
- GARDIAN lasers typically use infrared frequencies for reliability; salarians use near-ultraviolet lasers with six times the range, and the geth use ultraviolet, with a geth dreadnought cutting through multiple quarian ships at Rannoch. (source: GARDIAN)
- GARDIAN point-defense lasers are computer-controlled anti-fighter and anti-missile turrets that always hit at light speed but are limited by beam diffraction and heat buildup; salarians favor longer-ranged near-ultraviolet lasers. (source: Codex/Ships and Vehicles)
- Geth Dropship: an approximately frigate-sized geth troop carrier shaped like a wingless hornet, able to deploy Geth Armatures and Colossi mid-flight, give projectile air support, and anchor to strategic points with near-indestructible claws; itself a sentient mobile platform that can transfer its programs for repairs. (source: Geth Dropship)
- Geth Dropships were central to the geth assault on Feros, delivered reinforcements on Virmire, blocked the Citadel Tower exhaust plain during the Battle of the Citadel, deployed geth on Corang and Haestrom in 2185, and joined the geth armada over Rannoch. (source: Geth Dropship)
- Geth Plasma Shotgun fired three tracking plasma balls instead of standard pellets, had longer range than normal shotguns, could not score headshots, bypassed Guardian shields and the shield gate, and in Mass Effect 3 was found during Priority: Rannoch. (source: Geth Plasma Shotgun)
- Geth Plasma Shotgun: a three-barrelled geth shotgun (Mass Effect 2 and 3) firing cluster rounds of superconducting projectiles that fragment and flash-convert air to plasma, overloading shields and barriers; a two-stage trigger allows charge-and-release shots up to double damage. Cerberus acquired it from the geth per the Illusive Man. (source: Geth Plasma Shotgun)
- Geth Pulse Rifle in Mass Effect 2 was obtainable only on Hardcore or Insanity during Tali's recruitment at Haestrom; in Mass Effect 3 it was found during Priority: Geth Dreadnought and had very high accuracy, near-zero recoil and large magazine capacity but low per-shot damage. (source: Geth Pulse Rifle)
- Geth Pulse Rifle: a geth assault rifle (Mass Effect 2 and 3) firing lightweight phasic slugs at a variable sine-curve fire rate, very effective against shields and barriers but weak against armor; rarely recovered intact and hard to repair, with replication attempts failing. (source: Geth Pulse Rifle)
- Geth Spitfire: a geth minigun in Mass Effect 3 that magnetically flings clusters of superconducting toroids which shatter and flash-convert to plasma; fire rate and damage ramp up the longer it is held. It has an inherent 1.75x damage multiplier against shields and barriers. (source: Geth Spitfire)
- Geth Spitfire: a geth minigun that magnetically flings clusters of superconducting toroids which shatter and flash-convert to plasma on impact; Alliance and quarian intelligence theorize it was designed to destroy other geth, and the quantities found in the field suggest a synthetic schism forced it into production. (source: Codex/Weapons, Armor and Equipment)
- Graal Spike Thrower: a krogan double-barreled shotgun in Mass Effect 3 designed for hunting thresher maws, firing oversized flechettes for deep wound channels and fitted with blades for use if the wielder is swallowed; shots can be charged for up to double damage. (source: Graal Spike Thrower)
- Graal Spike Thrower: fires rendered flechette projectiles with travel time, ignores armor damage-reduction penalties, penetrates armor plating on Brutes and Atlases, ignores shield gates, and can deal bonus damage to weak points; its design came from designer Corey Gaspur's nightmare about a man with a nail gun. (source: Graal Spike Thrower)
- Graphite (C): a crystalline carbon mineral and electrical conductor collectible in Mass Effect: Andromeda, mined with the Nomad on Elaaden and Eos, used to craft weapons such as the M-90 Indra, N7 Valiant, and Scorpion, plus Nomad upgrades. (source: Graphite)
- Graybox: a memory-recording implant launched by Synthetic Insights in 2160 CE, originally for treating Alzheimer's, storing snapshots of a person's memories; removal or software bugs cause extensive brain damage, so its use was restricted to researchers and spies who needed eidetic memory. (source: Graybox)
- Graybox: known users were Kasumi Goto and Keiji Okuda; Keiji's heavily encrypted graybox held evidence of an Alliance black ops massacre at a batarian station with Reaper tech, and Donovan Hock murdered Keiji to rip it from his head; its contents ultimately reached Spectre Jondum Bau. (source: Graybox)
- Grenade Upgrades: single-effect modifications for Mass Effect grenades - Anti-Thorian Gas (disables Thorian thralls, kills Creepers), Cryo Explosive (snap-freeze, reduces enemy accuracy), Fusion Explosive (polonium radiation poisoning), High Explosive (larger blast, force, and radius), and Incendiary Explosive (clinging thermal paste burn over 5 seconds). (source: Grenade Upgrades)
- Grenades: hand-tossed explosive charges used across the series; the Alliance Mark 14 had retractable gliding fins, a 10-second fuse, surface adhesion, and field-mod variants (high explosive, flashbang, incendiary, cryo, micro-nuke); later games used cooldown or limited-pool grenade powers, and many ME3 enemies throw them ignoring shield/health gates. (source: Grenades)
- HKSW gear was a hidden manufacturer line accessible only via console commands: Diamond Back rifles, Viper shotguns, Cobra pistols, Python sniper rifles, and Spectre and Janissary armor lines, all with higher stats than standard Hahne-Kedar equivalents (source: Hahne-Kedar Shadow Works).
- Hahne-Kedar Shadow Works (HKSW) was a non-canon cut-content Hahne-Kedar subsidiary founded a decade before Mass Effect, supplying rugged low-maintenance weapons and armor to Alliance special forces (source: Hahne-Kedar Shadow Works).
- Hahne-Kedar was an Earth-based manufacturer and major Systems Alliance supplier; its weapons were stock quality (Lancer, Storm, Kessler, Avenger lines) but its armor lines (Mantis, Predator, Scorpion, Ursa, turian-only Silverback) were above average, with a field destabilizer giving improved shield efficiency (source: Hahne-Kedar).
- Hahne-Kedar weapons used reverse-engineered technology, giving lower damage than rivals; the company also made the FENRIS, LOKI and YMIR mech units and, in Mass Effect 3, weapon-damage-boosting customizable armor components (source: Hahne-Kedar).
- Haliat Armory produced the Thunder assault rifles, Tornado shotguns, Stiletto pistols and Equalizer sniper rifles; in Mass Effect its license cost 6,250 credits from Ledra at Zhu's Hope on Feros (source: Haliat Armory).
- Haliat Armory was a small turian weapons manufacturer, distinct from Elanos Haliat, licensed by the Hierarchy to sell surplus on the open market; it specialized in high-quality basic-level weapons using absorbent recoil technology (source: Haliat Armory).
- Heavy Weapons: weapon class dealing damage through all protection types at high power cost; in Mass Effect 2 they used mission-found power cells and fired without reloading, while in Mass Effect 3 they were battlefield pickups discarded after use, with models such as the M-920 Cain, M-490 Blackstorm, ML-77 Missile Launcher, Collector Particle Beam, Reaper Blackstar, and Geth Spitfire. (source: Heavy Weapons)
- Heavy and inaccurate at range, the Venom was reissued to frontline soldiers during the Reaper War; it returned in Mass Effect: Andromeda as a semi-automatic shotgun firing rebounding microgrenade clusters (source: Venom Shotgun).
- Heavy pistols: primary weapon class introduced in Mass Effect 2 to replace the first game's pistols; semi-automatic, low rate of fire, high per-shot damage and armor-effective, they became highly moddable in Mass Effect 3 with models including the M-3 Predator, M-5 Phalanx, M-6 Carnifex, Acolyte, Arc Pistol, Paladin, Scorpion, and N7 Eagle. (source: Heavy Pistols)
- Heavy weapons were carried only by Shepard, used power cells rather than thermal clips, and were removed as inventory selections in Mass Effect 3, appearing only as limited-use mission pickups. (source: Weapons)
- Human Armor manufacturers included Aldrin Labs (Hydra, Onyx), Armax Arsenal (Predator), Kassa Fabrication (Colossus, highest damage protection), Cerberus Skunkworks (Freedom/Hoplite, strong tech/biotic protection) and Jormangund Technology (Crisis/Hazard, strongest shields) (source: Human Armor)
- Human Armor was the armor category wearable by humans and asari in the first Mass Effect, sold in light, medium and heavy weight classes across levels I to X and rated for Damage Protection, Shields, and Tech/Biotic Protection (source: Human Armor)
- Humanity found the Charon Relay disguised as Pluto's moon Charon, following a Prothean data cache on Mars; the Protheans used relay principles to build the Conduit (source: Mass Relay).
- In 2183 omni-gel was used with omni-tools for electronics and decryption work and to repair vehicles such as the M35 Mako; Shepard could carry up to 999 units. (source: Omni-gel)
- In Andromeda the Reegar Carbine used an invisible laser to ionize air and create a path for a high-amperage shock, radiated energy to nearby enemies, and could damage foes through thin walls (source: Reegar Carbine)
- In Mass Effect 2 Tech Armor boosted maximum shields and fully restored them on activation, lasting until shields were knocked out; rank 4 evolved into Assault Armor (bigger pulse) or Power Armor (more shields plus power damage) (source: Tech Armor).
- In Mass Effect 2 and 3, Shepard started with default N7 Armor, customizable piece by piece at the Normandy SR-2 armor locker or swappable for full sets; Mass Effect 3 squadmate armor sets conferred gameplay bonuses while Mass Effect 2 squadmate armor was cosmetic. (source: Armor)
- In Mass Effect 2 the Praetorian's signature "Death Choir" attack was a near-instant-kill ground blast that also fully recharged its barrier, used after each quarter of its armor was stripped by weapons fire; it was immune to Stasis, Charge, and Dominate. (source: Praetorian)
- In Mass Effect 2 the Terminus Armor came from the Terminus Weapon and Armor DLC with an email from the Illusive Man, boosting storm speed, shields and reserve ammo but giving no weapon-damage bonus; in Mass Effect 3 it instead boosted melee damage, shield strength and ammo capacity, favoring close-range Vanguards (source: Terminus Armor).
- In Mass Effect 2, Overload dealt double damage to shields and could stun synthetics and overheat enemy weapons, with rank 4 evolutions Heavy Overload (synthetics explode on death) and Area Overload (wider radius); in Mass Effect 3 it lost its impact area but gained triple damage to all barriers/shields, minor anti-organic damage, and Chain, Neural Shock, and Shield Damage evolutions (source: Overload).
- In Mass Effect 2, SMGs dealt bonus damage to shields and biotic barriers but were weak against armour; all classes except the soldier could use them from the start. (source: Submachine Guns)
- In Mass Effect 3 Praetorians swept their twin particle cannons in a wide cover-penetrating line (the game's most damaging enemy ranged attack), used a long-range pounce and an instant-kill grab, and could be "possessed" for extra durability. (source: Praetorian)
- In Mass Effect 3 Tech Armor instead gave stacking flat damage reduction protecting health and shields at the cost of a power-speed penalty, no longer broke when shields fell, and its blast dealt 1.5x damage to shields through walls and cover (source: Tech Armor).
- In Mass Effect 3, SMGs were a light weapon type favoured by power-oriented classes, generally the least damaging weapon class though some models rivalled assault rifles; a High-Velocity Barrel made any SMG effective against armour. (source: Submachine Guns)
- In Mass Effect 3, Stasis had no effect on armored targets, broke when the target took major health damage, and could be evolved at rank 6 into a Bubble that traps enemies walking into it or a Vulnerability that raises damage taken by 50% (source: Stasis)
- In Mass Effect 3, Tactical Cloak's damage bonus applied to all damage including powers and grenades, lingered briefly after a deliberate decloak, and its recharge time was proportional to how long the cloak stayed active, with a three-second minimum. (source: Tactical Cloak)
- In Mass Effect the Geth Armory produced the Rage, Berserker, and Battlemaster heavy krogan armors (identical stats, differing only in highlight color) and the Geth Pulse Rifle, a standard geth infantry weapon firing a brief cyan energy beam highly effective against shields. (source: Geth Armory)
- In Mass Effect the first game, weapons had a Level (I-X) for quality and accepted weapon and ammunition mods; manufacturers included Hahne-Kedar, Armax Arsenal, Elkoss Combine, Kassa Fabrication, Rosenkov Materials, and Spectre Master Gear. (source: Weapons)
- In Mass Effect, armor came in light, medium, and heavy categories with class-based restrictions; levels I-VI had one upgrade slot and VII+ had two, and manufacturers included Hahne-Kedar, Kassa Fabrication, Rosenkov Materials, Sirta Foundation, and Devlon Industries. (source: Armor)

- Throw was a biotic ability across the trilogy that used mass effect fields to hurl a target away with damaging force, useful for crowd control, knocking enemies off ledges, and slamming them into cover or thrown objects (shield-bypassing damage) (source: Throw)
- Throw's distance scaled with caster rank and inversely with target mass, so lightening powers like Pull, Singularity or Slam maximized it; in Mass Effect 3 its low cooldown let it detonate any type of biotic Power Combo (source: Throw)
- Tied with the Asari Sword as Andromeda's most powerful melee weapon, the Krogan Hammer swung vertically to smash the ground, knocking human-sized enemies back and releasing an area shockwave for crowd control, but with a slow one-second-plus windup; usable by Ryder, Nakmor Drack and the Krogan Gladiator. (source: Krogan Hammer)
- VIs served as extranet search tools, tour guides (Avina), and lab and database managers (Mira on Noveria), and were used by human and elcor militaries to process reports and react faster than organics; armor upgrades and the L4 biotic implant used VI technology (source: Virtual Intelligence).
- Warships are classed by weight: frigates (fast scouts that operate in wolf-pack flotillas and can land on planets), cruisers (standard patrol combatants), kilometer-long dreadnoughts (capital ships with long spinal main guns, held as strategic reserves), and dreadnought-sized carriers that fight mainly through launched fighters. (source: Codex/Ships and Vehicles)
- Weapon mods are fitted at a weapon bench and apply to every weapon of their type for Shepard and squadmates; many DLC barrel and scope mods add 50% weapon weight, and several mod pairs are mutually exclusive. (source: Weapon Mods)
- When cast by Shepard, Throw arced toward the crosshair and could curve around cover, while squadmates fired it in a straight line from their own position (source: Throw)
