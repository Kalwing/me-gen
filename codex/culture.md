# Culture

- A captain gave a returning quarian two code phrases: one signalling a successful Pilgrimage and safe reintegration, the other an alert phrase meaning return under duress, which caused the quarian navy to destroy the returnee's ship. (source: Pilgrimage)
- A drell prayer of redemption to Kalahira, asking her to "wash the sins from this one" and set them "on the distant shore of the infinite spirit," was recited at Thane's deathbed as his own wish for Shepard rather than for himself (source: Thane Krios).
- A quarian sentenced to exile has their name struck from every ship's registry; Rael'Zorah's posthumous exile would have branded him a traitor and cautionary tale forever, and Balkanized the Fleet when his crimes became public (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 3) — The Treason Trial and Her Fatal Flaw).
- A salarian's clan was reckoned in circles, from a first circle of about 100 close relatives out to fourth or fifth circles numbering in the millions, with loyalty strongest to the first circle. (source: Salarian)
- After the hanar evacuated the drell from dying Rakhana, the drell repaid the debt through the Compact, an agreement to perform tasks the hanar cannot physically manage; high-ranking hanar are often inseparable from their drell attendants. (source: Rakhana)
- Ardat-Yakshi, asari born unable to safely meld with others, can only be purebloods, which the justicar Samara theorized is the origin of the stigma against purebloods. (source: Pureblood)
- Asari mining corporations once adopted vorcha orphans and raised them non-violently on Parasc, proving the vorcha habit of communicating through violence was not entirely innate, though their short lifespans kept such careers brief (source: Vorcha).
- Asari religion is mainly the pantheistic siari faith ("All is one"); an older monotheism worships the goddess Athame, whom the Protheans secretly impersonated. Asari society is an inclusive "electronic democracy" of city-states with no politicians that defers to Matriarchs in a crisis, and asari pass through Maiden, Matron and Matriarch life stages across a thousand-year lifespan. The Justicar Order enforces a strict code, the Oaths of Subsumation. (source: Codex/Aliens: Council Races)
- At Shepard's Citadel apartment party (Mass Effect 3: Citadel DLC), former college bartender Samantha Traynor mixed a full cocktail menu to order: Garrus got a Dextro Heat Sink, Kasumi a Memory Stealer, James Vega a Rojo Loco, Jack a vodka-whiskey-bourbon-and-energy-drink mix, and Miranda a Perfection made without its usual strawberry liqueur; she could also recite exact ingredients for the Full Biotic Kick (bourbon, Tuchanka dry, orange twist, ginger beer) and the Quad Kicker (spiced rum, bourbon, ginger ale, curry powder, which she omitted being allergic). (source: Drinks)
- Batarian belief holds that the soul leaves the body through the eyes at death, making treatment of the corpse unimportant unless the eyes are removed by enemies, which is a grave insult (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Dr. Mordin Solus).
- Being a dextro-based species in a levo-dominated galaxy is thought to have shaped turian culture around the idea that nothing is given and everything must be earned (source: The Turian Hierarchy Before the Citadel — Early History).
- Blasto, the fictional hanar action-movie Spectre hero, headlined a long-running in-universe film franchise (Blasto: The Jellyfish Stings, Blasto 6: Partners in Crime, Blasto: Eternity is Forever) that drew criticism from hanar anti-defamation groups over the term "jellyfish" and persisted as pop culture into the Andromeda galaxy via Blast-Oh's cereal and new sequels. (source: Blasto)
- Citadel law prohibited evangelising on the Presidium, confining preaching to designated areas and requiring an evangelical permit, enforced by C-Sec. (source: Preaching Hanar)
- Clan Urdnot's Shaman held that becoming shaman required many rites, including surrendering one's name, so torturous they made him wish for death, with further rites performed every dawn and dusk on Tuchanka (source: Shaman).
- Descent from Warlord Shiagur remains a krogan status symbol, marked by clan brands; being compared to Shiagur is a high honor, and her name is invoked in a song Mordin Solus may sing for Eve (source: Shiagur).
- Drell are largely deeply religious polytheists, worshipping gods including Amonkira (Lord of Hunters), Arashu (Goddess of Motherhood and Protection) and Kalahira (Goddess of Oceans and Afterlife), and believing body and soul form a separable Whole. Their eidetic memory gives vivid total recall, and they repay the hanar through the Compact, an obligation often discharged by dangerous work such as combat; being asked to serve is a great honor. (source: Drell)
- Drell faith holds body and soul as separate entities, letting an assassin regard his body as a tool of whoever commissions a killing; drell also possess eidetic memory, and the drell term "tu-fira" ("lost in another") and the endearment "siha" (a warrior-angel of Arashu) come from Thane (source: Thane Krios).
- During the asari golden age of learning, twenty-eight knowledge-seeking Matriarchs left Thessia with no destination; only six were heard from again, and only Matriarch Dilinaga's remains and writings were recovered, becoming celebrated study objects held at the Thessia Guildhall and later the Farinah Museum (source: UNC: Asari Writings).
- Earth's cultural influence is disproportionate to humanity's brief time on the galactic stage; pre-war colonial wealth fueled a golden age of industry, commerce and art, greened cities with arcology skyscrapers, and widened the gap between rich and poor. (source: Earth; Codex/Humanity and the Systems Alliance)
- Elcor communicate through scent, infrasound and micro-movements and prefix speech to other species with an explicit emotive statement; their culture is governed by the precedent-bound Courts of Dekuuna. (source: Species)
- Elcor embraced theater despite their famously flat affect: director Francis Kitt's all-elcor Hamlet, using scent and micro-movement invisible to non-elcor to convey emotion, ran 14 hours and became an award-winning hit performed from 2185 to 2186, followed by an all-krogan Macbeth; elcor also preserved cultural artifacts such as a mating-totem held in the Citadel Archives and the venerated ancestral wisdom text the Code of the Ancients. (source: Elcor)
- Exiled from Rannoch and at war with the geth for 300 years, quarian society remains under military jurisdiction and expects every quarian to support the Fleet, balancing population against limited food and ship capacity. (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 1) — Mass Effect 1)
- Food and cuisine: while most creatures simply eat what nature provides, sapient species turn raw foods into complex dishes, and levo- and dextro-amino-acid physiologies need separate variants of many items, with the wrong chirality harmful or lethal. Named dishes include batarian burgat ("the Other Blue Meat") on sticks, varren skewers and jerky, pyjak (a staple on Omega), Rupert Gardner's calamari gumbo from an asari recipe, and nutrient paste manufactured by the Citadel's recyclers and heavily eaten by quarians. The Protheans allegedly ate the primitive races of their era. (source: Foods)
- For quarians the word "home" carries exceptional weight after 300 years of exile; the greeting "Keelah se'lai" roughly means "by the homeworld I hope to see one day," and Tali'Zorah calling Shepard her home meant more to her than saying "I love you" (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 5) — The Romance Arc and Conclusion).
- Fornax, a human erotica magazine launched in 2167, billed itself as "the galaxy's finest xenophilia," reached a monthly run of 127 million copies, and became the first magazine to offer full five-sensory stimulation; niche specialty editions included Genit-elcor and Krogasm, the latter purchased by Grunt according to his Shadow Broker dossier. (source: Fornax)
- Galactic sports culture included biotiball, whose 2185 Usaru Maestros–Washington Hackers game set records; the zero-g Terminus favorite kailat ring-ball, scored by a color visible only to asari, salarians and bees; and krogan kowla, whose demonstration to the newly contacted raloi in 2185 ended in a brawl that killed three and got krogan delegates barred from the rest of the welcome ceremony. (source: Entertainment)
- Given to the hanar at six under the species' Compact, Thane trained through exercises like standing barechested on one foot on a pole amid crashing waves, and had to petition a hanar priest handler to be released from the Compact to marry (source: Thane Krios).
- Hanar are extremely polite ocean-dwellers who carry a public "Face Name" and a private "Soul Name" and often revere the Protheans as the Enkindlers. (source: Codex/Aliens: Non-Council Races)
- Illium News and Omega News broadcasts, though nominally reporting the same galactic events, editorialized in opposite directions: Illium's unsubtitled ads pitched luxury cosmetics, indentured-labor contracts and a Blasto film (prompting a public apology over hanar "jellyfish" slurs), while Omega's male-voiced broadcasts openly cheered attacks on human colonies and ran ads for Devlon Industries pistols. (source: News Stories)
- Illium's culture is that of asari "new money": glamour, luxury and safety maintained by near-total surveillance, legal indentured servitude, and self-congratulatory media glorifying "sexiest CEOs" and "richest residents" lists. (source: Illium)
- In Mass Effect: Annihilation the lullaby recording was a cover for a computer worm; installer Oliver Barthes was murdered by captain Qetsi'Olam vas Keelah Si'yah, who later sang it while spreading the Fortinbras cure suitless until she died. (source: My Suit and Me)
- In the 2012 Dark Horse comic Blasto: Eternity is Forever, Blasto's asari partner Cerulean Star explained that asari believe in "embracing eternity," to which Blasto replied that embracing was the one thing he excelled at. (source: Mass Effect: Blasto: Eternity is Forever)
- Krogan cultural tradition holds that the Shaman may allow an outsider to attempt the Rite of Passage, and prizes understanding of krogan ideals over hollow traditionalism, praising Shepard for besting Gatatog Uvenk with words or a headbutt (source: Shaman).
- Krogan culture centers on the clan, overseen by a Shaman, with a coming-of-age Rite of Passage in which the aspirant, backed by a krantt of chosen allies, faces waves of varren and klixen and must survive a Thresher Maw; killing the Maw earns lasting respect. Krogan fiercely guard their medical records after the salarians used them to build the genophage, and centuries of genophage decline bred a dangerous fatalism; ancestral dead are interred in the burial caverns called the Hollows. (source: Grunt: Rite of Passage; Genophage; Clan Urdnot)
- Larger crews raised a quarian captain's social status, so living space was at a premium, with freighter cargo holds converted into fabric-lined personal compartments. (source: Migrant Fleet)
- Linking suit environments is the most intimate act two quarians can perform and the culture's ultimate gesture of trust and acceptance; it makes both parties badly, potentially fatally ill, as when Shala'Raan and Tali's mother shared a clean room for Tali's birth and were sick for a week. (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 2) — Mass Effect 2: Leadership and Family)
- Linking suit environments is the quarians' most sacred gesture of trust, physically dangerous given their weak immune systems; Tali admitted she trusted only Shepard that much (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 5) — The Romance Arc and Conclusion).
- Medi-gel, the ubiquitous battlefield treatment, is a genetically engineered bioplasm from the Sirta Foundation combining anesthetic, disinfectant and clotting agent, and is technically in violation of Council anti-genetic-engineering law. (source: Codex/Weapons, Armor and Equipment)
- Modern salarians were largely irreligious, though ancient faiths featured the "stones of life" Mosiiba, Mosiino, and Mosiives, and some believed in a wheel of life resembling reincarnation. (source: Salarian)
- Morinth used her cultural tastes to lure victims, name-dropping the elcor artist Forta (whose work can supposedly drive viewers mad), the vid Vaenia, the sensory band Expel 10, and the drug Hallex, all of which Shepard could invoke to hold her attention during Samara's loyalty mission. (source: Samara: The Ardat-Yakshi)
- "My Suit and Me": an old quarian lullaby about a child's bond with their environment suit and the dream of reaching Rannoch; a recording was installed in the ark Keelah Si'yah's quarian cryopods to play once a century during the ~600-year trip to Andromeda. (source: My Suit and Me)
- On Thessia, food and drink were served with small amounts of element zero, useless to non-biotics, so visitors were given a separate menu; the drink elasa could be consumed by asari and non-asari alike (source: Thessia)
- Popular military-historical novels by human author Drew Karpyshyn, Revelation and Ascension, dramatized real Alliance history (the First Contact War investigation and the Cerberus Ascension biotics program) for a galaxy-wide readership. (source: Codex/Publications)
- Prothean society was heavily regulated: games of chance were punishable by death, staring contests were a pastime suited to their four eyes, and they designated individuals as avatars of traits such as vengeance (Javik) or victory. (source: Prothean)
- Protheans had few compunctions about eating sentient species, including dextro-amino races; hanar were boiled or fried and salarian liver was a delicacy, while asari were exempt from Prothean mating restrictions. (source: Prothean)
- "Pureblood" is an offensive asari term for the child of two asari parents, seen by some as wasteful because interspecies mating is believed to give the asari race evolutionary insight. (source: Pureblood)
- Quarian society lives aboard the ~50,000-ship Migrant Fleet, sending its youths on the Pilgrimage to bring back a gift worthy of a berth; the fleet's liveships grow its food, and quarians have almost no immune system. (source: Codex/Aliens: Non-Council Races)
- Quarian society uses no currency: unneeded goods are left in communal areas for anyone to take, while food and medicine are centrally rationed to protect the Fleet from shortage. (source: Quarian)
- Quarians live inside their environment suits even among family; a simple suit rupture can be fatal, and quarian children do not wear suits until adolescence, with receiving a first suit marking a coming-of-age. (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 2) — Mass Effect 2: Leadership and Family)
- Rakhana once bore usharet flowers, sweet-smelling scarlet blossoms of cultural significance to the drell, used in perfumes and artful topiaries. (source: Rakhana)
- Rare salarian females were cloistered by tradition; Dalatrasses controlled politics while males rose in business, academia, or the military but rarely held political office, Councilor Valern being a notable exception. (source: Salarian)
- Salarian full names encoded, in order, homeworld, nation, city, district, clan name, and given name, and salarians were normally addressed by clan or given name. (source: Salarian)
- Salarian society is shaped by their biology: haplo-diploid and roughly 90 percent male, with cloistered females called Dalatrasses dominating politics, the Salarian Union functioning like a set of medieval European noble houses. (source: Codex/Aliens: Council Races)
- Salarians celebrated Betau, the first day of their New Year, marking the end of southern-hemisphere winter on Sur'Kesh, by repaying debts and petitioning favors. (source: Salarian)
- Salarians embraced "knowledge is power," treating spying on other races as a matter of course rather than dishonor. (source: Salarian)
- Since the Unification War turians wear elaborate facial markings of their home colony; going "barefaced" implies untrustworthiness and is slang for a politician (source: Turian).
- Snack and candy culture spanned dextro and levo physiologies: turian chocolates, a levo-only Candy Assortment of chocolates and caramels, Blast-Oh's breakfast cereal (marketed on Blasto's fame), and Jack's fondest food memory, chocolate chip cookies, the first thing she ate after escaping Pragia. (source: Foods)
- Species-specific drinking customs ranged from krogan sovak juice, drunk at births with belching contests that name the infant, and the incendiary krogan cocktail burukh (set on fire, then extinguished, and drunk hot), to the hanar's mindfish, a hallucinogenic skin oil rather than a true drink, and turian horosk, strong enough to send drinkers into a coma. (source: Drinks)
- Speculation about Menae's classified resources became a recurring subject in turian vids, novels and poetry. (source: Menae)
- Stripped of ritual the Pilgrimage maintained genetic diversity across the Migrant Fleet's small isolated shipboard populations; departing quarians were fitted with immunity-boosting implants and given survival training, weapons and armor. (source: Pilgrimage)
- Tabletop and video games doubled as species characterization: the asari fleet-strategy game Kepesh-Yakshi (some versions deliver a "neural feedback" shock on losing a piece), the krogan live-action-roleplaying game Krantt: The Ragening, and the krogan children's game Firebreathing Thresher Maws of Doom, played with miniature flamethrowers. (source: Entertainment)
- Thane Krios followed the old drell polytheistic religion, praying to Amonkira (lord of hunters) to find a target, Arashu (goddess of motherhood and protection) when defending another, and Kalahira (goddess of oceans and the afterlife) after a kill (source: Thane Krios).
- The Citadel is the political, cultural and financial capital of Citadel space; the broker Barla Von calls the crowded Ward arms, not the Presidium, its true cultural heart, and the Presidium runs an artificial 20-hour day-night cycle. (source: Citadel; Codex/Citadel and Galactic Government)
- The Citadel's Game Shop, run by an opinionated salarian salesman, doubled as satire of contemporary gaming culture: he griped about censorship and copy-protection while name-dropping in-universe titles like the 400-year-old card game Solitaire, the 11-billion-player MMO Galaxy of Fantasy, and the Grand Theft Auto pastiche Grim Terminus Alliance. (source: Game Shop)
- The enviro-suit is central to quarian identity, with a first fitting as a rite of passage and linking suit environments seen as the ultimate gesture of trust and affection. (source: Quarian)
- The Followers of the Exalted Light of the Word were a batarian-associated religious movement on Omega that funded the Mad Prophet's broadcast sermons, preaching from a scripture called "the Word" that the "lesser races" (krogan, vorcha, humans) were a blight on galactic purity and that humans were "the hands of the Reapers." (source: Mad Prophet)
- The hanar revere the Protheans as the "Enkindlers," and some hanar are intensely religious; on the Citadel Presidium in 2183 a hanar attempted to preach about the Enkindlers, arguing that stating "the truth" was not preaching and needed no permit. (source: Preaching Hanar)
- The Migrant Fleet holds roughly 17 million quarians under severe resource scarcity; it is illegal for parents to have more than one child unless the population is in decline, and every quarian must contribute to the Fleet's survival (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 4) — Mass Effect 3 and Rannoch).
- The Migrant Fleet was nominally under martial law but functioned democratically: each ship had an elected civilian council, ship representatives sat on the Conclave, and the five-member Admiralty Board could override the Conclave only by resigning or facing arrest afterward, done only four times in three centuries. (source: Migrant Fleet)
- The one absolute rule of a Pilgrimage gift was that it must not be obtained by harming another; on joining a new ship a quarian's title changed from "nar" (child of) their birth ship to "vas" (crew of) the ship they had earned. (source: Pilgrimage)
- The Pilgrimage is the quarian rite of passage into adulthood: a young quarian must bring back something of value the Migrant Fleet cannot produce itself, such as raw materials for hull patches; a quarian who has not completed it carries "nar" in their name, changing to "vas" a ship name afterward. (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 1) — Mass Effect 1)
- The Pilgrimage was a quarian coming-of-age rite in which young adults left their birth ship to find something of value to offer a prospective captain, proving they would not burden a ship's resources before being accepted as permanent crew. (source: Pilgrimage)
- The Pilgrimage was both a rite of passage and a safeguard against inbreeding; young quarians received lessons on life outside the Fleet, gifts, and immunodeficiency treatment before leaving, and stealing a ship was a capital crime. (source: Migrant Fleet)
- The Pillars of Strength, a batarian religious artifact recovered from the homeworld Khar'shan during the Reaper War, was returned to a batarian preacher at the Citadel refugee camp to restore his people's faith and morale. (source: Kite's Nest: Pillars of Strength)
- The prevalence of batarian criminal gangs made the batarian language a lingua franca across the governmentless Terminus Systems, whose member species were united only by rejection of Citadel authority and the Citadel Conventions (source: Terminus Systems).
- The Protheans held evolution, the "Cosmic Imperative," as the driving force of the universe, justifying the forced conquest and assimilation of any species that resisted the empire. (source: Prothean)
- The quarian language is Khelish, source of "bosh'tet" and the benediction "Keelah se'lai" ("By the homeworld I hope to see someday"); they formerly practiced ancestor worship via personality-imprint VIs until the geth destroyed the ancestor databanks. (source: Quarian)
- The quarian Pilgrimage is a rite of passage into full adulthood in which a young quarian leaves the Migrant Fleet to bring back something of value; Tali'Zorah undertook hers around 2183 (source: Tali'Zorah nar Rayya).
- The quarian romance film Fleet and Flotilla, about a turian and a quarian who share one night before duty separates them, serves for Tali as an idealized fantasy of duty and love reconciled and a form of escapism from quarian expectations (source: Tali'Zorah vas Normandy — Character Analysis (Part 3) — The Treason Trial and Her Fatal Flaw).
- The rachni regard speech and thought as music, "singing" to soothe their young; they call death "the great silence" and their homeworld the "singing planet," and communicate via pheromones and an organic quantum-entanglement link rather than telepathy. (source: Rachni)
- The Salarian Union was a matrilineal government structured like medieval European nobility, made of fiefdoms, baronies, duchies, planets, and marches each ruled by a single Dalatrass, with power brokered through dynastic interbreeding. (source: Salarian)
- The Silversun Strip's Castle Arcade and Silver Coast Casino offered interactive games including Shattered Eezo, the Claw Game, Relay Defense, and a nonfunctional Towers of Hanoi machine, alongside casino staples Quasar, roulette and varren racing; a vorcha ran an unwinnable Three-Card Monte hustle outside the Armax Arsenal Arena. (source: Silversun Strip)
- The Turian Hierarchy was a hierarchical meritocracy with 27 citizenship tiers, topped by Primarchs who each ruled a colonization cluster and voted on national matters; demotion stigmatized those who over-promoted, not the demoted individual (source: Turian).
- The turian term "bare-faced" — meaning beguiling or not to be trusted, and slang for a politician — dates to colonists adopting facial markings during the Unification Wars to distinguish themselves from other colonies (source: The Turian Hierarchy Before the Citadel — Early History).
- The volus revered the Book of Plenix, an artifact calling for charity and forgiveness of debts should the volus be drawn into war (source: Volus).
- Thessia had a distinctive fashion sense with patrons even among non-asari, and native plants such as the honey-scented irssal blossom, whose seeds Milky Way colonists carried toward Andromeda (source: Thessia)
- Turian religion held that groups and places have "spirits" (e.g. Temerarus) that transcend the individual and inspire the living without intervening; turians enjoy full freedom of religion, and an older faith worshipped titans at Temple Palaven, tended by the Valluvian Priests until the temple was sealed after spaceflight (source: Turian).
- Turian society is a hierarchical meritocracy of 27 citizenship tiers topped by Primarchs, prizing personal accountability and honor, with the military serving as an all-encompassing public-works institution. Turian religion holds that groups and places have inspirational "spirits." Turians wear colony facial markings, and "barefaced" is slang for the untrustworthy and for politicians; a cultural bias against biotic soldiers dates to the Unification War. (source: Codex/Aliens: Council Races)
- Turian society was highly regimented around a strong ethic of public service and self-sacrifice: every citizen aged 15 to 30 served the state, and turians equated the self with the group and prized personal accountability, with lying about one's own actions being the worst sin (source: Turian).
- Volus are an ammonia-breathing turian client race whose cultural contribution to the galaxy is financial: they authored the Unified Banking Act. (source: Codex/Aliens: Non-Council Races)
- Volus culture was dominated by trade of land, resources, and even tribe members; Irune had abolished warfare as an institution of state, settling disputes through social castigation, bargaining, or economic sanctions (source: Volus).
- Volus had two given names but no family names, since claiming to own a person was offensive, and referred to other races by homeworld (e.g. "Earth-clan"), calling quarians "Migrant-clan" or "clanless"; they treated the question of volus gender as intrusive and irrelevant (source: Volus).
- Volus religion was polytheistic, with deities such as Cherk Sab (luck) and Zada Ban (punishment) having planets named for them, and a language dialect called Iperian Volus; their government, the Vol Protectorate, was a tax-paying, troop-providing client state of the Turian Hierarchy (source: Volus).
- Vorcha society was built around combat, which they used individually and in groups as their default form of communication, making them primarily non-verbal; they lived in communal clans that split off younger members when they grew too large (source: Vorcha).
- Young quarians undertake a Pilgrimage to bring something of value back to the Migrant Fleet before joining a new ship as adults; names carry "nar" (child of, birth ship) or "vas" (crew of, adult ship) plus the vessel name. (source: Quarian)
- Aria T'Loak personally managed Afterlife's entertainment calendar, spacing out acts like the Sinyah Sisters and the Bekke Burlesque, restocking the club's fight pit with krogan and human fighters instead of vorcha, booking hanar jugglers "for a laugh," and weighing a "VIP Only" casino-ship night where invited guests "who need killing" would be left to kill each other. (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Aria T'Loak)
- Jack, writing poetry under the pen name "Jacqueline Nought," submitted a piece to the publication Galactic Poetry Monthly that was rejected; it ended "I mark myself / Black and jagged / To cover the scars / That make me a monster / A warning / This is not a place of honor / No esteemed dead are buried here." (source: Shadow Broker Dossiers/Subject Zero (Jack))
- Batarian society ran on a purchasable social caste system reinforced by institutionalized slavery, and batarians believed species with fewer than their own four eyes were less intelligent, reading social rank through body language. (source: Batarian)
- Ashley Williams was devoutly religious and an admirer of the poet Alfred, Lord Tennyson, quoting his verse even as she distrusted aliens and the Citadel Council on principle without being outright xenophobic. (source: Ashley Williams)
- Asari mythology credited Athame's guides with teaching the earliest asari civilization: Janiri taught agriculture and the seasons and Lucen taught the stars, while Athame herself taught mathematics and medicine; the common asari oath "by the goddess" invokes her, and the Temple of Athame on Thessia preserved relics including her sword and shield and fragments of the Athame Codex describing this teaching. (source: Athame Doctrine)
- Illium Fashion Week 2185 was consumed by controversy when an editor's racist remarks about a designer's quarian-inspired collection prompted the designer to boycott the event's Luminary Gala. (source: Cerberus Daily News - August 2010)
- Human holidays persisted into the galactic era: Christmas was still celebrated in 2185, alongside secular institutions like a Citadel-relocated New York Times Square New Year's Eve ball (stolen by the con man Rolan Quarn, who left a signed note) and a widely followed record-length galactic chess game. (source: Cerberus Daily News - December 2010)
- Identified Ardat-Yakshi asari were given an ultimatum of lifelong monastery isolation or execution, a policy the asari enforced partly to protect society and partly because they viewed the affliction as an embarrassment to hide from non-asari; monastery residents produced goods like asari honey marinade and honey mead for the galactic market and some received supervised visits to Thessia. (source: Ardat-Yakshi)
- Matriarch Aethyta held unconventional views for an asari elder, advocating that the asari become more militant in galactic affairs, that young asari improve society during their maiden years rather than become strippers or mercenaries, and that the asari build new mass relays; the last proposal was ridiculed and drove her to leave Thessia for Illium. (source: Aethyta)
- N7 was traditionally the only school rank permitted on Alliance uniforms, and there was no stigma attached to not earning an N-designation; only trainees who survived actual combat "in an admirable and effective fashion" during the N6 phase of Interplanetary Combatives Training received the N7 designation. (source: Alliance News Network Blogs)
- Benezia's dying words claimed she did not see the light promised by asari religion; earlier, mating with another asari to conceive Liara T'Soni had been considered a faux pas in asari society, though raising a child alone was not uncommon among asari. (source: Benezia)
- Advocacy groups in Citadel space argued a conscious AI deserved the same rights as organics and that the term "artificial" was institutionalized racism, preferring "synthetic"; AI research remained tightly restricted, with only four Citadel-licensed corporations (including Synthetic Insights, Ltd.) permitted to develop AI by 2183, and C-Sec once gunned down three unarmed mechs housing the Citadel's last AIs after their legal appeal for personhood went unheard. (source: Artificial Intelligence)
- Attitudes toward biotics varied sharply by society: human biotics faced widespread public prejudice and distrust (partly from myths that they could read or control minds), with religious and ideological groups opposed to biotic modification and occasional mob attacks on biotics who misused their power, even as the Alliance military welcomed biotics with heavy enlistment incentives, front-line assignments, and an enlarged 4,500-kilocalorie daily ration; turian biotics were uncommon and distrusted enough to be segregated into dedicated Cabal units, salarian biotics were rare and prized, and krogan biotics were few but so powerful that before the genophage biotic Battlemasters were feared and swiftly promoted through military ranks. (source: Biotics)
- Chora's Den, a Wards gentlemen's club known for its asari dancers, was rated "livelier but deadlier" than its rival Flux; Wards gossip held that the crime lord Fist, who ran Chora's Den, had corpses buried under Flux's stage. (source: Chora's Den)
- Turian dueling customs blended lethal formality with media taboo: General Partinax accepted separatist leader Kihilix Tanus's challenge to a duel, favoring a sword called a mexta ("harder to cheat in swordplay than with firearms") before the two settled on a "barrier room" pistol duel monitored by both sides' witnesses; per custom the recorded footage was not meant for public release, yet a bootleg leaked and out-drew even human downloads despite the taboo against showing turian war dead. (source: Cerberus Daily News - June 2010)
- Turian culture treated ritual self-execution as an accepted response to shame: after being blamed for the deaths of human journalist Grant Keyes and his camerawoman on Taetrus, Sergeant Sanus Iheras died of an apparently self-inflicted gunshot, a practice the reporting described as common after "great transgressions" to expiate a family or unit's dishonor and to close any resulting investigation. (source: Cerberus Daily News - June 2010)
- After the Vallum Blast on Taetrus, cross-species mourning rituals marked turian grief as a galactic event: Earth's EU prime minister declared "the whole galaxy bleeds blue," and Thessia's Matriarch Tiala ordered the Serrice Republican Guard to play the turian anthem "Stars Behind Me" during changing of the guard, an honor last performed after the turians' Krogan Rebellions defeat at Uchalda. (source: Cerberus Daily News - May 2010)
- The blockbuster simulstim "Nekyia Corridor" took its title from an asari mourning tradition, "nekyia," for seeking guidance from the dead; the film's plot centered on a pilot's FTL-suicide crash into a city, a plot point directors chose not to cut even after the real Vallum Blast, framing the sim instead as a meditation on death. (source: Cerberus Daily News - April/May/July 2010)
- St. Valentine's Day, a human holiday of romantic gift-giving, was reported spreading among the asari and volus by 2185, embraced for its "unusual mix of commerce and reproduction." (source: Cerberus Daily News - February 2010)
- The salarian New Year, Betau, marked the end of Sur'Kesh's southern winter and was traditionally a time for repaying debts and petitioning favors; Alliance Prime Minister Amul Shastri observed it with Dalatrass Narra by exchanging a cloned-lineage Arabian horse for a vintage aircar. (source: Cerberus Daily News - July 2010)
- Thessia hosted the annual, week-long "One Future Summit," an ecological-conservation convention drawing hundreds of thousands of attendees to debate terraforming, while a radical fringe ran a parallel underground "One Chance Summit" where ecoterrorism tactics were openly discussed. (source: Cerberus Daily News - February 2010)
- Cultural-heritage disputes played out between salarians and the University of Serrice over artifacts an asari expedition removed from the salarian colony of Mannovai four centuries earlier during a batarian attack; the university framed its custody as preservation and tribute to the salarians who died smuggling the pieces out, while Mannovai's parliament called it "flippant cultural plundering." (source: Cerberus Daily News - November 2010)
- A "Living History" lecture series on Gataly reopened Krogan Rebellions-era wounds when krogan Warlord Ravanor Tusk demanded asari reparations for krogan forced labor, prompting asari Matriarch Adora Ellja to deny it happened; historian Koni Sollia later conceded a small number of asari commandos had enslaved fewer than a hundred krogan (punished at the time), while pointedly noting no reparations had ever been raised for the tens of thousands of asari and others krogan forces enslaved during the same war. (source: Cerberus Daily News - October 2010)
- A cross-cultural intellectual-property dispute arose when volus inventor Ora Thubb accused the Turian Hierarchy of appropriating his Eupulmos respiratory device without payment; turian officials dismissed volus-style patent claims as against turian "meritocracy," reflecting a broader pattern of turians disregarding client-race patent law, and Thubb was later found murdered. (source: Cerberus Daily News - July 2010)
