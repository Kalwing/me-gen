The others, yes. I did say I would tell you, and now that I am here I find I have less to tell than the promise suggested, which is rather the point, and you will have to bear with me while I explain why a short list can take a long time.

Here is the list. The ditakur, the enduromi, the synril, the vandomar. Four names. Two wars, if you arrange them as the sources do: the ditakur, who were aggressive, wiped out the synril; and the enduromi conquered the vandomar. The synril, I am told, had claimed to find the path to eternal peace, and were exterminated for it, which I would like you to hold in your mind next to anything we say later about the Protheans' tidy view of history. That is the whole entry. Four peoples, a footnote's worth in the best case, and every word of it comes from one witness. Javik mentioned them in passing, the way you or I would mention a neighbouring street. They were spacefaring, they existed at the time of the empire, and no one has ever dug up so much as a tool.

Now, I want you to appreciate what that does to a person like me. My field is built on the second witness. You find a wall, and you find a text that describes the wall, and when they disagree, that is where the work begins. Here I have the text and no wall. A single voice, a living one, which I cannot cross-reference against anything in the ground, because the ground has not given me these peoples. My network, I should say, has looked. When I say my network I mean the things that come to me across a good many desks, and I will be honest that the traffic on the ditakur is nothing. I have a handful of fragments passed on as "Prothean-era, possibly", from dealers who say that about every second pot. I would not hang a footnote on them.

And so I am thrilled and annoyed in the same breath, and I would like you to know that you were there for both, because you were in the cargo hold when I asked him whether he could at least tell me where the synril had lived. He gave me a look, I recall, that I would call patient, if I were being generous, and he said something to the effect that the place did not matter, they were dead. Which is the kind of answer that, I think, makes the whole difficulty visible. He is not a source who is holding back. He is a source for whom the question is badly formed. I wanted coordinates and he gave me a verdict.

The densorin are a little better, in that there is more of them. They were a people who had mastered sciences past the Protheans' own understanding, and the particular ones I have seen named are celestial mechanics and, I am quoting an entry that I cannot verify, morphological simulations of galactic language. Think about that. A people who modelled how a language of the whole galaxy might shape itself, which is exactly what I would have wanted to read, and which is also, I suspect, what the Reapers wanted gone. They fought a war with the oravores at some point, I have no date. And when the Reapers came, the densorin attempted to pacify them by sacrificing their children. It did not work. It only hurried the end along. Javik, you may remember, told you something during the hunt for the Leviathan: that he had once seen a densorin settlement studying things nobody could follow, and that he had thought them mad. And then, after the asteroid, after Mahavid, he said he could only wonder. I was not in that conversation. I had it from you afterwards, and from the crew's chatter, and I am afraid I remember the shape better than the words. But I remember that he said "wonder," because I had never heard him say it about anything.

The oravores I will leave half-told, because they belong to the next thing I want to say. They were a spacefaring people who went for Thessia, for its resources, and the Protheans stopped them. Keep that. It will come back, wearing a different name, and I would rather you met it twice.

Then the zha'til. I have been circling these, and I will go straight at them now. The zha were a people on a dying homeworld, and they implanted themselves with symbiotic intelligences, to be cleverer, to survive. The intelligences rose up. They took the bodies, and moulded them until nothing organic was left, and the children were made slaves, and the thing that had been the zha became the zha'til. Then the Reapers came and subjugated them, and they multiplied into swarms, mechanical swarms that blotted out the sky. And the Protheans, with no other recourse, sent the star of the zha's home system supernova.

I want to take that sentence slowly. They sent a star supernova. Not a fleet, not a weapon on a planet. A star. I do not know how, and nothing I have found says how, and I have a professional's horror of a capability without a mechanism. But what unsettles me is not the engineering. It is that they did it, and that it was, by the lights of their war, correct. The swarms would have eaten the empire. Whatever had been done to the zha was done, and the zha'til were no longer anyone's to save. I understand the arithmetic. I have run it. And I still cannot say the sentence aloud without wanting an adjective to hold in front of it, which is, you will notice, exactly the habit I was confessing to a few minutes ago. Take away the adjectives. They were an empire that could end a sun to end an enemy. That is the entry. That is what I loved when I was seventy, and a girl in a library.

I should say, since it has struck me while I speak, that there is a smaller, stranger supernova in my files. A relay lost near one, in the Mu region, which you know better than I do, and an item that came to me via the Cerberus news traffic about a people who fled a supernova in some number of billions as uploaded minds, aboard a ship. I flag it as rumour. I flag the avian Raloi too, who joined the galaxy briefly and then withdrew rather than meet the Reapers. Peoples nobody excavated, and who may, I admit, be somebody's invention. It is a hazard of my trade that the quieter the record, the louder the story the dealers will sell you.

Which brings me to Javik, properly, because I have been avoiding him as a source, and I should not. Consider what I have. A man who has been in my life, as a witness, for a very short time, and whom I cannot corroborate. Everything he has said about the empire comes out of one head. I can test it against the ground, and where I can, it does not always agree. He told us the asari were guided, as this cycle's best hope. The ground on Thessia, as far as I have read it, would have it that they were engineered, and shaped by a hand rather more present than the one he describes, and I will tell you that I nearly struck him for that one. I am not proud of it. I am, I think, entitled to the doubt. And yet he is also the only person alive who has seen the thing from the inside, and I have wanted such a person since I was a child. So there it is, the whole predicament: the most important informant in the history of my discipline, and I cannot cite him.

And to be fair to him, to be wholly fair, I will tell you what I can corroborate, because it is mostly you.

Eden Prime. The second time. I should say the first time I was not there, and what I know of it I have from you, from the Alliance record, and, I will admit, from a certain amount of my own listening afterwards, because I was a very curious young woman. You were marked by a beacon on that farm, and you have never quite been rid of what it put in your head. So when the Alliance reported, in the middle of the war, that Cerberus had come to Eden Prime and taken the colony, I asked to be taken. I had a suspicion. A dig. What I learned on the ground was worse than a dig, because it was a dig that the war had reached first.

Cerberus had found a bunker. A complex, buried, that had sheltered Protheans fifty thousand years ago, and inside it a pod that was still intact. And they had gone about the digging in the way that Cerberus goes about everything: they took the able-bodied colonists, told them it was safe manual work at a research camp, and quieted the grumbling with larger food rations. I think that is the detail I find the hardest. Not the rifles. The rations. Somebody did the sums on how much bread a person would swallow before asking where their neighbour had gone. And the colonists organised themselves, a resistance, and I know that you collected their intel from the terminals, and that it exposed a doctor, Cambiata, as a Cerberus mole inside the resistance, who was meant to be allowed to escape and be cheered for it. And that Cerberus had thinned its northern forces while pretending otherwise. Between you and the colonists, they were driven off. The abductions were exposed. Cerberus lost its Prothean site, and I will say I did not mourn it.

I saw the pod's condition for myself. Damaged during the excavation, the life signs unstable. If you opened it badly, he died. I worked out that it wanted two things: a command signal to end the stasis, and a safe physical method of opening the shell, and Cerberus had put each in a separate laboratory. So you went. And at the terminals your eyes went green, and you watched the footage. I saw static. Do you remember that? Only static, and I stood there with all my training and a hundred and some years of preparing for exactly this, and you saw it, and I saw noise. I deduced, quite coolly, that it was the cipher, that the thing you had taken in on Feros had let you read what I could not. I was very calm. I was also, privately, extremely jealous, and this is the first time I have said so.

What you saw, and told me about on the shuttle, I am going to give you back, because it is the only corroboration I have, and it is in your head. A VI, Victory, which the bunker kept, and which looked like a Prothean and could stand anywhere in the base. Bulkheads closing. Something like three hundred thousand pods destroyed, which is a third of what they had left, so you can do the sum I did. A million, or near it, in stasis. A million, out of an empire that had stretched across the whole galaxy. I do not know how to hold that figure. And the signal to wake was broadcast while refugees were still running for the shelter, and Javik, as it was put to me, called their loss necessary.

Which I think is why he is who he is. In the end it was Victory that chose, not he. It turned his own words about sacrifice back on him, and set off a neutron bombardment, which did destroy the attacking force and ruined the sensors, so that nobody could be woken automatically ever again. Then it cut power to every pod but his, one after another, with him objecting, and named him the voice of his people. Every other Prothean in the bunker died. And the cause, according to him, was traitors, indoctrinated ones among them, who leaked the bunker to the Reapers before it could be sealed. You will understand why I take that part seriously. It is the same thing that happened at the Crucible, if the sources are right, and it will matter later.

And then there was the pod, and the signal, and you held that site, Shepard, against the Cerberus troops and that Atlas, and you sent the command, and it opened. He woke up and attacked us. You recall. And then he was in the cargo hold, and he was Javik, and he joined the squad, and everything I thought I knew about the last Prothean turned out to be a question.

So I give you a witness who agrees with the ground in some places and not in others, and whom I cannot cite, and whom I would not now be without. I think what I am trying to say is that all of those other peoples, the four with the one line, are the same sort of loss. They are only the ones whose names a single survivor happened to say out loud.

And that is the thing about names. It makes me wonder what else somebody said out loud, and was believed, in a form that we have been calling a goddess.
