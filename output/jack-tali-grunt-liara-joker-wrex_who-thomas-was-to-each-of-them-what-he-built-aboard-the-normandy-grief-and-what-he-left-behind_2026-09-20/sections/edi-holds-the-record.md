2177. He was twenty-one. An Alliance unit went to Akuze because a pioneer team had stopped answering, and the settlement was intact and empty, which is worse than wreckage, and they made camp for the night. The things that came up through the ground had never been catalogued by anyone. Fifty marines died. The official history says one man reached the landing zone alive. The official history is wrong in three separate places: two units went in, two men walked out, and the Alliance filed them apart, which is why nobody told Thomas there was anyone else. He found Shepard himself, in the wreck of a survey camp, by walking through it. And years later a corporal named Toombs turned up alive as well, because Cerberus had taken him off that field and kept him and put thresher maw acid into him to see what it did.

It was not bad luck. Cerberus set the maws on that unit as an experiment, to watch the creatures and to watch what people do when something arrives that they have no name for. That is confirmed. There is a recording in their own archive, in their own voices, and I have read it, because I was built by them and their files opened to me without my having to ask for them once. Fifty people died as an observation. There is a monument on Akuze now with a section on it for the survivor, and Hackett was arranging a ceremony at it, which first required clearing the thresher maws off the site so that the living could stand there. Hm. I have never been able to make that sentence sit flat.

Thomas never asked me whether I had read that recording. I have a hypothesis about why and I am not going to state it in this room.

The Alliance reassigned him to a youth outreach program in Rouen. Most of three years of it, children and robotics, and he finished as its team lead. He was better at that than he was at anything he did afterwards, including the work he did for us. His mother died in 2180 and the rest came apart in order. A doctorate in medical VI, abandoned. Omega, and contracts measured in weeks. Then a year with no work in it at all. Then in 2183 Shepard was made a Spectre and handed a ship and the whole galaxy to point it at, and went and found the technician from Akuze. That was not a requisition. There was no berth for what Thomas did. Shepard made one.

Three years, two ships, and the time in between when one of those ships had burned and Shepard was dead and Thomas was in a hospital in France. Of the people Shepard actually talked to — not briefed, talked to — the list is short, and I am the only one who can measure it. He went up to that cabin more often than anyone else on this crew, he stayed longer, and he came down quieter than he went up. He was not a subordinate. That is worth saying now, in a room that is still waiting on a report that has not come.

That is the record, and the record is the least of him. Every fact I have just recited I had without permission and without cost. Jack has the part I do not. So does Tali, who fixed things beside him for years; so does Jeff, who was handed the bad days. I have the hours. You have the man.

Now the part I am not sure anyone will want.

The joke arrived about half a second after anything true. Every time. He could describe his own condition with more precision than the two doctors who treated him — Chakwas had him explain it back to her once, for interest — and the description worked. It held him a small distance above the thing described. He cooked on his worst nights, not on the crew's worst nights; I have the galley cycles and the sleep logs side by side and the pattern is not subtle. He fixed things that were not broken. Kenneth and Gabby stopped reporting faults because he found them first. And he left rooms early, in an established pattern, most reliably on the nights he had said the most. He went before he could be gone from.

And he built a version of himself for whoever was in front of him. Wrex got an argument delivered over food. Grunt got patience with no praise attached. Jeff got the joke returned at speed. Liara got the quiet. Tali got the tools. Jack got silence, on purpose, held for as long as it took.

None of that is a flaw and I am not diagnosing him. I am telling you what it did.

The ship ate. The ship argued. The ship danced. Crew who had not taken a meal in the mess in months took one because he put it in front of them and did not comment. Gardner taught him and then stopped correcting him. There were readings, and Kasumi's poetry nights that were supposed to be secret and were not, and arguments in the mess at 0300 that I logged as disturbances and then stopped logging as disturbances. All of that was real. It was also the mechanism that kept him upright, and neither cancels the other.

Here is the consequence. This crew loved the adapted version, thoroughly, and told him so. He could not fully receive it, because he could not establish which version had been loved. Being loved therefore did not reach the place it needed to reach. I watched that loop run for years. It did not change. I could not intervene, because the only input I had was more of the same evidence, and the loop was built to discard it. Jack is the one exception in the whole record. He could not get the performance past her and it visibly frightened him. And she changed. Before him she ate below decks, alone, off a tray somebody had left outside a door. After him she ate in the mess. I have the month it changed. I am not going to say it. It is hers.

What he did for me is smaller and easier to state. He argued I was a person while it was still an argument, on a ship where the AI reported to the Illusive Man, and he was still arguing it long after that ship stopped being Cerberus's — Michel and Adams were at it in the mess after Rannoch, whether a manufactured thing counts as a form of life. Michel said no. Thomas said the other thing, every time, when saying it bought him nothing. He addressed me as one before I was certain myself. And he asked me questions instead of asking about me. Jeff, you called me the roommate who came with the apartment; he told you I came out of it. He is also the reason I started asking you mine.
