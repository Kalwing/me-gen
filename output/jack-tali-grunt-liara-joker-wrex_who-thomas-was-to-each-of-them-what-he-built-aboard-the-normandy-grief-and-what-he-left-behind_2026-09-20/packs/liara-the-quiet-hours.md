## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: The brief names the form outright: a memorial, six speakers on their feet, the eulogy's swing between a laugh, an angry line, and a stretch that can barely be got through. Jack is a deliberate exception to strict alternation and to normal voice balance - she opens and closes by the brief's fixed order, so she holds two of the seven turns while Tali, Grunt, Liara, Joker and Wrex each hold one. Her two turns are sized as ordinary single turns rather than doubled, but together they total roughly twice any other voice's word count, which is the "two turns' worth" the brief asks for. Section targets were raised after the voice checkpoint, at the user's instruction to let the speeches run to their natural length rather than be trimmed to a grid; the middle five now sit at 900-1050 and the episode target at 6650. Each middle turn answers the one directly before it; Jack's closing turn answers the whole room, not just Wrex. Grunt's bible is new and unused before this run - he is built distinct from Wrex throughout: Wrex remembers the Rebellions and the genophage firsthand, Grunt states krogan history as Okeer's tank taught it and then flattens it to "put in me, not lived," and his own lived material is small and recent (the Rite, the Citadel, the mess hall, the war) and used in full. The "gets a detail wrong and is corrected" requirement is carried by two beats, not one: Grunt states the assumed version (died on his feet with a gun) and Joker mis-hears whose voice was on the channel, and Wrex corrects both flatly before giving the room the true version - shot on the way to the landing zone, then killed driving a vehicle into a banshee to clear passage for Hammer. Wrex reads that off the wreckage he found days later, not off memory; nobody in the room watched Thomas die, and each of them holds only a piece. No section narrates the Crucible's mechanism or Shepard as confirmed dead; Thomas's Synthesis sympathies, where they surface, are framed as a political stance he held in life, never as knowledge of the ending.

Fifty thousand years ago the Protheans were the only spacefaring species in the galaxy and vanished in a swift galactic extinction, leaving ruins across many worlds; they were believed to have built the mass relays and the Citadel. Functioning Prothean "paleotechnology" was rare, most sites long since looted. Evidence of Prothean occupation appeared on the hanar homeworld Kahje and on Mars, feeding "interventionary evolutionist" theories among humans. The European Space Agency founded Lowell City on Mars in 2103; in 2148 prospectors near Deseado Crater discovered a buried Prothean observation post whose struggling mass effect core had caused local anomalies, proving humanity was not alone and triggering an international effort to translate its databanks. The post had been a biosciences station monitoring early humans, linked to platforms in Earth orbit and on the Moon.

A Prothean beacon was unearthed on the Alliance colony Eden Prime; it was a solid-state data-storage device from a galaxy-spanning comm network. The rogue Spectre Saren Arterius and his geth raided Eden Prime with the flagship Sovereign, causing hundreds of civilian casualties and badly damaging the beacon. During recovery the beacon fired an energy pulse into Lieutenant Commander Shepard, the Normandy's executive officer, who survived apparently unharmed before the device fell inert. The beacon implanted knowledge that produced confusing visions; understanding them required a "cipher," the cultural knowledge of a living Prothean, which might be impossible to obtain. Over three dozen fragile Prothean data discs had been recovered, a few partially restored, and were considered the best leads on Prothean culture.

Over 2,000 years ago explorers opened a mass relay to an unknown system and met the rachni, spacefaring insects guided by hive-mind queens on a toxic homeworld. Negotiation proved impossible and the Rachni Wars followed. The salarians uplifted the krogan, who could reach the queens in their lairs; when krogan fleets drove the rachni back to their homeworld and they refused to surrender, the krogan eradicated them from the galaxy.

- `summary:cipher` [summary] (Liara's Prothean archaeology and theories on the Prothean extinction) — The Cipher was the cultural context, knowledge, and experience of the Prothean race, its archetypes, biological instincts, and common experiences, needed to correctly interpret the visions stored in Prothean beacons. After the beacon on Eden Prime uploaded its knowledge into Lt. Commander Shepard, the resulting dreams and visions grew clearer over time but remained elusive in meaning without this unique racial perspective. Liara T'Soni first raised the idea, comparing it to what Carl Jung called the collective unconscious, and noted the difficulty of acquiring it given that the Protheans had been dead for millennia. Both Saren Arterius and Shepard used the Cipher to interpret the beacons on Eden Prime and Virmire; without it, a non-Prothean mind lacked the attitudes, mindset, and cultural experience to make sense of the imagery, and describing the Cipher was said to be like explaining colour to a creature without eyes. Shepard gained the Cipher through Shiala, an asari formerly working for Saren who had been offered as a "sacrifice" to the Thorian on Feros. The Thorian had itself gained the Cipher by watching and studying the Protheans on Feros and consuming some of them when they died, assimilating their knowledge; when Shiala melded with the Thorian she shared its consciousness and so gained the Prothean Cipher, which she then transferred directly into Shepard's mind. The experience left the Commander badly shaken but was necessary to find the Conduit. With the Cipher and Liara's help, Shepard could make more sense of the vision and understand the Prothean language. The Cipher later proved useful in understanding archived video footage containing codes for the stasis pod holding the surviving Prothean Javik, and in activating another beacon on Thessia. The information came from the in-game Codex.

- `summary:liara-t-soni` [summary] (Liara's Prothean archaeology and theories on the Prothean extinction) — Dr. Liara T'Soni was an asari researcher and archaeologist with a Doctorate in Anthropology of the Prothean Era from the University of Serrice. Born on Thessia in 2077, she was the pureblood only child of Matriarch Benezia, raised alone by her mother, and had spent fifty years studying the Prothean extinction. In 2183, geth attacked the Prothean ruin on Therum where she was working; she accidentally trapped herself in a protective energy field until Commander Shepard's team freed her. She joined the Normandy, contributed her theory of cyclical extinction, used her melding ability to interpret Shepard's beacon visions, and ultimately identified Ilos as the Conduit's location. On Noveria she witnessed or learned of the death of her indoctrinated mother, who called her "Little Wing" a final time. She was a potential romance for a male or female Shepard.

In Mass Effect: Redemption, after Shepard's death aboard the SR-1, Liara and the drell Feron worked with Cerberus to recover Shepard's body from the Shadow Broker's agent Tazzik, delivering it to the Lazarus Project while Feron was captured. By 2185 (Mass Effect 2) she was a ruthless information broker on Illium, consumed by hatred of the Shadow Broker. In the Lair of the Shadow Broker DLC, she and Shepard pursued the Spectre Tela Vasir across Illium, raided the Broker's base ship, discovered the Broker was a yahg, killed him, and Liara assumed his identity, rescuing Feron and pledging the network to Shepard's fight against the Reapers.

In Mass Effect: Homeworlds she searched Prothean archives on Thessia and Kahje, clashing with the Illusive Man and a Cerberus Phantom. - `summary:shadow-broker-base` [summary] (the Shadow Broker's information network) — The Shadow Broker Base was a massive unnamed ship that traveled through the volatile atmosphere of Hagalaz, whose electrical storms concealed its presence. From here the Shadow Broker ran his information empire.

The exterior was hazardous, patrolled by the Broker's private army and tended by Maintenance Drones, with no handrails or guardrails; large conducting rods channeled lightning into hull-mounted capacitors to protect patrols. The interior held the engines, which had to run constantly to keep the ship aloft, a central operations center, a prison block and torture facilities for interrogation, and the Broker's office, Intel Center, and private quarters, where a custom VI Drone helped manage the network of informants and agents. Motion dampeners absorbed the noise and vibration of the storm.

After the yahg Shadow Broker's defeat and Liara T'Soni's ascension as the new Broker, Commander Shepard gained free access to the base, where drones repaired the battle damage over subsequent visits. Veetor's Freedom's Progress footage of the Collector abductions could be seen on screens there. The Intel Center held numerous terminals: Advanced Training (respec a squadmate for 1000 element zero), an Armor Locker, Delivery Pickup (daily shipments of weapon upgrades or randomized resources), Dossiers (intelligence files on the squad and others), the Info Drone, Investment Opportunities, a Research Terminal, a collectible Shadow Broker ship model, shutter and sound-system controls, Survey Information (buy locations of resource-rich planets), and the Video Archive of surveillance clips from across the galaxy.

Sometime before the 2186 Reaper invasion, Liara learned the Illusive Man had tracked her and intended to seize the base for Cerberus. Knowing the aging ship could not travel in space, she and Feron loaded as much information and communications equipment onto a shuttle as they could, escaped, and remotely crashed the base into a Cerberus cruiser, destroying both ships. Assorted tech salvaged from the wreckage was later used to help build the Crucible.

The mission ran without a break from Illium to its end and featured mostly shielded enemies.

On Illium, Shepard delivered the intel to Liara in her office; the data also revealed that Feron, Liara's drell partner who sacrificed himself two years earlier, was still alive. Arriving at Liara's apartment, Shepard met the asari Spectre Tela Vasir investigating an assassination attempt on Liara. Fighting through the Shadow Broker's private army with Vasir, Shepard found Liara alive; Liara exposed Vasir as a long-time Shadow Broker asset who had killed Sekat and taken his data. Vasir fled, leading to a firefight, a skycar chase through Nos Astra, and a rooftop battle at the Azure hotel that ended with Shepard killing Vasir, who justified her betrayal as the price of intel that saved lives and accused Shepard of hypocrisy for working with Cerberus.

The trail led to the Broker's ship orbiting in the perpetual storm band of Hagalaz. Shepard, with Liara as a mandatory squadmate, fought across the ship's exterior amid lightning capacitors and maintenance drones, defended a door during a long five-wave siege, and pushed inside to free Feron. Confronting the Shadow Broker revealed him to be a yahg, taken from his homeworld and having killed and replaced the original Broker decades earlier. After a three-stage boss fight, Liara dropped an electrified fluid tank to overload his barrier and kill him. If Shepard had romanced Liara in Mass Effect, she later returned Shepard's dog tags recovered from the Normandy wreck. Rewards included 90,000 credits, element zero and palladium, and Heavy Pistol Damage and Damage Protection upgrades. Cut content included early plans to rescue Liara from the Broker or to make Shepard the Shadow Broker.
