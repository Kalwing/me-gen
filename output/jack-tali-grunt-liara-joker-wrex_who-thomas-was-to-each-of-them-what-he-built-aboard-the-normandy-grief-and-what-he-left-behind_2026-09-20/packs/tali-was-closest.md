## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: The brief names the form outright: a memorial, six speakers on their feet, the eulogy's swing between a laugh, an angry line, and a stretch that can barely be got through. Jack is a deliberate exception to strict alternation and to normal voice balance - she opens and closes by the brief's fixed order, so she holds two of the seven turns while Tali, Grunt, Liara, Joker and Wrex each hold one. Her two turns are sized as ordinary single turns rather than doubled, but together they total roughly twice any other voice's word count, which is the "two turns' worth" the brief asks for. Section targets were raised after the voice checkpoint, at the user's instruction to let the speeches run to their natural length rather than be trimmed to a grid; the middle five now sit at 900-1050 and the episode target at 6650. Each middle turn answers the one directly before it; Jack's closing turn answers the whole room, not just Wrex. Grunt's bible is new and unused before this run - he is built distinct from Wrex throughout: Wrex remembers the Rebellions and the genophage firsthand, Grunt states krogan history as Okeer's tank taught it and then flattens it to "put in me, not lived," and his own lived material is small and recent (the Rite, the Citadel, the mess hall, the war) and used in full. The "gets a detail wrong and is corrected" requirement is carried by two beats, not one: Grunt states the assumed version (died on his feet with a gun) and Joker mis-hears whose voice was on the channel, and Wrex corrects both flatly before giving the room the true version - shot on the way to the landing zone, then killed driving a vehicle into a banshee to clear passage for Hammer. Wrex reads that off the wreckage he found days later, not off memory; nobody in the room watched Thomas die, and each of them holds only a piece. No section narrates the Crucible's mechanism or Shepard as confirmed dead; Thomas's Synthesis sympathies, where they surface, are framed as a political stance he held in life, never as knowledge of the ending.

The Reapers had constructed a towering conduit—a massive structure built from organic and metallic components—in the war-torn city. This conduit, built according to mass relay principles but modified by Reaper technology, served as a direct transport mechanism connecting the ground of Earth to the Citadel, which had been mysteriously repositioned above the planet. The conduit functioned unlike standard mass relays: it emitted a constant, brilliant beam of blue light into the sky, continuously transporting matter (primarily the dead and dismembered remains of slaughtered populations) directly from Earth's surface to the Citadel. The structure had no visible element zero core and, critically, allowed living organic matter to survive transit—arriving at ballistic but survivable speeds—rather than disintegrating as with conventional relays. The Reapers appeared to have multiple destination termini on the Citadel, as different individuals arrived at separate points on the station. The assault ran in two ground stages. First Shepard's shuttle landed to knock out a Reaper anti-aircraft "Hades Cannon" with M-920 Cain rounds so the Hammer ground force could land; the team then regrouped at a Forward Operating Base where Shepard held final words with surviving allies before Anderson gave the battle plan. The second stage pushed through occupied London to a missile battery, where Thanix missiles coordinated with EDI on the Normandy destroyed a Reaper Destroyer blocking the road to the conduit. Anderson then arrived by APC for the final charge. The galactic assault on the conduit was devastating. Allied forces from across the galaxy—humans, turians, asari, salarians, quarians, krogan, and others— launched a coordinated ground assault to penetrate the structure and secure access to the Citadel. However, Harbinger, the Reaper flagship and commander of the local Reaper forces, personally defended the conduit with overwhelming firepower, killing or maiming most of the allied assault force. The majority of the attacking soldiers were annihilated, their bodies adding to the biomass flowing through the conduit itself. Despite the horrific casualties, Commander Shepard and Admiral Anderson managed to fight their way through the structure and reached its heart. Anderson, wounded but resolute, helped push Shepard toward the conduit's exit point. Shepard survived the ascent through the conduit and materialized on the Citadel, while Anderson arrived at a slightly different point on the station. Upon reaching the Citadel, Shepard gained access to the Crucible's control mechanisms and the Catalyst—the artificial intelligence that had guided the Reaper cycle for countless eons. At this critical juncture, Shepard was confronted with a series of final choices that would determine the fate of the galaxy: to Destroy all synthetic life (eliminating the Reapers and the geth but also killing EDI), to Control the Reapers (uploading Shepard's consciousness to command them), to achieve Synthesis (merging organic and synthetic life), or to Refuse the Catalyst's offer (allowing the cycle to continue unchanged). The consequences of this choice—affecting the survival of the geth, EDI, the Reapers themselves, and organic civilization—would echo across the galaxy for generations.
