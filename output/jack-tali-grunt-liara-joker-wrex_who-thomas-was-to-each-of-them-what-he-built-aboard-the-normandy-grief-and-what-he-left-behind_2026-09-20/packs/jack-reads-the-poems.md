## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: The brief names the form outright: a memorial, six speakers on their feet, the eulogy's swing between a laugh, an angry line, and a stretch that can barely be got through. Jack is a deliberate exception to strict alternation and to normal voice balance - she opens and closes by the brief's fixed order, so she holds two of the seven turns while Tali, Grunt, Liara, Joker and Wrex each hold one. Her two turns are sized as ordinary single turns rather than doubled, but together they total roughly twice any other voice's word count, which is the "two turns' worth" the brief asks for. Section targets were raised after the voice checkpoint, at the user's instruction to let the speeches run to their natural length rather than be trimmed to a grid; the middle five now sit at 900-1200 (Joker's raised again after assembly, to carry the two illnesses beat in full) and the episode target at 6950. Each middle turn answers the one directly before it; Jack's closing turn answers the whole room, not just Wrex. Grunt's bible is new and unused before this run - he is built distinct from Wrex throughout: Wrex remembers the Rebellions and the genophage firsthand, Grunt states krogan history as Okeer's tank taught it and then flattens it to "put in me, not lived," and his own lived material is small and recent (the Rite, the Citadel, the mess hall, the war) and used in full. The "gets a detail wrong and is corrected" requirement is carried by two beats, not one: Grunt states the assumed version (died on his feet with a gun) and Joker mis-hears whose voice was on the channel, and Wrex corrects both flatly before giving the room the true version - shot on the way to the landing zone, then killed driving a vehicle into a banshee to clear passage for Hammer. Wrex reads that off the wreckage he found days later, not off memory; nobody in the room watched Thomas die, and each of them holds only a piece. No section narrates the Crucible's mechanism or Shepard as confirmed dead; Thomas's Synthesis sympathies, where they surface, are framed as a political stance he held in life, never as knowledge of the ending.

- she opens it where she stands, having just told the room she would read them alone and on her terms, and does not acknowledge the contradiction
- the fragments are unfinished, in French, addressed to her, and she reads them badly out loud - the actual text, crude and tender and funny in the same breath, and she is furious that they are funny
- she reacts to what is in them rather than summarizing them: the ones that are filthy, the one that is a list of things he wants from a body getting old, the one that makes her a place he would sleep under
- the line about moving from the posture of loving to the much harder one of being loved, which she understands is about him and takes personally anyway
- he never sent these and she has to decide out loud whether that means they were for her or not
- ties back to the empty voice note without repeating it: that one had nothing on it and this has too much, and neither is an answer
- she does not finish reading, and she does not resolve anything - unfinished text, unfinished grief, Shepard still missing, and she sits down mid-thing

Abilities fell into three categories: telekinesis (mass-lowering fields), kinetic fields (mass-raising fields), and spatial distortion (shifting fields that rip apart objects).
