## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: The brief names the form outright: a memorial, six speakers on their feet, the eulogy's swing between a laugh, an angry line, and a stretch that can barely be got through. Jack is a deliberate exception to strict alternation and to normal voice balance - she opens and closes by the brief's fixed order, so she holds two of the seven turns while Tali, Grunt, Liara, Joker and Wrex each hold one. Her two turns are sized as ordinary single turns rather than doubled, but together they total roughly twice any other voice's word count, which is the "two turns' worth" the brief asks for. Section targets were raised after the voice checkpoint, at the user's instruction to let the speeches run to their natural length rather than be trimmed to a grid; the middle five now sit at 900-1050 and the episode target at 6650. Each middle turn answers the one directly before it; Jack's closing turn answers the whole room, not just Wrex. Grunt's bible is new and unused before this run - he is built distinct from Wrex throughout: Wrex remembers the Rebellions and the genophage firsthand, Grunt states krogan history as Okeer's tank taught it and then flattens it to "put in me, not lived," and his own lived material is small and recent (the Rite, the Citadel, the mess hall, the war) and used in full. The "gets a detail wrong and is corrected" requirement is carried by two beats, not one: Grunt states the assumed version (died on his feet with a gun) and Joker mis-hears whose voice was on the channel, and Wrex corrects both flatly before giving the room the true version - shot on the way to the landing zone, then killed driving a vehicle into a banshee to clear passage for Hammer. Wrex reads that off the wreckage he found days later, not off memory; nobody in the room watched Thomas die, and each of them holds only a piece. No section narrates the Crucible's mechanism or Shepard as confirmed dead; Thomas's Synthesis sympathies, where they surface, are framed as a political stance he held in life, never as knowledge of the ending.

## Required facts
- (grunt-rite-of-passage-tuchanka) Some time after being awakened aboard the Normandy, the tank-bred krogan Grunt began suffering violent instability and asked Commander Shepard to take him to Tuchanka so he could earn a place among a true krogan clan. Shepard brought him to the Urdnot camp, where the clan leader agreed that Grunt could attempt the Rite of Passage to join Clan Urdnot. Before the Rite, Gatatog Uvenk, a traditionalist battlemaster and leader of Clan Gatatog, publicly challenged Grunt's legitimacy, denouncing him as a factory-made fake unworthy of clan membership and appealing directly to the Urdnot Shaman, who was annoyed by the interruption. Shepard forced Uvenk to back down by affirming that Shepard's team would stand as Grunt's krantt, either through words or a headbutt; Uvenk withdrew his objection but swore to settle the matter later. Uvenk's attitude depended on who led Clan Urdnot: he openly opposed Urdnot Wrex's reforms as weakness, but was deferential if Urdnot Wreav held power. Grunt undertook the Rite in a ruined arena, striking the Keystone to summon escalating waves of varren and klixen and finally a thresher maw, which he could kill outright or merely survive until the timer expired. Immediately afterward Uvenk arrived by shuttle with several Gatatog Warriors. If Grunt had killed the maw, Uvenk grudgingly acknowledged his strength and offered him in-name-only membership in Clan Gatatog as a trophy to boost Uvenk's own standing; if the maw had only been survived, Uvenk dismissed Grunt as impure. Grunt violently rejected the offer, and the ensuing firefight ended with Uvenk and all his warriors dead. Grunt was formally inducted into Clan Urdnot, gained loyalty to Shepard, and expressed satisfaction at Uvenk's death.

- `summary:thresher-maw` [summary] (Grunt's Rite of Passage and the thresher maw on Tuchanka) — Thresher maws were enormous subterranean carnivores that spent their lives eating or hunting for food, bursting from the ground without warning when disturbed. They reproduced via spores that could lie dormant for millennia and survive deep space and atmospheric re-entry, so maw spores appeared on many worlds, spread by earlier generations of space travelers, possibly predating the asari.

They lived alone in large underground nests, growing over 30 meters above ground with a body nearly twice that size beneath the surface; an adult's body never fully left the ground, with only the head and tentacles erupting to attack. They fed through an unusual photosynthesis using absorbed solar radiation plus minerals and ores consumed from the ground, favoring bodies with little or no atmosphere. Mostly immobile above ground, they moved at high speed while burrowing, absorbed tremendous damage, and attacked by spitting viscous acid, bursting up beneath prey, and smashing targets with their claws while emitting infrasound. Shields were completely ineffective against them.

The Alliance first encountered thresher maws on the colony of Akuze in 2177, where they killed nearly an entire marine rescue force. If Shepard has the Sole Survivor background, Shepard alone survived; Corporal Toombs, met later on Ontarom, claimed Cerberus deliberately caused the Akuze massacre to study the maws and held him captive for experiments, and Cerberus later used the same tactic to lure Admiral Kahoku's team into a nest on Edolus. Maws are found on many uncharted worlds, usually one per planet, including Antibaar, Chohe, Nepheron, Nonuel, Presrop, Xawin, Trebin and Edolus.

In Mass Effect 2, a thresher maw is the final test of Clan Urdnot's Rite of Passage on Tuchanka, lured to the proving grounds by ground-pounding pistons to attack Grunt's krantt. Gatatog Uvenk notes that the last krogan to kill a maw on foot was Urdnot Wrex. In Mass Effect 3, Kalros, a legendary maw called "the mother from which all other thresher maws spawn" and the largest ever seen, roams Tuchanka near the Shroud facility. Shepard activates two maw hammers to summon her against a Reaper Destroyer guarding the Shroud; she coils around it and drags it underground. Her size prompts Javik to remark that maws are now too big to ride, implying they were smaller in Prothean times. The concept was confirmed by art director Derek Watts to draw on Dune's sandworms, and also resembles D&D Ankhegs, the Star Wars Sarlacc, and the real Bobbit worm.
