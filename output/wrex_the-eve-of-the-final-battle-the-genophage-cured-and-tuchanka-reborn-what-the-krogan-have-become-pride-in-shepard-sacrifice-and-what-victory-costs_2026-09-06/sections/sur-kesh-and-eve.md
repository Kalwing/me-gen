Camp doesn't fade before I'm off it — my head's already back on Sur'Kesh. Only time in this whole war I left Tuchanka and got back on that ship. Victus called it — turian primarch, wanted a neutral deck to put salarians, turians and krogan in one room without anybody reaching for a gun first, so he used the Normandy. Smart, for a turian. I sat across from the dalatrass, Linron, and told her no alliance, no krogan blood spent holding Palaven, not one round, until the genophage was cured. She did the thing salarians do where they talk fast and long enough you're supposed to forget what you asked. I don't forget.

So I told the room what I knew. STG had females. Alive. Survivors of Maelon's work on Tuchanka, the ones who didn't die screaming — and instead of destroying them when the Special Tasks Group got shut down, some salarian with a conscience problem — Padok, no, that's not it either — tucked them away on Sur'Kesh and sat on it. You leaned on Victus, Victus leaned on Linron, and she gave up the location rather than lose the whole alliance over it. I've hated salarians a long time, on general principle, but that room taught me something about the specific ones — they'll experiment on you, lose their nerve halfway, then guard the evidence instead of fixing it. Consistent, at least.

We went in hard. Cerberus had the same tip we did and hit the base while we were still finding the elevator down. Assault troopers, centurions, an Atlas walking around like it owned the place. We fought down level by level behind a containment pod they were lifting out ahead of us, and I didn't like one second of it. Salarians keeping krogan females in cells — the sight of it does something to a krogan that isn't tactical. I'd told you I could handle myself. I couldn't, not entirely. Every meter down that shaft I wanted to stop being careful and start being something —

Grunt would've torn that Atlas apart with his bare hands and asked why we bothered bringing guns. Different fight, different day.

They opened the pod at the bottom and there was one alive. One. The rest didn't survive what Maelon did to them, whatever mercy he thought he was offering. She came up out of that pod, grabbed my shotgun straight out of my hands before I'd registered her moving, and put two Cerberus troopers down cold. Told me — "I can handle myself." First fertile krogan female I'd stood in front of in longer than I want to say, and she wasn't a prize somebody was trading, wasn't a warlord's wife kept behind walls the way Tuchanka does it. She talked like she'd already decided what the krogan were going to be, the same argument I'd made to my own father once and gotten an ambush for my trouble. I didn't say much to her that day. Didn't need to.

Mordin named her Eve, once she was in the medbay — human word, human ship, seemed fair to him. Started building the cure off her and off Maelon's data — the data you wouldn't let Mordin burn on Tuchanka, years before any of us knew what it'd be worth. Hackett came through not long after saying the Crucible was finally under construction. I told the room the same thing I'd told Linron. The whole species gets the cure, or the krogan sit this war out and watch the galaxy burn without us. That's how we ended up back there for the finish.
