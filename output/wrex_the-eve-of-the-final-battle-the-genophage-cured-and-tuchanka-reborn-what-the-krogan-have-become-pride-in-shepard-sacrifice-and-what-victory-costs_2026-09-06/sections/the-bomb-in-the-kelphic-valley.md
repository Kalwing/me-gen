Turians buried a bomb under my dirt after the Rebellions, in case we ever stood up straight again. What I didn't tell you is what it cost to find that out — because that part wasn't mine. Wasn't my platoon down there, wasn't my dead. I got it secondhand, from you, standing in a hangar looking like you'd carried something heavy a long way.

Victus's boy had a whole platoon under him. Ninth Platoon. From what you told me, the kid tried to be his father — Adrien Victus fights like a krogan who learned to count. At Taetrus he sat on his hands and let a spy ring and a pack of separatists chew each other down to bone, then walked in and took the ground without burying anybody. I'd have done it faster and worse. His son wanted that reputation. So instead of walking the platoon in loud and eating losses up front like any sane commander plans for, he threaded them through narrow ruins to spare them a head-on fight. Good instinct, wrong ground. Reapers caught them somewhere with no room to swing, and that was the platoon, gone, because the boy tried to be subtle in a fight that wanted to be stupid. I've watched that mistake before, plenty of times, just different uniforms on the corpses. He knew whose fault it was, too. Didn't hide from it. I'll give him that. Never met the kid. Never will. Died well, and that's the kind of—

Cerberus found that bomb before we did. Somebody in a white suit standing over a two-hundred-year-old turian secret with a finger on the switch, and if it had gone off it wasn't just going to kill whoever was standing near it. Every krogan clan chief was already circling like varren over the genophage cure — cure that and suddenly there's something worth being greedy about again. Add a turian bomb going off under a gathering of clans and there's no alliance left to salvage. Wreav would've had every gun on this planet pointed at Palaven, not in a week, inside a day.

You went down there anyway and talked the boy back into finishing his mission instead of sitting in the dirt drowning in what he'd already lost. That part's yours, not mine, and it's the part I don't forget.

What it bought us — near enough seventy-five turian ships' worth of war effort — that didn't burn on each other's fleets instead of Reapers. And a krogan population still breathing in that valley when the sun came up, instead of three hundred dead, near enough, women and children among them. Victus wrote me after. Didn't cry about it, turians don't, but I could tell he meant every word — proud his son died with his platoon's respect, grateful you gave him a shot at earning it back instead of dying ashamed. I wrote something shorter back. Man loses his only son keeping krogan and turians from tearing each other apart two hundred years after his people tried to bury us the first time. Nothing clever to say to that. You just say you're sorry and mean it.
