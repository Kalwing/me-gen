Virmire. Now there's a name that still sits wrong in my mouth. Pretty place, if you like water — a whole planet gone soft and green, waves on rock, warm air. Wasted on Saren. He'd turned it into a fortress, guns dug into cliffs that were supposed to be selling sunburns to tourists. Give organics something nice and somebody always finds a way to put a gun on it.

A salarian named Kirrahe found the place first. STG, the ones who go in quiet and come out quieter, if they come out at all — his team started at eleven, or thirteen, some number like that, and half of them were gone by the time we got there. He tried to call the Council for a proper fleet. Signal got chewed up in transit, so what he got instead was one frigate and us. Krogan don't put much stock in salarians as fighters — sneaks, the lot of them, spend more time thinking than swinging — but I'll say this for Kirrahe. He stood in front of what was left of his men before we went in and gave them a speech to hold the line, and they held it. Three teams, Aegohr, Mannovai, Jaeto, walking straight at Saren's front door so we could go in the back. That's not sneaking. That's throwing yourself on a knife so somebody else's knife lands clean.

Inside, Saren had krogan. Grown, cloned, a whole army of them, and a cure. A real one, working, genophage gone, and I stood there and looked at it and something in me that had been asleep for two thousand years woke right up.

I'll tell you plain what I don't tell most people. I put my gun on you that day. On the beach, after we knew Kirrahe meant to blow the whole facility — cure and all — rather than let Saren keep it. Told him straight, help me out here, Shepard, the lines between friend and foe are getting a little blurry from where I stand. We were tools for the Council once. Wiped out the rachni for them and they neutered us for our trouble. I doubt Saren would've been more generous, but a cure's a cure. Ash was there, weapon up, and there's a world where she pulls that trigger before I do anything and I'm dead in the sand and that's the—

So the base went up. Bomb built out of the Normandy's own drive core, because that's what a Spectre with no fleet has to work with. You talked me down first — didn't shoot, didn't wait for me to make the call for you either. Told me the cure was Saren's, built to buy an army loyal to him and nobody else, a leash with a cure wrapped around it, not a gift. I've been talked down by better speakers than you and put a headbutt through their skull for the trouble. This was the one time it worked. All right, Shepard, I said. You've made your point. I don't like this, but I trust you enough to follow your lead. Just one thing. When we find Saren, I want his head.

Krogan army gone, cure gone, whatever Saren's people had actually built lost with it. Some doctor built it for him — Droyas, I want to say, or close to it. And somebody had to stay at the detonator to buy the rest of us time to clear. Ashley stayed. Didn't come back. Kaidan did. Same woman who'd had her weapon up on that beach a few hours earlier, ready to put me down if I made you do it myself. Never got around to telling her I'd have done the same standing in her boots.

Funny thing, looking back on it now with a few decades and a bottle between me and that beach. The real cure, when it finally came, didn't come from Saren's stolen science at all. Came years later, from a salarian who'd helped build the genophage in the first place, deciding to spend what was left of his life undoing it. I was ready to die on that beach for a lie. Turned out the truth was slower, and it came from the last direction I'd have —
