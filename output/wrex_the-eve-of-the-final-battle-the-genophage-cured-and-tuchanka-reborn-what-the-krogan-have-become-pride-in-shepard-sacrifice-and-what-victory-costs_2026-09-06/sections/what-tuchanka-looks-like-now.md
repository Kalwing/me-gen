The camp sounds different now. I'll tell you, since I've got nothing better to do with my mouth than talk and drink. There's crying in it. Not the kind that means something's wrong — hatchlings crying, at all hours, because that's what they do, and nobody on Tuchanka has heard that sound in more numbers than one at a time in about fifteen hundred years. First few nights it woke half the camp, warriors reaching for weapons out of habit. Now they sleep through it. That's the change, right there. Not the cure. The sleeping through it.

Eve's out there right now, up before I am most mornings, walking the breeding pools with the shaman and telling clan reps who gets priority water and whose females go where. For males, a good show of force usually sorts things out. Eve decides once, and that's the end of it — better at that than I ever was. I win fights, she wins arguments, and on Tuchanka you need both or you end up like every krogan warlord before me, dead and forgotten under a mountain of skulls nobody remembers the names of. She's carrying my kids. Said that plain, once, to Shepard, standing on Earth with everything on fire, because there wasn't time to make it bigger than it was. If Mordin's cure holds and one of them's a girl, Eve wants to name her for him. Won't be the last kid on this planet named after a salarian. Strange thing to get used to.

Clans have been coming in since word of a real cure got out — not crawling, walking in with their own weapons, because that's the only way a krogan clan comes anywhere. Weyrloc's mostly gone, folded into Urdnot after Guld died — mostly women and children who didn't ask for his mess. Nakmor sent an ambassador years back when I was still holding this camp together with threats and good luck, and they stayed loyal when it mattered. There's a clan out past the Hollows — three families, four, I've lost count. Gatatog took convincing, some of it with words, some with my forehead. Let the holdouts starve proud. I've got neutral ground and fertile females and they've got rocks and grudges. I know which side time favors.

That's the part that keeps me up more than the fight tomorrow does. A krogan people that can breed again is the same krogan people that scared the galaxy into the genophage in the first place. I didn't fight this hard to cure us just so we go make the same mistake with better numbers. So it's food rationing instead of raiding, medicine research instead of new guns — Fortack still complains I've got him growing crops instead of blowing things up, and good, let him complain. Every clan I bring in gets told the same thing: your young get raised, not spent. A hatchling's worth more alive at forty than dead at four proving a point in somebody's border skirmish.

My armor's laid out by the dais for tomorrow. Family piece, older than the Rebellions, more generations on it than I've got fingers to count. First thing I said when I finally got my hands on it was, my ancestors actually wore this piece of junk? Wearing it anyway.
