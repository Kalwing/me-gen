# Read-through — cold (episode)

## hardware-and-software (tali)
Verdict: The engine-room Tali is here, talky and funny, and the best lines are very good. But the turn is an inventory of gifts, shelf, corner, comments, Rayya, locker, hands, bench, Raan, each one introduced like a heading, and it runs long before it lands.

### Style
- C1. "I'm angry about these next two, and you should know I'm telling them because I loved him" / "Okay, this one I'm doing on purpose, so let me." / "The food I'll get to, it was real" — she announces her own running order three times. Her bible says she doesn't know the shape of the evening, and these lines make her sound like she's reading off a list. → Drop the announcements and go straight in: "The bench mount. I'm still angry about it. A support for his station…" and "You all know the joke about his hands. You know it because he did it."
- C2. "which is the worst way" / "which is so much worse" / "It's the worst way to cook anything" — the same intensifier three times in about a page, so it starts to read as a tic. → Keep the cooking one. Change the formulas one to "kindly, which is unbearable" and the shelf one to "you can't thank a person who won't admit it."

### AI-isms
- C3. "And it was so nobody else could. That's why he went first. So nobody else would get the chance, and nobody would see him wince when they did." — this says the same point three times, then explains what the joke meant. The setup ("He did it before you could, and louder") already got it across. → "And it was so nobody else could. Joker and I knew what it cost." Cut the middle two sentences.
- C4. "and we didn't fix anything, which on that ship, with that ship, was a statement." / "Nobody gets to pretend tonight, that's the deal, and if I don't say it that's one more thing about him only he had to carry." — polished lines placed at the end of a paragraph to close it, the move her bible rules out. → "…for a long time afterward and we didn't fix anything. Which, on that ship. Yes." For the second: "Nobody gets to pretend tonight. That's the deal. Sorry, Joker."

### Naturalness
- C5. Adams is the punchline tag again and again: "Adams noticed. Adams sulked for a day" / "I don't think either of us noticed. Adams noticed." / "Adams told me." / "I'm sorry, Adams, it's true." Once or twice it's a good running gag. Five times, it's a device the listener can see coming. → Keep "Adams noticed" on the drive-core alerts and "Adams told me" on the shelf, and let the others go.
- C6. The Raan paragraph ("And then Raan. Admiral Raan, who's wonderful and terrifying…") runs about 350 words and folds in the Pilgrimage kids a second time (already told in para 11), Shepard briefing Raan, the comm gear, the fight, Garrus's warning, and the coffee. Said out loud, you can't follow it. → Split it at "And Garrus had warned him!" and cut the re-explanation of the Pilgrimage kids down to "And then she heard about the kids. The Fleet talks."

### Read
- Works: "He said it like he'd checked it." This is the best line in the turn, so leave it alone.
- Works: "and he asked me about it like — somewhere you could get a bad landlord." and "Sorry. I'm doing it. You'll get used to it, everyone does, ask Garrus."
- Works: "I thought, he did this so I'd never have to say thank you, which is the nicest — no. It made me so angry." — she contradicts herself in real time, which is exactly her voice.
- Drags: C7. From "The SR-1." to "And then Raan." every paragraph opens on a topic tag ("The SR-1." "The SR-2. Cerberus." "The Rayya." "Where was I. The locker." "The bench mount." "And then Raan."). Seven anecdotes in a row, each set up the same way, reads like a filing system. → Let at least three of them come in sideways, through something she just said. For example, the locker could come out of the coolant-line story ("…he marked it on my side, on the locker, you know the locker—").

## plates-outside-the-hole (jack)
Verdict: Mostly convincing. It's crude, unsteady, and the anger at the classroom refusal really lands. Two composed lines and a pronoun tangle let it down.

### AI-isms
- C8. "He did that the whole time. I thought it was soft. Fuck. It's not." — a "thought it was X, it's not" reversal built to close the section. Jack's bible names the crafted antithesis as something she doesn't do. → "He did that the whole time. Every night. Fuck. Every night, and I thought he was just being nice."
- C9. "But he got loose. That was all I wanted. Loose. Nobody ever got loose around me." — a three-step build that ends on an aphorism. → "But he got loose. Took him one song. Took me ten years." Or just stop at "But he got loose."
- C10. "That was the deal." comes three times ("that was the whole deal" / "Best fucking deal I ever had" / "Nodded at me on the way out. That was the deal."). The callback is built like a written refrain. → Keep the first two and cut the one after the poetry nights.

### Naturalness
- C11. "He already thought he wasn't worth it, that you were miles ahead. / I'm saying this to you, Tali" — the "you" lands on Tali one sentence before she says she's addressing Tali, so a listener first hears it as Thomas. → "He already thought he wasn't worth it. Thought she was miles ahead of him. — Yeah, I'm saying this to you, Tali, don't give me the head tilt."
- C12. "After what Tali said, yeah, I get it now, that was him trusting me" — she explains the subtext of the tattoo scene for the room. → Cut "that was him trusting me": "After what Tali said, yeah. Hands like that, and he just holds them out to me."

### Read
- Works: "you know, nobody ever asks the bomb how it's doing." / "Like a coat somebody left on a chair." / "moved like a guy getting electrocuted by someone he liked."
- Works: "You made a joke and now you're, now there's no — fuck you. Fuck you, Thomas. I'm still pissed." This is the heart of the turn.

## the-help-desk-is-closed (joker)
Verdict: Funny and alive, with the best gags in the episode. It's also the longest bit-reel, and the two serious stretches slide into clinical summary.

### AI-isms
- C13. "Like his brain had to decide between lying there with it, and accepting the idea that someone could care about it. He couldn't stand the idea that someone had made him something and he was just going to lie there and take it." — this is analysis, not Joker, and it explains the sandwich story twice. Then "Still... I wish he had asked for help sometime." closes it on a tidy moral. "having a breakdown in his bunk" is also clinical, and it clashes with "it wasn't a sad thing." → "And he looks at it, and he looks at me, and he gets up. Crying. Puts a jacket on, goes down to the mess, makes me a better one. Pickle in it. I ate both. The first one was mine, I had to." Then let the silence do the work.
- C14. "The nights are the thing. You can't explain the nights to people who haven't done them. Lights that never go all the way off. Somebody in the next bed breathing like a ship's engine." — a lyrical written passage. Then "I'm saying we had the whole story, both ways. Neither of us had to be gentle." states the thesis outright. → Keep one image ("Somebody in the next bed breathing like a ship's engine") and replace the thesis with something that shows it: "We'd compare. Ugly stuff. Stuff you don't say to Chakwas."

### Naturalness
- C15. "All I'm saying is his head sure was messed up. In the best way possible, buddy." — this falls flat after the broken X-ray joke and reads like a line written to soften the joke, not one he'd blurt. → End on the failed joke: "That one's not good. Don't laugh, Wrex. I can see you not laughing."
- C16. "So when they would giggle for hours" / "I've had both legs in a cast and I'd never been that hungover" / "I brought a ringer. Horsechoker and mouthwash, I don't, it was a long day." — off idioms and a garbled sentence. → "So when they'd been at it for three hours"; "I've had both legs in casts. Never been that hungover."; "I had a secret weapon. Horsechoker. With mouthwash in it. Don't ask."

### Read
- Works: "Fighting him was like, like, pushing on a door that opens." / "Armed, and a little bit crying." / "moving like a folding chair on a bad day." / "EDI pretends she's not listening. She is. Hi, baby."
- Works: "I thought, who taught me that, and then I knew, and then I had to go stand in a corridor for a while." This is the best ending in the episode.
- Drags: C17. The middle (alarm, seitan, Grissom, scorecard, Ghorza, Blasto) is six bits in a row with no feeling between them. "I miss it so much I could scream" only saves the last one. → Cut the scorecard or Ghorza. Either way, let the turn breathe before the hospital stretch.

## a-counter-is-a-short-distance (liara)
Verdict: The formal cadence is right and the self-corrections help. It slips into essay three times, and the flaws section is staged.

### AI-isms
- C18. "One center, holding what everyone else needs, deciding who gets it. The Protheans died of that shape, and my people grew rich on it, and then the Reapers came to Thessia and it was the same shape again." — a three-part build with a figure ("that shape") that closes the argument neatly. This is the essayist rhythm her bible warns about. → "One center, deciding who gets what. The Protheans. Us. I wanted to tell him he had it backwards. I could not find where. I am still looking, Thomas."
- C19. "But it seems she was stronger than his patterns." — therapy-speak, and it closes the turn's last paragraph on a verdict. → "And then he met Tali, and I waited for him to do it, and he did, for about a week. And then he came back. I did not predict that."

### Naturalness
- C20. "This room has not been polite about him, and I would rather not be the exception. The first is that…" — "The first" has nothing to refer back to, and announcing the flaws makes this sound like a lecture. → "This room has not been polite about him, so. He could describe his own wound like a paper he had peer-reviewed…" Then later: "And another thing."
- C21. "The thing I had been told for a hundred years was a hobby was what they had been standing on." — a stacked subject you can't parse aloud. "which is a more impressive result than I would have predicted from someone staring into a coffee cup" — the listener can't tell if the coffee-cup person is her or Thomas. → "They told me for a hundred years it was a hobby. It was what they were standing on." And "…from me, staring into a coffee cup."
- C22. "Joan of Arc, a great French warrior, burned there, in part for her faith and in part for being a woman who would not stay where she was put." — "a great French warrior" is vague in a way Liara never is, and the paragraph turns into a codex entry with Thomas only as the source. → Tie it to his telling: "He told me about Joan of Arc with his coffee going cold, angrier than I expected, mostly about what the nationalists did with her afterward."

### Read
- Works: "who had not been my mother for some time, only wearing her." / "He had more of a... nebula." / "Hm. No. He knew." / "If anyone here has one please do not give it to me tonight."

## same-tank (grunt)
Verdict: The cleanest voice in the episode. It's short, blunt, and warm without saying so. A couple of seams show.

### Style
- C23. "The plants thing, I've got my own." — sounds like Grunt knows the jokes were divided up among speakers, and it's an odd line to say aloud. → "He gave me a plate once and I looked for the meat…" with no preamble.
- C24. "One small human at a krogan table. Not at him. With him." — a stock antithesis. → "One small human at a krogan table, and they were laughing at what *he* said."

### Naturalness
- C25. "I chop in a straight line now. Joker said it." — Joker hasn't said it tonight, so the listener is lost. → "Joker caught it. I chop in a straight line now."

### Read
- Works: "I just wanted to see how long you could talk without a person in it." / "Brother. That's what he was. I don't say it. I have a batch number, not blood. I'm saying it." / "Wrex says it's a toy. Wrex is wrong." / "I've seen him lose to a door."

## worst-punch-i-ever-saw (james)
Verdict: Warm, physical and live. The seitan correction against Joker is great real-time talk. But two over-written lines in the punch and armory beats break the voice, and the Spanish is heavy.

### AI-isms
- C26. "He punched like a kid, but the morals of a hero." — ungrammatical, and it's a thesis line. Just before it, "thinking that standing up for her wasn't already more than most would have done" is a garbled double negative. → "I'm sitting there thinking, man, you stood up. Most guys in that bar didn't even stand up. Terrible punch. Right guy to hit."
- C27. "It was like getting ready to piss off on everyone, like the dancing prepared him to rebel and throw bad punches at the face of the world." — a written metaphor that comes out garbled. The scream already said it. → "I never saw anybody that happy about something that violent. Dios mío." Stop there.

### Style
- C28. Spanish piles up: "Cabrón… Mentiroso… Hermano… hermano… Tembleque… Hombre… Dios mio… no sé, no sé." His bible warns against turning him into stock phrases. → Keep "Tembleque" (it carries story), one "hermano", and the "no sé" at the end. Put the others in English.

### Naturalness
- C29. "I ate a kebab standing over the sink like I was hiding a body. A week. Mentiroso." — the listener can't tell whether "A week" is a concession or sarcasm. → "Four days, a week, whatever, Grunt, you're still a mentiroso."
- C30. "He trained hard. Most days he didn't feel like it, but I pushed him and I think he was glad for it." — a flat, report-style summary. → Give the scene: "He'd show up at six looking like death, do three pull-ups, and tell me about every one of them."

### Read
- Works: "like a guy looking at a menu he can't read." / "And his mouth opens and what comes out is tattoos." / "the one that sounds like a truck falling down a staircase" / "like a kid showing his coach what he learned."

## the-senior-officer (kaidan)
Verdict: The dry Kaidan is good, and so are the poutine and lager bits. The Spectre story is told three times over, and the climax uses the most familiar beat in the book.

### AI-isms
- C31. "And what came into my head wasn't an order, or the regs, or, God, Anderson. It was a voice. A little hoarse." — a "wasn't X, it was Y" built on a list of three. Then the following sentence restates what Thomas said for the third time. → "And what came into my head was a hospital room. Him, with a stack of cards, saying, mildly, that I'd better be the one who answers. So I lowered it."
- C32. "He heard the question every time and never answered it, and then he built me forty minutes of an answer, and that's him taking it, I think. Sideways. I'll take it as a yes." — this explains what the playlist meant. → "Forty minutes of country and French pop. I'll take it as a yes."

### Naturalness
- C33. "in two years we agreed on exactly one thing, which was that, that nobody should hold that much power unless they're answerable for it. Just the one. It was a good one." — this repeats para 27 almost word for word, and para 31 repeats it again. → Cut this paragraph down to "I was irritated. I was lying in a bed and he'd made a speech at me. Anarchist and officer, and we agreed on that one thing."
- C34. "And toasted to love." — a fragment with no clear subject. → "He'd have made it a toast to love, the romantic. To Thomas."

### Read
- Works: "Give me a minute to be an officer about it." / "the only promotion I ever got for having the worse pill case" / "a debrief with the subject absent" / "They sat there like two delegations." / "lager was what you ordered when you'd already decided not to be surprised."

## where-is-the-soft-one (wrex)
Verdict: Strong. The London account is the episode's spine, and it holds. A few lines explain a verdict that should just be left standing.

### Style
- C35. "Frightened, and walking toward the front anyway, which is the only kind of brave I've ever respected." — a comparison tacked onto a rating, the exact thing his bible forbids. → "Frightened, and walking toward the front anyway."
- C36. "I nodded back. That's what I did with it. I nodded. The krogan way, that's what I was raised to believe." — the last clause explains. → "I nodded back. That's what I did with it. I nodded."
- C37. "This pyjak was as smart as he was dumb. And he was smart." — this muddles the verdict. → "Bullshit. He was smart. About everything but that."

### Read
- Works: "he was one of the few where it stayed told." / "A rumor of lunch." / "count the plates." / "You can't hit a man while you're answering him." / "I've had longer goodbyes from people I wanted dead." / "Turn that off. The music. Off. Thank you."
- Works: "I'm telling it here, because this is his fire." This is composed, but it's earned. Keep it.

## the-fourth-leaf (edi)
Verdict: The case-building EDI is very good: the eleven tasks, the unflagged anomaly, the smoothing filter, the Akuze file. Three passages break into flat, generic prose, and the playlist walk-through reads like a music review.

### AI-isms
- C38. "He talked about art, and philosophy, and history, with me often. I don't think he realized how much that impacted my vision of the world. I logged our conversations, and made my own playlist." — generic list of three plus corporate phrasing ("impacted my vision"). Nothing in it sounds like EDI. → "He talked to me about Klimt at 0300 once, for forty minutes. I have the log. I made my own playlist from it. It has no name yet."
- C39. "The commander's metabolic scans were often lower after those talks. Sometimes higher. But Shepard would always display more resolve after those talks. It was emotional regulation, for both. A process that started years ago." — "display more resolve" is wooden, and the thesis line ("emotional regulation, for both") gives away what the data was meant to suggest. → "The Commander's vitals after those talks varied. His next orders were always clearer. I don't know what was said. I didn't log the cabin."
- C40. "A physical memory he took… It was the first time someone saw this body as an interface for friendly encounter. It's in my memory, but I know he would have deemed the physical artifact important." — stilted ("deemed", "interface for friendly encounter"). → "It's taped beside a photo he took of us, the first time we danced, the day after I got this body. I have the image in memory. I printed it anyway. He'd have printed it."

### Naturalness
- C41. "Although, he would probably have shared it, if it could have helped any of you process it." — this undercuts the refusal she just made. → Cut it.
- C42. Last-playlist paragraph: "then a French song about a woman who watched every war from in front of a screen and finally went to look at one, set right after the anthem, burning the flags it just raised" — a critic's voice running track by track, ten items long. → Keep the structural reading ("He put the joke where the dread was"), name three tracks, and stop: "I'll give you the order if you want it. Not tonight."

### Read
- Works: "I flagged that once as an anomaly. Then I unflagged it. It wasn't one." / "I find I am not interested in a better version." / "He was ahead." / "I haven't announced anything tonight, Jeff. You'll have to decide." / "I miss him."

## thomas-answer-me (tali)
Verdict: The recording and the breakdown are the best writing in the episode and should stay as they are. The long run-up, and the closing lines where Tali narrates the room back to itself, weaken them.

### Naturalness
- C43. "And that goes by. Nobody says anything. Joker is looking at his hands. Wrex has his cup halfway… Kaidan has his eyes shut and his lips moving" and "James is on his feet already… Jack's pulling Liara out of her seat by the sleeve… And I'm moving. We're all moving." — this is a novelist's present-tense stage narration of the room, spoken aloud to the people in it. Nobody does that. It also ends the episode on a tidy group-hug image. → Make it direct address, broken off: "Joker. Joker, look up. — Kaidan, you don't speak French, what are you — okay. — EDI, next. Gasolina. James, yes, obviously. Grunt, that counts as dancing, I'm counting it. Liara, Jack's got your sleeve, just go. — I'm still in the whole suit. I don't care."
- C44. "And then they drifted, for years. Shepard got a medal and Thomas got a classroom full of kids back home… And he gave Shepard perspective. Never orders." — biography read out in summary, coming right before the recording, when tension should be at its highest. → Shorten to what he told her in the corner and what she resents: "Two survivors, and the Alliance filed them separately. Shepard found him on Omega fixing coolers for noodles. He said no three times. Of course he did." Then go to the promise.
- C45. "I want it said once tonight, by someone who loved both of them." / "like a man who knew exactly what it weighed" — announced and composed. → "Akuze. EDI's right, it's a file. He gave me the shape, in the corner."

### AI-isms
- C46. "That's the thing I keep. Out of all of it. A shaking hand, and the wrong words." — her bible names "What I keep instead is this" as a line she wouldn't say, and this is that line. → "He just passed behind me, left it there a second, went on with the tray. — Anyway. Mine. EDI told you about the drum and bass…"

### Read
- Works: "he told me everything in order and none of it in the order that mattered." / "Don't go look. Go look. I don't know." / "You had a breath and you used it to, to breathe." / "Say here." / "This is a very good suit. It recycles all of it. Nobody has to hear that. They have, I know, it's a small room."
- Drags: C47. The recording is promised in paragraph 5 and played in paragraph 51. In between come about fourteen anecdotes (Omega-4, the four pages, the week away, the shuttle bay, the photo, "Answer me", Legion, two decks, the house, the rule, the wall, the terminal, the poems, the death text, Akuze, the promise). → Cut two or three (the wall and Legion could go). Or move the photo and "Answer me" to just before "I'll play it", so the last stretch builds toward the recording.

## Across the episode
- C48. Relay-baton symmetry. Every section ends by naming the next speaker ("Jack, can you—" / "Joker. Come on" / "Liara, you've been looking at the ceiling" / "Grunt… Come on." / "Vega. You were there. Go." / "Wrex, you've got the look." / "EDI, you're up." / "Tali, whenever you want the room"), and every section opens by answering that call ("Yeah. Yeah, okay. Sit down, Tali" / "Okay, Jack, thanks" / "Yes, Jeff, I was looking at the ceiling" / "Go, he says." / "James. It's not on you." / "Alenko." / "How often, Wrex."). Ten turns in a row with the same hinge feels mechanical. → Break it at least three times. Let someone stand up unasked, let one turn trail off without nominating anyone, and let someone interrupt mid-turn.
- C49. "Shepard's missing" is said five times: Tali 1 ("Shepard's still out there, somewhere"), Joker ("The Commander's missing. Missing."), Grunt ("Shepard's missing. If I could go hunt I would."), EDI ("that field stays as it is"), and Tali 2 ("I'd go find him tomorrow"). Each one ends its paragraph on a similar note. → Keep Joker's open channel and Tali 2's anger. Cut Grunt's, and make Tali 1's a half-line.
- C50. "Last week" as the opener for the habit-now-mine beat: Jack ("And I caught myself doing it. Last week.") and Joker ("Last week, Adams and I are arguing"), with Grunt's "I chop in a straight line now" built the same way. → Change one time marker ("Tuesday," "the other night"), or have one speaker notice the habit while speaking, right now in the room.
- C51. "Forty minutes" comes up in five turns: Tali 1 (reading the file), Jack (counting bolts), Liara (Samara), James (Cortez talking), Kaidan ("forty minutes of an answer"), plus EDI's "about forty minutes" playlist in the notes. → Vary it: "most of an hour," "till the bolts ran out," "a whole shift."
- C52. Topic-tag paragraph openers in almost every turn ("The SR-1." "Grissom." "The scorecard." "Blasto, though." "Now, my father." "The Broker." "Horizon." "Huerta." "Vancouver."). Together they make the evening sound like ten people reading index cards. → In each turn, let at least half the stories come out of the previous sentence instead of a heading.
- C53. Playlists come up in six turns (Jack, Joker, Kaidan, Wrex, EDI, Tali 2), each introduced as a set piece ("That's the name of the playlist." / "Where's my. The playlist." / "But there's a playlist."). → Two or three could drop theirs, since EDI already lists them.
