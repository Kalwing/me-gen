# Read-through — cold (episode)

## plates-outside-the-hole (jack)
Verdict: Mostly reads like a real, drunk, furious person. The anger paragraph and the napkin callback are the best writing in the episode. Two or three paragraphs go smooth and turn into a list, and a few lines are built like thesis sentences.

### Style
- C1. "Neither of us ever asked about the ink, either, and that was on purpose, both of us, and let me tell you what that's worth, everybody else wants to know what a tattoo means, … Kasumi and me ran poetry nights in the cargo hold. Not everybody, don't make it a thing, it was her and me, and Grunt sometimes, to listen, and it was a handful of times total. … It wasn't good. He told me one of mine sounded like some raw artist, I want to say a musician, I swear I forgot who…" — This is one long, flowing paragraph that walks through three subjects: the ink, the poetry nights and the compliment. It runs on connected clauses, with no hard stops and almost no swearing. It is the smooth Jack. The qualifiers ("Not everybody… a handful of times total") sound like someone correcting the record, not Jack talking. The "raw artist… I forgot who" beat is vague and goes nowhere. → Split it up. Keep "I'd say mirror and he'd say maw. That's it." as its own short paragraph. Cut the poetry nights down to: "Poetry nights with Kasumi. Shut up. He came a few times, sat on a crate in the back. Said one of mine sounded like I'd been eating somebody's records. Nobody ever — whatever. It was shit. He meant it anyway." Drop the head-count.
- C2. "He said no, it's the only thing it isn't." — A crafted antithesis, polished, with a turn in the middle. In Jack's mouth, or as something she quotes him saying, it sounds written. Jack's own "okay, loser" then has to carry the scene. → Give his refusal as she'd tell it: "He said no. Said it wasn't just anything with him. Some shit like that. And I thought, okay, loser."

### AI-isms
- C3. "He didn't come down with a face on and a hand out. He came down with a band name, left before I had to decide anything, and showed me he gave a shit without once making me say it back." — This is "not X, it was Y", then a tricolon, then a summary of what the earlier paragraph already showed ("Never more than I'd take. Never stood in the door…"). The meaning gets explained twice. → Stop at "And he's the one guy, the one guy on that whole ship who heard me. Took it seriously." Then go straight to the plates. The plates prove the rest.
- C4. "I never said thanks. He never wanted it, that was the whole deal. Nobody said the deal out loud. Best fucking deal I ever had" — This is a thesis sentence about what the relationship meant, which her bible names. The breaking-off "and he's, and he — okay." saves it, but "the whole deal / said the deal out loud" spells out the subtext. → "I never said thanks. Didn't want it, I don't think. Best fucking deal I ever had, and he's, and he — okay."
- C5. "He already thought he wasn't worth it, that she was miles ahead." / "You were never the one that needed to hear it." — The first line explains why the nod mattered right after she has shown it ("like I'd handed him something heavy"). The second is a neat closing aphorism. → Cut the first sentence. Rough up the second: "I'm saying it to you, Tali, so don't give me the head tilt. You already knew. He's the one who didn't."

### Naturalness
- C6. "I'm saying this to you, Tali, so don't give me the head tilt. You were never the one that needed to hear it." — Taken cold, it's confusing. She tells Tali she's saying it to her, then says Tali never needed to hear it. A listener can't work out who the line is aimed at. → See the rewrite in C5. Make clear that the "you're too good for him" line was always news to him, not to her.

### Read
- Works: "he moved like a guy getting electrocuted by someone he liked" is the best simile in the episode. Keep it.
- Works: "Like a coat somebody left on a chair." The bomb joke, and "I laughed so hard I had to put my head between my knees. You fucker."
- Works: The whole "I asked him three times. Four. … nobody gets to tell me it's okay" paragraph. It's unsteady, aimed at him, and has real heat. Don't smooth it.
- Works: The napkin callback ("A napkin! I folded it! Like a hat!") and "Every night, and I thought he was just being nice." This is the emotional payoff of the section.
- Works: "and he's going, dare, dare, and he — shit."
- Drags: C7. The Eezo, robot, Archer kid and unicorn paragraphs come back to back, four anecdotes in a row. Each one is fine, but in sequence they read as a tour of Grissom. Attention drops before the anger arrives. → Merge the robot and the Archer kid into one paragraph. Keep the "light goes out. Click." beat, and let the unicorn be one clipped line inside it.

## the-help-desk-is-closed (joker)
Verdict: Funny, warm, and his timing works. The anarchist fight, the "you win" thread and the Adams payoff are excellent. The structure is a run of labelled bits, though, and the hospital paragraph slips into composed, speechy prose.

### Style
- C8. "The scorecard." / "Oh, and the body pillow." / "Blasto, though." / "The ratings." / "The sandwich, since we're here." / "Okay. The ducts." — Nearly every paragraph opens with a noun label. That's a list of bits read off a card, and "since we're here" is the signposting his bible warns against. → Let some of these come in mid-sentence from the paragraph before. Ghorza could come out of the birthday ("and for my birthday — don't, EDI —"). The sandwich could come out of the ratings ("and you know what, the one time I tried to do something for him…"). Cut "since we're here."
- C9. "The scorecard. … James, you were fourth and you still think the judges were biased. Thomas said it was an objective system." — A thin paragraph with no feeling and no landing. It's filler between stronger bits. → Cut it, or fold it into one aside in the party paragraph: "he put himself dead last on Garrus's scorecard, under me, and I was *seated*."

### AI-isms
- C10. "Lights that never go all the way off. Somebody in the next bed breathing like a ship's engine. Three in the morning and your body's done something…" … "it was the same room, it was only a different thing breaking." … "I'd put that against any medal on this ship. And the one person you want in that room is somebody who doesn't need it explained." — Three images in a row, then a crafted antithesis, then a medal line, then a thesis sentence. This is the composed eulogy register, not Joker. "I'm saying it out loud because he'd want it said straight and I'm sick of the careful voice" also announces the sincerity instead of just being sincere. → Keep the facts. Make it plainer and more broken: "The nights are the thing. Lights never go off. Guy next to you breathing like a bad coupling. Three a.m., and you think, this is the one I don't joke out of. I was scared. Still am. He was too — same room, different thing breaking. Three times he was in one of those beds because he'd tried to stop. Three. He told me one night, I said okay, told him mine, we argued about the pudding. He tried, and he got up, and he kept getting up." Drop the medal line and the "one person you want in that room" line.
- C11. "Like his brain couldn't do it, somebody making him something and him just lying there taking it." — This explains the sandwich scene's subtext right after showing it. → Cut it. "Embarrassed. Crying. … So he throws a jacket on" already says it.
- C12. "We sat in a duct and told each other we were in no state to be useful, and then we were useful anyway. Don't ask me how." — A paragraph-closing aphorism with a tag. → End on the line before: "…and if you think that's not the same, it's the same." Or end on something physical: "Then he handed me a spanner, wrong size."

### Naturalness
- C13. "Three days in the cockpit like that. … At some point someone brought coffee. I don't know who. We drank it." — Taken cold, the Tiptree story stops before anyone hears anything. A listener wants to know whether word came, and for whom. It reads like a missing beat, not a deliberate trail-off. → Add one plain clause: "…We drank it. Mine called first. His didn't." Or have him visibly refuse to go on ("And then — yeah. Next.").

### Read
- Works: "Fighting him was like, like, pushing on a door that opens." The whole anarchist/pilot exchange is the best comic writing in the episode.
- Works: "That's a crew. Armed, and a little bit crying." / "You were gaining strength out of pure belief, which, honestly, that's what the whole military is."
- Works: The Adams "you win" payoff: "and then I had to go stand in a corridor for a while." It's earned.
- Works: "Don't laugh, Wrex, I can see you not laughing." / "moving like a folding chair on a bad day." / "Hi, baby."
- Drags: C14. The section is very long, and the middle (scorecard, Ghorza, Blasto) piles up jokes without lifting the stakes. Blasto earns its "I miss it so much I could scream." The scorecard doesn't. → See C9. Trimming one bit also tightens the run-up to the hospital paragraph.

## a-counter-is-a-short-distance (liara)
Verdict: Her voice is clearly hers and often lovely, with the precise, self-correcting asari academic coming through. The Rouen/entropy paragraph turns into an essay nobody could follow aloud, and the last two paragraphs are laid out like a debrief.

### Style
- C15. "Her story was hers, he said, and everyone's is theirs alone; the world is built less by individuals than by what surrounds them; a narrative can always be twisted, but what people did, and what followed from it, stays put." … "Love, as I understood it, was the disorder that cannot be spent. … love cannot be compressed; it is already its own shortest description. So no one can be reduced to their sequence, and no one can be made a saint or a monster, except, he said, the people who work to shrink it." — These are semicolon chains and stacked abstractions. Read aloud, they can't be followed, and "except… the people who work to shrink it" has no clear referent. It's exactly the "sentences a listener can't follow aloud" failure. Thomas also disappears behind the ideas. → Cut it to one thought per sentence, with Liara visibly losing the thread: "Joan of Arc, he said, burned for not staying where she was put. And then the nationalists took her and made her theirs. That made him angrier than the burning. Then he went to entropy, and I lost him. Love was the disorder that does not run down. Something like that. And from information theory, that love cannot be compressed. I wrote it on a napkin. I have the napkin. I do not have the argument."
- C16. "He did science the way other people do art: took from everywhere, made his own connections, and it made sense, and it made none, and he cared more about what a machine meant than how it worked." — A summary of his mind, with a colon drumroll, and it repeats the "nebula" image she already used well. → Cut it. "He had more of a… nebula" already did this job.

### AI-isms
- C17. "It was what they had been standing on. … One center, holding what everyone else needs, deciding who gets it. The Protheans died of that. My people grew rich on it." — Mirrored antithesis built to land. → Keep the thought but make it hers and messier: "The Protheans died of exactly that. And we — well. We did rather well out of it, for a while." The "for a while" leads into Thessia.
- C18. "This room has not been polite about him, and I would rather not be the exception. Two things, then. The first is… The second is…" — Numbered structure, a signposted turn, a debrief rhythm. Her bible names this. → Drop "Two things, then" and the ordinals. Go straight in: "He could describe his own wound like a paper he had peer-reviewed." Then follow on from it with "And he told me once he had left a few relationships…".
- C19. "But it seems she was stronger than his patterns. I had not predicted that." — "Patterns" is clinical. The line also states the meaning in one tidy closer. → "And then he met Tali, and I was afraid for her. I was wrong. I am very glad to have been wrong. Tali — I am sorry, I am looking at you, I will stop."

### Naturalness
- C20. "which is a more impressive result than I would have predicted from myself, staring into a coffee cup." — The modifier is misplaced: who was staring into the cup? It also comes at the end of an already overloaded sentence about the asari mercenaries. → "The second time I said something I had not planned. The commandos who came to this war came out of that evening. Not bad for a woman who could not order a drink."
- C21. "Hm. No. He knew. He tried to meditate with Samara." — There's no transition from the Broker straight into Samara, inside the same paragraph. → Start a new paragraph and add a hinge: "He was not good at sitting still, in any case. He tried to meditate with Samara…"

### Read
- Works: "who had not been my mother for some time, only wearing her."
- Works: "I noticed I told it in order of military value, coordinates, warning, death, and he was the first person who asked me to say it in the order it happened to me." This is the best line of the section. "It took until the tea was cold."
- Works: "He took it about as far as the tea." / "Forty-one. No, forty, one is a vent." / "If anyone here has one please do not give it to me tonight."
- Works: "simply standing at a counter with nothing in his hands" and "I am not certain which of the two was the grief."
- Drags: C22. The Athame paragraph and the Rouen paragraph come back to back as two long idea-blocks. Even read warmly, attention drops in the second one. → See C15. Shortening Rouen by half also lets the Thane paragraph land.

## the-fourth-leaf (edi)
Verdict: The strongest voice in the episode, for long stretches: evidence first, then the claim, with dry jokes nobody announces. It weakens where it falls back on generic sincerity ("changed the way I see the world"), in an unclear Commander paragraph, and in the long playlist walkthrough.

### Style
- C23. "He talked about art, and philosophy, and history, with me often. I don't think he realized how much that changed the way I see the world. I logged our conversations, and made my own playlist." — Generic and greeting-card. Nothing here was observed first, which is the opposite of how she works everywhere else. The syntax is awkward too ("with me often"). → Give a datum, then the claim: "We had four hundred and twelve conversations about art, history and philosophy. I have them. I have started a playlist of my own from what he mentioned in them. It is nine tracks. I have not named it."
- C24. "It was the first time someone saw this body as an interface for a friendly encounter. It's in my memory, but I know he would have wanted the physical artifact." — "Interface for a friendly encounter" is clumsy and hard to parse. "I know he would have wanted" is a certainty EDI would flag as an inference. → "The first time anyone asked this body to dance rather than to do something. I have the moment in memory at full resolution. I printed it anyway. I think he would have. That is an inference."

### AI-isms
- C25. "The Commander's metabolic scans were often lower after those talks. Sometimes higher. But Shepard always came out of them more resolved. I would call it emotional regulation, for both of them. A process that started years ago." — The data contradicts itself ("lower… sometimes higher") and doesn't support the claim. Then comes a vague thesis sentence and a fragment that sounds like voice-over. → Either cut it, or make it a real observation: "He went up to the Commander's cabin more than anyone aboard and stayed longer. The Commander slept better on those nights. Not every one. I logged it as a correlation. I did not tell either of them."
- C26. "It was not an order. It was not a test." — A reflexive "not X, not Y" pair. → "He wasn't ordering anything, and it wasn't a test; I checked."

### Naturalness
- C27. "It opens on a short instrumental… Then a moody song set in a hospice, and then a French punk song… Then two songs… Then the reason: the anthem… Then a song screamed… Then what you heard… Then the refusal…" — It's a track list read item by item. Even with good asides ("I think that is a door shutting", "I'm recording that as an observation"), it's the longest paragraph and the room's attention would drop. "Burning the flags it just raised" is also unclear cold. → Keep the opening "door shutting", the anthem observation and "He put the joke where the dread was." Compress the middle to "two songs that laugh at where he was headed, one song about a woman who finally went to look at a war." End on "I haven't added anything after it."
- C28. "It was never the worst of it, whatever he made of it in public. That is what I'll say." — An announced limit. It sounds staged, and the room can feel her drawing a line. → Cut "That is what I'll say." Let the next sentence (the smoothing filter) just start.

### Read
- Works: "By the fifth one Kenneth was saying that he honestly could not remember asking for anything, and Thomas said that was kind of him and went on to the sixth."
- Works: "I flagged that once as an anomaly. Then I unflagged it. It wasn't one." / "I find I am not interested in a better version."
- Works: "by a factor of — I had it, and it doesn't matter. He was ahead." This is the most moving line in the section, and it shows the bible's "starts giving the precise figure" tell at its best.
- Works: The Akuze paragraph, from "My current answer is that he had decided I was not that file" to the end.
- Works: "I haven't announced anything tonight, Jeff. You'll have to decide." / "I miss him." The standalone line is earned.

## thomas-answer-me (tali)
Verdict: This is the heart of the episode, and it mostly delivers. It's talkative, funny, and falls apart for real at the recording. The run-up is too long, though: three or four "here's what it meant" explanations and a late Akuze/Shepard detour drain tension just before the play.

### Style
- C29. "And I should tell you why that night mattered, because he never would have. For him, being that close to someone was the top of the mountain: you show every flaw, every ugly corner, the whole mess, and you're loved anyway. That was the whole religion. … So the antibiotics were my decision. He'd never have made me make it." — She announces that she's about to explain, then gives a composed exposition with a colon, a tricolon and "the whole religion", and repeats the point twice. She's giving testimony, not talking. → Make it her, interrupted, with a joke: "And — he never asked. That's the thing. Not for any of it, not the helmet, not a glove. He liked the suit. He liked the little lights, he told me they were *expressive*, the bosh'tet. He'd have lived next to me in it forever. So the antibiotics were me. I decided. Don't look at Chakwas, she didn't know either."
- C30. "I've been avoiding the house, I can see it in my own face." — Cold, this is odd. She's masked, and nobody can see her face, including her. → "I've been avoiding the house, I can hear it in my own voice."

### AI-isms
- C31. "And I need you to hear how big that sentence was." / "his eyes got wet, because we both knew that probably wasn't going to happen" / "He'd been scared of it, I think. It meant too much to him, it was a vow, not a word." — The meaning is spelled out right after the moment that already carried it. "I stood in the doorway and he looked up and he didn't say anything funny" makes "how big that sentence was" unnecessary. → Cut "And I need you to hear how big that sentence was." Cut "because we both knew that probably wasn't going to happen" and let "too fast" do it. Keep "a vow, not a word" once only: it comes back in the breakdown, and it lands better there.

### Naturalness
- C32. "and told me to go save our galaxy, my hero, his own world, something like that, a stupid one" — Cold, the list is garbled ("my hero, his own world"), and you can't tell what he called her. → "and told me to go save the galaxy — 'my galaxy', he said, he meant me, it was a stupid one — and he forced a laugh."
- C33. "It starts. It's loud and it's quick, and a person's shouting in French. And on your screens it comes up, line by line…" / "The filter hums. That's the only sound in here." — These are stage directions in her mouth: describing to the room what the room is hearing. The second works as grief. The first reads like narration. → Shrink the first to what she would actually say over music: "There — that's him, that's the kind of thing he — read it, read the screen, it's choking on the names, ignore it."

### Read
- Works: "he stood there like a wall that had been told to apologize." / "Once a fork." / "the way you hear a generator running out of fuel."
- Works: The filter-chain paragraph ("I had the chain built… and then I just sat there with my hand on the console.") It's a perfect engineer's way of grieving.
- Works: "Don't go look. Go look. I don't know." / "item nine" / "Because the machine kept making them sound like instructions."
- Works: "He wrote that he only touched her hair." followed later by "I could just touch his hair." The echo is quiet and devastating.
- Works: The whole recording and breakdown: "You had a breath and you used it to, to breathe." / "This is a very good suit. It recycles all of it. Nobody has to hear that. They have, I know, it's a small room."
- Works: The room-reading ending: "Kaidan — you told us an hour ago you don't speak French. What are you mouthing? No. Don't tell me. Keep doing it."
- Drags: C34. Between "I'll play it, but not yet" and "Okay. Okay, here. I'll play it." there are about twenty paragraphs. The Akuze and Omega/Shepard stretch ("Okay. Akuze. EDI said it was a file…" through "I'd go find him tomorrow") is backstory that arrives just as tension should be rising. Attention drops right before the climax. → Move Akuze/Shepard earlier, after the house, or cut it to three sentences. Then let the "Thomas. Answer me." injector paragraph lead straight into the play.

## Across the episode
- C35. Baton-pass relay. Every section opens by answering the previous speaker by name and closes by handing the floor to the next one by name. Jack: "Joker. Come on, someone, say the dumb things." Joker: "Liara, you've been looking at the ceiling… you've got the long one". Liara: "Grunt. … you do not have to be." EDI: "Tali, whenever you want the room, it's yours". Openers: "Okay, Jack, thanks" / "Yes, Jeff, I was looking at the ceiling" / "How often, Wrex." / "Thank you, EDI." One or two of these feel like a room. All of them feel like a format. → Let at least two sections end without nominating anyone (trail off, sit down, or drink). Let one open mid-thought with no reply to the last speaker.
- C36. The "he never asked" beat lands in four voices. Jack: "He knew what that was. He never asked." Liara: "He never asked me for a name. Not once." EDI: "He never asked me whether I had read it." Tali: "He never asked to see my face, not once". Each is good alone, but together they flatten into one motif, and Tali's, which matters most, loses its force by arriving last. → Rephrase or drop it in at least two of Jack, Liara and EDI. For example, Jack: "He knew. Didn't bring it up. Ever." Liara could show it without saying it.
- C37. The "the one time he didn't joke" beat recurs. Jack: "He didn't make a joke, which, think about that." Liara: "Thomas said 'no', plainly, in a voice I had not heard him use." Tali: "he looked up and he didn't say anything funny." Same problem as C36. Tali's should be the only one that names it. → Jack can simply say "He nodded." Liara's "no" can stand without "in a voice I had not heard him use."
- C38. A repeated-number emphasis tic. Jack: "I asked him three times. Four." Joker: "Three times… Three." and "Three days in the cockpit like that. Three days." Tali: "three jokes in a row. Three." and "Two decks! … two decks". Each one is a beat, and the beat is identical every time. → Keep Joker's "Three." and Tali's "Two decks!". Drop the echo in the other cases.
- C39. The tremor/shaking hands show up in four of the five sections. Jack: "His hands are shaking, I'm putting a needle in a guy whose hands won't hold still". Joker: "you just do it from the wrists". EDI: "about six hertz, right hand". Tali: "a hand, low on my back. Just there. Shaking." Each fits where it is, but cold it starts to read as his defining trait. → Keep EDI's (precise) and Tali's (intimate). Make Jack's about the needle without the shaking. Joker's joke can stay as a single quip.
- C40. "Okay." as a paragraph reset or one-line paragraph: Jack "Okay. Okay." Joker "Mm. Okay." and "Okay. The ducts." Tali "Okay." x2 and "Okay. Okay, here." It's natural in moderation. Across one evening it becomes a shared verbal tic, so the voices sound alike at their most emotional moments. → Give each speaker a different reset: Jack swears, Joker uses a pilot word ("Right. Correcting."), Liara uses a breath or "Well.", and Tali uses "Keelah." or a technical aside.
