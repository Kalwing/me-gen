# Read-through — cold (voice)

## hardware-and-software (tali)
Verdict: Sounds like a person, and it's the best section in the episode: rambling, warm, angry, funny. But it runs long, and in the middle it slips into announced set-pieces and lines that were plainly written in advance.

### Style
- C1. "He didn't say it like a verdict, he said it like something he'd checked, something he knew from the inside of a house." This is a composed antithesis with a literary image tacked on. "From the inside of a house" is a writer's phrase, and Tali's bible bans exactly this kind of placed, weighed line. → "He didn't say it like he was being wise. He said it like he'd tested it. Like he'd lived in it."
- C2. "He gave the win away every single time, and I haven't touched it." This is a thesis sentence that sums up the tally story the room just heard. The story already lands with "you held the tools" and "so it's yours". → Cut that sentence and go straight to "I haven't touched it. I'm not going to scrub it…"
- C3. "I thought, he did this so I'd never have to say thank you, and that's the nicest and most infuriating thing anyone has ever done to me." It's a balanced, finished verdict. The spite joke after it saves the paragraph, but this line is still a written one. → "I thought — he did this so I'd never have to thank him. Bosh'tet. It's the nicest — it made me so angry. And then I didn't say thank you for two months, out of pure spite."

### AI-isms
- C4. "And it was so nobody else could. That's why he went first. So nobody else would get the chance, and nobody would see him wince when they did." The same point is made three times, which over-explains the subtext. Then "Nobody gets to pretend tonight, that's the deal, and if I don't say it that's one more thing about him only he had to carry" closes on a line written to land. → "He went first so nobody else could. And so nobody'd see his face when they did. Joker and I knew what it cost. He knew we knew, and none of us said it, ever, until — well. Now." Then stop.
- C5. "like a kid caught with a stolen cookie" is a stock image in a section that is otherwise full of specific ones. → "like I'd caught him in my locker."

### Naturalness
- C6. "Okay, this one I'm doing on purpose, so let me." / "The flaws. Two of them." Both announce what's coming. A grieving person doesn't introduce a segment, and the second sounds like an agenda item. → Drop "The flaws. Two of them." and start straight on "And the bench mount — I'm still angry about the bench mount." Keep "I'm angry about both… I loved him" but say it after the first story, not before.
- C7. "Days after that, in engineering, second shift, he told me something he'd never said out loud to anyone. He told me." This sets up a secret and then holds it back, so to a listener it plays as a tease. The doubled "He told me" reads as a written echo. → "Days after that, second shift, he told me something he'd never told anyone. I'm keeping it. We sat on the engine room floor a long time and didn't fix anything, which on that ship was a statement."
- C8. "I never took the suit off in there, I want to be clear, you don't, not on a warship, that's not what it was for." The room is her crew and they already know this. It sounds like she's answering a question nobody asked. → Keep one clause: "never the suit off, not on a warship."

### Read
- Works: "Two people apologizing at each other until somebody cracked." Also "That's the whole romance, Joker, put your hand down. A free bench." Also "somewhere you could get a bad landlord." And the Raan fight ("Loud enough that Gabby left… I let him have the coffee."). These are the best things in the episode. Protect them.
- Works: the comments in the code ("if she asks, Adams"). This is the strongest single image of him.
- Drags: C9. The dextro-shelf paragraph ("There was a shelf in the mess… Adams told me.") runs about 250 words with four separate beats: the shelf, the Pilgrimage meal, being watched, and cooking by smell. Each is good, but together they slow the section right before the station and the code comments, which say the same thing better. → Keep the shelf, the unwatched meals and "Nobody was eating from it. He kept stocking it." Cut the Pilgrimage-meal aside and the "bright kind voice" airlock line.

## plates-outside-the-hole (jack)
Verdict: Jack is clearly Jack: crude, unsteady, furious and tender. A few lines tidy up into neat reflections she wouldn't stop to build.

### Style
- C10. "It's what he did the whole time, and I thought it was soft, and it's the hardest shit I ever tried." This is a crafted antithesis built for the end of a paragraph. Her bible forbids exactly that. → "That's what he did. Every night. I thought it was soft. Fuck. It's — I'm terrible at it."
- C11. "He knew. He'd known the whole time. Didn't need me to say it, he'd been walking around with that in his chest for weeks and he never once argued, never went, who, me? Never dared question it. Not for a second. That's how I knew he was in it" says one idea five times. "Never dared question it" sounds lifted from a written character note, not something Jack would say. → "And he nodded. No joke. He already knew, he'd been carrying it around for weeks. That's how I knew he was in it."

### Naturalness
- C12. "Before Tali, before it was a Help Desk, before Joker had a name for it" is a reflexive tricolon. It also uses the name she then asks Joker to say at the end ("say the dumb name… I need somebody to say it"). A listener hears her say it herself four paragraphs earlier, so the hand-off doesn't make sense. → "Before Tali, before Joker started with his fucking intercom…", so the name stays unsaid until Joker's turn.
- C13. "And then he held them up to every single person on this ship like a kid with a drawing. He wanted people to see. That was new. For him that was new." The image does the work, and the two lines after it explain it. → End on "like a kid with a drawing."

### Read
- Works: "he moved like a guy getting electrocuted by someone he liked." Also "Like a coat somebody left on a chair." Also "nobody ever asks the bomb how it's doing." And the Grissom "Click. Like I'd hit a switch." These are great, so leave them alone.
- Works: the "Fuck you, Thomas. I'm still pissed" stretch is the real break this voice needs.

## the-help-desk-is-closed (joker)
Verdict: Funny and real in places, with an excellent hospital swerve, but it's a long run of bits ("There was the alarm." "The scorecard." "Oh, and the body pillow." "Blasto, though.") that a listener starts to count.

### AI-isms
- C14. "Lights that never go all the way off. Somebody in the next bed breathing like a ship's engine. Three in the morning and…" is a tricolon of mood images. Then "it was the same room, it was only a different thing breaking" is a composed antithesis, and "We told each other everything. I'm saying we had the whole story, both ways. Neither of us had to be gentle." sums it all up. This is the most written paragraph Joker has. → Keep one image (the lights) and "I was scared in there. Still am." Keep "it was the same room". Cut the summing-up last three sentences and go straight to the bad X-ray joke, which already does that job.
- C15. "We sat in a duct and told each other that we were in no state to be useful, and then we were useful anyway, and I've never decided if that was the better part." This is a polished closing aphorism, and his bible bans the thesis sentence. → "We sat in a duct and agreed we were useless. Then we went and were useful. I don't know. Anyway."

### Naturalness
- C16. "I'd gone back to EDI by then. She'd been asking, for a while, and I'd said no, and I got her unshackled, and it was the right call, EDI, I said it to your face already and I'll say it in front of everyone, I was the jerk." This is hard to follow aloud. What was she asking, and why was he the jerk? It pulls focus from Thomas just as the duct story starts. → "I'd just let EDI off the leash. Right call. Ship was empty…"
- C17. The anarchist/pilot argument is unattributed back-and-forth ("That's not an answer. It's the only answer… That's literally a federation. I'm going to put you out the airlock."). Spoken aloud, the listener loses who said what. → Add tags: "he goes, that's not an answer. I go, it's the only answer…"
- C18. "Mm. Okay. Not a joke. So, hospitals." This announces the turn to the serious part, and his bible bans signposting. → "Mm." Then straight into "I had a lot of them, you know that…"

### Read
- Works: "Fighting him was like, like, pushing on a door that opens." Also "Armed, and a little bit crying." Also "out of pure belief, which, honestly, that's what the whole military is." Also "like a guy serving a subpoena." Also "The drone was more believable." And the "you win" ending ("like I'd said it in Turian"). Keep all of them.
- Drags: C19. The bits run (alarm, protein swap, scorecard, body pillow, Blasto) adds up to about 600 words of mostly gags. Blasto alone is about 200 words, and "never once been billed for anything" doesn't land. → Cut the scorecard (it's the weakest and repeats the "gave away the win" idea) and the body pillow, or squeeze each into one sentence. Trim Blasto to the hanar's inner life plus "I miss it so much I could scream."

## a-counter-is-a-short-distance (liara)
Verdict: Distinctly Liara: dry, precise, funny about herself. It has the single best observation in the episode. Two endings are polished a little too much, and one line lands hard on Tali in a way that may not be intended.

### Style
- C20. "He never made Thessia lighter. He never made Ilos lighter either, and Ilos was his best evidence." This mirrored pair is built to close a paragraph. Her bible calls out the essayist's rhythm. → "He never made Thessia lighter. Or Ilos, and Ilos was his best evidence, he could have — he didn't."
- C21. "Things he told me that I am still carrying, and then a second kind, which is whatever I did with them afterward, and I could not tell you the date I finished with that, because I have not." This is muddy, and a listener can't parse the "second kind". → "I have some of those too. Things he gave me that I have not finished putting down."

### Naturalness
- C22. "Tali, you will know whether I was right. I think you do. I would like to have been wrong. I would like that very much." At a wake, in front of the widow, this sounds like Liara saying he would have left Tali too. If that's intended, it's brutal. If not, it's a misfire. Either way the room would go cold, and the doubled "I would like" sounds like a placed ending. → If the point is that he did run from Tali and came back, say so: "Tali, you know better than I do whether he did it again. I hope he didn't. I think he came back." Otherwise leave Tali out of it.
- C23. "Now the flaws, since this room has not been polite and I would rather not be the exception." Tali also announced flaws, so here it sounds like a format the room has agreed on. → "He had faults. I'll give you two, since nobody here has been polite."

### Read
- Works: "I noticed I told it in order of military value, coordinates, warning, death, and he was the first person who asked me to say it in the order it happened to me." This is the best line in the episode. Also "it would be rude to leave mine alone in the room." Also "who had not been my mother for some time, only wearing her." Also "Kropotnik" and "Murray Bookman". Also "Hm. No, I am quite sure he did." Also "I did, no. Well. Rouen."

## same-tank (grunt)
Verdict: Short, blunt and right. It reads like a young krogan who doesn't make speeches, and it's the cleanest voice work in the episode.

### Style
- C24. "But weak ones don't ask if they're real for years and keep getting up." This is a `because`-style clause welded onto his verdict "Strong." The challenge after it rescues it, but the explanation weakens the rating. → "Strong. That's what he was. Not his arms, I've seen him lose to a door. Strong. Say different, say it to my face."

### Naturalness
- C25. "What did I give him. A drink." It's fine on its own, but in context it's the same announced device as Tali, James and Kaidan (see C45). → "I gave him a drink. When I said come out, he came out."

### Read
- Works: "Brother. That's what he was. I don't say it. I have a batch number, not blood. I'm saying it." This is the most moving short line of the night. Also "this human forgot a date I didn't know was a date." Also "He wasn't salting enough, I wrote that. Leave them alone, they're better." Also "it's just Tuesday."

## worst-punch-i-ever-saw (james)
Verdict: Good storyteller, easy cadence. The punch, the curb and Gasolina are vivid. The Vancouver stretch gets confusing, and he leans on the tremor three times.

### Style
- C26. "like a guy reading a menu in a language he studied but never spoke" is a crafted simile with a turn in the middle, more novelist than Marine. → "like a guy looking at a menu he can't read."
- C27. "A whole list, in order, like a bug report." This borrows an engineer's word, which is fine for Tali but slightly off for James. → "like he was reading my service jacket back to me."

### AI-isms
- C28. "I think about that curb more than I think about the punch." This closing aphorism tells the room what the story meant. → "I still think about that curb." Or just cut it. "He didn't believe me and he was right, it was a terrible punch, but, mm." is already a better ending.

### Naturalness
- C29. "I'd been, como se dice, real sure about the guy a week before and not sure about anything by then." Who is "the guy"? Shepard, presumably. "Como se dice" in that spot sounds like a word search for something that doesn't need one. Then "I started keeping a chair warm for the guy". Is that Thomas now? Two "the guy"s in one paragraph point at different people. → "I'd been real sure about Shepard a week before…" and "I started keeping a chair for Thomas."
- C30. "Tembleque", "a seismic event", "the hand's going worse than I ever saw it, like something running underneath", plus the thumb. That's four tremor beats in one section. → Keep the curb hand (it carries the story) and Tembleque. Drop the seismic-event bar line.
- C31. "Wrex, you know the joke, you know exactly the one". It's an in-joke the listener never gets, so it leaves a hole. → Give the setup: "he told the one about the krogan and the elcor mid-hang and I went."

### Read
- Works: "One of the bishops was missing and he'd put a salt shaker in." Also "she did not tip him, I'll say that." Also "the one that sounds like a truck falling down a staircase." Also "three people in half an armor, plates banging." The correction of Grunt ("four days… Mentiroso") is exactly how talk at a wake moves.

## the-senior-officer (kaidan)
Verdict: Kaidan's measured warmth is right, and the beer and poutine material is a joy. But the section stacks composed similes and tidy turns, and its Huerta climax uses the most AI-shaped construction in the episode.

### AI-isms
- C32. "And what came into my head wasn't an order, or the regs, or, God, Anderson. It was a voice." This is a "not X, not Y, not Z — it was W" drumroll at the climax. → "I don't remember thinking about orders. I remember a hospital room, and him saying a Spectre's unaccountable, and I'd better be the one who answers. So I lowered it."
- C33. "He took the awkward out of the room and carried it himself." This is an aphoristic closer that sums the crush paragraph up. → Cut it. "which, now that I say it, was a thing he did to everybody" already lands.
- C34. The similes stack up: "like a piece of salvage", "like two men who'd agreed on a border", "like he was passing the salt", "like a weather report", "in the voice of a man turning down dessert", "like two delegations". Each is good, but together they sound like a writer at work. → Drop "weather report" (it repeats "passing the salt") and "turning down dessert".

### Naturalness
- C35. "It's the only promotion I ever got that I couldn't have gotten without a migraine." The logic is tangled and the joke doesn't land aloud. → "Only promotion I ever got for having a worse head."
- C36. "I keep thinking about three people jumping around in half a suit of armor and that one of them was me, in a manner of speaking, if I'd been where I should have been. I wasn't." This is confusing, because it wasn't three people plus him. → "I keep thinking I should've been the fourth guy in that room. I wasn't."
- C37. "Both are allowed." Ending on it is a deliberate bookend with "both were allowed" from the Rahna story. It's tidy, like a speech built to close. → Let the last paragraph end on "And if you can't, that's fine," without the echo.
- C38. "EDI, I know, you got the better end of that trip." This is obscure, and the listener doesn't know what it refers to. → Cut it.

### Read
- Works: "drinking a lemon that's been through something." Also "lager was what you ordered when you'd already decided not to be surprised." Also "Best diplomacy I saw all year, and I was at a Council coup." Also "Do you know what that cost me? Everything." Also "a debrief with the subject absent." And the honest "I'm not going to invent a sighting so I can feel included."
- Drags: C39. The playlist paragraph ("I owe him one back… I'll take it as a yes.") is about 230 words and makes three moves: the question, the cable run, the playlist. Then it closes on "that's him taking it, I think. Sideways." The hot-sauce bit right after it is better and shorter. → Cut the cable-run count and "you'd have to ask Tali's side of the ledger". Keep the playlist and "once with the volume down, which I recommend to cowards."

## where-is-the-soft-one (wrex)
Verdict: Flat, heavy and krogan, with the strongest image of his death in the episode. The last third repeats its own image and explains itself, which a Wrex who rates and stops wouldn't do.

### Style
- C40. "which is more than a lot of the krogan I've buried managed, and I'd say it to any of their faces" is a comparison trailing off the end of a rating, exactly what his bible bans. → "He walked toward a line with a rifle held wrong. Drink."
- C41. "like a hot pan", then "with a pan", then "a rifle held like a pan". The image is great once, but repeated three times it becomes a motif he's working. → Keep the first, full version. Change the second to "with that rifle" and drop the third (see C40).

### AI-isms
- C42. "I called him the pyjak. If you've never met one: they steal your food, they hide in the ducts, they panic at everything and they get up again anyway. I'll miss the pyjak." This uses a colon as a drumroll, then a four-item list that explains the nickname for the listener. Everyone in this mess has met a pyjak, so "if you've never met one" is staged. → "I called him the pyjak. Steals your food, panics at everything, gets up anyway. I'll miss the pyjak."

### Naturalness
- C43. "Flour was the only bag in that kitchen that wasn't tired." It's opaque, and the listener can't tell what it means. → "Behind the flour. First place I looked."

### Read
- Works: "he was one of the few where it stayed told." Also "A rumor of lunch." Also "count the plates." Also "He drove it in… because the rifle was never the plan. I read the rest off the wreckage." Also "He'd have hated me for a week. He'd have been alive to do it. I nodded back." Also "A father with a knife, I know what to do with."

## the-fourth-leaf (edi)
Verdict: EDI is unmistakable and moving: precise, literal, devastating by accident. The last-playlist paragraph turns into a track listing, and a couple of sentences slip into human, written cadence.

### Style
- C44. "He asked — no. It wasn't an order and it wasn't a test, that's the point." A dash-interruption followed by "not X and not Y, that's the point" is a human-writer tic, not EDI. She would just state the observation. → "He asked me how I was. It was not an order. It was not a test. I told him I didn't understand the question…"

### Naturalness
- C45. The last-playlist paragraph is about 280 words and walks through ten tracks. "I think that is a door shutting", "burning the flags it just raised" and "the most honest sequencing in the file" are a critic's phrasing, and the semicolons are written, not spoken. → Keep "That's his method; I recognize it. He put the joke where the dread was and let it sit there," the anthem observation ("I checked the list for anything about the Alliance or about the Commander and there isn't one") and "I haven't added anything after it." Squeeze the rest to one sentence of the running order.
- C46. "I'd taken the precaution regardless." What precaution? The listener can't place it, and it muddies the best moment. → Cut it, or say what it was.

### Read
- Works: "By the fifth one Kenneth was saying that he honestly could not remember asking for anything, and Thomas said that was kind of him and went on to the sixth." Also "I've decided to treat that as a request he hadn't finished making, since he was a person who asked what you wanted before he cooked it." Also "I find I am not interested in a better version." Also "by a factor of — I had it, and it doesn't matter. He was ahead." Also "he had an opinion on everything, and he doesn't get one on this." Also "I miss him." It's earned and it stands alone. Do not touch it.

## Across the episode
- C47. **The relay baton, every time.** Each section opens by echoing the previous speaker's last word and ends by handing off to the next speaker by name, with a reason drawn from something they're doing. Openings: Jack "Rude.", Joker "The dumb name.", Liara "Ceiling.", Grunt "Forty.", James "Go, he says.", Kaidan "Seven, for courage.", Wrex "Alenko. You never pointed a gun", EDI "How often, Wrex." Closings: "you've been looking at the ceiling for ten minutes", "you've been holding that beer ten minutes", "you've got the look". It's charming twice. Nine times it becomes a mechanism you can see, and "ten minutes" appears three times (Joker, James, Kaidan). → Break it in at least three places: someone just stands up, someone answers a speaker from two turns back, someone ends without naming anyone.
- C48. **"What I gave him," announced.** Tali: "That's what I gave him, if you want to know what I gave him." Grunt: "What did I give him. A drink." James: "What I gave him is small." Kaidan: "I'll say what I gave, because he'd want it measured." Four speakers name the same device out loud, so the room sounds like it's working through a questionnaire. → Keep the gift in two or three of them, and cut every announcing phrase.
- C49. **Flaws, announced.** Tali: "The flaws. Two of them." Liara: "Now the flaws, since this room has not been polite". → See C6 and C23.
- C50. **"Caught myself doing it. Last week."** Jack: "And I caught myself doing it. Last week." Joker: "And the thing I caught myself doing. Last week." Kaidan: "that's the habit I caught, since someone's keeping track". Same framing, same time marker. Kaidan even flags it as a running item. → Vary the entry. For example, Joker tells the Adams moment cold, with no "caught myself" setup. Kaidan drops "since someone's keeping track".
- C51. **"For the record."** Tali: "I'd like that on the record." Jack: "I want that noted." Kaidan: "I'd like it noted that I never once pointed a gun". Wrex: "I want it said he wasn't the first". EDI: "I would like it noted that I was not going to volunteer". Five speakers share one tic. → Keep EDI's. Cut or reword the others.
- C52. **The memory hedge as formula.** Kaidan: "Something like that. That was the gist." Wrex: "That was the gist of it, I've lost the rest." James: "the gist being". Liara: "or near enough". Tali: "something like". Five speakers use "gist" or "something like" to soften a quote. → Let the hedge come out in each voice's own way (Grunt just wouldn't quote, EDI would quote exactly what she has a log of, Jack would mangle it). Use "gist" no more than once.
- C53. **"Shepard is missing."** Tali, Joker, Grunt and EDI each give a paragraph saying they won't call him dead. Each is fine alone, but four in a row thins it out. → Keep Joker's open channel (the best) and EDI's single field-stays-as-it-is line. Cut Grunt's, or reduce it to one line. Tali's chair by the cabinet can stay as a gesture without the "nobody's said different" preamble.
- C54. **The tremor.** Tali (hands joke, bench), Jack (the needle), Joker ("I'm vibrating at you"), James (curb, Tembleque, seismic event), EDI (smoothing filter). A cold listener notices the hands come up in six of nine turns. → Keep Tali's, Jack's and EDI's. Trim James (C30). Joker's line can go.
- C55. **Length.** Tali, Joker and Kaidan are each over 1,500 words, and the mood runs the same high-low-high in all three. The short turns (Grunt, Wrex) hit hardest. Most of the trims suggested above (C9, C19, C39, C45) fall in the long turns.
