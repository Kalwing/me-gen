## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: Above all a eulogy: every speaker laments his loss, tells how they bonded with him and what they loved about him, and celebrates him. The brief asks for nine people on their feet in one room, each saying what there is to say about the dead man: a eulogy, not a two-voice dialogue. Spine is mixed: Thomas is an original character and not in the corpus, so no event or scene names him; sections anchor on the occasions his notes place him in (the Pragia aftermath, the run-up to Omega-4, Tuchanka, Rannoch, the Citadel shore leave, Grissom, the final push) and the speaker's own record of those. Jack holds the first and last turns (~2,500 words each, long as asked), the other seven run 450-1,000 (Grunt, EDI, James short and blunt; Liara, Joker, Tali fullest). Nine sections is inside the form's 6-14 range. Each turn opens by picking up the one before: Jack's grief to Tali's engine room, Tali's hospital-and-hands to Joker's bad days, Joker's jokes to Liara's late nights, Liara's mother story to Grunt's blunt question, Grunt's kitchen guarding to James's training and banter, James's Hammer glimpse to Wrex, Wrex's speech to EDI's data, EDI's four-leaf-clover tally back to Jack. Alcohol and music run through the turns as the room's furniture (Thomas's disco, pop, rock, rap, metal, folk, gabber, electro, and black-metal playlists, ryncol, the taped-up recipes), not as a section of their own. Canon branch: genophage cured, Wrex leads the krogan, geth-quarian peace (Legion dead), Grunt alive, Thane dead; crew do not know what Shepard chose, and his death is unconfirmed. Thomas's unfinished poems for Jack are private to Jack; the French lines are his to be quoted sparingly and, per memory rules, never verbatim-perfect by anyone but Jack.

She refers to it, in her own words and from memory, and only in pieces: that he wrote it in French over a drone that swelled until it was deafening, then silence, then a light melody; that it was against the pine box, the permitted speech and the ashes scattered in an authorised zone. Uncontrolled, almost wailing, shouting while she cries, furious and begging in the same breath, sentences that collapse into his name, into swearing, into sounds. It is not composed and not neat. The piece he wrote is part of it: he never looked at bodies so it wouldn't be real, there is no body for him either, and she cannot do what he did; the tattoo is what she can touch.
- Shepard and him, from where she stood: the two Akuze survivors, how they changed each other (Shepard handed him a reason to get up; he gave Shepard perspective, never orders, and made the Boss human enough to dance), and that she is furious at Shepard too, wherever he is, for taking Thomas to the end of the world and maybe not coming back either
- Her opening turn already used the wail and 'come back', 'I'm not toasting him', Grissom from the back of the room, and the 'you're perfect, lay it all on me' / 'you're not broken, come here' exchange. Do not repeat them: the break here goes further and lands differently, Grissom is told from another angle (what she saw on his face, or what he did with the kids), and this time she does raise the glass after all
- Then a pause, long enough to be felt, breathing, the room waiting. She ends on a good thing, small and true and warm, about him or the way he lived (a laugh, a plate, a dance, a hand on her back), spoken quietly and with a crack. Then the last toast, and the sound or song she asks for as the room closes. No neat summing-up line; the warmth is a thing he did, not a moral.
- This is a eulogy: lament the loss, tell how you bonded with him and what you loved about him, and celebrate him; sad in your own way, never a lore recital

- `summary:jack` [summary] (Jack Alive voice note) — Jack, also known as Subject Zero and Jacqueline Nought (birth name Jennifer), was a notorious human criminal and possibly the most powerful human biotic alive. Born in 2161, she was abducted by Cerberus in 2165, reportedly from Eden Prime, after accidental in-utero element zero exposure gave her biotic potential; her mother was told she had died. She became the central subject of Cerberus' experiment at the Teltin facility on Pragia, which sought to maximize human biotic potential regardless of cost. Every successful enhancement was applied to her; other biotic children were used as expendable test subjects and frequently killed. She was tortured physically and psychologically, kept behind a soundproofed one-way mirror, and forced into gladiatorial fights against the other children, being shocked for hesitating and injected with narcotics for killing, conditioning her to associate killing with euphoria. The other children came to hate her as the source of their suffering.

During a transfer, the subjects rioted, freeing Jack, who flew into a fury and killed nearly everyone in the facility before stealing a shuttle and escaping into the Dakka system. The ship that picked her up used her and sold her as a slave. The Illusive Man shut Teltin down shortly after, later claiming it had "gone rogue." Jack took the name Jack, reacting violently to "Subject Zero," and lived a violent life of piracy, gang membership, vandalism (crashing a space station into a hanar moon), and a stint in a cult (from which she kept her shaved head). In Mass Effect: Foundation she assaulted a Cerberus facility, killed Doctor Angersol, defied the Illusive Man, tried to free biotic student trainees, fought Kai Leng and Rasa, and was knocked out and captured by Blue Suns.

Jack was eventually imprisoned on the Blue Suns prison ship Purgatory, where she was assaulted and raped by guards and prisoners and then murdered every attacker; the Warden kept her in cryostasis until Shepard's team bought her freedom in 2185. Rendered a borderline psychopath but still haunted by her past, she joined Shepard in exchange for her Teltin files, then asked Shepard to help her destroy the abandoned facility with a bomb planted in her old cell (the mission Jack: Subject Zero). There the team encountered Aresh, a fellow test subject trying to restore Teltin; Shepard could stop Jack from killing him. If Miranda's loyalty mission was done, Shepard had to mediate a fight between Jack and Miranda over Cerberus' blame; siding with Miranda cost Jack's loyalty and ended any romance.

Jack was a romance option for a male Shepard. Casual sex ended communication; a slower approach revealed her past, including the partner Murtock who died to save her, leaving her survivor's guilt, and culminated in a tender, emotional love scene. Mordin Solus offered Shepard biotics safety advice. Rejecting her as a "lost cause" made her hostile.

By 2186 (if she survived the Suicide Mission), Jack had grown her hair into a ponytail with shaved sides, matured, and taken a teaching post at Grissom Academy training combat biotics, considering her students the family she never had. During Cerberus' raid to kidnap the students (Grissom Academy: Emergency Evacuation), Jack defended them alongside Shepard and Kahlee Sanders, punching Shepard on reunion for reasons depending on prior choices. She disciplined Ensign Rodriguez and Jason Prangley and led the students in support of Shepard's squad. If Shepard did not arrive in time, Jack appeared as an indoctrinated Phantom at Cronos Station and had to be killed. If romanced, she could rekindle or end the relationship at Purgatory Bar. In the final battle she could be contacted by comm; in a surviving-galaxy ending she is seen gazing at the stars, visiting her students' graves, or alive with her students, depending on choices. If killed, her name appears on the Normandy memorial wall as Jacqueline Nought. In the Citadel DLC she fights in the Armax Arena, introduces her biotic varren Eezo from Thessia, and can tattoo a romanced Shepard so his body can be identified. Jack was voiced by Courtenay Taylor and written primarily by Brian Kindregan.

Designated Subject Zero and later named Jack, she was the focal point of all research; other children were expendable, with lethal enhancements tested on them first and only beneficial ones applied to Jack. Scientists tortured her physically and mentally to study how pain and distress affected biotics. The other children hated her because Cerberus regularly killed them in experiments meant to advance her abilities. Their only interaction was forced gladiatorial combat: she was shocked for hesitating and injected with narcotics for killing without hesitation, hardwiring a link between euphoria and killing. The abuse made her one of the strongest human biotics alive at a very young age.

Eventually the subjects rioted and attacked the guards, damaging the facility and freeing Jack, whose pent-up rage sent her into a blind fury; she nearly killed everyone present, then stole a shuttle and escaped Pragia. A passing freighter picked her up, used her, and sold her into slavery. In the violent, crime-filled years that followed she committed murder, assault, kidnapping, drug use, theft, arson, piracy, and vandalism, and joined a cult. She attacked another Cerberus facility using biotic children to free them, fought off Cerberus agents while covering the children's escape, but was overrun and captured by Blue Suns mercenaries, to Cerberus's frustration, and taken to the prison ship Purgatory. There guards and prisoners beat and raped her; after healing she murdered every attacker. Warden Kuril decided she was not worth the Blue Suns' effort and offered to sell her back to Cerberus. In 2185 Commander Shepard, revived and working with Cerberus, arrived to complete the purchase and recruit Jack against the Collectors.
