## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: Above all a eulogy: every speaker laments his loss, tells how they bonded with him and what they loved about him, and celebrates him. The brief asks for nine people on their feet in one room, each saying what there is to say about the dead man: a eulogy, not a two-voice dialogue. Spine is mixed: Thomas is an original character and not in the corpus, so no event or scene names him; sections anchor on the occasions his notes place him in (the Pragia aftermath, the run-up to Omega-4, Tuchanka, Rannoch, the Citadel shore leave, Grissom, the final push) and the speaker's own record of those. Jack holds the first and last turns (~2,500 words each, long as asked), the other seven run 450-1,000 (Grunt, EDI, James short and blunt; Liara, Joker, Tali fullest). Nine sections is inside the form's 6-14 range. Each turn opens by picking up the one before: Jack's grief to Tali's engine room, Tali's hospital-and-hands to Joker's bad days, Joker's jokes to Liara's late nights, Liara's mother story to Grunt's blunt question, Grunt's kitchen guarding to James's training and banter, James's training to Kaidan's first-ship nights, Kaidan back to Wrex's ramp, Wrex's speech to EDI's data, EDI's four-leaf-clover tally back to Jack. Alcohol and music run through the turns as the room's furniture (Thomas's disco, pop, rock, rap, metal, folk, gabber, electro, and black-metal playlists, ryncol, the taped-up recipes), not as a section of their own. Canon branch: genophage cured, Wrex leads the krogan, geth-quarian peace (Legion dead), Grunt alive, Thane dead; crew do not know what Shepard chose, and his death is unconfirmed. Thomas's unfinished poems for Jack are private to Jack; the French lines are his to be quoted sparingly and, per memory rules, never verbatim-perfect by anyone but Jack.

- `summary:biotic-acclimation-and-temperance-training` [summary] (Kaidan BAaT Vyrnnus Rahna) — Biotic Acclimation and Temperance Training, abbreviated BAaT and pronounced "baht," was a programme established in 2160 to train potential human biotics. Children exposed to element zero who showed biotic potential worth augmenting were sent by the company Conatix Industries to Gagarin Station, also known as Jump Zero, for training, with support from the Systems Alliance. Most of those sent were teenagers, who nicknamed the programme "Brain Camp."

Anxious to avoid interference, the authorities worked to separate the trainees from their families, convincing parents that biotic abilities, and therefore the children themselves, were dangerous. Isolated aboard a remote station with no contact from home, the teenagers formed close circles of friends who worked, trained, and socialized together. Officially, BAaT existed to evaluate the trainees' abilities so an understanding of biotics could be compiled, but the central problem was that humans knew almost nothing about biotics and had no idea how to train the students. As Kaidan Alenko put it, instructors struggled just to keep the children from breaking their own limbs.

Frustrated by slow progress, Conatix sought experts to speed things up but, to avoid making humans appear weak, declined to ask the Citadel for help. Instead, in 2166, the company quietly hired several biotic turian mercenaries, the most notable being Commander Vyrnnus. Vyrnnus was a poor teacher who, though he accelerated progress, was brutal with trainees and used hunger and thirst as training incentives. Several students broke under his regime, and a few died from training accidents or strain. When Vyrnnus deliberately injured a trainee named Rahna, he suffered a broken neck in the resulting melee and died. Partly because of the diplomatic problems this caused with the turians, the early BAaT programme was shut down in 2169 and its records classified to conceal the Alliance's mistakes.

Human biotic training afterward was handled by the military and some private firms, though some techniques developed during the original BAaT programme remained in use. Notable BAaT students included Hendel Mitra, Kaidan Alenko, and Rahna.
