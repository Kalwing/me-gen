## Subject

## Form
**eulogy** — The narrator on their feet in a crowded room, speaking about someone who has died. Sections are the things there are to say about the dead — a habit, a fight, a debt, the once they were wrong, the once they were right when it counted. The tone swings: a story that gets a laugh, a line that lands angry, a stretch that can barely be got through. What holds it together is that none of it is polite — every word is meant.

This run's note: Above all a eulogy: every speaker laments his loss, tells how they bonded with him and what they loved about him, and celebrates him. The brief asks for nine people on their feet in one room, each saying what there is to say about the dead man: a eulogy, not a two-voice dialogue. Spine is mixed: Thomas is an original character and not in the corpus, so no event or scene names him; sections anchor on the occasions his notes place him in (the Pragia aftermath, the run-up to Omega-4, Tuchanka, Rannoch, the Citadel shore leave, Grissom, the final push) and the speaker's own record of those. Jack holds the first and last turns (~2,500 words each, long as asked), the other seven run 450-1,000 (Grunt, EDI, James short and blunt; Liara, Joker, Tali fullest). Nine sections is inside the form's 6-14 range. Each turn opens by picking up the one before: Jack's grief to Tali's engine room, Tali's hospital-and-hands to Joker's bad days, Joker's jokes to Liara's late nights, Liara's mother story to Grunt's blunt question, Grunt's kitchen guarding to James's training and banter, James's Hammer glimpse to Wrex, Wrex's speech to EDI's data, EDI's four-leaf-clover tally back to Jack. Alcohol and music run through the turns as the room's furniture (Thomas's disco, pop, rock, rap, metal, folk, gabber, electro, and black-metal playlists, ryncol, the taped-up recipes), not as a section of their own. Canon branch: genophage cured, Wrex leads the krogan, geth-quarian peace (Legion dead), Grunt alive, Thane dead; crew do not know what Shepard chose, and his death is unconfirmed. Thomas's unfinished poems for Jack are private to Jack; the French lines are his to be quoted sparingly and, per memory rules, never verbatim-perfect by anyone but Jack.

Designated Subject Zero, Jack became the sole focus of the research. Other abducted biotic children were treated as expendable: lethal or risky enhancements were tested on them first, and only beneficial results were applied to her. Scientists tortured her physically and psychologically to study how pain and stress affected biotic ability, kept her behind a soundproofed one-way mirror where she could see and hear the other children but not the reverse, and forced her into gladiatorial fights against them. She was shocked for hesitating and injected with narcotics for killing without hesitation, conditioning her to associate killing with euphoria. The other children came to hate her as the source of their suffering. The abuse made her one of the most powerful human biotics alive while still very young. Eventually the subjects rioted during a transfer, damaging the facility and freeing Jack, who flew into a blind fury and killed nearly everyone present, including a distraught doctor, before stealing a shuttle and escaping into the Dakka system. The Illusive Man later claimed the site had "gone rogue" and ordered it shut down; clean-up crews found the guards and most subjects dead, gave the surviving children amnesic treatments, and delivered them to the Alliance as "slaver attack" survivors. A passing ship that picked up Jack used her and sold her into slavery, beginning a decade of piracy, gang membership, and imprisonment that ended aboard the Blue Suns prison ship Purgatory.
