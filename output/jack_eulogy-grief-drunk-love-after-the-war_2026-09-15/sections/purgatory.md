All right. All right — since apparently we're doing this one thing at a time and nobody's letting me sit down between rounds. Purgatory. You want the real version, not the one I gave you a minute ago like some fucking bumper sticker. Sit still.

Purgatory wasn't a prison. It was a warehouse with a turian's name on the door. Blue Suns owned it outright, ran it for profit. Whole business model was simple as a knife: hold you, bill your homeworld for the privilege, and if your people stopped paying, you got dropped somewhere without a suit or a plan and called "released." I've done time in worse holes run by dumber people. None of them had the balls to call it charity work. Kuril did. Barefaced son of a bitch told Shepard he was doing it "for the good of the galactic community" like he wasn't blowing prisoners out of airlocks between meals to keep the rest of us quiet. I heard that line secondhand, years later, and I still wanted to break something. Still do.

They kept me in the deep end. Cryo, past the courtyards where the regular scum got to walk around under guard glass, down a fork nobody without clearance was allowed near. Four YMIR mechs standing over one frozen woman, because apparently that's what it costs to store something you're scared of instead of just killing it. You want to know what cryo's like from the inside? It's not sleep. It's nothing, and then it's everything at once — your whole body screaming awake into a room full of strangers pointing guns — and the first thing my brain did before it even had words back was reach for the biotics. Four mechs. I didn't count them going in. I counted the pieces after.

I put a hole clean through the hull wall doing it — didn't mean to, my control wasn't what it is now, I was just angry and free for the first time in longer than I want to say, and the ship started dying around us for it. Whole sections venting straight to vacuum, alarms going off in three languages, prisoners loose everywhere doing exactly what I was doing, just smaller. Kuril made his last stand behind three separate shield generators like the coward he was, hosing the hallway down with a Revenant, still screaming about the good of the galaxy right up until Shepard put him down. I wasn't watching that part. I was too busy getting to the airlock and putting the last two guards through it myself.

And then I saw the logo. Cerberus, right there on the hull of the ship supposedly rescuing me, and something in my chest just — no. No, absolutely fucking not. I went for the first person wearing it, a batarian who'd already decided to go for me, and Shepard shot him before I got the chance to finish what my body wanted to finish on its own. That's the first thing he ever did for me, and I hated him for it. Hated him standing there in his nice armor with Cerberus written on his squad like he owned me twice — once by the check that bought me off that ship, once by having a gun faster than mine that day. Cerberus lapdog. That's what I called him, more or less, to his face, on the ship, in front of his crew. A collection agent with better cheekbones. I made him earn every inch of trust after that. Made him wait. Made Jacob and the cheerleader sit through me pointing a very different kind of promise at their database instead of a thank-you.

Here's the joke, since apparently somebody in this room actually remembers it — Jacob, don't you dare pretend you weren't laughing, I watched you try to swallow it — when I told the Illusive Man's little errand boy the price of my cooperation was every file Cerberus had on me, Miranda about swallowed her own tongue. I told him the last time somebody paid this much to get me out of a cage, I ended up owing them a decade. Cheapest I ever got sold was that day, and I still made him pay twice.

They pulled the rest of the block loose getting to me — every cell on that ship went, not just mine — and some psychopath named Billy started sending Shepard love letters about it for months after. Purgatory itself didn't last the year. Structural damage does that when you fight a biotic riot through the load-bearing walls. Good. Whatever Cerberus paid the Blue Suns for me, they got it back tenfold in a wreck at the bottom of a nebula, and I moved into the sub-deck under engineering because it was dark and far from Miranda's office and nobody bothered me there for a while. I didn't say thank you to Shepard for any of it. Not that day. Took me the better part of two years to work up to that, and now the son of a bitch isn't even here to hear it twice.
