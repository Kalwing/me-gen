Next one's called the Bubble, because that's what everybody on this boat still calls it, like it was a party trick and not the thing that almost took my head off from the inside. Somebody get me a real drink before I start this one — that watered-down engine cleaner isn't cutting it.

Omega 4 Relay. Nobody who went through it came back. Not a rumor — a body count going back years, a whole graveyard of ships nobody bothered to name because there wasn't enough left to bury. Shepard flew us through it anyway, on a Reaper IFF we stole off a dead god in the dark, straight at a station sitting on the edge of a black hole like the galaxy's asshole had a lid on it. We ate a security grid meant for something with tentacles. Blew an Oculus apart in the cargo bay. Took out a Collector cruiser trying to eat us whole. Still crash-landed hard enough I bit through my own lip. That's before the mission even started.

Inside was worse. Three parts, and I was in all three, so don't let anybody hand you the cleaned-up cut. Kasumi went into a vent barely wider than her shoulders while Garrus ran the loud, stupid, up-front diversion, because that's what he's for and he loves it, the bastard. We got through, found the room with the pods — the crew, the colonists, hundreds of them stacked up like meat waiting on a truck. Shepard wiped the fog off one, and it was Lilith, some kid from Horizon who never asked for any of this, and she screamed for about four seconds before the Collectors' machine turned her into paste right in front of us. Shepard didn't wait around once they took the crew. Went in fast, got the rest of them out whole — Chakwas, the whole damn crew, thanks to that — but somebody always pays first so the math works out for everyone else. Nobody puts that on the recruitment poster. Fuck the recruitment poster.

Then came the swarms. Too many for Mordin's little gadget, and somebody had to hold a biotic field over a small team while the rest ran a diversion through a different hallway. Miranda offered like it was nothing, like any warm body with an amp could do it. It can't. It fucking can't. Shepard picked me.

Here's what "holding the Bubble" actually means, since I'm the only one who was inside it. You don't get to stand there. You run — backward half the time — throwing everything you've got into a shape you can't see and can't stop holding for one second, or the swarm gets through and somebody behind you loses their whole nervous system to something with too many legs. My head was screaming the entire way. Not metaphor. Actual biotic feedback, like somebody's got a hand inside your skull squeezing. I kept it up until the door sealed. Then I hit the deck and couldn't feel my own hands for an hour. Nobody wrote that down in the after-action report. Report says: barrier held, zero casualties. It does not say what it costs to be the barrier. It never fucking does. That's the whole trick of "nobody died" — somebody always pays, it just doesn't always show up on the list.

Thane walked the survivors back to the ship instead of finishing the run with us. Miranda and Garrus led the two squads and didn't get shot doing it, which on that station, with that much coming at them, is its own fucking miracle. Not us. We watched a thing built out of thousands of liquefied humans — some half-formed Reaper larva the size of a building — get its feeding tubes torn apart and dropped into a hole. Then the Illusive Man got on the line and asked Shepard to save the tech for Cerberus instead of burning it. Smug bastard, safe in some chair a hundred light-years away, still trying to make money off the thing that almost ate us. I told him exactly what I thought of that — called him a user, same as the Collectors, no better — and Shepard blew the whole base to hell anyway, and we ran the explosion out the door by about four seconds. Cerberus salvage crews went back for the wreckage regardless, of course they did, folded whatever survived into some station of theirs, because that's what that organization does with a fire — picks through the ash for parts. But the base itself, gone. My call got heard that day. First time in my whole life an institution asked what I thought and then actually did it.

And here's the part where the old fight starts, the one this ship has never once settled — do you mourn, or do you celebrate, when the mission goes right and everybody's alive to hate it. Half the crew wanted a party, somebody wanted music, and I wanted to go somewhere quiet and hit something until my arms stopped shaking, and Joker just handed Shepard a datapad full of Reaper schematics and a live feed of a fleet the size of a plague heading for home, and nobody felt much like celebrating after that. That night everybody walked away. Every single one of us. Tali and Legion both breathing, which mattered more than any of us knew yet — turned out that's the reason a war between their two peoples ever got to end in peace instead of a second extinction, years later, on Rannoch. Garrus, Miranda, Thane, Kasumi, me. Whole. All of us standing on a ship that had every right to be a coffin, arguing about whether we'd earned the right to feel good about it.

Compare that to this. Compare a night where every name I just said got to walk off that mission and hear their own boots on the deck, to this one — where I'm standing in front of you with a drink and no body to bury, and a room that can't decide the same fucking thing it couldn't decide six years ago. Mourn him or drink to him. So I'm doing both, because apparently that's the only lesson that mission ever actually taught me. You don't get to pick just one. You never did. And don't any of you look at me like I just said something soft, because I'll break the next glass I see.
