## Required facts
- (london-conduit-assault) In the climactic endgame of Mass Effect 3, as Reaper forces ravaged Earth and the Crucible—an ancient superweapon built by the Protheans—required activation at the Citadel, Commander Shepard and Admiral David Anderson led a massive galactic assault on London, England. The Reapers had constructed a towering conduit—a massive structure built from organic and metallic components—in the war-torn city. This conduit, built according to mass relay principles but modified by Reaper technology, served as a direct transport mechanism connecting the ground of Earth to the Citadel, which had been mysteriously repositioned above the planet. The conduit functioned unlike standard mass relays: it emitted a constant, brilliant beam of blue light into the sky, continuously transporting matter (primarily the dead and dismembered remains of slaughtered populations) directly from Earth's surface to the Citadel. The structure had no visible element zero core and, critically, allowed living organic matter to survive transit—arriving at ballistic but survivable speeds—rather than disintegrating as with conventional relays. The Reapers appeared to have multiple destination termini on the Citadel, as different individuals arrived at separate points on the station. The assault ran in two ground stages. First Shepard's shuttle landed to knock out a Reaper anti-aircraft "Hades Cannon" with M-920 Cain rounds so the Hammer ground force could land; the team then regrouped at a Forward Operating Base where Shepard held final words with surviving allies before Anderson gave the battle plan. The second stage pushed through occupied London to a missile battery, where Thanix missiles coordinated with EDI on the Normandy destroyed a Reaper Destroyer blocking the road to the conduit. Anderson then arrived by APC for the final charge. The galactic assault on the conduit was devastating. Allied forces from across the galaxy—humans, turians, asari, salarians, quarians, krogan, and others— launched a coordinated ground assault to penetrate the structure and secure access to the Citadel. However, Harbinger, the Reaper flagship and commander of the local Reaper forces, personally defended the conduit with overwhelming firepower, killing or maiming most of the allied assault force. The majority of the attacking soldiers were annihilated, their bodies adding to the biomass flowing through the conduit itself. Despite the horrific casualties, Commander Shepard and Admiral Anderson managed to fight their way through the structure and reached its heart. Anderson, wounded but resolute, helped push Shepard toward the conduit's exit point. Shepard survived the ascent through the conduit and materialized on the Citadel, while Anderson arrived at a slightly different point on the station. Upon reaching the Citadel, Shepard gained access to the Crucible's control mechanisms and the Catalyst—the artificial intelligence that had guided the Reaper cycle for countless eons. At this critical juncture, Shepard was confronted with a series of final choices that would determine the fate of the galaxy: to Destroy all synthetic life (eliminating the Reapers and the geth but also killing EDI), to Control the Reapers (uploading Shepard's consciousness to command them), to achieve Synthesis (merging organic and synthetic life), or to Refuse the Catalyst's offer (allowing the cycle to continue unchanged). The consequences of this choice—affecting the survival of the geth, EDI, the Reapers themselves, and organic civilization—would echo across the galaxy for generations.

- `commander-shepard_009` [page] (the Crucible firing and Shepard's death in the Synthesis ending) — During the final battle for Earth, Shepard is gravely wounded in an attack by Harbinger. After reaching the Citadel and dealing with the Illusive Man, Shepard is greeted by the Catalyst \- an ancient A.I. that controls the Reapers. Destroy the Reapers: In this ending, Shepard shoots a key energy tube, causing a massive explosion. Using the mass relays, the energy fired by the Crucible destroys all synthetics across the galaxy, including the Reapers, EDI, and the geth. The mass relays and the Citadel are either severely damaged but eventually repaired or (if the player's Effective Military Strength is low) blown up while causing galactic destruction. If the player's Effective Military Strength is high enough, a brief scene shows Shepard taking a deep breath amidst a pile of rubble. 2. Control the Reapers: In this ending, Shepard grabs hold of two electrical handles, which slowly disintegrate the Commander's body. Using the mass relays, the energy fired by the Crucible uploads Shepard's mind as the new Reaper master consciousness. The Reapers depart Earth, the mass relays are damaged by the Crucible firing, and the Citadel remains intact. The Reapers rebuild the galaxy, including the mass relays, and the new Shepard A.I. vows to protect "the many" – though its manner of carrying out this duty is affected by whether Shepard's Morality was more Paragon or more Renegade. 3. Synthesis: In this ending, Shepard jumps into the Crucible's energy beam, which absorbs and dissolves the Commander. Using the mass relays, the energy fired by the Crucible transforms all life in the galaxy into a new hybrid of synthetic\-organic life (with all individuals developing green\-highlighted skin and glowing eyes). The Reapers depart Earth, and both the mass relays and the Citadel are damaged by the Crucible firing. The Reapers work together with everyone to rebuild the galaxy, including the mass relays, and share the knowledge of previously\-harvested civilizations. 4. Refusal: In this ending, Shepard refuses to choose any of the three possible options or shoots the Catalyst. The Catalyst darkly acknowledges the Commander's decision and disappears, shutting down the Crucible and leaving it unable to fire. Shepard is left standing alone, turning to see a Reaper ship destroy an Alliance dreadnought. The Reapers succeed in harvesting all advanced organic life throughout the galaxy, allowing the cycle to continue. This new cycle is implied to have solved the Reaper threat thanks to information passed down by "those who came before". If, however, Shepard chose to destroy the Reapers, and the player's Effective Military Strength was high enough, the squadmate hesitates to put Shepard's name on the wall and instead smiles hopefully.
- `summary:stargazer` [summary] (the Crucible firing and Shepard's death in the Synthesis ending) — The Stargazer was a humanoid entity of indeterminate species and background who narrated the deeds of "The Shepard" to a child companion in the closing scene of Mass Effect 3. Which of two Stargazers appeared depended on Commander Shepard's final choice in the battle for Earth.

If the Crucible was activated, a male Stargazer finished telling the tale, saying it happened long ago and that many details had been lost to time. If the Crucible was not activated and the Reapers continued their cycle, a female Stargazer of an unknown species finished the tale, explaining that her people knew of Shepard's actions and the war through archives left behind by Liara T'Soni; that information had saved the contemporary generation from the threat and was why they lived in peace. In either version the child asked to hear more, and the Stargazer replied that there was time for one more story.

According to trivia, the scene evolved from an early storyboarded Synthesis ending presented by a "Reaper Queen" featuring an asari mother and daughter. The male Stargazer was voiced by former astronaut Buzz Aldrin.

- `summary:bonus-content-disc-technology-space-vehicles-human` [summary] (drunk grief tipping into a final declaration) — The page transcribed art director Derek Watts' gallery commentary for "The Technology of Mass Effect: Space Vehicles - Human," from the 2007 Mass Effect bonus content disc. Watts walked through numerous discarded and final human ship concepts, describing the design goal of making Mass Effect's ships look distinct from those in other games and films.

Early ideas included a ship whose main body was encased in an external skeletal brace, narrow geometric hulls, and a human dreadnought concept that was rejected for looking like "a giant ruler" with too little shape. The final human dreadnought carried large forward cannons and deliberately reused Normandy elements, including matching wings and engines, so all human ships shared a visual family; wings could fold in to appear more aerodynamic for mass relay transit.

Watts described human fighter concepts, several derived from early Normandy sketches, and multiple early Normandy designs that leaned toward a freighter or smuggler look, with a rover bracketed in an open midsection or cargo containers sliding in from the rear; one referenced the Millennium Falcon's off-centered cockpit. The final human fighter again borrowed Normandy engines, intakes, wings, and cockpit. The final freighter used a cockpit main body with a long central spine and a variable number of attachable cargo canisters (from a few up to fifteen or twenty), and appears with strewn canisters in several levels. The final shuttle, referencing Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within, incorporated the Normandy engine and was intended for the Citadel hangar, though Watts was unsure it made it into the final game because ship textures were expensive and levels grew heavier during production.
