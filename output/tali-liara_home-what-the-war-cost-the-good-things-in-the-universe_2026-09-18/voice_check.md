## tali — two-homes-at-sunset

Sit down, Liara — here, next to me, on the flat rock, and don't give me that look, I can see you already calculating load tolerance. Some things never change. You did that with the whole Mako once, before we'd even climbed in — gave us the odds, some grim percentage I've mercifully forgotten — and then it went badly exactly the way you said, and you looked far too pleased about it. You've gotten better at guessing since the SR-1, for what it's worth. Back then you'd have told me the odds and then wandered off toward the ruins anyway, chasing some cipher, deaf to the rest of us shouting your name. I'd go haul you back myself, and somehow I never minded. Mind the edge, though, it drops further than it looks. Tikkun's an old star, redder than yours, a little dimmer, and Rannoch sits close enough in that the whole sky goes the color of a heat sink at the end of a long shift. I know. Don't say it. I can hear myself.

You'll have to excuse the face, while we're at it — this is what I actually look like, in case you've somehow avoided seeing it uncovered in all these years. The geth did it: some of them uploaded themselves into quarian suits after the peace, of all the places to end up, and spent months rewriting our environmental systems to mimic infections on purpose, patiently, until our immune systems stopped panicking at every stray protein in a room. I get about an hour before my eyes start running and my sinuses stage a small rebellion, and I take it anyway, every evening. Don't look at me like that. It's not sentimental, it's just air.

This is the actual reason I wanted you up here, though. This cliff. I stood right about there — no, further, past you — the day the fleet came down. Not landed. Came down, burning, half of Han'Gerrel's Heavy Fleet throwing itself at the geth like people who'd been told their homeworld was three hours away and believed it. I watched hulls take fire they were never built for, glow along the seams the way a drive core does right before you evacuate the engine room, and I did the arithmetic without meaning to — souls per ship, how much of the Fleet that fire cost — and got an answer I didn't want. And then it didn't happen. They stayed up. All these years an engineer, and that's still the best I've got — "they stayed up," like the ships decided something on their own.

Shepard's not up there anymore, or anywhere I can point a scope at — you'd know that better than I would, you had the network, and don't you dare tell me you didn't check. I checked. You checked. We have never once actually talked about how hard we both checked. Years now. No wreckage that was more than wreckage, no last transmission, nothing to bury, and eventually the whole crew just quietly stopped waiting for one. No body, so no burial — just the wall. I cut his name into the plate myself, you remember — worst weld I've done since my Pilgrimage, and I wouldn't let anyone else touch it — and then everyone took a turn saying something about him, and most of it was funny, and some of it wasn't. And then, apparently, this — you, finally sitting on my rock instead of asking your assets about it. You did this once already, you know. The two years, mourning him and plotting something, whichever order you actually did it in. I don't know how you manage a second round of it without coming out strange.

I was down there with him, for the record, not watching from somewhere safe — on the ground, with Legion, some prototype laser I'd half-calibrated myself, and this Reaper's voice coming through slower than a person's, all order and chaos, calling what it was doing to us "intervention," and something underneath that about a species it had been built out of, before Thessia even had writing — don't ask me the name, I don't remember it, and I'm not sure I wanted to know it in the first place. Fleet still hanging fire overhead, Legion and I finally deciding to trust each other one more time, and it held. Legion handed every geth on Rannoch something I'd have called a soul a year earlier, and didn't survive doing it. Han'Gerrel stood the whole Heavy Fleet down instead of finishing what his admiralty board started. We'll get to Legion — I can see you gearing up to ask. Give me a minute.

So: you've had assets crawling this planet for two years, watching the resettlement through somebody else's eyes from three systems off, and this is the first evening you've actually come and sat on it yourself. You are, and have always been, constitutionally incapable of doing nothing — it's practically a professional hazard at this point, ask Garrus. Tell me what your network said about this place. And then tell me what it's actually like, for you, to sit still.

## liara — what-the-network-still-watches

I was not calculating load tolerance. I was calculating whether you were about to make me relive the Mako speech, and bracing accordingly. And for the record, I gave you a percentage once, on one vehicle, and I have never lived it down in — what is it now, seven years? Eight? You brought it up on the Normandy, you brought it up on Rannoch during the fighting, and you are bringing it up again on a rock. At some point, Tali, the joke becomes a tradition, and I am forced to respect it. As for wandering off after ciphers — yes. I did that. More than once. I remember Garrus shouting my name across half a dig site while I stood in front of a wall doing sums in my head, and I remember being genuinely surprised, every time, that anyone had noticed I was gone. I have gotten marginally better. I still would have gone.

And I did check. You are correct, and I am not going to pretend otherwise just because you said it first and I would have preferred to be the one who said it first. I checked constantly, for years, through channels I am moderately ashamed of and one I am not ashamed of at all, and you are right that we have never once compared notes on it, which is either very sensible of us or very cowardly, and I genuinely do not know which. I am not doing it yet, before you get your hopes up. Ask me again after the second drink.

What my network told me about Rannoch — mostly, that it lies. Not maliciously. It just runs on people who are guessing, dressed up as people who are certain. I had three separate confirmations that the first families had walked out under open sky for longer than an hour before I believed any of them, and one of those three turned out to be an asset reselling me my own intelligence with someone else's name stapled to the top of it, which I only found funny after I stopped being furious about it. So: mostly wrong, occasionally right, never as omniscient as everyone assumes the job makes you. You'd think, after everything, people would stop being impressed by "Shadow Broker" as a job title. They never do.

As for sitting still — Tali. I have been asking myself that since you sat me down. It turns out I do not know how, and watching you watch me not know how is possibly the most honest conversation we have had all evening.

I'll tell you where the network actually came from, though, since you keep implying I sprang into it fully formed like some kind of information deity. I took it off a yahg. An actual yahg, Tali — a species so far off Council radar that half the reports I get on his people even now are guesswork dressed as certainty, which I suppose is fitting, since that is exactly how he ran the operation for sixty years without anyone catching him at it. He'd been the real Shadow Broker's prisoner once, decades before either of us was born, and killed him, and wore the job afterward like a stolen uniform that happened to fit. Shepard and I found him on a ship that rode out a permanent storm rather than hide from it, and we spent most of the assault fighting across the hull in the open before we ever got inside — I remember the lightning far better than I remember the corridors, if that tells you anything about which part of my brain was actually running. Feron was inside. You never met him properly, I don't think — my partner, from the two years, the ones you just tried to get me to talk about. He was strapped into a chair built so that pulling him free would kill him, and I tried anyway, because apparently that is what I do with people I have already failed once. The yahg died badly and without much dignity, which I will spare you the details of over drinks and give you if you ever actually ask. And somewhere in the chaos right after — answering his own agents in his own voice so the whole structure did not collapse before I understood what I now owned — I stopped hunting the Shadow Broker and became him. Her. I never did settle on which, and I have given up correcting people about it. Feron stayed on. Runs the Terminus side now, which I think is his way of never being strapped to anything again without a say in it.

The part I trust least in the whole network, if you want the honest answer, is the geth. Everything I have is secondhand — intercepts, asset chatter, careful people hedging about platforms they never actually stood next to. You did. So go on. Tell me what I have wrong before Legion comes up and I have to admit I've been quoting you badly for years.
