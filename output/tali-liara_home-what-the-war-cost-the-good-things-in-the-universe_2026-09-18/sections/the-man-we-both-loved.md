I was not rationing him, Tali. I was choosing my ground, which is a different discipline entirely, and one I would think an engineer could respect — you do not start excavating a site from the middle of it either. But you are right that I have been circling. So. A room. Not the trial, which I only ever had from you and a transcript I was not supposed to have read. A room I was actually standing in.

Understand first the one thing I will not tell you, because I would rather say so than let you keep flinching. Whatever he was to you, in your bed and your suit seals and the two of you rebuilding a planet together after — I was never that, and never wanted to be, and it would insult what we had to call it some lesser, unlucky version of yours. I told him once, years before any of this, that I was tired of leaning on him for everything — after Therum, after he pulled a krogan battlemaster off me while I did very little useful with my own biotics — and he told me that was simply what friends did for each other, and seemed genuinely puzzled I'd say it like a debt. I have turned that over more times than I should admit. I do not think he ever understood how rare that sentence was, coming from a human who could have made it about something else entirely, and did not.

So. The base outside London. You will remember the noise better than I will — I mostly remember the smell, ash and wet stone, and Coats's voice behind me telling Shepard how inspiring he would be, which struck me even then as a strange thing to say to a man about to walk into a beam of light made of corpses. I was on the ramp with a turret and too many husks reaching the buildings behind me, and by the time Shepard found me I had already decided how I would report it — clipped, useful, the wounded count first, feelings later or never. That was the plan. I do not entirely remember what I actually said. Something about the wounded, I am fairly sure. And then something else, underneath it, that was not useful at all, that I have spent years failing to reconstruct honestly even to myself, because I suspect what I actually did was quote him back to himself and pretend it was new.

Here is the quote, or near enough. Long before that day, on the old ship, I once asked him — more bluntly than I usually allow myself — what the point of any of it was, drinks, plans, any of it, if we might all die tomorrow. He told me it was not tomorrow yet. It became a thing I said to myself on worse days than that one. And then it was tomorrow, on a ramp in London, and I remember wanting badly to tell him that, and worrying it would sound like a eulogy delivered too early, so I think I said some smaller version of it and let him decide what to do with it. He always did know what to do with the underneath of a sentence. It was, I have come to think, the actual shape of what I loved in him — not the courage, which everyone reaches for first, but the fact that he kept believing people were worth the trouble of listening to closely, all the way through, Wrex included, Javik included, me included on days I made it difficult.

Wrex was two doors down giving the krogan a speech I only caught the tail end of, all that Urdnot bluster with something steadier underneath it, and past him Javik standing with an expression I had learned by then to read as approval he would never phrase as such. I did not see Garrus and Victus on the ramp myself — that was earlier, before my post — but I heard the two of them laughing about something on the way past, which felt, that day, like an act of considerable defiance.

In the last room were you, and EDI, and Anderson, going over a plan that came down, in the end, to the oldest kind there is: tanks and infantry straight at the conduit, a Reaper Destroyer sitting in the road like it was daring anyone to try it. Anderson laid it out the way I imagine he had laid out a hundred plans before, calm past the point calm should have been possible, and asked for a percentage that never sounded like enough people. I watched Shepard's face while he listened, and I still do not know what he was doing with what he heard.

That room was yours after that, Tali, not mine. Tell me what he said, walking in.
