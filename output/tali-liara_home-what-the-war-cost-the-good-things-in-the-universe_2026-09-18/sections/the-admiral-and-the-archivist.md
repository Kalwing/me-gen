Aethyta. Goddess, you even do the pause right — you have been around me too long. Yes. The one who tends a bar, though calling it "a bar" the way you just did makes her sound almost respectable, and I promise you she is not. She is a matriarch who spent decades pouring drinks on Illium and then the Citadel specifically so that people like her own daughter would talk to her without knowing who they were talking to, and I did not know, for most of a war, that the gruff bartender who kept steering conversation back to my mother was steering it on purpose. I still do not entirely know what to do with her. I am a hundred and some years old — barely out of maidenhood, by our reckoning — and I have had a father for less time than you have had a career, and I still catch myself rehearsing what to call her before I open a channel, as if the word needs clearance before it is allowed out.

She told me, more or less, that the other matriarchs had been watching my whole career the way you would watch a fault line — my mother, the Shadow Broker business, Shepard's Cerberus history stapled onto mine whether I liked it or not — and that if I stepped wrong they would not debate it, they would simply end it, the way asari handle things that embarrass them quietly and permanently. I do not remember her exact words for that part, only that I believed her instantly, and that Shepard apparently answered her threat the way Shepard answers everything, which is badly and on my behalf. We talked, eventually — about my mother, who Aethyta was married to for the better part of a century and never once stopped loving, whatever the records say — and she is the reason I have a nickname I never asked for and cannot make her stop using. Little Wing. I complained about it once, to you, and you told me it was better than half of what your crew calls each other, which is fair, and unhelpful.

I bring her up because you handed me your father tonight without quite finishing him, and I would like to hand you mine back without pretending the two are equivalent, because they are not — yours is dead and requires nothing further of you, and mine is extremely alive and expects a monthly correspondence. But they are both a thing a parent leaves a daughter to carry rather than a person she gets to simply have, and I have been sitting with that shape of it since you said his name. Your father left you a trial. Mine left me a matriarch I have to reckon with for the rest of my very long life, on her terms.

I only know your trial the way I know most of what happened while I was off the Normandy — secondhand, and badly, in fragments Garrus gave me years later over a drink and fragments you gave me even later than that, so forgive me if I get the order wrong. You had sent your father geth components, deactivated ones, believing it was some project of his and not knowing — or so I understood it — what he actually meant to do with them. The Board charged you with treason for it anyway, and somewhere in the hearing — I want to say Shala'Raan announced it, though it might have been whoever was moderating — word came that the lab ship had gone dark, overrun by the very geth he had been working on, and that he was presumed dead. You would not accept that on a Board's say-so and went to see for yourself, with Shepard, and you found him exactly as dead as they said, killed by his own experiment. What I heard is that the evidence on that ship could have cleared you outright by ruining him instead, and that Shepard stood up in front of five admirals who had already half-convicted you and argued from nothing but your record — years of it, everything you had already done for people who were about to sentence you anyway — and won, without ever spending your father's name to do it. His stood. Yours cleared. I could be wrong about which admiral said which line to you in that room. I am not wrong about what it cost you to let him keep the name.

I have never had to answer to a board for anything I've done, Tali, which is either a mercy or the reason I got so comfortable being unaccountable to begin with — I appointed myself to what I run, and nobody gets to strip a title I never had. You stood in a chamber built to strip yours and walked out still wearing it, in front of admirals who wanted you gone before you ever spoke. I do not think I say this to you often enough, so let me say it plainly for once, without hedging it into something smaller: that took more nerve than anything I did on Hagalaz, and I include the yahg in that. Tell me the part I have wrong. I know there is a part I have wrong.
