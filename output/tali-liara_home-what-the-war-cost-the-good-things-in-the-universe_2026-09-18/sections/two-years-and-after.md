A version of him that had not finished being gone. Yes. That is exactly the shape of it, and I did not have the phrase until you handed it to me a moment ago, which I resent slightly, since it took me years to get anywhere near it and you managed it over a drink you had already half finished.

The first time, I did not sit still for any of it. I hired a drell information trafficker off a lead thinner than most of my sources would ever tolerate — his name was Feron, and half of Omega treated him as a joke I had not yet learned to take seriously — because someone told me Shepard's body was neither confirmed dead nor confirmed anything else, and I decided that ambiguity was an instruction rather than a fact. We were ambushed for it twice: once by Blue Suns the old Shadow Broker sent to slow me down, once by Cerberus, who reached us before the Broker's people did and turned the whole search into a proposition. I sat in a room with a man made mostly of confidence and cybernetic eyes and let him talk me into becoming both a source and an asset in one conversation, because he had a location and I did not. Feron got me onto Omega, then onto the Broker's own base disguised as his prisoner, which is a sentence I have never enjoyed telling nearly as much as you enjoy hearing it. We lost the body twice on the way to keeping it, and the last thing I actually did in that whole search was hand him to strangers with an agenda I did not trust, on the strength of one man's optimism, and walk away not knowing whether I had rescued him or simply chosen who got to keep him. I did not see him again for two years, and when I finally did, it was because Cerberus had rebuilt him wrong first and then, apparently, right, in a laboratory I was never shown.

So the first grief had an object. A body I fought for, a coordinate I could chase, an organization to be furious at that also happened to be funding the project. This time there was no body, no coordinate — a beam of light, then silence, then eventually a wall.

You already know what happened to the network in between, since you made me tell it on the cliff, so only this much: when the Illusive Man came for the base a second time, I put the whole ship into the side of his cruiser and moved what mattered of it into Miranda's old quarters aboard the Normandy, and spent the rest of the war searching for Shepard three decks below where he actually lived, without him ever knowing the office existed. Some of what survived the wreck ended up inside the Crucible. The rest is still sitting in caches across systems I have not had the reach to collect from since — which Feron would call very on brand, building an intelligence empire and never once turning it on the one query that mattered until it was too late to matter.

You cut the plate yourself. Garrus stood at that wall once before, after the Cerberus coup, and said nothing useful the whole time — the only correct response that wall has ever had — and he did it again the night Shepard's name went up instead of a list from the Collector attack. I was not there after Thessia myself, as you well know — I went looking for Javik instead, then spent that day alone behind a closed door. Everyone took a turn, you said. I did too, though I do not entirely trust my memory of what I said — I had rehearsed three versions walking there and delivered, I suspect, a fourth I have not fully reconstructed since. It was, at least, mercifully short of the word hero, which he would have hated.

The network still runs, and you already knew that, and you were entirely too smug about it on the cliff. I have queries standing that have returned nothing for months, and I have not closed one of them, which is either the most honest thing I do all week or a way of still looking for a body that no longer exists to be found. Ask me again after the second drink, I told you. This is the second drink, more or less, and the honest answer is: dimmer. Mostly settled. Never quite one thing — some weeks grief, some weeks only a habit I never got around to breaking, and I no longer expect to tell those apart.

I did not climb all the way up your cliff to end an evening on a wall with his name cut into it, though, and I will not let you steer me there. Tell me something the war did not manage to take from you. I have a list of my own, and for once I would like to hear yours first.
