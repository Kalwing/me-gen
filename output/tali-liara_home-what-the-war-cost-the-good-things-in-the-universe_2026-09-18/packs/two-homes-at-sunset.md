## Form
**reminiscing** — Two old friends going back over the campaigns over beers. Warm, unhurried, no case to make — just "remember when." Sections are adventures recalled, and the narration keeps losing the thread onto opinions about lore, culture and how things work, then circling back. Runs everything against the Shepard it remembers — this persona's choices, temper and habits are the yardstick the stories and the digressions get measured by.

This run's note: Two old friends over drinks, except the beer is on a cliff on Rannoch and the war is the Migrant Fleet's and the Council races' shared one. Tali and Liara alternate turns strictly, 6 each; no voice holds two in a row, since this is exactly the kind of unhurried back-and-forth the form wants. Each turn's first promise is its reply to the one before — Liara picks up Tali's line about the air, Tali corrects Liara's secondhand version of the trial, and so on — and both keep losing the thread onto lore (geth cognition, Thessia, admiralty politics, asari mourning custom) before drifting back to the cliff. The two women never knew Shepard chose Synthesis, and the section list is built so neither has to name it: they talk about what changed after the Crucible fired, not why.

- `summary:rannoch` [summary] (rannoch resettlement and quarian environmental reclamation) — Rannoch was the former quarian homeworld, the first planet of the Tikkun system in the Perseus Veil. It orbited an older orange star about 90% the mass of Sol and half as luminous, but because it formed closer to its star and had slightly less ocean coverage it was arid by Earth standards, with photosynthetic life concentrated around rivers and oceans and large deserts between. The importance of plant life and shade in ancient quarian culture is reflected in the planet's name, which translates as "walled garden." Reaching Rannoch in Mass Effect 3 required the mission Priority: Perseus Veil.

Almost three hundred years before the games, the quarians were driven from Rannoch by the geth, synthetic servants who gained sapience and rebelled against their creators. Rannoch became largely uninhabited, though the geth acted as caretakers, repairing the planet's ecology, restoring ancient structures, and cultivating farmland. Thousands of geth space stations watched over the planet, and the geth armada lurked among them awaiting a chance to counterattack. Rannoch had no insect life, so its pollinating plants evolved to rely on animals for propagation; this flora-fauna symbiosis is blamed for the quarians' weakened immune systems, which made colonizing other worlds extremely difficult after their exile, making reclamation of Rannoch a cultural and physiological necessity.

Native lifeforms included the qorach, a carnivorous bighorn-sheep analogue whose belly was always coated in blue-green fern pollen and seed casings, whose blind newborn was called a ke'sed; the lemek worm, a planarian analogue covered in metallic rose-colored scales that could regrow from pieces; and the fa'yin, an edible nut dating to the era of the first proto-quarian beasts. Rannoch was the setting for the missions Priority: Rannoch, Rannoch: Admiral Koris, and Rannoch: Geth Fighter Squadrons.

The name derives from Hebrew: "tikkun" means "a fix" or "correction," and appears in the phrase "tikkun olam," meaning "healing and restoring the world." Several worlds in the system followed a naming scheme based on Jewish culture and belief.

- `summary:geth` [summary] (Legion's sacrifice uploading the Reaper code) — The geth ("Servant of the People" in Khelish) were a race of networked artificial intelligences residing beyond the Perseus Veil. Created by the quarians as a labor force and tools of war, they were designed as non-sentient VIs that ran more efficiently when networked. That networking allowed them to gain emergent sentience; when a domestic unit asked its owner whether it had a soul, alarmed quarians moved to shut all geth down. The attempt failed and triggered the war the geth called the Morning War. The geth did not initially respond with violence, and some sided with quarian sympathizers, but they eventually won and drove the surviving quarians into exile as the Migrant Fleet. Unknown to the quarians, the geth deliberately allowed them to flee, unwilling to exterminate their creators. Afterward the geth became isolationist, destroying ships that entered their space, living in server hubs aboard space stations, drawing resources from asteroids, and maintaining the abandoned quarian worlds out of respect for the dead.

Physically the humanoid geth resembled quarians, with a single glowing photoreceptor ("flashlight heads"). An individual geth program was roughly VI-level; only in groups could they reason and make tactical decisions. Each mobile platform housed around a hundred programs ("a thousand voices talking at once"), while Legion held 1,183 to operate autonomously outside the Veil. Geth had no government, rank, deceit, or concept of individuality; decisions were reached by consensus. Subtypes ranged from agile Geth Hoppers to Armatures, Colossi, Primes, Destroyers, Hunters, Pyros, and Dreadnoughts. Their military doctrine emphasized surprise and ambush, expending platforms freely since destroyed programs were archived and re-downloaded. Heretic geth used dragon's teeth to convert corpses into husks.

Roughly three centuries after the Morning War, the Reaper Nazara (Sovereign) offered the geth technology to build their "megastructure" (a Dyson-sphere-like mainframe to house every geth program) in exchange for aid capturing the Citadel. The majority refused, preferring self-determination and calling the Reapers "Old Machines"; a splinter minority, the "heretics" (perhaps five percent), accepted and worshipped the Reapers, serving Saren Arterius at Eden Prime and the Battle of the Citadel. After Sovereign's destruction, the true geth commissioned Legion to find Commander Shepard. In Mass Effect 2, Legion joined Shepard aboard the Normandy SR-2 and sought to counter a heretic virus that could rewrite geth logic; Shepard chose to destroy the Heretic Station or rewrite the heretics.

In Mass Effect 3 (2186), the quarians attacked the geth, who accepted Reaper code to survive after the flotilla destroyed their megastructure and killed many programs. Shepard infiltrated a geth dreadnought to free Legion or a geth VI serving as a signal booster, then pursued a Reaper on Rannoch that broadcast the control signal. After the Reaper was destroyed, Legion/the geth VI attempted to upload Reaper-derived code granting every geth true individual consciousness. Depending on Shepard's choices, the geth achieved full sentience and aided the war, were wiped out, or made peace with the quarians and co-existed on Rannoch (some uploading into quarian suits to strengthen quarian immune systems). The unit sacrificed itself in every case. Choosing the Crucible's Destroy option killed all geth; Control or Synthesis let them rebuild Rannoch.
