## Form
**reminiscing** — Two old friends going back over the campaigns over beers. Warm, unhurried, no case to make — just "remember when." Sections are adventures recalled, and the narration keeps losing the thread onto opinions about lore, culture and how things work, then circling back. Runs everything against the Shepard it remembers — this persona's choices, temper and habits are the yardstick the stories and the digressions get measured by.

This run's note: Two old friends over drinks, except the beer is on a cliff on Rannoch and the war is the Migrant Fleet's and the Council races' shared one. Tali and Liara alternate turns strictly, 6 each; no voice holds two in a row, since this is exactly the kind of unhurried back-and-forth the form wants. Each turn's first promise is its reply to the one before — Liara picks up Tali's line about the air, Tali corrects Liara's secondhand version of the trial, and so on — and both keep losing the thread onto lore (geth cognition, Thessia, admiralty politics, asari mourning custom) before drifting back to the cliff. The two women never knew Shepard chose Synthesis, and the section list is built so neither has to name it: they talk about what changed after the Crucible fired, not why.

Quarian facial structure and hair makes them the most similar to humans out of all known sapient species. They also have three thick fingers on both hands which include a thumb, an index finger, and a long finger, similar to the middle fingers for humans, as well as three toes on each foot. Their lower legs are bowed backwards significantly, compared to asari or humans. Aside from hands and legs, their general body shape and sexual dimorphism is similar to humans. Male quarians, however, appear to lack a third toe. Like humans, quarian blood is red. The most distinguishing feature of quarian biology is their weak immune system, compounded by centuries of living in sterile environments. As a result, all quarians by necessity dress in highly sophisticated enviro\-suits, to protect them from disease or infection if they are injured. Their suits can be compartmentalized in the event of a tear or similar breach to prevent the spread of contaminants (similar to a ship sealing off bulkheads in the event of a hull breach). Along with their suits, quarians also have extensive cybernetic augmentations integrated into their bodies. A quarian's lifespan is roughly equal to a human's, but is prone to be less if infection breaks into the suit. Quarian immune systems have always been relatively weak, as pathogenic microbes were comparatively rare in their homeworld's biosphere. Furthermore, what few viruses and other microbes were native to their homeworld were often at least partly beneficial to them, giving them a symbiotic relationship with their environment. After living aboard the Migrant Fleet for generations, the quarians' immune systems have atrophied further still due to the years in the sterile environment of the Migrant Fleet. - `summary:quarian` [summary] (quarian dextro food and suit induction ports) — The quarians were a nomadic humanoid species renowned for skill with technology and synthetic intelligence. After their homeworld Rannoch was conquered, they lived aboard the Migrant Fleet, a vast collection of salvaged and secondhand ships traveling as one. Roughly three hundred years before 2183 (around the late 1850s CE) the quarians created the geth as VI-grade labor, but as they modified the geth toward more complex tasks the machines became sentient. When a geth unit asked its overseer whether it had a soul, the panicked quarian government ordered all geth terminated. The geth defended themselves; billions of quarians died and the survivors were driven into exile. The Citadel Council refused aid and stripped the quarians of their embassy, while a treaty forbade attacking the geth.

Quarians are shorter and slighter than humans, with three-fingered hands, backward-bowed lower legs, UV-capable vision, and red blood. Their defining trait is a weak immune system, worsened by generations in sterile environments, forcing them to live permanently in sealed enviro-suits with extensive cybernetic augmentation. Removing a suit requires antibiotics and immuno-boosters and still carries risk; the most intimate act between quarians is linking suit environments, which reliably causes illness. Like turians they are a dextro-protein species, and their diet is largely vegan.

Quarian culture centered on the survival of the Migrant Fleet, home to roughly 17 million. Couples were limited to one child to hold zero population growth. Young quarians undertook a Pilgrimage to bring something of value back to the Fleet before joining a new ship (never their birth ship) as adults. Names carried four parts: given name, clan name, "nar" (child of, birth ship) or "vas" (crew of, adult ship), and vessel name; Tali'Zorah went from nar Rayya to vas Neema to vas Normandy. The common language was Khelish, source of terms like bosh'tet and the benediction "Keelah se'lai" ("By the homeworld I hope to see someday"). Quarians once practiced ancestor worship using personality-imprint VIs until the geth destroyed the ancestor databanks. Their economy used no currency; unneeded items were left in communal areas, while food and medicine were centrally rationed. The Fleet was governed under nominal martial law by the Admiralty Board and elected Conclave. The clandestine Nedas Movement ("Mered'vai Rannoch," forget Rannoch) rejected reclaiming the homeworld and later signed on with the Andromeda Initiative.

Before the Reaper invasion the Admiralty Board voted to retake Rannoch, arming the Civilian Fleet and striking geth systems, destroying a geth Dyson sphere. The geth accepted Reaper upgrades, turning the war against the quarians until, with Shepard's help on Rannoch, a Reaper Destroyer was killed and the upgrade signal cut. Depending on Shepard's choices the geth were destroyed and Rannoch reclaimed, the quarians were wiped out, or the two races made peace and coexisted. A separate quarian effort sent the ark Keelah Si'yah, carrying about 4,000 quarians and other races, toward Andromeda, where the Fortinbras Plague broke out in transit.

- `summary:pilgrimage` [summary] (quarian Pilgrimage tradition) — The Pilgrimage was a quarian rite of passage. On reaching young adulthood, quarians of the Migrant Fleet had to leave their birth ship, travel the galaxy, and find something of value to bring back as a gift for a prospective captain, proving they would not be a burden on a ship's thin resources before being accepted as a permanent crew member. Stripped of ritual, the practice was a means of maintaining genetic diversity across the small, isolated shipboard populations of the Fleet, since marrying within one's birth vessel would sharply raise the risk of inbreeding. It also let quarians experience life beyond the Flotilla and appreciate their own people and culture, often the only chance they would get, as their skills became indispensable to the Fleet once they returned. Exceptions were made for the sick or unfit, and gifted individuals could occasionally receive special dispensation from the Admiralty Board.

Finally, the essay covered suit culture: quarians live in their suits even among family, a simple rupture can kill, and linking suit environments is the most intimate act two quarians can perform, making both parties badly and potentially fatally ill as the culture's ultimate gesture of trust. When Raan took the infant Tali and sealed her in a sterile bubble, Raan and Tali's mother first linked their suit environments to share a clean room for the birth, which made both of them sick for a week. Children do not wear suits until adolescence, and receiving a first suit is a coming-of-age.
