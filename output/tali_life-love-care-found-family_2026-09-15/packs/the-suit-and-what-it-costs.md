- `summary:quarian` [summary] (linking suit environments trust) — The quarians were a nomadic humanoid species renowned for skill with technology and synthetic intelligence. After their homeworld Rannoch was conquered, they lived aboard the Migrant Fleet, a vast collection of salvaged and secondhand ships traveling as one. Roughly three hundred years before 2183 (around the late 1850s CE) the quarians created the geth as VI-grade labor, but as they modified the geth toward more complex tasks the machines became sentient. When a geth unit asked its overseer whether it had a soul, the panicked quarian government ordered all geth terminated. The geth defended themselves; billions of quarians died and the survivors were driven into exile. The Citadel Council refused aid and stripped the quarians of their embassy, while a treaty forbade attacking the geth.

Quarians are shorter and slighter than humans, with three-fingered hands, backward-bowed lower legs, UV-capable vision, and red blood. Their defining trait is a weak immune system, worsened by generations in sterile environments, forcing them to live permanently in sealed enviro-suits with extensive cybernetic augmentation. Removing a suit requires antibiotics and immuno-boosters and still carries risk; the most intimate act between quarians is linking suit environments, which reliably causes illness. Like turians they are a dextro-protein species, and their diet is largely vegan.

Quarian culture centered on the survival of the Migrant Fleet, home to roughly 17 million. Couples were limited to one child to hold zero population growth. Young quarians undertook a Pilgrimage to bring something of value back to the Fleet before joining a new ship (never their birth ship) as adults. Names carried four parts: given name, clan name, "nar" (child of, birth ship) or "vas" (crew of, adult ship), and vessel name; Tali'Zorah went from nar Rayya to vas Neema to vas Normandy. The common language was Khelish, source of terms like bosh'tet and the benediction "Keelah se'lai" ("By the homeworld I hope to see someday"). Quarians once practiced ancestor worship using personality-imprint VIs until the geth destroyed the ancestor databanks. Their economy used no currency; unneeded items were left in communal areas, while food and medicine were centrally rationed. The Fleet was governed under nominal martial law by the Admiralty Board and elected Conclave. The clandestine Nedas Movement ("Mered'vai Rannoch," forget Rannoch) rejected reclaiming the homeworld and later signed on with the Andromeda Initiative.

Before the Reaper invasion the Admiralty Board voted to retake Rannoch, arming the Civilian Fleet and striking geth systems, destroying a geth Dyson sphere. The geth accepted Reaper upgrades, turning the war against the quarians until, with Shepard's help on Rannoch, a Reaper Destroyer was killed and the upgrade signal cut. Depending on Shepard's choices the geth were destroyed and Rannoch reclaimed, the quarians were wiped out, or the two races made peace and coexisted. A separate quarian effort sent the ark Keelah Si'yah, carrying about 4,000 quarians and other races, toward Andromeda, where the Fortinbras Plague broke out in transit.

- `summary:pilgrimage` [summary] (quarian immune system) — The Pilgrimage was a quarian rite of passage. On reaching young adulthood, quarians of the Migrant Fleet had to leave their birth ship, travel the galaxy, and find something of value to bring back as a gift for a prospective captain, proving they would not be a burden on a ship's thin resources before being accepted as a permanent crew member. Stripped of ritual, the practice was a means of maintaining genetic diversity across the small, isolated shipboard populations of the Fleet, since marrying within one's birth vessel would sharply raise the risk of inbreeding. It also let quarians experience life beyond the Flotilla and appreciate their own people and culture, often the only chance they would get, as their skills became indispensable to the Fleet once they returned. Exceptions were made for the sick or unfit, and gifted individuals could occasionally receive special dispensation from the Admiralty Board.

In preparation, quarians were surgically fitted with immunity-boosting implants, since growing up in the sterile ships left them with virtually no natural immune system, and were given survival lessons plus weapons and armor. Some never returned, whether through misadventure or by choosing a different life. For safety a captain gave a departing quarian two code phrases for their return: one signalling success and safe reintegration, the other an alert phrase indicating return under duress, which would cause the quarian navy to immediately destroy the returnee's ship.

Pilgrimage gifts varied widely: food, fuel, technology, knowledge, or even a new starship for the Fleet. Some gifts were valued more than others, a derelict towed back for salvage being treated disdainfully, and individuals from prominent families were expected to return with something special, but a captain would usually accept even a modest gift out of tradition. The one absolute rule was that the gift must not be obtained by harming another, quarian or not. On completing the Pilgrimage and joining a new ship, a quarian's name changed: the childhood title "nar" ("child of"), followed by the birth ship's name, was replaced by "vas" ("crew of"), followed by the name of the ship they had earned the right to join.
