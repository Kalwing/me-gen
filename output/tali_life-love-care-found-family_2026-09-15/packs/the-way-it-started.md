- `quarian_009` [page] (Keelah se'lai homeworld meaning) — Now that the quarians are reduced to a small, insular population, they have one common language known as Khelish. Words from the language listed below are taken from intra\-quarian conversation. - *bosh'tet* — curse/insult, likely being relative to "son\-of\-a\-bitch" or "bastard". - *esu se'lai* — likely a form of "goodbye" - *geth* — "Servant of the People" - *Mered'vai Rannoch* — "Forget Rannoch" - *nar* \[ship\-name] — child of - *nedas* — nowhere - *keelah* — "By the homeworld" - *keelah se'lai* — "By the homeworld I hope to see one day." - *keelah si'yah* — "By the homeworld I hope to find one day." - *ke'sed* (or *kes'ed*) — offspring of the qorach animal, figuratively means "naive" when used on people - *Pallu'Kaziel* — "Nevertheless, Justice Comes" - *Rannoch* — "walled garden" - *tasi* — no\-one - *tiral azhana* — "handful of water" - *vas* \[ship\-name] — crew of - *von* — "one who has a weak bladder" Quarian names are composed of four parts—the quarian's given name and clan name separated by an apostrophe, the title ("nar" means "child of", referring to their birth ship, while "vas", adopted after the quarian has completed their Pilgrimage and joined a ship, means "crew of") and the name of their vessel. When Tali first met Shepard, for instance, she was going by the name of her birth ship (the Rayya) because at that point she had not yet completed her Pilgrimage and was thus not yet able to join a quarian crew. Later, though, she became known as "Tali'Zorah vas Neema", indicating that she had completed her Pilgrimage and was welcome to join the Neema. On occasion quarians may impose their naming conventions on other races. Commander Shepard, for example, may be referred to as "Shepard vas Normandy" if the Commander chooses to interact with the quarians on their home territory. In certain formal situations, quarians appear to use both their adult ship and childhood ship. Tali'Zorah identifies herself as "Tali'Zorah vas Neema nar Rayya" during docking procedures following summons to the Migrant Fleet regarding accusations of treason.

- `summary:quarian` [summary] (Keelah se'lai homeworld meaning) — The quarians were a nomadic humanoid species renowned for skill with technology and synthetic intelligence. After their homeworld Rannoch was conquered, they lived aboard the Migrant Fleet, a vast collection of salvaged and secondhand ships traveling as one. Roughly three hundred years before 2183 (around the late 1850s CE) the quarians created the geth as VI-grade labor, but as they modified the geth toward more complex tasks the machines became sentient. When a geth unit asked its overseer whether it had a soul, the panicked quarian government ordered all geth terminated. The geth defended themselves; billions of quarians died and the survivors were driven into exile. The Citadel Council refused aid and stripped the quarians of their embassy, while a treaty forbade attacking the geth.

Quarians are shorter and slighter than humans, with three-fingered hands, backward-bowed lower legs, UV-capable vision, and red blood. Their defining trait is a weak immune system, worsened by generations in sterile environments, forcing them to live permanently in sealed enviro-suits with extensive cybernetic augmentation. Removing a suit requires antibiotics and immuno-boosters and still carries risk; the most intimate act between quarians is linking suit environments, which reliably causes illness. Like turians they are a dextro-protein species, and their diet is largely vegan.

Quarian culture centered on the survival of the Migrant Fleet, home to roughly 17 million. Couples were limited to one child to hold zero population growth. Young quarians undertook a Pilgrimage to bring something of value back to the Fleet before joining a new ship (never their birth ship) as adults. Names carried four parts: given name, clan name, "nar" (child of, birth ship) or "vas" (crew of, adult ship), and vessel name; Tali'Zorah went from nar Rayya to vas Neema to vas Normandy. The common language was Khelish, source of terms like bosh'tet and the benediction "Keelah se'lai" ("By the homeworld I hope to see someday"). Quarians once practiced ancestor worship using personality-imprint VIs until the geth destroyed the ancestor databanks. Their economy used no currency; unneeded items were left in communal areas, while food and medicine were centrally rationed. The Fleet was governed under nominal martial law by the Admiralty Board and elected Conclave. The clandestine Nedas Movement ("Mered'vai Rannoch," forget Rannoch) rejected reclaiming the homeworld and later signed on with the Andromeda Initiative.

Before the Reaper invasion the Admiralty Board voted to retake Rannoch, arming the Civilian Fleet and striking geth systems, destroying a geth Dyson sphere. The geth accepted Reaper upgrades, turning the war against the quarians until, with Shepard's help on Rannoch, a Reaper Destroyer was killed and the upgrade signal cut. Depending on Shepard's choices the geth were destroyed and Rannoch reclaimed, the quarians were wiped out, or the two races made peace and coexisted. A separate quarian effort sent the ark Keelah Si'yah, carrying about 4,000 quarians and other races, toward Andromeda, where the Fortinbras Plague broke out in transit.

- `summary:rannoch` [summary] (house on Rannoch) — Rannoch was the former quarian homeworld, the first planet of the Tikkun system in the Perseus Veil. It orbited an older orange star about 90% the mass of Sol and half as luminous, but because it formed closer to its star and had slightly less ocean coverage it was arid by Earth standards, with photosynthetic life concentrated around rivers and oceans and large deserts between. The importance of plant life and shade in ancient quarian culture is reflected in the planet's name, which translates as "walled garden." Reaching Rannoch in Mass Effect 3 required the mission Priority: Perseus Veil.

Almost three hundred years before the games, the quarians were driven from Rannoch by the geth, synthetic servants who gained sapience and rebelled against their creators. Rannoch became largely uninhabited, though the geth acted as caretakers, repairing the planet's ecology, restoring ancient structures, and cultivating farmland. Thousands of geth space stations watched over the planet, and the geth armada lurked among them awaiting a chance to counterattack. Rannoch had no insect life, so its pollinating plants evolved to rely on animals for propagation; this flora-fauna symbiosis is blamed for the quarians' weakened immune systems, which made colonizing other worlds extremely difficult after their exile, making reclamation of Rannoch a cultural and physiological necessity.

Native lifeforms included the qorach, a carnivorous bighorn-sheep analogue whose belly was always coated in blue-green fern pollen and seed casings, whose blind newborn was called a ke'sed; the lemek worm, a planarian analogue covered in metallic rose-colored scales that could regrow from pieces; and the fa'yin, an edible nut dating to the era of the first proto-quarian beasts. Rannoch was the setting for the missions Priority: Rannoch, Rannoch: Admiral Koris, and Rannoch: Geth Fighter Squadrons.
