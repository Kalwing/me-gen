- `drugs_016` [page] (red sand addiction) — Red sand is a stimulant with biotic\-enhancing properties. Its abuse is disproportionately high among non\-biotic friends and relatives of biotics, often out of a misguided desire to understand what their loved ones are experiencing. Red sand was originally created by criminal triads on Mars from refined element zero, and may take its name from the planet. As eezo is also used to create medicines, red sand's creation may have been a "fortunate" lab mistake. When taken nasally, red sand creates a brief but intense euphoria, and gives the user very short\-term telekinetic biotic abilities. Side effects include red\-tinting of the user's vision, discolouration of the teeth and longer\-term withdrawal symptoms. Red sand is normally sold in small bags. The slang expression for using it is "dusting up" or being "sand\-blasted". Asari and batarians are immune to red sand, whilst humans are the only race confirmed to be susceptible to its effects. The batarians are known to buy addicts as slaves, when the addicts can no longer afford to support their habit. Grayson genuinely loved her, and their separation when she joined the Ascension Project deepened his red sand addiction.

In Mass Effect: Ascension, Grayson relayed orders and medicines to the undercover operative Dr. Jiro Toshiwa at the Project. When Jiro's cover was blown, Grayson was ordered to extract Gillian but was blocked by Kahlee Sanders, and travelled with Kahlee, Hendel Mitra, and Gillian to Omega, where his old partner Pel double-crossed him and imprisoned the group for sale to the Collectors. Lemm'Shal nar Tesleya mounted a rescue; Grayson broke free, killed Pel, and obtained Migrant Fleet access codes from the sick quarian Hilo'Jaa. Believing quarians had taken Gillian, he led a Cerberus raid on the Idenna with the traitor Golo, using the scout ship Cyniad as a rigged decoy. Kahlee convinced him Cerberus only wanted Gillian as a weapon; Grayson shot Golo, warned Kahlee the Cyniad was trapped, and afterward was placed in Kahlee's custody, escaped, and messaged the Illusive Man that he was leaving Cerberus forever, protecting Kahlee with blackmail.

In Mass Effect: Retribution, Grayson lived on Omega under the alias "Paul Johnson", overcame his addiction, worked as an enforcer for Aria T'Loak, and began a relationship with her daughter Liselle. Kai Leng found and captured him, and the Illusive Man had him implanted with Reaper technology as an experiment. The nanites transformed him, heightening his strength, speed, and biotics while slowly indoctrinating him; Cerberus used red sand to wear down his resistance. The Reapers used his body to slaughter turian soldiers, seize a ship, and lure Kahlee via a distress call. Aria then forced the captured Kahlee to send a message drawing Grayson to Omega for an ambush; he tore through the mercenaries and, driven by the Reapers, travelled to Grissom Academy, deceived its authorities, killed guards and scientists, and transmitted Ascension Project data to the Reapers. Attacked by Anderson and abducting Kahlee, he was finally shot down by Anderson with a shotgun; as the Reapers released control he smiled at Kahlee, and Kai Leng put two bullets in his skull.

In Mass Effect: Deception, Anderson and Sanders brought his body to the Citadel Council as evidence of Reaper involvement, but the Council attributed the mutations to Cerberus, and the Illusive Man ordered Kai Leng to retrieve the corpse. In Mass Effect 3, Shepard found video logs on Cronos Station detailing Grayson's fate and how his Reaper implants helped Cerberus adapt the technology for its forces; he was also mentioned by Henry Lawson and by Anderson.

- `summary:quarian` [summary] (quarian society no currency communal goods) — The quarians were a nomadic humanoid species renowned for skill with technology and synthetic intelligence. After their homeworld Rannoch was conquered, they lived aboard the Migrant Fleet, a vast collection of salvaged and secondhand ships traveling as one. Roughly three hundred years before 2183 (around the late 1850s CE) the quarians created the geth as VI-grade labor, but as they modified the geth toward more complex tasks the machines became sentient. When a geth unit asked its overseer whether it had a soul, the panicked quarian government ordered all geth terminated. The geth defended themselves; billions of quarians died and the survivors were driven into exile. The Citadel Council refused aid and stripped the quarians of their embassy, while a treaty forbade attacking the geth.

Quarians are shorter and slighter than humans, with three-fingered hands, backward-bowed lower legs, UV-capable vision, and red blood. Their defining trait is a weak immune system, worsened by generations in sterile environments, forcing them to live permanently in sealed enviro-suits with extensive cybernetic augmentation. Removing a suit requires antibiotics and immuno-boosters and still carries risk; the most intimate act between quarians is linking suit environments, which reliably causes illness. Like turians they are a dextro-protein species, and their diet is largely vegan.

Quarian culture centered on the survival of the Migrant Fleet, home to roughly 17 million. Couples were limited to one child to hold zero population growth. Young quarians undertook a Pilgrimage to bring something of value back to the Fleet before joining a new ship (never their birth ship) as adults. Names carried four parts: given name, clan name, "nar" (child of, birth ship) or "vas" (crew of, adult ship), and vessel name; Tali'Zorah went from nar Rayya to vas Neema to vas Normandy. The common language was Khelish, source of terms like bosh'tet and the benediction "Keelah se'lai" ("By the homeworld I hope to see someday"). Quarians once practiced ancestor worship using personality-imprint VIs until the geth destroyed the ancestor databanks. Their economy used no currency; unneeded items were left in communal areas, while food and medicine were centrally rationed. The Fleet was governed under nominal martial law by the Admiralty Board and elected Conclave. The clandestine Nedas Movement ("Mered'vai Rannoch," forget Rannoch) rejected reclaiming the homeworld and later signed on with the Andromeda Initiative.

Before the Reaper invasion the Admiralty Board voted to retake Rannoch, arming the Civilian Fleet and striking geth systems, destroying a geth Dyson sphere. The geth accepted Reaper upgrades, turning the war against the quarians until, with Shepard's help on Rannoch, a Reaper Destroyer was killed and the upgrade signal cut. Depending on Shepard's choices the geth were destroyed and Rannoch reclaimed, the quarians were wiped out, or the two races made peace and coexisted. A separate quarian effort sent the ark Keelah Si'yah, carrying about 4,000 quarians and other races, toward Andromeda, where the Fortinbras Plague broke out in transit.

- `migrant-fleet_004` [page] (Migrant Fleet rationing liveships) — The Migrant Fleet is broken up into various clans, sometimes spread over several ships. Each individual ship has long been retrofitted to house as large a crew as possible. Over time the quarians thin out the vessels they can't use or are too damaged to repair, pooling the credits to buy and convert new ships. Due to the high value of ships, stealing one is a capital crime among the quarians. Conditions aboard every vessel in the Migrant Fleet are extremely cramped. One cruiser, the Idenna, had a quarian population of nearly seven hundred, while an Alliance cruiser of comparative size would have only around eighty crewmen. Space is at a premium because of the sheer numbers of quarians living aboard the Flotilla. Captains are also keen to increase the size of their crew, as this increases their status in quarian society. Living space is therefore a priority; the cargo holds of freighters, for example, are converted into small compartments for individuals to live in, often lined with colourful fabrics to make it an individual space and reduce noise. Quarians also serve volunteer rotations aboard the three Liveships which form the heart of the Fleet. Enormous vessels which are recognised as being incredible feats of aerospace and agricultural engineering, the Liveships provide much of the food for the quarian Migrant Fleet. If even one of these vital ships was destroyed or damaged beyond repair, millions would starve. For this reason they are positioned in the center of the Migrant Fleet and heavily defended by the quarian navy. Little more is known about the Liveships, since only quarian ships are allowed to enter the flotilla. One of the foodstuffs grown there is the keleven, a breed of blooming high\-protein bioengineered celery. Everything the quarians do must help to ensure the continued survival of the Migrant Fleet. The Pilgrimage forms a large part of this, as well as being a cultural rite of passage and a safeguard against inbreeding. The Pilgrimage also gives quarians a chance to explore galactic society and appreciate their own people back on the Flotilla. Young quarians are prepared for their Pilgrimage by having lessons in life outside the Migrant Fleet, receiving gifts to help them, and being treated for immunodeficiency before they are allowed to leave. Apart from their Pilgrimages, quarians typically spend their entire lives living shipboard and contributing to the Flotilla. In addition, quarians do not normally welcome outsiders onto the Migrant Fleet, because visitors carry an unacceptable risk of contagion; taken together, these factors mean quarians tend to be quite insular, caring little about the galaxy outside the Fleet. Upon the Migrant Fleet's arrival in a system, the quarians begin strip\-mining local planets for resources. In colonized systems, quarians are also often hired "under the table" by corporations for their specialised skills, replacing existing workers. These practices make the approach of the Migrant Fleet a very unpopular prospect. Some species make a "gift" of fuel, food, or ships if they know the Flotilla is approaching in order to discourage the quarians from entering their systems.

- `summary:migrant-fleet` [summary] (Migrant Fleet rationing liveships) — The Migrant Fleet, also called the Flotilla, was the massive collection of starships that became home to the quarians after the geth drove them from Rannoch about three centuries before the trilogy. It consisted of roughly 50,000 ships housing some 17 million quarians, the largest array of spacefaring vessels in the galaxy; it could take days to pass an entire mass relay, and some vessels dated from the original flight. It was rarely welcoming to outsiders, since any risk to the Fleet was a risk to the species, and quarians normally left only for the Pilgrimage.

Conditions aboard were extremely cramped, with cargo holds converted into individual living compartments; the cruiser Idenna held nearly 700 quarians where a comparable Alliance cruiser held around 80. Larger crews raised a captain's social status. Three heavily defended Liveships at the Fleet's center provided most of its food, growing crops such as the high-protein celery keleven; losing one would starve millions. The Fleet was nominally under martial law but functioned democratically: each ship had an elected civilian council, representatives sat on the Conclave (civilian government), and a five-member Admiralty Board could override the Conclave only by then resigning or facing arrest, which had happened only four times in three centuries. The navy was small but aggressive, shooting to kill ships whose motives could not be established. On arrival in a system quarians strip-mined planets and undercut local labor, making their approach unpopular. The Fleet was organized into four groups: the Patrol Fleet (Shala'Raan vas Tonbay), the Civilian Fleet including the Liveships (Zaal'Koris vas Qwib-Qwib), the Heavy Fleet (Han'Gerrel vas Neema), and Special Projects research vessels (Daro'Xen vas Moreh).

In Mass Effect: Ascension (2183), Kahlee Sanders, Gillian Grayson and Hendel Mitra were brought aboard the Idenna by Lemm'Shal nar Tesleya to escape Cerberus. Captain Ysin'Mal argued the Fleet was dying for lack of spaceworthy ships and would be unsustainable within 90 years; a Cerberus attack led by the traitor Golo finally persuaded the Fleet to send cruisers on long-term missions. In Mass Effect 2 (2185), Shepard and Tali travelled to the Fleet for Tali's treason trial over geth parts sent to her father Rael'Zorah, culminating in retaking the lab ship Alarei. In Mass Effect 3 (2186), Daro'Xen's scanning countermeasure derived from Rael'Zorah's research crippled geth ships, prompting a 3-2 Admiralty vote to invade geth space; the quarians armed the Civilian Fleet, fitted Thanix weapons, destroyed a geth Dyson sphere, and drove the geth back to Tikkun. The geth accepted Reaper upgrades and turned the war until Shepard disabled the Reaper signal on Rannoch. Depending on Shepard's choices the Fleet was destroyed, the geth were annihilated, or quarians and geth made peace; if the quarians survived, the Fleet joined the defense of Earth. Notable vessels included the Alarei, Idenna, Neema, Rayya, Qwib-Qwib, Moreh, Tonbay and the Liveships Rayya, Shellen and Chayym.

- `summary:treaty-of-farixen` [summary] (Migrant Fleet rationing liveships) — The Treaty of Farixen was an arms-control agreement among the major Citadel races limiting the number of dreadnoughts each could keep in its fleets. Agreed at the Farixen Naval Conference, it fixed a construction ratio because of the ships' destructive potential. The peacekeeping turian navy, which makes up most of the Citadel Fleet, sat at the top of the pyramid with the largest allowance; the asari and salarians came next; and Council associate races such as the hanar and volus were allowed the fewest. The ratio was set at 5:3:1 (turian to Council to associate). Signing the treaty was a requirement for any race seeking a Citadel embassy and associate Council membership. Carriers, though as large as dreadnoughts, were not covered by the treaty, so any number could legally be built.

Humanity joined the treaty in 2183 after the Farixen Conference, and following the Battle of the Citadel and its Council seat was presumably entitled to the 3:5 Council ratio. If the Council was left to die aboard the Destiny Ascension during that battle, news reports said the turians no longer felt bound by the treaty and stepped up shipbuilding.

By 2186, an Alliance News Network interview by Diana Allers revealed that the quarian Migrant Fleet, after acquiring Blue Suns salvage on Korlus, had armed every ship in its Civilian Fleet, even the liveships, with Thanix cannons, effectively converting them into dreadnoughts and violating the treaty. When the Citadel condemned this, Admiral Han'Gerrel vas Neema argued that since the Council had closed the quarian embassy, the quarians were no longer bound by the treaty. Shepard could raise the matter with Admiral Shala'Raan vas Tonbay, who said it was not technically a violation because liveships were designed for food cultivation, not combat, and that she would formally apologize to the Council after the war if necessary. The treaty is modeled on the 1922 Washington Naval Treaty, and the unrestricted growth of carriers parallels that treaty's unintended boost to aircraft carrier development.
