## The Fleet Gone Quiet

## Teltin, Still Standing in Her Head

## What Biotics Actually Cost

## My Kids, Tomorrow

## The Arena and the Noise

You're doing the thing again where your jaw's locked like you're chewing on tomorrow. Stop it. I've got a better memory to put in there instead, and it's mine, so you don't get to argue with it.

Silversun Strip. The simulator on the Armax floor, the one that smells like ozone and rich people's cologne. You'd already run a match without me, which, rude, but fine, I forgive you, because I messaged you after and said get down here, I want to hit something and I want it to hit back. You showed up. We ran the Brimstone map, two rounds, elite Cerberus troops, the whole fake-blood light show. First round's just work. Warm-up. Bodies going down, credits ticking, nothing in it but rhythm.

Second round they drop an Atlas on us.

I didn't wait for you to call it. I went in low, cracked the canopy plating with a couple of warps stacked back to back, and when the shields finally folded I put a biotic charge straight through the cockpit glass and rode the recoil of that thing coming apart. It swung that claw arm at me on the way down, missed by about a foot, would've crushed me flat if the timing had been real instead of a program deciding to be generous. You were somewhere behind me yelling something, probably tactical, probably useless, because by the time you got there I'd already taken it apart myself. Simulated pilot, simulated everything, didn't matter. Felt real enough in my arms. That's the whole trick of that place — it doesn't have to be real to burn off what's real in you.

And then it went up, this big dumb fireworks show of fake shrapnel, and I grabbed your collar and kissed you behind the wreckage like the world was ending in the good way instead of the bad one. Which, fine, technically it might've been both. Don't read into it more than that. We didn't get together over dead bodies, Shepard, whatever some report somewhere probably says about me. We got together over a lot of things. That was just one good night of them.

That's the part I need you to actually hear, because I know how it sounds from outside. Girl who was built by Cerberus to be a weapon, gets off on breaking a mech apart with her mind. Cute story for people who never had to do it for real. Here's the difference — Teltin, that noise wasn't mine. Every drop of biotic output they pulled out of me belonged to somebody in a coat with a clipboard. The Arena's the same body doing the same math, except this time I chose it, I walked in, I walked out, and nobody's writing it down for a file. That's why hitting something on my own terms is about as close as I get to church. Not peace, don't picture candles. It's a room where the violence is mine and I get to decide when it stops. Most of my life I didn't get that. A mosh pit's the same deal, actually — bodies slamming, nobody asking permission, and somehow everybody walks out fine, or fine enough. People who've never been in one call it chaos. It's the opposite. It's the only honest crowd I've ever stood in. Same rules as a fight, mostly — you go in knowing you might get an elbow to the face, and you go in anyway, because getting hit by somebody who isn't trying to kill you is its own kind of relief.

Here's the part that'll actually make you laugh, if your face still remembers how. After we won, I bought my own ally license so I could fight next to myself in the sim later. There's a version of me in there now, digital and stupid, taking orders from whatever poor bastard queues her up. I keep picturing some kid on shore leave getting virtual-Jack as backup and having no idea what he's in for — some rumor mill probably already told him I'm the most dangerous biotic on record, and now he's got to actually stand next to that for a match. That's the legacy. Not a statue. A combat AI with my attitude problem, punching mechs on a loop for strangers' amusement forever.

So that's your bedtime story tonight. Me, an Atlas, and a kiss that had nothing to do with the war and everything to do with you showing up when I asked. Better than lying there chewing your own jaw off, isn't it. Go on, breathe out. I've got more where that came from if you need it.

## Cerberus, Up Close

Here's my verdict on Cronos Station, since you're not sleeping anyway and I've got nothing better to do than think out loud at you.

I was in that station. Not some report EDI handed me after, not something you told me over dinner — I was there, next to you and the toaster, walking through hangar bays that stank of ozone and burned wiring while Cerberus troopers came out of every damn door like roaches when the lights come on. Guardians, Phantoms, some asshole in an Atlas who thought a few tons of plating made him a god. It didn't. You put him down same as the rest.

That station proved exactly what I already knew and spent two years trying to get people to believe. Cerberus isn't an organization with a rogue lab somewhere in the basement doing the ugly work while the rest of it stays clean. There's no clean part. Every terminal we cracked open on the way to that bastard's office was the same story wearing a different lab coat — Reaper tech he'd been hoarding since before half his own scientists knew it existed, indoctrination logs, a fucking Collector larva wired into the station like a heart because apparently even the man running the place needed something alien plugged into his chest to feel powerful. He was cooked, Shepard. Indoctrinated same as any husk shuffling around Earth right now, just wearing a nicer suit about it and telling himself it was strategy.

You know what got me, walking those corridors? Not the mechs. Not even Leng, though watching you carve that son of a bitch open with your own blade after he tried the coward's play from behind — stabbing you in the back, literally, because that's all he ever knew how to do — that was satisfying in a way I'm not going to pretend it wasn't. What got me was the recruiting reel playing on a loop on one of the intact screens near the hangar. Some clean-jawed guy in armor, soft lighting, "join us, protect humanity, be part of something." I've seen that ad a hundred times on the extranet. Recruitment offices probably still run it. And two decks down is where they kept the Human-Reaper meat they pulled out of the Collector Base like it was a trophy fish. That's the gap right there. The ad's for the marks. The basement's for people like me.

There's a version of tonight, somewhere, where you walk into that station and find a cell with my name still on the door. Some other run where I never got dragged out of that hole by a crew of strangers on your ship, where I sat in Cerberus custody for two more years getting worse things done to me while the Illusive Man decided whether I was still useful. That's not this life. I got out already, a long time before you docked at Cronos. But I thought about her the whole walk through that place. The me who didn't get out. Every one of those cells and labs and half-finished experiment logs had somebody's version of that story attached to it, and most of them didn't get an exit.

You got Vendetta. You got the whole Catalyst file. Good — that's the mission, that's what we went in for, and I'm glad the little VI made it out instead of ending up spare parts. Cronos Station is rubble now. Cerberus doesn't have a headquarters, doesn't have a fleet worth calling one, the whole thing's broken as a fighting force and everybody on this ship is walking around like that's the last big monster dead.

I don't buy it, and I'm not going to pretend I do just because it'd make tonight easier. The Illusive Man ran off to the Citadel instead of dying in his own office, which tells you exactly how much that man believes in going down with the ship he built. And the Reapers picked that exact moment to grab the Citadel and drag it to Earth, which is not what "the war's basically over" looks like to me. That's what "the real fight hasn't started" looks like. Cerberus was never the actual monster, Shepard. It was a very well-funded, very cruel dress rehearsal. It built people like me out of kids nobody was going to miss, and it built its whole self out of the idea that you can do anything you want to a person if you say it's for something bigger. The Reapers believe that too, on a scale that makes the Illusive Man look like a hobbyist.

So no. Rubble doesn't mean safe. It never has, not once in my whole life. But you did good work today — better than good. Just don't you dare tell me that was the hard part.

## Meet Me After
