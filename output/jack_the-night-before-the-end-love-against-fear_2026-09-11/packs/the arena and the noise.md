Cerberus's research arm built it by combining the armor and firepower of a YMIR mech with the tactical judgment of a trained human pilot seated inside. An element zero core allowed it to be air-dropped onto a battlefield with minimal impact damage. Its thick armor included a transparent canopy of a proprietary polycrystalline composite that Alliance engineers hypothesized was a synthetic sapphire with crack- and heat-resistant interlayers. Though unwieldy in the field, its antipersonnel machine guns and heavy durability made it a serious threat, and unprepared encounters were expected to cause heavy casualties.

Offensively the Atlas carried a rocket launcher that dealt area damage plus burn-over-time, and a high-powered mass accelerator cannon fired in bursts (four shots in single-player, three in multiplayer). Destroyed Atlas mechs exploded violently, dealing severe damage through cover and terrain even after death. Defensively it had durable shields and armor, destructible armor plating on the shoulders, knees, and crotch, smoke grenades, and weak points at the canopy and rear exhaust port. It took 24% less damage from all Power Combos.

Recommended tactics included staying in cover, using armor-debuffing powers such as Expose-evolved Warp, Cryo Blast, and Proximity Mine, repeated Warp-Throw biotic explosions, Sabotage (to which Atlases built immunity over time), and the Cobra Missile Launcher in multiplayer for instant kills. In single-player, once its shields were down, sustained fire could shatter the canopy and leave the pilot, who had no more health than an Assault Trooper, vulnerable; killing the pilot could let Shepard hijack the Atlas and potentially earn an Achievement. Empty Atlas units could be found intact during Grissom Academy: Emergency Evacuation and Priority: Cerberus Headquarters. Per The Art of the Mass Effect Universe, it was first conceived as a heavier YMIR variant with a soldier inside before a protective canopy was added and the frame enlarged. - `summary:morality` [summary] (physical release through violence or music) — Morality was the reputation mechanic of the original Mass Effect trilogy, tracked as separate "Paragon" (blue, compassionate and heroic) and "Renegade" (red, apathetic and ruthless) scales rather than a single good-evil axis, so good acts did not cancel out bad ones. High scores unlocked significant "good" or "bad" dialogue options. In Mass Effect, Paragon and Renegade points unlocked ranks in the Charm and Intimidate skills, into which Shepard invested points to open new dialogue choices; thresholds at 10%, 25%, 50% and 75% granted skill points and combat bonuses, becoming a Spectre granted more, and reaching 80% of either unlocked the assignments UNC: Besieged Base (Paragon) or UNC: The Negotiation (Renegade). Character backgrounds set starting points. The Mass Effect ending's lighting, music (Vigil's or Sovereign's theme) and closing shot of Shepard varied by morality.

Mass Effect 2 replaced the skills, deriving Charm and Intimidate success directly from Paragon and Renegade percentages (points earned versus points available in explored areas), and added dialogue Interrupts: Paragon interrupts to spare, comfort or call out others, Renegade interrupts for physical or violent action. Paragon choices healed Shepard's facial scars while Renegade choices deepened them, and the final scene's background star turned blue or red depending on the Collector Base choice. Mass Effect 3 merged both into a single Reputation meter that both point types fed, retaining interrupts; the Control ending narration differed by Paragon or Renegade leaning. Mass Effect: Infiltrator used binary Paragon/Renegade choices for Randall Ezno that changed his scars and biotic color but were not tracked on a scale. Mass Effect: Andromeda abandoned the morality system entirely. The page also documents several infinite-point exploits.
