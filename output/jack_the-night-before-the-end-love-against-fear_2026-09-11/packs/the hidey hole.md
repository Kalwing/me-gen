Jack arrives aboard the Normandy broken: psychologically tortured, conditioned to enjoy killing, then used and discarded by everyone she met after escaping her captors, so she lives on edge waiting for betrayal. She refuses to move on from her past. Her tattoos mark prisons, memorable kills, personal reasons, and some "just because"; her haircut is from the cult she ran with. She wears little clothing deliberately, believing nearly everyone wants to use her for sex, and uses it to read intentions. Kelly Chambers notes she pushes people away yet approaches sex casually and warns Shepard to "think twice" if he wants her respect. Given her childhood, Jack is entirely desensitized to death and crime; Cerberus built a Pavlovian link between narcotic euphoria and killing, so she feels bliss where others feel regret, kills without hesitation ("Every time someone dies and it's not me, my chances of survival go up"), and would kill a hostage as the simpler option. Her "no regrets" stance borders on narcissism and a refusal of accountability; asked whether she would do anything differently, she answers a flat "No."

The real wound is that she wants a family and acceptance but pushes everyone away first. The one exception was Murtock, a man she loved and ran jobs with. After robbing a heavily guarded weapons frigate they were separated; Murtock came back for her rather than take the score, Jack escaped in a shuttle, and he was left behind. Days later the shuttle played his pre-recorded message about the future they were meant to have and how much he loved her. Jack outwardly denies fault but shows survivor's-guilt signs. Her view of relationships: "You let someone get that close, it just means they need a shorter knife."

If Shepard shows genuine, non-sexual interest, her shell cracks; sleeping with her early instead breaks trust. Wanting to move on, Jack decides to blow up Teltin. There she meets Aresh, a dark reflection also stuck in the past, who wants to restart the facility to give the suffering meaning. Shepard can argue Jack out of killing him; letting Aresh live and destroying the facility gives her a revelation about what she could have become. With continued emotional support a relationship forms; in place of a sex scene they only kiss and lie in bed together. After the Collector Base and Shepard's surrender to the Alliance, Jack is alone again until the Alliance offers her a teaching post at Grissom Academy training biotic children ("Teltin if it were run by good people"). She wears more clothing, grows out her hair, and argues for her students to serve in a defensive, protective role rather than as biotic artillery. Reunited with Shepard, she says his attitude rubbed off on her and that her students and Shepard are the closest thing to family she has had. On shore leave, doubt briefly reverts her to her old self before Shepard again refuses to leave her. Watts walked through numerous discarded and final human ship concepts, describing the design goal of making Mass Effect's ships look distinct from those in other games and films.

The final human dreadnought carried large forward cannons and deliberately reused Normandy elements, including matching wings and engines, so all human ships shared a visual family; wings could fold in to appear more aerodynamic for mass relay transit.

Watts described human fighter concepts, several derived from early Normandy sketches, and multiple early Normandy designs that leaned toward a freighter or smuggler look, with a rover bracketed in an open midsection or cargo containers sliding in from the rear; one referenced the Millennium Falcon's off-centered cockpit. The final human fighter again borrowed Normandy engines, intakes, wings, and cockpit. The final freighter used a cockpit main body with a long central spine and a variable number of attachable cargo canisters (from a few up to fifteen or twenty), and appears with strewn canisters in several levels. The final shuttle, referencing Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within, incorporated the Normandy engine and was intended for the Citadel hangar, though Watts was unsure it made it into the final game because ship textures were expensive and levels grew heavier during production.

Per the wiki's trivia, Wilson continued a BioWare tradition (like Corporal Jenkins before him) of an early squadmate-style character killed and effectively replaced by someone with the same abilities, in this case Miranda.
