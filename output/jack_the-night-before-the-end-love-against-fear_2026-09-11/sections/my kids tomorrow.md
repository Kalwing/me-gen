You're finally out, breathing slow, and I should be right behind you. I'm not. I'm sitting up with a datapad glowing blue on my knees, running a duty roster like it's going to rearrange itself if I stare hard enough. Third time tonight. Same names. Same units. I keep checking anyway.

This isn't new. I did this in the Purgatory, remember, back when the worst thing on the horizon was a coup that already failed. Shore leave, actual music playing, and there I am in a booth with a drink going warm, reading rosters for my kids instead of drinking it. You came and sat across from me and gave me that look — the one that means either keep at it or put it down, your call which — and I kept at it, because somebody had to, and because apparently that's who I am now. Dependable. I still hate the word. I blame you for it, boss man, don't think I've let that go.

You asked if I'd gone soft, fitting in snug to military life. I told you our little suicide squad taught me something about teamwork I didn't ask to learn. Two years running a bunch of Cerberus test-tube experiments through a facility that wanted to eat them, and somehow I come out the other side able to file paperwork on a schedule. Somebody should study that. Not Cerberus. Fuck Cerberus, forever, on principle, but somebody should.

Here's the part I actually fought for. When the brass wanted my kids on the front — artillery, biotic strike teams, throw the eighteen-year-olds at the meat grinder because their powers pool nice on paper — I said no. Loud. Repeatedly. I told them these kids trained for barrier work, not for dying pretty in a firefight report. So they went into the 103rd instead, spread out, holding shields over marines who'd otherwise be paste, modding ammo so some grunt's rifle actually does something useful for once. Nobody writes songs about ammo mods. Doesn't matter. That's the job. That's what I begged for.

The 103rd, for what it's worth, isn't some backwater posting I shoved them into to keep them safe and out of the way. Every marine in that outfit went through infantry training first, clerks included, officers who cut their teeth on the Skyllian Blitz running them through drills in every climate the Alliance could find — arctic one week, jungle rot the next. My kids ended up folded into the closest thing Earth's got left to a real special-forces division, and Kahlee had the nerve to tell command my teaching style was "energetic, if unorthodox," like that wasn't her polite way of saying I scream at children until they stop dying. Fine. Whatever gets them respected instead of pitied. I'll take unorthodox over dead every single time.

And don't think that makes me feel better, because it doesn't, not all the way. Support isn't the front line, and support still isn't safe, whatever the recruiting posters say — you put a kid behind a barrier under Reaper fire and the barrier can still fail, the position can still get overrun, the shuttle pulling them out can still not make it. I know that math same as I know everything else that's ever tried to kill me. I lied to myself for about a week that support meant safe. It doesn't mean safe. It means they get a better shot than dying for nothing on some hot LZ some colonel picked off a map he's never stood on.

They already proved it, on Palaven. My kids threw barriers for a krogan squad so the krogan could get close and start cracking skulls, and between the two of them they got a thousand turian civilians onto evac ships that would've burned with everybody still inside. A thousand people. Some biotic brats I was ready to write off as spoiled Ascension Project kids two years ago, out there holding a line so somebody's grandmother could get on a shuttle. I read that report and I had to walk it off in a supply corridor for ten minutes before I could say anything to anyone.

Tomorrow's the real one, though. Not Palaven. Earth. My kids are down there in that mess same as everybody's kids, and I don't get to punch anybody to make it better, and I don't get to run the roster from a booth in a bar this time — I get to sit here with you instead, useless, waiting for a comm channel to tell me who's still breathing.

So yeah. I'm scared. Say it once, quiet, and I'll deny it in the morning — I'm scared for them, all of them, Rodriguez with her mouth and the rest of the little shits who think I don't know they call me the Psychotic Biotic behind my back. I earned that name once for the wrong reasons. I'd like to earn it tomorrow for the right one — teaching them well enough that they all come home to argue with me about curfew again.

Put the light out. I'm not sleeping either, but I'll stop reading this thing if you make me. Come here. Let tomorrow be tomorrow's problem, for six more hours.
