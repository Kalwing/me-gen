## The Night They Fished Grunt Out of C-Sec

Grunt. I heard about the noodle stand before you'd finished paying for it. Half of C-Sec was telling the story by morning and none of them got it right, so tell me if I've got it wrong.

He was in Huerta because Utukku ate his company. Aralakh — best warriors from every clan, and clans brawled over who got sent. They held the tunnel so the queen could crawl out, and the rachni came in waves until there was nothing left holding anything. Grunt walked out of that alone. So they put him in a hospital bed on the Presidium ring, which is a stupid thing to do with a krogan. Bored krogan is worse than an angry one. He had somebody lower him out a window on a rope. Fell several storeys. Then went drinking.

You know what that statue is, Shepard? A krogan warrior standing in the lake by the Tower. The Council put it up because we bled the rachni out of their space for them, and after the Rebellions half the embassies petitioned to have it hauled down. Council said no. Said history's history whether you like it or not. First honest thing they ever did.

I used to stand under it and tell you what it meant. Proud and fierce nation, now brutes for hire to the highest bidder. Told Tali once that in two hundred years that statue might be the only proof we existed. Then you fixed that, so I don't have to say it anymore.

And that tank-bred pyjak climbed it. Didn't look at it. Climbed it, and had his picture taken sitting on it. That's the right way to treat a memorial to my people. You don't stand there being sad at it.

Then C-Sec turned up and he threw a bottle of ryncol at their shuttle. Ryncol burns, Shepard. Melts machinery too — 190 proof and acidic. Pilot jumped, Grunt took the shuttle, flew it into a noodle stand.

Then bought noodles. Hungry. Used your name to stay out of the brig.

Best night anybody had on this station in a year, and neither of us was invited.

## A Drink and a Bag of Ice

Three days I've been on this station arguing krogan expansion at the Council. Three days of asari asking where exactly my people intend to *put* the children, like we're a spill on the carpet. Sit — there, where I can see the door out of one eye.

Spent the afternoon at Khan's old place. Only room on the Strip that doesn't smell of committee. Dead arms dealer, varren racing piped off Tuchanka, a pit boss named Krud on the Quasar booths, and an announcement every ten minutes about not touching the waterfalls. They call them the hanar urinals. That's a proper establishment.

Pour. And the ice — the bag, not a cup. I've had this conversation with your species before.

You want to know what the cure cost me, Shepard? Sleep. Two of them cornered me on the shuttle up here. On the shuttle. Clan brands out, tracing lineage at me like a deed of sale. I've been shot at by the Blood Pack in tighter quarters and enjoyed it more. Every female on Tuchanka knows my part in it, and every one wants the firstborn out of me.

There's precedent. Shiagur — warlord, one of the last fertile females after the turians dropped it on us. Her males fought each other for the right, and when the turians put her down at Canrum at the end of the Rebellions, her admirers killed thousands out of spite. Council histories call it barbarism. It was arithmetic. Fertile was worth more than a fleet, and everyone knew it.

And Bakara encourages it. Encourages. She's a shaman — a week sealed in a cave in the dark with nothing but her own heartbeat, and she dug out with a shard of crystal she found down there. And she uses it on *me*. I told her the first one gets named Mordin. She didn't argue. Man went up a tower for us.

Turn your head. No — the other way. Thank you.

Ahh. That's better.

Korbal, Shepard. Victory or death.

...Ha. You would say that. No, you're right. I can't do the death half anymore. Nobody warns you about that — a thousand years I had nothing to lose but my own hide, and I spent it cheap in a dozen bad contracts. Now I've got a clan, and a woman, and something coming that isn't dead in the womb. First krogan since the genophage who's allowed to be afraid.

More ice.

## The Rite, and Who Earns a Name

A krogan gets one name at birth. Just the given half — Wrex, Grunt, whatever noise came out of somebody. The clan half you earn at the Rite, or you die without it and nobody puts your skull in the Hollows. That's what the whole thing is for.

Shaman sends you out, you strike the keystone. Varren first, always varren, a herd of them. Then klixen off a harvester you can't shoot down. Then the ground goes soft under your boots and you find out what you are.

Five minutes. Survive the maw five minutes and you get a clan.

I killed mine. Wrex went in. Urdnot Wrex came out.

You did the same standing krantt for Grunt, and Uvenk had to say out loud it hadn't been done in generations. Shields are nothing to a maw. Not the hard part. The hard part is that anything metal you hide behind is gone in one hit, so you pick the stone pillar and stay on it while it moves. Most run. Most get eaten running.

Grunt walked out Urdnot. Uvenk walked out dead.

Our Shaman gave up his name to take the job. Rites at every dawn and every dusk, forever. Grunt told him it sounded terrible. He agreed.

## One Last Party on the Citadel

Hold the bottle still, Shepard. My aim's fine, it's your hands that wander.

What I remember out of that apartment is Grunt's forehead. Up on the balcony with Zaeed and Javik and the traffic sliding past under us, and the tank-bred whelp calls me a fossil. A year out of the tank and he's got opinions about my age. So we settled it. Nobody outside gets a headbutt right — they think you swing. You don't swing. You drop your weight through your legs and meet him coming, and whoever's still upright was correct. He's got the thicker plate. I've got a hundred years of knowing where a young krogan puts his balance. Then you got between us like a C-Sec officer breaking up a varren pit, and Anderson's furniture lived. Call it a draw. I'll take a draw.

He told me the other thing quiet, after. Two of Aralakh Company's recruits didn't want him leading them. One had to be dealt with. The other got killed in a fight first. He said it the way you'd say the weather. That's the correct way to say it.

Then the talk got off your pilot and his missing sidearm, so I put my planet on the table. A day on Tuchanka, that's all. Nobody took it. Aralakh means eye of wrath — Raik word, old — and its fourth rock had jungles once, thick ones, rooted in shallow silty seas. Then we split the atom around ourselves and put out our own sun with the ash. Our own ice age after that, clans eating clans, and me not born yet to see the good part. Salarians turned up during the rachni war and handed us a resurgence, then took it back with interest. The smart ones sealed themselves in the deep bunkers to outbreed the genophage. It found nine in ten of them down there anyway. Now there's a tower standing out there holding the sky off us, and the CDEM sitting in orbit telling tourists nobody's coming to collect their bodies.

Krogan parties end in a few violent deaths. Yours ended with Grunt asleep under your shower saying he'd found a blue rock for Liara. So I said I needed to shoot something, and you didn't say no. Bottles on the counter — that TM88 swill was on every table anyway, Garrus dared you to drink it — and Javik and Zaeed lining up beside me. Vakarian had just finished telling your pilot that a gun he can't reach isn't a gun. That's why it counted, Shepard. Everybody in that room armed, drunk, and nothing to shoot but glass.

Because a week earlier it wasn't glass. Some hired analyst named Brooks — Cerberus, the one who wrote the dossiers that put the lot of you in the same room for the Collector run — decides her Illusive Man has gone soft in the head and wakes up the spare you. A copy Cerberus grew out of the leftovers, six months out of stasis and still learning how to be one. Ambushed you in a sushi restaurant. Walked the casino under your name. It wanted your Spectre codes and your command authority so it could take your ship off you, and it bought CAT6 to hold the Archives while it tried. You put every one of them down and it sealed you in a box you couldn't shoot your way out of anyway, then called your comms officer in your voice to have the Normandy warmed up. If that Glyph thing hadn't found the release you'd still be in there — refresh my memory, didn't you use to win these things? Its codes broke the moment somebody checked them. Brooks went to a cell instead of a grave. In the old days I'd have shot her.

And you reached for the thing wearing your face, and it let go rather than take your hand. It thought attachments were the weak part. It had that exactly backwards, and it went out the airlock proving it.

That photograph's still hanging by the bar down there. Everyone in the galaxy who'd stand next to you, in one frame. Tomorrow's Earth. Pour it.
