The following is a transcription of the fourth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary work The Final Hours of Mass Effect 3, published in 2012\.

## Contents

### Codename SFX

It was September 2003, almost a decade ago, when Casey Hudson sat down for lunch with Ray Muzyka and Greg Zeschuk at Yiannis Taverna on Whyte Avenue in Edmonton. Hudson was finally ready to share some of the ideas that had been stirring in his head about the team's next science fiction game. The working title the team came up with was SFX, and Hudson had great ambitions, as outlined in a one\-page document he presented to the doctors. The first line said it all: "With online capabilities and gameplay features that will redefine the genre, SFX will become a mainstream phenomenon and a must\-have game for the Xbox 2."

Hudson's vision was to create a game somewhere between the turned\-based role playing of KOTOR and the fast\-paced action of the sci\-fi shooter Halo. He imagined a rich story with memorable characters, exploration in a spaceship like his favorite game Starflight, and a robust online feature in which fans could trade and share the spoils of war over Xbox Live.

There were no details yet about the main character or the story, but Hudson did have one thing figured out: He wanted the doctors to commit to making a trilogy of games in this unknown and unnamed universe. Yes, he wanted BioWare to make its own Star Wars. So taken aback by Hudson's bold ambition, Muzyka burned his tongue on some hot soup at lunch and still remembers it to this day.

Although initially shocked at the bet\-the\-company scale of Hudson's proposal, the doctors couldn't fault him. The truth was, they'd long evangelized the idea of BioWare creating its own worlds, not borrowing others like D\&D and Star Wars. Microsoft, eager to publish a BioWare game after the success of KOTOR, expressed early interest in making the trilogy exclusive to the new Xbox. Hudson was given the green light to begin exploring ideas with his core team from KOTOR. A launch date was projected for about three and a half years down the road, in 2007\.

That may sound like a long time, but the team knew they couldn't rush the development of the game's overarching fiction. In fact, plans called for the core SFX team to spend the entire first year working on the universe with little regard for game development. Every day the team would sit around a conference room table for four hours and debate the most basic questions about their sci\-fi. How far in the future will it be set? Will there be aliens? What about time travel?

Karpyshyn remembers the discussions playing out like a real\-time message board thread where the team debated the merits of every recent sci\-fi property including Star Trek and Babylon 5. In one meeting Joss Whedon's *Firefly* was discussed. Hudson hadn't seen it, so he was given the DVDs to watch as homework. (He ended up liking it more than he thought he would.)

Eventually, the team zeroed in on the idea of a universe set in the near future, but with a twist that would allow for faster\-than\-light travel: the discovery of ancient alien technology. The hero would be modeled after a Jack Bauer or James Bond—like character who could act with impunity in outer space, doing whatever is necessary to keep intergalactic peace.

As discussions continued, Derek Watts began sketching ideas for the world and characters might look like. When he threw down a concept piece of a dinosaur with a gun, the team instantly fell in love and thus the Krogan \[sic] were born. Budget concerns made everyone realize they wouldn't be able to create male and female versions of all the aliens, so plans were drawn up to include an all\-female alien race, the Asari. \[sic] Watts also began conceptualizing ideas for Star City, a Manhattan\-like hub for the player's operations that would later be known as the Citadel.

In January 2004 Hudson and the team wrote a document titled "Master Slaves" that helped crystallize the overarching plot. The concept was that a now\-extinct alien race, then known as the Nazari, had left behind technology that unlocked the possibility of faster\-than\-light travel, interconnecting the galaxy like never before. Eventually, humans would discover this technology and the fact that they were a slave race created by the aliens to do their bidding, hence the name "master slaves." What the team still hadn't figured out was how this discovery would take place or how it could be turned into a three\-act story. Would the aliens somehow return to the present day? And if not, who would be the adversary?

Karpyshyn took a stab at answering some of these questions in his first pass at the story. Originally the game starred a human hero named Captain Anderson (yes, you read that right) who would face off against Saren, a villain with a mosquito's stinger coming out of the back of his head. Saren would lead a warmongering race of small bat\-like creatures called the Batarians who were hell\-bent on stealing the alien technology.

That head\-to\-head conflict between Anderson and Saren was a good start, but if the story of SFX was to span three games, the team needed a theme more universal than a race to find an alien artifact. That theme would soon emerge from an unlikely place: a Japanese research lab.

Developed by Japanese researcher Hideyuki Sawada, the KTR voice robot was an unnerving experiment. Sawada's goal was to see if a robot with soft silicone lips could imitate the sounds of the human mouth using artificial cords. Watamaniuk found a video of it on YouTube and started playing it in the team meetings. At first its repetitious annunciations were absurd and funny, but eventually the team began to think about its implications. Was Raymond Kurzweil right? Within 30 years could we really reach that point of singularity where man and machine are indistinguishable?

If that crucible is forthcoming, the tension between organic and synthetic life could be an appropriate theme to frame the SFX trilogy. "When you think about it, any civilization out there that's more advanced than us is probably peaceful," says Hudson. "Everyone has to
go through the crucible of developing nuclear weapons and either destroying themselves or passing through that." At a high level, Mass Effect could be the story of the next crucible a civilization faces, when technology rivals organic life.

A rise of the machines plot is nothing new in the annals of science fiction, but the team came up with a unique twist to build on the master slaves idea. Perhaps the machines had risen to power long ago, and now, ominously lurking in deep space, decide to return and harvest sentient life as fodder for a machine army. To strengthen the machine versus human overtones, they excised the bat army in favor of a robot army named the Geth. \[sic] At first Watts and his team imagined the Geth as organic beings with synthetic fluid running through their veins, similar to Bishop from Aliens. Eventually, however, the Geth evolved into the current robot\-like form.

After a year and half of deselopment the game still was carrying the codename SFX. The team debated a series of names such as Space Age, Element Zero, Threshold and the Epsilon Effect, but could never come to an agreement. One day Hudson walked into the room and said, "What would you guys think of naming the game Rainbow?" Everyone was momentarily shocked until Hudson finished his thought: "Rainbow....Six, the Tom Clancy games." The point was that the name didn't really matter. Eventually co\-founder Zeschuk surfaced the name Mass Effect, which tied into the idea of the science behind the game's "mass relay" time travel devices, and the concept of one individual being able to change the course of history.

Two years into development Mass Effect was starting to take shape as a science fiction property. The actual game, however, was in a far worse state. In order to up the action, BioWare had licensed Epic Games' Unreal Engine, the same technology behind the Gears of War series (and Infinity Blade on iPad). Focusing on action was an uncharted territory for the team, and there were growing pains as they tried to balance tactical combat with fast\-paced action. Levels had been designed on planets such as the snowy world of Noveria, but there was little in the way of focus. Hudson would play the level and then call in Watamaniuk to ask, "What exactly is supposed to be happening on this level?" No one knew the answer. The levels were being built in a vacuum, with little knowledge of how they'd eventually fit into the story.

Frustrated, Hudson came into the office one Saturday during the summer of 2005 and realized that the game wasn't making the progress he wanted to see. The plot wasn't fully fleshed out, the gameplay was unfocused, and no one was happy with the design of the main character, now known as Commander Shepard, who looked a bit like a medic, not the Jack Bauer of space. After years of experimentation, the pressure was starting to mount. Microsoft was planning to announce the game at a press event in October 2005\.

**SFX (September 15, 2003\)**

---

- A spectacular new Sci\-Fi roleplaying game and IP created by BioWare. With online capabilities and gameplay features that will redefine the genre, SFX will become a mainstream phenomenon and a must\-have game for the Xbox2\.

**Imagine**

- SFX takes place in a thrilling futuristic setting, with a combination of familiar locations and exotic worlds.
- It is the dawn of the interstellar age, with only a few explored worlds and many more accessible via exploration.
- Sleek vehicles and vistas create a uniquely identifiable look to the universe, while rendering techniques depict the player's experience with a stylish ultra\-realism.

**Play**

- An epic BioWare story will be at the center of the powerful, character driven story.
- Gameplay will build on the elements from SW:KotOR \- but will be more accessible to the mainstream audience.
- Players will lead a small party of 2 or 3, selected from a larger group of adventurers. Players can switch to control other party members at any time, and characters not controlled by the player will perform player\-designated actions and AI scripts.
- The combat system will allow dazzling combat choreography and intense ranged combat, but will also be more responsive than a round\-based system.
- Players will be able to freely explore a galaxy of procedurally generated planets in an upgradeable starship, to obtain valuable items and resources. Players can explore areas freely, or travel to special locations as part of the single\-player story.
- Xbox2 Live support will allow an additional dimension of gameplay through trading and communicating with players around the world.
- Story progression, planetary exploration, and online trading will create an emergent style of gameplay that is unprecedented in the RPG genre.
- Because of the Xbox 2 Live capability and the procedural galaxy exploration system, the game continues limitlessly beyond the end of the single\-player story.

**Trade**

- Through Xbox2 Live, you can meet friends online in special locations in the game to chat and trade valuable items in a virtual environment.
- Some items found in the game will be different from one Xbox2 system to another, and they can be combined with other items online to create powerful new items not available without online trading.
- A dynamic in\-game economy will make trading online compelling, as precious items may be valued differently from one Xbox2 system to another.
- Xbox2 Live can also tie the entire SFX community together in other exciting ways, such as the addition of new locations, special items, new quests, and online news updates about the best players.

**The SFX Project \- Development Strengths**

- SFX will be developed by the team that developed SW:Kotor. Their experience will be directly applied to making a bigger and better RPG franchise on the Xbox2\.
- SFX will be developed on a new engine, custom tailored to push the Xbox2 to its limits through a focus on realistic lighting, physics, and render quality.

**Target Release: 2007**

**Master Slaves (January 8, 2004\)**

---

1. **Hook**: Humanity has recently drawn the attention of the many powerful alien species competing for control of the galaxy, all of whom have based their cultures on the not fully understood technology left behind by a now extinct creator race. Though scientifically inferior, humanity has suddenly become an important species in the galactic community because of our inherent ability to use this ancient technology.

**Extra details:**
Travel across great distances is accomplished through the use of jump stations/hyper gates left behind by the creator race. Upon discovering and activating one of these gates on Mars, humanity has opened up our system to the galactic community as a whole.

Within the galaxy there are numerous powerful alien empires struggling against each other to expand their spheres of influence. These aliens have established a galactic council on Star City, a gigantic space station that serves as the political and economic hub of the galaxy. Each of these species sees humanity and their affinity for the creator technology as both a potential threat and a potential resource that can be exploited for their own advancement.
2. **Basic Criteria**:
a. **Concept Twist**: Humans were engineered as the slave class of the creator race millennia ago.
b. **Special Character**: You an \[sic] elite human that has become an important tool to both the Earth and the aliens.
c. **Exploration**: The character is able to explore the galaxy in search of Creator Artifacts, and answers to the relationship between the Humans, The Creators, and their technology. The player will visit established worlds of the various alien empires, as well as exploring uncharted or undeveloped areas to establish new jump stations and expand the galactic frontier.
d. **Moral Compass**: The character could work for some military/civilian agency that is trying to establish humanity in the greater galactic community. The Star City is a central nexus for explorers like the character hunting down important artifacts and information. Instead of constantly returning to Earth an embassy could be established so that the character has easy access to aliens and agency \[sic] they serve.
3. **Start Location**: Earth
4. **Time Period**: Near Future
5. **Setting**: Familiar technology but enhanced via the alien technology on earth. \[sic] Alien technology can be more advanced and unfamiliar.
6. **Space Travel**: The character travels locally by using faster than light drive. However for long distance trips Jump Gates are used. These gates are a good way of opening up new zones of exploration for the player. You can travel between stars locally out to X light years but if you want to travel a greater distance a gate must be used. In this way we could open up new star clusters to explore and better control the flow of the game. Each cluster could also have an over arching theme, story\-line or purpose.
7. **Aliens**:
a. Quantity: Aliens are common once the player leaves Earth
b. Species: Many different species.
c. Interaction: Interact similar to modern nation states.
d. Technology: Varying levels of technology.

### Who's Who: Friends and Enemies

- **David Anderson**: Admiral David Edward Anderson is a career military officer in the Systems Alliance Navy. Born in London in 2137, he later moved to Arcturus Station and became the first graduate of the Alliance's now\-renowned N7 marine program. Anderson is voiced by long\-time video game actor Keith David.
- **Steven Hackett**: As the top\-ranking official of the Alliance Navy and commanding officer of the Fifth Fleet, Admiral Hackett is in constant communication with Commander Shepard. He is responsible for mobilizing the Alliance fleet to fend off the Reaper attacks on Earth.
- **Illusive Man**: The self\-appointed protector of humanity, the Illusive Man is the mysterious leader of Cerberus, a shadow organization that created the Lazarus Project to revive Commander Shepard during the events of Mass Effect 2. While Shepard worked for him in the second game, Mass Effect 3 finds the two at odds.
- **Kai Leng**: As the Illusive Man's top assassin, Kai Leng is a deadly Earthborn operative for Cerberus who has a take no prisoners attitude. He is seen for the first time in Mass Effect 3 as he battles against Shepard across multiple planets.
- **Saren**: Dead by the end of Mass Effect 1, Saren was a highly decorated Turian \[sic] Spectre who defended galactic peace at all costs. That all changed when he formed an alliance with the machines and was brainwashed into turning against organic life.
