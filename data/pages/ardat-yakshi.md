**Ardat\-Yakshi**, meaning 'Demon of the Night Winds' in an old asari dialect, is a rare genetic condition in asari, specifically affecting their nervous systems.

## Background

*See also: Ardat\-Yakshi Codex Entry*
The condition does not harm the afflicted asari herself; during mating, the Ardat\-Yakshi's nervous system completely overpowers that of her mate's, causing hemorrhaging in the victim's brain, and ultimately death in extreme cases. The Ardat\-Yakshi becomes smarter, stronger, and deadlier after each encounter. Ardat\-Yakshi who "feed" on their victims in this way have an innate ability to influence and even dominate the minds of others, a talent that grows stronger with repeated mating and allows them to find new partners. Ardat\-Yakshi feel compelled to mate, and the compulsion grows stronger each time they succeed until it becomes a full\-blown addiction.

Ardat\-Yakshi often exhibit a lack of empathy that can reach psychopathic levels, believed to stem from the inability to properly meld with others as most asari can. Even in less severe cases on the "AY spectrum" where mating is not necessarily lethal, Ardat\-Yakshi have difficulty cooperating with others or maintaining stable relationships. Ardat\-Yakshi with the lethal form of the condition who continue to seek fresh mates can cause "astronomical" death tolls if not stopped, potentially even reaching into the tens of thousands of victims over the course of their lifetimes.

The condition is inherited and congenital, but impossible to fully diagnose until adolescence at the earliest, by which time it is too late to correct. When identified, Ardat\-Yakshi are given an ultimatum: live in isolation or face execution. These extreme measures are ostensibly justified by the need to protect both afflicted individuals and society. However, there is another reason for this hardline policy. While some primitive asari cultures revered the Ardat\-Yakshi as gods of destruction, in modern times they are viewed as an embarrassment, a disgrace the asari go to great lengths to hide from public view, especially from non\-asari. Ardat\-Yakshi are only born of unions between two asari, which may explain the prejudice against purebloods, at least in part. Ardat\-Yakshi themselves are sterile and cannot produce offspring.

Ardat\-Yakshi imprisoned in monasteries often turn their energies towards useful pursuits. Their manufactured produce often ends up on the galactic market as sought\-after commodities, like asari honey marinade, or asari honey mead. Some monastery residents are allowed strictly supervised visitation to Thessia, out of the belief that they have the right to know their homeworld. However, if an Ardat\-Yakshi is judged to be of unsuitable temperament to leave the monastery, this offer is not extended.

## Mass Effect 2

Falere, Rila, and Morinth, all daughters of Samara, have the active form of the Ardat\-Yakshi condition. Morinth chose to embrace her condition, while Falere and Rila opted for a life of peaceful seclusion. According to Samara, her children are the only three living Ardat\-Yakshi, though she was likely referring only to the most dangerous form of the condition. Around 1% of the asari population fall somewhere on a spectrum where mating is not always lethal, which is considered a latent form of the Ardat\-Yakshi condition that allows them to live largely undetected within asari society.

While it is not known how much an Ardat\-Yakshi's strength increases after each mating, or whether the victim makes any difference, Morinth's continuous use of her condition over centuries allows her to match her justicar mother's biotic power despite the large difference in age, experience, and formal training between them. Morinth rationalizes her lifestyle, claiming that the Ardat\-Yakshi are the "genetic destiny" of the asari and that her mates experience otherworldly bliss in their final moments.

If Shepard mates with Morinth, the Commander is struck dead almost immediately, becoming another victim of the condition.

## Mass Effect 3

An Ardat\-Yakshi monastery on Lesuss comes under attack from the Reapers, the objective being to capture those afflicted by the condition and turn them into Banshees. Asari High Command dispatched several commandos to investigate, but when they failed to report back in High Command implicitly tasks Commander Shepard to investigate. If she survived the suicide mission, Samara also travels to the monastery to check on her daughters Rila and Falere.

If Shepard encounters her, Samara indicates that she is there not only out of motherly concern, but because her duties as a justicar demand it. The Justicar Code apparently requires justicars to put to death any Ardat\-Yakshi that cannot be safely sequestered in a monastery. However, Samara finds herself unable to follow through with this and attempts suicide rather than be forced to execute Falere, her last living child. If Samara is stopped by Shepard, she will eventually agree to Falere's plan to continue living in total isolation at the ruined monastery.

If Samara is not present, Falere is left behind at the monastery as its sole survivor, or she is shot by the Commander for being a security risk should the Reapers return to convert her.

## Mass Effect Andromeda: Annihilation

The Ardat\-Yakshi condition apparently has a viral component, as it is one of the many diseases that went into the making of the highly contagious Fortinbras Plague.

## Trivia

- 'Ardat' may be a reference from the Akkadian word Ardat\-Lili, in turn associated with the Middle Eastern storm demon Lilith. 'Yakshi' may be a reference to Yaksha, benevolent nature spirits found in Hindu, Jain and Buddhist Mythologies. Specifically, 'Yakshi' (from the broader classification of nature spirits known as Yaksha) was an ancient Indian deity portrayed as a female earth spirit, symbolizing fertility in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain faiths. Yakshi are portrayed more as deadly seducers in South Indian tradition, particularly around Kerala.
- Ardat\-Yakshi may be based off the medieval legends of a succubus, a female demon who takes the form of a human woman in order to seduce men, usually through sexual intercourse, resulting in the deterioration of health or death.
