The following is a transcription of the ninth chapter of Geoff Keighley's documentary work The Final Hours of Mass Effect 3, published in 2012\.

## Contents

### The Illusive Date

"Only one thing is certain: If Shepard doesn't bring help soon, there won't be an Earth left to save." With that line of dialogue from an unnamed soldier pinned down in London during an all\-out Reaper invasion, Mass Effect 3 was officially announced in December 2010 at the Spike Video Game Awards for a holiday 2011 release. Fans were thrilled that the final chapter of the trilogy was less than a year from release, but back in Edmonton the team was starting to worry about its aggressive deadline relative to the scope of the project.

By March those worries turned into real concern. Aaron Flynn, BioWare's general manager, had risen through the programming ranks at the company after giving up a career as a fruit fly geneticist. As he grappled with Dragon Age II, a BioWare product that was not turning out quite as the studio had hoped, in March 2011 he also faced a roomful of Mass Effect developers who expressed concern about hitting the promised holiday release date. Walking back to his office, he called Muzyka and left a message saying that they should talk right away. Within 24 hours Flynn was on the line with Frank Gibeau, the president of Electronic Arts' games label, explaining that things were not coming together as quickly as the team had hoped. The decision was made to move the game's launch out of a crowded holiday season and into March of 2012\.

The team had bought a few more months of development, but Watamaniuk and Hudson knew that even with extra time they needed to make a few trims to the story. "It's not how much work you've done, it's how much work you have left to do," Watamaniuk told the team in a meeting at which the scope of the game was discussed. After much deliberation, what was known as the first "CAT" or catalyst mission, where the player would meet a Prothean alien for the first time, was removed from the overarching plot. The mission would later be completed as post\-release content, but to many on the team it was a heartbreaking loss.

Of course no decision on a game like Mass Effect is made in isolation. The removal of the Prothean mission had a domino effect on other elements of the story. In the original plot a key mission would take place on the planet Thessia, the Asari \[sic] home world. Part of that mission was to involve the Illusive Man kidnapping the Prothean and extracting information from him. But due to the character's removal from the main story, the designers needed to change around the Thessia mission so the Illusive Man picks up the Prothean data in another way.

Other changes were made as well. Originally, the game's second CAT mission was planned as the downfall of Thessia, to be followed by CAT 3, a Citadel\-based mission in which Shepard finds out that Cerberus is taking over the space station. Now the team was questioning that order. Cerberus was showing up too late in the plot, and there needed to be a mission later in the story that would remind players of the grim state of the galaxy. The decision was made to switch the order of these two key missions. Once again this had story implications, as any character who died during the Citadel mission would now be unavailable to appear on Thessia.

As the seasons began to change in Edmonton and spring arrived, the gameplay team worked to further tighten the combat with the addition of grenades that forced players to keep moving and not stay too long in one cover position. The multiplayer mode was an immense help for the single player campaign as well, as more designers were working around the clock to refine the core action mechanics.

Speaking of high\-stakes action, for months the team couldn't quite figure out how they wanted to deal with the threat of the Illusive Man and his inevitable showdown with Shepard. A Cerberus assassin named Kai Leng — with a snake tattoo on his back — would be introduced as Shepard's foil throughout much of the story, but ultimately everyone knew there needed to be a resolution to the philosophical battle between the Illusive Man, who wants to control the Reapers to protect humanity, and Shepard, who wants them outright destroyed. For a few months there were plans on the table for the Illusive Man to be further indoctrinated by the Reapers near the end of the game and turn into a larger, more cybernetic monster the player would have to battle. Although art was created for this sequence, it was ultimately dropped because it felt too predictable to end the series on a massive boss battle.

While work continued in Edmonton, in early June Hudson and Corey Gaspur jumped on a plane and headed to Los Angeles for the annual E3 video game expo. Appearing on stage at Electronic Arts' annual press conference, Hudson announced that Mass Effect 3 would arrive on March 6, 2012, and demonstrated a sequence with Shepard battling a monstrous Reaper on the Quarian \[sic] home world of Rannoch. For the next three days the team demonstrated the game to the press, retailers and special guests, at times sneaking up to 30 people in a demo room designed for 11\.

Returning from Los Angeles, the team took a few weeks to slow down before the final push began in earnest. Enjoying the warm Edmonton summer, Hudson went out one night for a jog and listened to John Carpenter's *Escape from New York* soundtrack on his iPod. Another night he just stayed at home and tried to learn how to play his favorite new song, *Death Cab for Cutie*'s "You Are a Tourist." As he thought about the game he realized that there was still much to do, and started to accept that some player requests, like a mission set on the Turian \[sic] home world of Palaven, just weren't going to be possible
this time around.

The next six months were going to be a brutal and unforgiving race to the finish line. So before he was lost to the world of triage meetings, bug reports and 15\-hour days, Hudson wanted to clear his mind by taking his wife out for a romantic flight over the mountains of Jasper. "Went for a little flight above the Rockies with my sweetie," he Tweeted from his iPhone on Canada Day, July 1\.

Flying through the clouds, Hudson felt completely in control of the world around him. As he landed back at the airport he pulled his plane into the hanger, locked it up and knew he probably wouldn't be back as soon as he wished. The hardest days on Mass Effect 3 were still to come — although Hudson had no idea just how bad things were about to get.

### Video: Casey Explains Why They Abandoned the Illusive Man Sequence

**Casey Hudson**: Well, resolving the storyline is one thing... And then, the other side of what we need to resolve in a video game, is resolving the gameplay. And... you know, you're progressing, you're building up your powers and getting stronger as a character. And then, what do you do with that in the end? And usually, you fight some sort of boss. And sometimes, it's a giant tentacle creature, regardless of what your story is really about. And, you know... So, obviously, some of the original ideas for where you would end up at the end of the story involved some kind of giant tentacle creature that would come out of nowhere and be the boss for Mass Effect 3. But, you know, it was just so video\-gamey, and nothing that we did to, you know, kind of work it into the themes, really answered the fact that ultimately it just felt like there was an end boss for the sake of having an end boss. And that raises the question of, you know, like, if video games are a medium for storytelling, do they always have to have an end boss?

### Who's Who: The Mass Effect 3 Squad

- Ashley Williams: Recently promoted to Spectre status, Lieutenant Commander Ashley Williams is a career military officer with the Systems Alliance Marines and a potential love interest for the Commander. Born to a military family, Williams enlisted directly out of high school, splitting time between Earth and hostile environment training on Saturn's moon, Titan.
- EDI: Voiced by actress Tricia Helfer, EDI (Enhanced Defense Intelligence) is the Normandy's on\-board artificial intelligence known for her "equitable working relationship" with Joker, the ship's captain. \[sic] In Mass Effect 3 she takes on a physical form by gaining control of a Cerberus infiltration unit's body.
- Garrus Vakarian: A turian who began military training at age 15, Garrus is a C\-Sec security officer who headed up the investigation of Saren's rogue activities in the first Mass Effect. Posing as the tactical genius Archangel on Omega in Mass Effect 2, Garrus reunites with the player both in that game and for Mass Effect 3\.
- James Vega: New for Mass Effect 3, James is one of the best soldiers in the Alliance. He's young, brash and dangerous. Some see Commander Shepard's newest recruit as reckless, but James will defend Earth to his dying breath. He is voiced by actor Freddie Prinze Jr and was inspired by the soldiers in the book WAR by Sebastian Junger.
- Kaidan Alenko: Kaidan commands 1st Special Operations Biotic Company, a specialized arm of covert operations within the Alliance Army. At the Alliance Special Warfare Center and School, he takes the best and the brightest from the Ascension Project at Grissom Academy and trains them to be elite biotic soldiers.
- Liara T'Soni: Dr. Liara T'Soni is an Asari \[sic] information broker with a background in scientific research on Prothean technology. Born on Thessia in 2077, she is the only child of the late Matriarch Benezia, although mother and daughter became estranged in the years before Benezia was indoctrinated by the Reaper known as Sovereign.
- Tali'Zorah nar Rayya: A fan favorite, Tali is a Quarian \[sic] whose masked face has never been seen by players. Born aboard a spaceship, she—like all Quarians—must wear an envirosuit due to her weak immune system. In Mass Effect, Tali discovers that Saren is responsible for the attacks on Eden Prime and shares this information with the player.
